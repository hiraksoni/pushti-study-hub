Project 1: Plant Nursery (पौधशाला) Pages 3–24

Setting up a school nursery, germination factors, rag-doll seed testing, potting soil, and stem cuttings.

Objectives & Core Concepts

A plant nursery is a dedicated, protected space where young plants (saplings/seedlings) are propagated, nurtured, and conditioned until they are strong enough to be transplanted into open gardens or farms.

Why Nurseries are Essential
  • Species Preservation: Multiplying native and endangered varieties safely.
  • Large Scale Production: Growing uniform, healthy seedlings simultaneously.
  • Climate Protection: Shielding tender sprouts from intense sun, drying winds, or torrential rain.
  • Pest Control: Managing nutrients and organic pests in a compact, controlled area.
Key Environmental Factors
  • Optimal Temperature: Enzymes require a specific warmth range for embryo awakening.
  • Humidity: High air moisture prevents seedling dehydration (like a water bowl placed near a winter heater/bukhari).
  • Soil Moisture: Balanced water without standing puddles that cause fungal damping-off.
  • Diffused Light: Direct midday sun scorches new shoots; agro shade-net provides soft light.

Activities 2 & 3: Site Layout & Shade-Net Installation

Activity 2: Site Selection & Zoning
  • Choose a flat, well-drained patch receiving 4–6 hours of morning sunlight.
  • Ensure close access to a functional water tap/hose.
  • Zone A: Germination seedbeds & seedling trays.
  • Zone B: Polybag potting and hardening area.
  • Zone C: Tool shed and compost/sand storage.
Activity 3: Erecting the Shade-Net
  • Clear weeds, debris, and gravel with a garden rake and hoe.
  • Dig 4 corner holes (minimum 40 cm deep); insert 7-foot sturdy bamboo poles.
  • Stretch 50% green agro-net across the top and sides.
  • Bind firmly with coir rope or binding wire to withstand wind gusts.

Activity 4: Seed Viability Testing (Rag-Doll Method)

Before sowing seeds in bulk, testing viability ensures that only high-quality seed lots are planted:

  1. Cut a 30 × 30 cm piece of porous cotton cloth or thick blotting sheet.
  2. Using a pencil, mark a grid with 3 × 3 cm squares.
  3. Soak the cloth in water; allow all excess dripping water to drain.
  4. Count exactly 100 seeds (e.g., moong, gram, methi, tomato) and place one seed per square.
  5. Roll the cloth into a loose cylinder ("rag doll"). Secure both ends with rubber bands.
  6. Keep in a warm, shaded spot for 3–5 days, misting lightly daily to keep damp.
  7. Unroll carefully, count how many seeds have sprouted healthy radicles (roots), and calculate:
Germination Percentage (%) = [ Number of Germinated Seeds / Total Seeds Tested (100) ] × 100

Example: If 88 seeds out of 100 sprouted healthy roots, germination rate is 88% (Excellent quality for nursery bed sowing).

Activity 5: Plant Raising & Vegetative Stem Cuttings

Method A: Raised Seedbeds & Polybags
  • Raised Beds: Build bed 15 cm above ground level (width 1 m) for rapid gravity drainage during rains.
  • Draw furrows 5–10 cm apart, sow thinly, cover with fine sieved compost.
  • Polybags: Punch 2–3 drainage holes at bottom of 4×6 inch bags. Fill with 1:1:1 potting mix.
Method B: Vegetative Stem Cuttings (4 Steps)
  1. Select mature, pencil-thick semi-hardwood stem (Rose, Hibiscus, Bougainvillea, Money Plant).
  2. Make a clean 45° slanted cut just below a growth node using sharp secateurs.
  3. Remove lower leaves; dip cut end in natural rooting promoter (aloe vera gel, honey, or cinnamon).
  4. Insert 3–5 cm deep into potting mix; firm soil and keep under shade until new leaf buds emerge.

Activities 6 & 7: Growth Monitoring & Unit Economics

Activity 6: Weekly Growth Record (Table 1.2)
  • Record date of sowing and date of first seedling emergence.
  • Measure stem height in cm weekly using a ruler.
  • Count total healthy leaves per plant.
  • Note pests (aphids, leaf miners) and apply organic neem oil spray.
Activity 7: Cost Calculation (Table 1.3)
Cost per Viable Sapling = Total Inputs (Seeds + Polybags + Soil + Shade-Net) / Healthy Saplings Produced

Teaches resource budgeting, financial literacy, and valuing skilled horticultural labor.

Nursery Safety Protocols

  • Hand Hygiene: Wash hands thoroughly with soap after handling manure, fertilizers, or potting soil.
  • Tool Handling: Always carry secateurs, trowels, and khurpis with sharp points facing downward. Never run with tools.
  • Ergonomics: Bend knees when lifting heavy soil bags or water cans to avoid back strain.
  • Gloves: Wear thick gardening gloves when pruning thorny stems (like rose bushes).

Project 2: School Habitat Garden (विद्यालय पर्यावास वाटिका) Pages 25–48

Creating micro-habitats to feed, shelter, and invite birds, butterflies, bees, and soil organisms into school campuses.

Coexistence with Local Wildlife

Unlike ornamental gardens grown solely for visual flower arrangements, a Habitat Garden is planned to supply the 4 vital life needs for local fauna: Food, Water, Shelter, and Safe Space.

Welcome Campus Visitors

Purple Sunbirds, House Sparrows, Bulbuls, Lime Butterflies, Plain Tigers, Honeybees, Solitary Mason Bees, Earthworms, and Garden Calotes Lizards.

Ecological Balance

Birds and ladybugs eat harmful crop pests (aphids, caterpillars). Bees and butterflies pollinate school garden fruit trees and flowers.

Act 3 & 4: 4 Ecological Layers & Animal Needs

  • Canopy Layer (Trees): Doves, squirrels, bats roosting and shade.
  • Understory Shrub Layer: Babblers, praying mantises, and tailorbirds foraging and hiding.
  • Herbaceous Layer (Flowers): Continuous nectar supply from spring to winter for bees.
  • Ground & Litter Layer: Decomposing fallen leaves retaining earthworm moisture and soil fertility.

Zone 1 & 2: Pollinator Patch & Water Dish

  • Set shallow terracotta plates (2–3 cm deep). Add flat landing stones so bees can sip water without drowning. Change water daily!
Zone 3 & 4: Bug Hotel & Mud-Puddle
  • Insect Hotel: Repurpose a wooden crate; fill with hollow bamboo canes, dry pinecones, and drilled wood blocks for solitary bees.
  • Mud Puddling: A small wet sand-and-salt patch where butterflies sip essential sodium minerals.

Act 7, 8 & 9: Fauna Logbook, Maintenance & Eco-Walks

Maintain observation table: Date • Species Spotted • Count • Activity (Feeding, Resting, Collecting Twigs) • Weather Conditions.

Conduct student-guided eco-walks for primary grade classes, explaining how each plant supports a specific bird or insect.

Safety & Non-Toxic Deterrence

  • Zero Pesticides: Chemical insect sprays kill beneficial pollinators and poison birds.
  • Mosquito Prevention: Never allow water in bird baths to sit uncleaned for more than 24 hours.
  • Bee Etiquette: Observe active hives from a calm distance; never throw stones or swat at bees.

Project 3: Tie and Dye (बंधेज एवं रंगाई) Pages 51–82

Indian Bandhani, Leheriya, Shibori, natural kitchen dyes (beetroot, turmeric), swatch portfolios, and product costing.

Indian Textile Heritage & Traditional Motifs

Tie and dye relies on resist-dyeing: sections of fabric are bound tightly so dye cannot penetrate, forming crisp patterns.

Regional Traditions
  • Bandhani / Bandhej (Gujarat & Rajasthan): Derived from Sanskrit bandh ("to bind"). Practiced in Kutch, Jamnagar, and Jodhpur.
  • Leheriya (Rajasthan): Diagonal wave patterns celebrating monsoon and Teej.
  • Sungudi (Madurai, Tamil Nadu): Fine dot patterns crafted by migrated Saurashtrian weavers.
  • Shibori (Japan): Ancient stitch-resist and pleat-clamping methods.
Traditional Bandhani Motifs
  • Ekdali: Single dot motif.
  • Tikunthi: Triangular cluster of 3 dots.
  • Chaubandi: Diamond arrangement of 4 dots.
  • Satbandi: Auspicious cluster of 7 dots.
  • Dungar Shahi: Mountain peak pyramid arrangements.

Tying Techniques (Practice Swatches)

Technique 1: Classic Bandhani (Seed Binding)
  1. Draw dots on pure bleached cotton fabric with water-washable pencil.
  2. Insert a mustard seed or pulse grain beneath the dot.
  3. Pinch fabric into a sharp cone peak.
  4. Wind strong cotton thread 10–15 times tightly around base without cutting; jump to next dot.
Technique 2: Shibori Stitch & Pleat
  • Nui Shibori (Stitch-Resist): Make simple running stitches along lines with thick thread; pull tight into dense accordions; double knot firmly.
  • Leheriya Folding: Accordion pleat fabric diagonally into a strip; bind tightly at 2 cm intervals with rubber bands or string.

The 5-Step Professional Dyeing Cycle

  1. Pre-Soaking: Dip bound dry cloth in lukewarm water so dye solution penetrates uniformly up to the tight threads.
  2. Warm Dye Bath: Immerse tied cloth into warm dye liquor (60°C–70°C) for 20–30 minutes, turning continuously with wooden tongs.
  3. Cold Rinse: Remove, squeeze gently, and rinse under cold running water until rinse water runs clear.
  4. Shade Drying: Dry completely in a breezy, shaded area (direct harsh sunlight fades natural dyes).
  5. The Big Reveal (Untying): Once fully dry, cut threads carefully using blunt-end embroidery scissors (avoid cutting fabric). Unfurl cloth to reveal crisp white resist rings and vivid dyed fields!

Ethical AI & Digital Privacy

  • Data Privacy: Never upload personal faces, addresses, or private photos without consent.
  • Bias Elimination: If training a dog classifier with only white dogs, the AI will fail to recognize black dogs. Data must be balanced and diverse.
  • Environmental Awareness: Training massive AI models requires heavy electricity and cooling water in server farms.

Act 5: 9-Step Paper-Mâché Head Sculpting

  1. Crumple waste newspaper into a tight, firm sphere (size of a cricket ball).
  2. Wrap tightly with thread or masking tape to fix the round sphere.
  3. Insert a hollow cardboard cylinder or 1 cm PVC pipe into base for finger/rod control; glue firmly.
  4. Mix flour (*maida*) or PVA white glue with water to thin creamy consistency.
  5. Tear newspaper strips into 1×3 inch ribbons (torn feathered edges blend seamlessly).
  6. Dip strips into glue paste; smooth over sphere in 4–5 overlapping crisscross layers.
  7. Roll small wet paper pulp cones to sculpt 3D nose, chin, ear ridges, and brow; paste firmly.
  8. Allow head to dry in the sun for 24–48 hours until completely hard.
  9. Apply white primer coat; paint expressive eyes, brows, mustache with acrylic colors; seal with varnish!

The 5 Pillars of Family Health

Physical & Mental Health
  • Balanced Nutrition: Seasonal vegetables, pulses, whole grains; low processed sugars.
  • Daily Physical Activity: 60 minutes of active play, cycling, walking, or yoga.
  • Sleep Hygiene: 8–9 hours of restful sleep without screens in the bedroom.
Environmental & Emotional Well-being
  • Clean Drinking Water: Filtered/boiled water; clean overhead storage tanks.
  • Sanitation: Waste segregation (wet/dry); zero open mosquito breeding sites.
  • Emotional Wellness: Calm family conversations, reading, and mutual care.

Act 4: Standardized First-Aid Kit Checklist

Dressings & Antiseptics
  • Sterile 4×4 inch gauze pads & rolled gauze.
  • Assorted adhesive bandages (Band-Aids).
  • Micropore medical tape & cotton wool.
  • Antiseptic solution (Povidone-Iodine / Dettol).
  • Silver Sulfadiazine burn cream.
Instruments & Basic Relief
  • Digital clinical thermometer.
  • Blunt-ended surgical scissors & tweezers.
  • Crepe elastic bandage for sprains.
  • Oral Rehydration Salts (ORS) sachets.
  • Emergency contact card (108 Ambulance, Family Doctor).

Life-Saving First-Aid Emergency Algorithms

Emergency SituationStandard First-Aid Protocol (What TO Do)Dangerous Mistakes (What NOT To Do)
Minor Thermal Burns Immediately hold or immerse burned skin under cool running tap water for 10–15 minutes to dissipate heat. Apply burn cream loosely. NEVER apply ice (freezes fragile tissue), butter, oil, or toothpaste! Do not pop blisters.
Bleeding Cuts & Abrasions Wash under clean running tap water. Press sterile gauze firmly directly on wound for 3–5 minutes until bleeding stops. Apply antiseptic and dress. Do not apply dirty cloth, soil, or turmeric paste to fresh open cuts.
Nosebleeds (Epistaxis) Sit upright and lean head slightly forward. Pinch soft nostrils firmly for 10 minutes continuously. Breathe through mouth. NEVER tilt head backward (causes blood to flow down throat into stomach, triggering choking and vomiting).
Dehydration & Heat Faint Move person to a cool, shaded spot. Elevate legs slightly. Sip freshly prepared ORS solution slowly. Do not give carbonated sugary sodas or ice-cold gulps.
