# Chapter 17: Active and Passive Voice
**Curriculum**: CBSE Standard Coursebook | **Subject**: English Grammar | **Chapter Key**: `Gram_Ch17`

## Executive Summary
This dossier delivers a comprehensive codification of Active and Passive Voice. It articulates the prerequisite condition of verb transitivity, details the step-by-step structural inversion of subject and object, pronounces pronoun inflection rules (nominative to accusative agent), maps out the auxiliary 'be' + Past Participle (V3) conjugation matrix across all eight passive-eligible tenses, identifies the four non-passive continuous/perfect tenses, and formulates transformation rules for negative statements, imperative commands/advice, and interrogative sentences.

## Core Competencies & Pedagogical Objectives
- Distinguishing Active Voice (subject as agent/doer) from Passive Voice (subject as receiver/patient of action)
- Understanding that only transitive verbs possessing a direct object can undergo passive voice conversion
- Mastering pronoun case transformations (I -> me, he -> him, she -> her, we -> us, they -> them, who -> whom)
- Synthesizing the auxiliary 'be' + V3 matrix across Simple, Continuous, and Perfect tenses (Present, Past, Future)
- Constructing passive transformations of negative statements and questions (Yes/No and Wh- questions with 'By whom')
- Executing imperative voice transformations using 'Let + object + be + V3' or 'You are requested/ordered/advised to + V1'

## Conceptual Framework & Theory Summary
### 1. Concept & Communicative Purpose of Voice
• Voice is the form of a verb that shows whether the grammatical subject performs the action (Active Voice) or receives the action (Passive Voice).
• Active: 'The chef cooked the meal.' (Focus is on the doer/agent 'the chef').
• Passive: 'The meal was cooked by the chef.' (Focus is on the result/patient 'the meal').
• When is Passive preferred?
  1. When the agent is unknown or irrelevant: 'My bicycle was stolen.'
  2. When the receiver/action is of primary importance: 'The patient was admitted immediately.'
  3. In scientific, academic, and journalistic discourse: 'The test tube was heated over a flame.'

### 2. The 3 Invariable Transformation Rules
To change a sentence from Active to Passive:
1. Subject-Object Inversion: The direct object of the active verb becomes the grammatical subject of the passive verb.
2. Verb Inversion (The 'Be' Formula): The main verb is changed into its Past Participle (V3) preceded by an appropriate tense-matching form of the auxiliary verb 'be'.
3. Agent Preposition: The original active subject becomes the agent preceded by the preposition 'by' (e.g., 'by him', 'by Mary'). Note: If the agent is vague ('someone', 'people', 'they'), it is standardly omitted.

### 3. Pronoun Case Declension in Voice
When pronouns shift from active subject to passive agent (or vice versa), they decline from Nominative to Objective case:
• I -> by me
• We -> by us
• You -> by you
• He -> by him
• She -> by her
• They -> by them
• It -> by it
• Who -> By whom

### 4. Tense-by-Tense Passive Formula Matrix
1. Simple Present: active (V1 / V1+s/es) -> passive (is / am / are + V3)
2. Present Continuous: active (is/am/are + V-ing) -> passive (is / am / are + BEING + V3)
3. Present Perfect: active (has/have + V3) -> passive (has / have + BEEN + V3)
4. Simple Past: active (V2) -> passive (was / were + V3)
5. Past Continuous: active (was/were + V-ing) -> passive (was / were + BEING + V3)
6. Past Perfect: active (had + V3) -> passive (had + BEEN + V3)
7. Simple Future: active (will/shall + V1) -> passive (will / shall + BE + V3)
8. Future Perfect: active (will have + V3) -> passive (will have + BEEN + V3)
9. Modal Verbs: active (can/must/may + V1) -> passive (can/must/may + BE + V3)
• Non-Passive Tenses: The 4 tenses with no standard passive forms in English are: Present Perfect Continuous, Past Perfect Continuous, Future Continuous, and Future Perfect Continuous.

### 5. Special Transformations: Negatives, Imperatives & Questions
• Negative Sentences: Place 'not' between the first auxiliary and the rest of the verb phrase:
  'He does not read the book' -> 'The book is not read by him.'
• Imperatives:
  a) Commands/Orders: 'Let + object + be + V3' ('Open the door' -> 'Let the door be opened') OR 'You are ordered to open the door'.
  b) Advice/Moral Duty: 'Object + should + be + V3' ('Help the poor' -> 'The poor should be helped') OR 'You are advised to help the poor'.
  c) Requests: 'You are requested to + V1' ('Please sit down' -> 'You are requested to sit down').
• Interrogatives:
  a) Auxiliary questions: 'Did he finish it?' -> 'Was it finished by him?'
  b) 'Who' questions: 'Who wrote this?' -> 'By whom was this written?' (or 'Who was this written by?').

**Question**: Why can the sentence 'The eagle soared gracefully in the clouds' NOT be converted into the passive voice?

- **Type**: `mcq` | **Difficulty**: `medium` | **Marks**: 1 | **Topic**: Transitivity Requirement
- **Options**:
  - Because 'soared' is an intransitive verb and has no direct object to become the passive subject.
  - Because the sentence has an adverb ('gracefully').
  - Because passive voice is not permitted for birds or animals.
  - Because the verb is in the past tense.
- **Answer**: Because 'soared' is an intransitive verb and has no direct object to become the passive subject.
- **Explanation / Critical Insight**: Passive transformation strictly requires a direct object in the active clause to serve as the new grammatical subject. Intransitive verbs cannot form a passive.

- **Type**: `long_answer` | **Difficulty**: `hard` | **Marks**: 5 | **Topic**: Tense Transformation Paradigm
- **Answer**: Active Base: 'She teaches English' (Subject: She; Verb: teach/taught; Object: English)

Systematic Passive Conjugation Across 8 Tenses:
1. Simple Present: 'English is taught by her.' (Formula: is/am/are + V3)
2. Present Continuous: 'English is being taught by her.' (Formula: is/am/are + being + V3)
3. Present Perfect: 'English has been taught by her.' (Formula: has/have + been + V3)
4. Simple Past: 'English was taught by her.' (Formula: was/were + V3)
5. Past Continuous: 'English was being taught by her.' (Formula: was/were + being + V3)

7. Simple Future: 'English will be taught by her.' (Formula: will/shall + be + V3)
8. Future Perfect: 'English will have been taught by her.' (Formula: will have + been + V3)

Note on Non-Passive Tenses: The four continuous perfect tenses (Present Perfect Continuous, Past Perfect Continuous, Future Perfect Continuous) and Future Continuous have no standard passive equivalents in formal English because compounding 'been being' is phonologically awkward and avoided.

### Item 77: `eng_gram17_la_002`
**Question**: Explain with clear examples how to transform: (a) Ditransitive sentences possessing two objects (Direct and Indirect), and (b) Imperative sentences expressing commands and requests.

- **Type**: `long_answer` | **Difficulty**: `hard` | **Marks**: 5 | **Topic**: Ditransitive & Imperative Voice Rules
- **Answer**: A. Passive of Ditransitive Verbs (Verbs with Two Objects):
A ditransitive verb takes both an Indirect Object (IO, usually a person) and a Direct Object (DO, usually a thing).
• Active: 'Mr. Sharma taught us Mathematics.' (IO = us; DO = Mathematics).
• Transformation 1 (Making Indirect Object the Subject — Preferred): 'We were taught Mathematics by Mr. Sharma.'
• Transformation 2 (Making Direct Object the Subject — Requires preposition 'to'): 'Mathematics was taught to us by Mr. Sharma.'
Both versions are grammatically valid, though making the person (Indirect Object) the subject is more idiomatic.

B. Passive of Imperative Sentences:
1. Orders & Commands: Use the formula 'Let + Object + be + V3'.
   - Active: 'Clean the blackboard.'
   - Passive: 'Let the blackboard be cleaned.'
   - Alternative: 'You are ordered to clean the blackboard.'
2. Moral Duty & Advice: Use the formula 'Object + should + be + V3'.
   - Active: 'Obey your elders.'
   - Passive: 'Your elders should be obeyed.'
3. Polite Requests: Use the formula 'You are requested to + base verb'.
   - Active: 'Please grant me leave.'
   - Passive: 'You are requested to grant me leave.'
- **Explanation / Critical Insight**: Tests advanced mastery of dual-object passive derivations and imperative structural categories.
