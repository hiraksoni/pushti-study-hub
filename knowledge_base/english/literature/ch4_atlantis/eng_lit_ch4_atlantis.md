# Unit 2 Chapter 1: Atlantis (Plato)

**Subject**: English Literature | **Module**: Unit 2.1 (Lit Ch 4) | **Author**: Plato (Translated from Classical Greek) | **Curriculum**: CBSE Class 7 English Literature (Coursebook) | **Version**: 1.0

## Executive Summary
Plato's legendary account of Atlantis from the dialogues Timaeus and Critias (360 B.C.E.). Describes the mythical concentric island empire founded by Poseidon, its golden age of virtue, wisdom, and architectural marvels, its descent into greed and imperialistic warfare against Athens, and its catastrophic destruction in a single day and night.

- Textual analysis of classical Greek philosophical allegory

## Plot & Literary Analysis

### Historical Origin & Transmission
First recorded by Plato in Timaeus and Critias around 360 B.C.E. Plato received the account through family transmission from Solon, who heard it circa 600 B.C.E. from an Egyptian priest at Sais, recounting an event 9,000 years prior.

### Concentric Architecture & Splendour
Engineered with three concentric moats of water and alternating land rings, spanned by bridges and subterranean ship tunnels. Adorned with metals including bronze, tin, and orichalcum, alongside lush gardens, hot and cold thermal baths, and a grand temple to Poseidon.

### Moral Fall and Cataclysm
As mortal blood diluted the divine nature of the Atlanteans, virtue was replaced by unchecked ambition and imperialistic greed. They launched an unprovoked assault on Athens. Angered by their arrogance, the gods unleashed violent earthquakes, floods, and tsunamis, submerging the island within twenty-four hours.

### 27. [ENG_LIT_CH04_LAQ_01] Why has the tale of Atlantis endured for over 2,300 years? How does the myth of a submerged civilization connect with modern environmental issues like rising sea levels?
- **Type**: `long_answer` | **Difficulty**: `hard` | **Marks**: 5 | **Topic**: Critical Thinking & Modern Connect
- **Answer**: The legend of Atlantis endures for two primary reasons:
1. The Utopian Dream: It captures humanity's eternal yearning to engineer an ideal society of sublime beauty, magnificent architecture, and abundance.
2. The Moral Cautionary Tale: It demonstrates that unchecked arrogance (hubris), militarism, and ethical decay inevitably lead to catastrophic self-destruction.

Modern Environmental Connect: While Atlantis sank due to mythological divine retribution, today real coastal settlements and island nations (such as Venice, Italy, and Majuli Island in Assam, India, as well as low-lying Pacific atolls) face devastating submersion due to anthropogenic climate change, thermal expansion of oceans, glacial melting, and severe river erosion. Just as the Atlanteans failed to respect natural and moral balance, modern civilization faces grave existential peril if ecological stewardship is neglected.
- **Explanation / Critical Insight**: High-order thinking connecting ancient classical literature with contemporary global geography and climate science.
