# Unit 3 Chapter 1: The One Who Survived: Ada Blackjack

**Subject**: English Literature | **Module**: Unit 3.1 (Lit Ch 7) | **Author**: Biographical Graphic Narrative (Adapted) | **Curriculum**: CBSE Class 7 English Literature (Coursebook) | **Version**: 1.0

## Executive Summary
The true graphic account of Ada Blackjack, a 23-year-old Iñupiat Inuit seamstress who joined Vilhjalmur Stefansson's ill-fated 1921 expedition to Wrangel Island in the Arctic Ocean to earn money for her tuberculous son Bennett. After three men vanished attempting an ice crossing and the fourth died of scurvy, Ada survived alone in sub-zero wilderness for months through hunting, trapping, and unyielding maternal devotion until her rescue in August 1923.

## Plot & Literary Analysis

### The Arctic Venture & Motivation
In 1921, destitute Iñupiat mother Ada Delutuk joined Stefansson's expedition to colonize Wrangel Island for 50 dollars a month to pay for hospital care for her tuberculous son Bennett. She sailed aboard the Silverwave with four men: Crawford, Knight, Maurer, and Galle.

### The Winter Isolation & Tragedy
When heavy sea ice blocked the 1922 relief ship, provisions ran out. Scurvy crippled Knight. Crawford, Maurer, and Galle attempted an ice crossing to Siberia in January 1923 and vanished forever. Ada lovingly nursed Knight until his death on 23 June 1923.

### Solitary Survival & Heroic Rescue
Left utterly alone, Ada taught herself marksmanship, constructed predator lookout platforms, trapped foxes, shot wild ducks, and kept a diary. Rescued on 19 August 1923 by Harold Noice, she returned home to save Bennett and lived a quiet life.

### 25. [ENG_LIT_CH07_LAQ_01] Compare the concept of a 'Legendary Hero' with that of an 'Everyday Hero' as portrayed in Ada Blackjack's saga. Why does Ada stand as an immortal symbol of human perseverance?
- **Type**: `long_answer` | **Difficulty**: `hard` | **Marks**: 5 | **Topic**: Heroism & Resilience
- **Answer**: 1. Legendary Hero vs. Everyday Hero:
A legendary or mythical hero (like Hercules or Achilles) possesses superhuman strength, divine parentage, enchanted weapons, and performs feats for worldly glory. In stark contrast, an everyday hero is an ordinary mortal without supernatural gifts who exhibits extraordinary moral courage, quiet discipline, and selfless devotion in daily crises. Ada Blackjack was neither physically imposing nor wilderness-trained; she was an impoverished, frightened young mother who confronted frostbite, famine, and polar bears simply because she loved her ailing child Bennett.

2. Immortal Symbol of Human Perseverance:
Ada's achievement endures because it demonstrates the boundless power of the human spirit when anchored by love. While seasoned male explorers succumbed to despair and perished on the ice, Ada mastered essential survival skills—operating firearms, designing traps, butchering seals, managing fuel—all while battling profound loneliness. Her legacy reminds humanity that the greatest heroism is not written in conquests or statues, but in the quiet, unbreakable determination to protect those we cherish.
- **Explanation / Critical Insight**: High-order thematic analysis contrasting classical mythology with realistic moral fortitude.
