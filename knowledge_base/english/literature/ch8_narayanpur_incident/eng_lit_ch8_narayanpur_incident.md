# Unit 3 Chapter 2: The Narayanpur Incident (Shashi Deshpande)

**Subject**: English Literature | **Module**: Unit 3.2 (Lit Ch 8) | **Author**: Shashi Deshpande (Sahitya Akademi & Padma Shri Awardee) | **Curriculum**: CBSE Class 7 English Literature (Coursebook) | **Version**: 1.0

## Executive Summary
Set during the 1942 Quit India Movement in the small town of Narayanpur. Following the arrest of their schoolteacher father, young college student Mohan, his schoolboy brother Babu, sister Manju, and their mother Amma engage in disciplined non-violent resistance. They smuggle a secret cyclostyling machine into their puja room to duplicate Mahatma Gandhi's banned speech, and are saved from a midnight British police raid through the covert patriotism of Sub-Inspector Patil.

## Core Themes & Competencies
- Historical comprehension: 1942 Quit India Movement and Gandhian non-violent protest
- Narrative structure: tracking suspense, plot arcs, and graphic organizers

- Character analysis: evaluating quiet courage (Amma) vs. youthful defiance (Mohan) vs. conflicting duties (Patil)
- Values education: civic responsibility, moral discipline, and patriotism

## Plot & Literary Analysis

### Historical Context & Silent March
August 1942, Quit India Movement. College students in Narayanpur march in complete silence holding Gandhiji's portrait aloft to hand an ultimatum to the British DSP, denying the police any pretext for a lathi charge.

### The Secret Press in the Puja Room
Mohan and revolutionary student Suman smuggle a cyclostyling machine into their house wrapped in newspapers to duplicate copies of the Mahatma's speech under candlelight.

### The Warning & Midnight Climax
Sub-Inspector Patil, an old friend of their jailed father, arrives at night to warn of an imminent raid. Amma trusts his national conscience and gives him the machine. Moments later, British police hammer on the door, only to find no incriminating evidence.

### 25. [ENG_LIT_CH08_LAQ_01] How does 'The Narayanpur Incident' portray the vital role played by women, youth, and ordinary families in India's freedom struggle? What made non-violent discipline a potent weapon against the British Empire?
- **Type**: `long_answer` | **Difficulty**: `hard` | **Marks**: 5 | **Topic**: Historical Analysis & Patriotism
- **Answer**: 1. Role of Women, Youth, and Families:
Shashi Deshpande reveals that the Quit India Movement was not fought solely on political platforms, but in ordinary living rooms by ordinary citizens. With the adult male breadwinner (Appa) in prison, the struggle was carried forward by Amma, college student Mohan, schoolboy Babu, young Manju, and female revolutionary Suman. Each member performed a vital duty: Mohan and Suman ran the underground press, Babu and Manju kept vigil at the doorway, and Amma provided steady moral leadership and strategic decision-making.

2. Power of Non-Violent Discipline:
The British colonial administration was heavily armed and specialized in suppressing riots with brute force. When students marched in absolute silence, refusing to shout slogans or throw stones, the armed DSP and police were rendered helpless. There was no provocation to justify violence, and the moral power of peaceful defiance exposed the illegitimacy of colonial rule. Sub-Inspector Patil's secret aid further proved that the moral force of the national movement was eroding the loyalty of the colonial police force from within.
- **Explanation / Critical Insight**: Comprehensive historical and literary essay examining youth participation and Gandhian resistance.
