## 1. Chapter Overview & Core Concepts

> **Syllabus Summary**: The Golden Age of the Gupta Empire (Chandragupta I, Samudragupta's military campaigns recorded in the Prayag Prashasti, Chandragupta II Vikramaditya and Navratnas), cultural and scientific developments (Kalidasa, Aryabhata), Fa-Hien's travel accounts, Harshavardhana's empire at Kannauj, Xuanzang's chronicle, and peninsular kingdoms (Pallavas and Chalukyas).

### 1. The Gupta Dynasty & Samudragupta
Founded in the 4th century CE by Sri Gupta, with **Chandragupta I** establishing imperial supremacy (~320 CE). His son **Samudragupta** (~335–380 CE) was a brilliant general whose conquest policies were documented by court poet Harishena on the Allahabad Pillar (**Prayag Prashasti**):
- Annexed rulers of Aryavarta (northern India) directly.
- Subjugated Dakshinapatha (southern rulers) who surrendered, were reinstated, and paid annual tributes.
- Neighboring forest and frontier kingdoms offered homage and daughters in marriage.

### 2. Chandragupta II & The Golden Age
**Chandragupta II (Vikramaditya)** (~380–415 CE) wiped out the Western Kshatrapas (Shakas), securing Arabian Sea ports. His court was graced by the *Navratnas* including the poet **Kalidasa** and the astronomer **Varahamihira**. **Aryabhata** authored *Aryabhatiyam*, discovering that Earth rotates on its axis and calculating solar/lunar eclipses. Chinese pilgrim **Fa-Hien** praised the peace, low crime, and benevolent administration of the empire.

### 3. Harshavardhana & Southern Dynasties
In 606 CE, **Harshavardhana** reunited northern India from his capital at Kannauj. Court poet Banabhatta penned his biography *Harshacharita*. Visiting Chinese pilgrim **Xuanzang** spent years studying at **Nalanda University**.
Harsha's southern expansion was halted at the Narmada River by Chalukya king **Pulakeshin II** (court poet Ravikirti at Aihole). Further south, the **Pallavas** ruled from Kanchipuram, pioneering monolithic rock-cut rathas at Mahabalipuram.

#### [HIS8_SA_EXT_001] Why is the Gupta period frequently described as the 'Golden Age of Ancient India'?
- **Difficulty**: `medium` | **Marks**: `3` | **Topic**: `Gupta Golden Age Accomplishments` | **Source**: `chapter_notes`
- **Model Answer**:
  1. **Literature**: Extraordinary poetic and dramatic masterpieces in Sanskrit by Kalidasa (e.g., *Abhijnanashakuntalam*, *Meghaduta*).
