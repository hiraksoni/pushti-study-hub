### Q12. [2 Marks] — Part II: Very Short Answer Questions
**Concept:** Cellular Respiration, Anaerobic Metabolism & Oxygen Debt | **NCERT:** Chapter 3: Life Processes in Animals

**Question:**
Why does the breathing rate of a person increase sharply after running a 100-meter sprint race? [2 Marks]

**Model Solution:**
<ol>
  <li>**Surge in Muscle Energy Demand:** Running a rapid 100-meter sprint demands a tremendous amount of instantaneous energy (ATP) in leg skeletal muscles.</li>
  <li>**Anaerobic Respiration & Oxygen Debt:** Since the circulating blood cannot supply oxygen fast enough to meet this intense aerobic demand, muscle cells respire anaerobically, accumulating *lactic acid* and incurring an **oxygen debt**.</li>
  <li>**Clearing Oxygen Debt:** After stopping, the breathing rate remains elevated to inhale excess oxygen rapidly. This surplus oxygen oxidizes accumulated lactic acid back into carbon dioxide and water, relieving muscle fatigue and restoring homeostasis.</li>
</ol>

**Marking Scheme & Rubric:**
*1 Mark for identifying rapid muscular ATP demand leading to anaerobic respiration and lactic acid/oxygen debt accumulation + 1 Mark for explaining that rapid breathing supplies extra oxygen to break down lactic acid. Total = 2 Marks.*

### Q13. [3 Marks] — Part III: Short Answer Questions
**Concept:** Pulmonary Ventilation Dynamics | **NCERT:** Chapter 3: Life Processes in Animals

**Question:**
Describe the mechanism of inhalation and exhalation in humans with specific reference to the movement of the ribs and diaphragm. [3 Marks]

**Model Solution:**
**1. Inhalation (Breathing In):**
<ul>
  <li>**Ribs Movement:** The external intercostal muscles contract, causing the ribs to move **upwards and outwards**.</li>
  <li>**Diaphragm Movement:** The dome-shaped muscular diaphragm contracts and **flattens downwards**.</li>
  <li>**Thoracic Volume & Air Pressure:** The combined movements expand thoracic cavity volume. This expansion lowers pressure inside the lungs below atmospheric pressure, causing atmospheric oxygen-rich air to rush into the alveoli.</li>
</ul>

**2. Exhalation (Breathing Out):**
<ul>
  <li>**Ribs Movement:** The intercostal muscles relax, allowing the ribs to move **downwards and inwards**.</li>
  <li>**Diaphragm Movement:** The diaphragm relaxes and curves upwards, returning to its original **dome-like resting shape**.</li>
  <li>**Thoracic Volume & Air Pressure:** The thoracic cavity volume shrinks, increasing internal pulmonary pressure above atmospheric levels, forcing carbon dioxide-laden air out of the lungs.</li>
</ul>

**Marking Scheme & Rubric:**
*1.5 Marks for inhalation (0.5M ribs upwards/outwards, 0.5M diaphragm flattens, 0.5M volume expands and air rushes in) + 1.5 Marks for exhalation (0.5M ribs downwards/inwards, 0.5M diaphragm relaxes to dome shape, 0.5M volume decreases and air expelled). Total = 3 Marks.*

### Q14. [3 Marks] — Part III: Short Answer Questions
**Concept:** Lipid Digestion & Large Intestinal Absorption | **NCERT:** Chapter 3: Life Processes in Animals

**Question:**
(a) What is emulsification of fats, and which digestive secretion aids this process? (1.5 Marks)
(b) What physiological role does the large intestine perform on undigested food waste? (1.5 Marks) [3 Marks]

**Model Solution:**
**(a) Emulsification of Fats:**
<ul>
  <li>**Definition:** Emulsification is the physical breakdown of large, insoluble dietary fat globules into microscopic droplets. This substantially increases the surface area exposed to water-soluble lipases for efficient enzymatic breakdown into fatty acids and glycerol.</li>
  <li>**Digestive Secretion:** **Bile juice** (specifically bile salts like sodium glycocholate and sodium taurocholate), synthesized by the liver and stored in the gallbladder, performs fat emulsification. (Bile contains no enzymes but creates an alkaline pH essential for lipase action).</li>
</ul>

**(b) Physiological Role of the Large Intestine:**
<ul>
  <li>**Water and Mineral Absorption:** The large intestine reabsorbs the majority of unabsorbed water, salts, and electrolytes from the liquid chyme entering from the ileum, preventing dehydration.</li>
  <li>**Fecal Compaction & Elimination:** It compacts the remaining non-digestible fibrous residue into semi-solid feces, which passes into the rectum for temporary storage until egestion through the anus.</li>
</ul>

**Marking Scheme & Rubric:**
*(a) 1 Mark for defining fat emulsification + 0.5 Mark for naming bile juice. (b) 1 Mark for water and mineral salt reabsorption + 0.5 Mark for compaction of undigested waste/egestion. Total = 3 Marks.*

(a) Trace the complete path of food through the human alimentary canal from the mouth to the anus. (2 Marks)
(b) Explain the chemical digestion of carbohydrates, proteins, and fats inside the small intestine, mentioning the enzymes involved. (3 Marks)
<div style='text-align:center; font-weight:700; margin:8px 0;'>OR</div>Mention 5-6 functions of the stomach. [5 Marks]

**Model Solution:**
**(a) Complete Path of Food in Human Alimentary Canal:**

<div class="flowchart-box">
  Mouth / Buccal Cavity &rarr; Pharynx &rarr; Oesophagus (Food Pipe) &rarr; Stomach &rarr; Small Intestine (Duodenum &rarr; Jejunum &rarr; Ileum) &rarr; Large Intestine (Caecum &rarr; Colon &rarr; Rectum) &rarr; Anus

**(b) Chemical Digestion in the Small Intestine:**

The small intestine is the site of complete chemical digestion where intestinal juice, alkaline pancreatic juice, and bile juice act together:
<ol>
  <li>**Carbohydrates:** Pancreatic amylase hydrolyzes remaining starches into maltose. Intestinal enzymes (maltase, sucrase, lactase) break down disaccharides into simple absorbable **glucose**.

  *Starch / Complex Sugars &rarr; Glucose*</li>
  <li>**Proteins:** Pancreatic trypsin and chymotrypsin (activated in alkaline medium) along with intestinal peptidases cleave peptones and polypeptides into individual **amino acids**.

  *Proteins & Peptones &rarr; Amino Acids*</li>
  <li>**Fats (Lipids):** After bile salts emulsify fat droplets, pancreatic and intestinal **lipases** digest emulsified fats into absorbable **fatty acids and glycerol**.

</ol>

<div class="or-box">
  <div class="or-badge">OR OPTION: 5-6 FUNCTIONS OF THE HUMAN STOMACH</div>
  <ol>
    <li>**Mechanical Food Reservoir:** Temporarily stores ingested food for 3 to 5 hours, regulating slow passage into the duodenum.</li>
    <li>**Mechanical Churning:** Coordinated rhythmic peristaltic contractions of its muscular walls churn, liquefy, and blend food with gastric secretions into an acidic semi-fluid called **chyme**.</li>
    <li>**Pathogen Destruction via HCl:** Gastric parietal cells secrete Hydrochloric Acid (HCl, pH ~ 1.5 - 2.5), which kills food-borne bacteria and microorganisms.</li>

    <li>**Gastric Mucosal Protection:** Mucus neck cells produce a continuous coating of bicarbonate-rich alkaline mucus that guards the stomach wall against corrosive self-digestion by HCl and pepsin.</li>
    <li>**Intrinsic Factor Secretion:** Secretes Castle's intrinsic factor, essential for the subsequent absorption of Vitamin B12 in the terminal ileum.</li>
  </ol>
</div>

### Q27. [3 Marks] — Part III: Short Answer Questions
**Concept:** Chemical Reactivity & Physical Material Justifications | **NCERT:** Chapter 2: Metals and Non-Metals

Provide scientific justifications for each observation:

(c) School acoustic bells are manufactured from bronze/brass instead of seasoned wood. (1 Mark) [3 Marks]

**Model Solution:**
**(a) Preservation of Sodium and Potassium under Kerosene Oil:**

Sodium (Na) and potassium (K) are highly electropositive alkali metals situated at the very top of the reactivity series. They react vigorously and exothermically with atmospheric oxygen and moisture even at ambient room temperature, liberating flammable hydrogen gas that catches fire spontaneously. Immersing them under chemically inert kerosene oil cuts off all contact with atmospheric oxygen and moisture.



**(b) Copper Core with PVC Cladding in Electric Cables:**

Copper has extremely low electrical resistivity and high conductivity, allowing high-voltage currents to transmit with minimal Joule resistive heating loss. Polyvinyl chloride (PVC) is an outstanding, flexible electrical insulator that forms a secure barrier, preventing electric current leakage, accidental short-circuits, and protecting people from electric shocks.



**(c) School Bells Made of Bronze/Brass instead of Wood:**

Metals and metal alloys like bronze (copper-tin) and brass (copper-zinc) are **sonorous**. When struck, their metallic crystal structure resonates with minimal internal damping, producing a clear, loud, sustained ringing acoustic tone that propagates across long distances. Seasoned wood is non-sonorous; it rapidly damps vibrational energy and emits only a dull, muffled thud.

**Question:**
Explain the scientific mechanism responsible for sea breezes during daytime and land breezes at night in coastal environments with reference to modes of heat transfer. [3 Marks]

**Model Solution:**
**Mode of Heat Transfer:** Thermal Convection in air driven by differential rates of heating and cooling of land and water (due to water's higher specific heat capacity).



**(a) Sea Breeze (During Daytime):**
<ol>
  <li>During daylight, land heats up significantly faster than seawater under solar radiation.</li>
  <li>The air above the warm land becomes hot, expands, decreases in density, and rises, creating a low-pressure zone.</li>
  <li>The cooler, denser, high-pressure air over the sea moves inland toward the shore to take its place.</li>
  <li>This cool breeze blowing **from the sea towards the land during the daytime** is termed a **sea breeze**.</li>
</ol>

**(b) Land Breeze (During Nighttime):**
<ol>
  <li>At night, solar heating stops. Land cools down much faster than seawater, which retains thermal energy much longer.</li>
  <li>The warmer air situated over the sea expands and rises.</li>
  <li>The cooler, denser air from the land flows seaward to replace the rising air over the water.</li>
  <li>This breeze blowing **from the land towards the sea at night** is termed a **land breeze**.</li>
</ol>

### Q38. [4 Marks] — Part IV: Case Study Based Question
**Concept:** Kinematics Telemetry, Average Speed & Dimensional Unit Conversion | **NCERT:** Chapter 3: Measurement of Time and Motion

> <strong>CASE STUDY: EXPRESSWAY TRAVEL & SPEED KINEMATICS</strong>
> 
Sunita and her father set out on an expressway journey from City A to City B. Before departing at 8:00 AM, Sunita noted that the car's odometer read 12,450 km. At 10:30 AM, they arrived at City B, and the odometer displayed 12,630 km. During the trip, Sunita checked the speedometer periodically and noted that it hovered around 80 km/h, though it dropped to zero at two toll gates.

**Question:**
(a) Differentiate between the measurement functions of an odometer and a speedometer. [1 Mark]
(b) Calculate the total distance traveled and the total elapsed travel time in hours. [1 Mark]
(c) Calculate the average speed of the vehicle in km/h for the entire trip, and convert this value into m/s. [2 Marks]

**Model Solution:**
**(a) Measurement Functions of Odometer vs Speedometer:**
<ul>
  <li>**Odometer:** Measures and records the **cumulative total distance** traversed by the vehicle, expressed in kilometers (km).</li>
  <li>**Speedometer:** Measures and displays the **instantaneous speed** of the vehicle at any particular moment in time, expressed in kilometers per hour (km/h).</li>
</ul>

**(b) Calculation of Distance and Time:**
<ul>
  <li>**Total Distance Traveled (d):**

  Final Odometer Reading - Initial Odometer Reading

  = 12,630 km - 12,450 km = **180 km**</li>
  <li>**Total Elapsed Time (t):**

  From 8:00 AM to 10:30 AM = 2 hours and 30 minutes

  = 2 + (30 / 60) = **2.5 hours**</li>
</ul>

**(c) Average Speed in km/h and Conversion to m/s:**
<div class="formula-box">
  **1. Average Speed (v<sub>av</sub>):**

  Average Speed = Total Distance Traveled / Total Time Taken

  v<sub>av</sub> = 180 km / 2.5 h = **72 km/h**


  **2. Conversion into m/s:**

  To convert km/h to m/s, multiply by 5/18 (since 1000 m / 3600 s = 5/18):

  v<sub>av</sub> = 72 &times; (5 / 18) = 4 &times; 5 = **20 m/s**
</div>

**Marking Scheme & Rubric:**
*(a) 1 Mark for distinguishing odometer (distance) and speedometer (instant speed). (b) 0.5 Mark for distance (180 km) + 0.5 Mark for time (2.5 h). (c) 1 Mark for average speed formula and 72 km/h + 1 Mark for unit conversion and 20 m/s. Total = 4 Marks.*

### Q39. [5 Marks] — Part V: Long Answer Question
**Concept:** Electrical Protection, Circuit Breakers vs Fuses & Radiation Principles | **NCERT:** Chapter 1: Electricity & Chapter 2: Heat Transfer and Temperature

**Question:**
(a) What is an electric fuse? On which scientific effect of electric current does it function? (2 Marks)
(b) Why are Miniature Circuit Breakers (MCBs) increasingly replacing traditional rewirable wire fuses in modern electrical switchboards? (1.5 Marks)
(c) Why is substituting an ordinary thick copper wire in place of an authentic rated fuse wire an extremely dangerous hazard? (1.5 Marks) [Evaluating]
<div style='text-align:center; font-weight:700; margin:8px 0;'>OR</div>(a) Describe a controlled experiment using two tin cans (one painted dull black, one polished shiny white) to prove that dark surfaces absorb and emit thermal radiation more effectively. (3 Marks)
(b) Why do we wear light-colored cotton garments in peak summers and dark woolen attire during winter? Explain using heat transfer principles. (2 Marks) [5 Marks]

**Model Solution:**
**(a) Electric Fuse & Working Principle:**
<ul>
  <li>**Definition:** An electric fuse is an essential electrical safety device connected in series with the live wire of a circuit to protect household wiring and appliances from damage caused by short-circuits and electrical overloads.</li>
  <li>**Scientific Effect:** It operates strictly on the **heating effect of electric current (Joule heating, H = I²Rt)**. The fuse wire is manufactured from a low-melting-point alloy (such as lead-tin). When an excessive current flows, Joule heating quickly melts the wire, breaking the circuit instantly.</li>
</ul>

**(b) Why MCBs are Replacing Traditional Wire Fuses:**
<ol>
  <li>**Automatic and Rapid Tripping:** An MCB automatically switches off within milliseconds via electromagnetic sensing whenever an overcurrent or short circuit occurs, offering superior sensitivity.</li>
  <li>**Quick Reset without Rewiring:** When a wire fuse blows, the melted wire must be manually rethreaded, posing risks of wrong wire thickness. An MCB simply trips its switch, which can be flipped back up into the 'ON' position once the fault is cleared.</li>
  <li>**Safety:** Eliminates the risk of electrical contact shocks during fuse wire replacement.</li>
</ol>

**(c) Severe Hazard of Using Thick Copper Wire as Fuse:**
<ul>
  <li>An authentic fuse wire is engineered with a strictly calculated low melting point to melt before domestic copper wires overheat.</li>
  <li>A thick copper wire has very low electrical resistance and an extremely high melting point (~1085 °C).</li>
  <li>If an overcurrent surge occurs, the thick copper wire will **not melt**. As a result, massive current will continue flowing through domestic wiring, causing wall wires to overheat, melt their PVC insulation, damage valuable appliances, and ignite devastating electrical fires.</li>
</ul>

<div class="or-box">
  <div class="or-badge">OR OPTION: RADIATION EXPERIMENT & SEASONAL CLOTHING</div>
  **(a) Controlled Tin Can Radiation Experiment:**
  <ol>
    <li>**Setup:** Take two identical tin cans of equal volume. Paint the exterior of Can A with matte black paint, and paint Can B with polished reflective white paint.</li>
    <li>**Absorption Phase:** Pour equal quantities of cold water (at 20 °C) into both cans. Insert a laboratory thermometer through a lid into each can. Place both cans side-by-side in direct bright sunlight for 1 hour.
      <ul>
        <li>*Observation:* Water temperature in the Black Can A rises significantly higher than in the White Can B, proving that dull black surfaces absorb radiant heat much more effectively.</li>
      </ul>
    </li>
    <li>**Emission Phase:** Now fill both cans with equal volumes of hot water at 70 °C and place them in a shaded, cool room for 15 minutes.
      <ul>
        <li>*Observation:* The temperature in Black Can A falls significantly faster than in White Can B, proving that dark surfaces are also superior heat emitters.</li>
      </ul>
    </li>
  </ol>

  
**(b) Seasonal Garments and Heat Transfer Principles:**
  <ul>
    <li>**Summer Attire (Light Cotton):** Light/white clothes reflect the vast majority of incident solar radiant heat rather than absorbing it. Cotton is porous and breathable; it readily absorbs body sweat and exposes it to air, causing continuous evaporative cooling.</li>
    <li>**Winter Attire (Dark Woolens):** Dark clothes absorb maximum radiant heat from surroundings. Wool is a poor conductor of heat, and its crimped fibers trap a large amount of stationary air (air is an excellent thermal insulator), preventing metabolic body heat from escaping into the cold environment.</li>
  </ul>
</div>

**Marking Scheme & Rubric:**
*(a) 1 Mark for fuse definition + 1 Mark for heating effect (Joule's law). (b) 1.5 Marks for MCB advantages (automatic tripping, easy reset, safety). (c) 1.5 Marks for thick copper wire high melting point hazard and fire risk. OR: (a) 1.5 Marks for absorption experiment + 1.5 Marks for emission experiment with clear observations. (b) 1 Mark for light cotton summer reflection & perspiration evaporation + 1 Mark for dark wool winter absorption & trapped air insulation. Total = 5 Marks.*
