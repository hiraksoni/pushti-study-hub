## 2. Core Conceptual Theory & Notes
### [chapter_notes] Definition & History
AI is the branch of computer science that creates systems capable of performing tasks that normally require human intelligence (learning, reasoning, pattern recognition). Term coined by John McCarthy in 1956 at the Dartmouth Conference. Alan Turing proposed the Turing Test in 1950.

### [chapter_notes] Stages of AI by Capability
1. Artificial Narrow Intelligence (ANI / Weak AI): Designed for a single specific task within fixed boundaries (Siri, chess engines, spam filters). Only form of AI currently in existence. 2. Artificial General Intelligence (AGI / Strong AI): Theoretical AI that equals human cognitive abilities across all tasks. 3. Artificial Super Intelligence (ASI): Theoretical AI that surpasses human intelligence across every domain.

### [chapter_notes] Types of AI by Functionality
1. Reactive Machines: Only respond to current inputs with no past memory (IBM Deep Blue). 2. Limited Memory: Uses recent historical data to make immediate decisions (Tesla self-driving cars, chatbots). 3. Theory of Mind: Proposed AI that understands human emotions, beliefs, and social interactions (in research). 4. Self-Awareness: Hypothetical conscious machines with their own emotions (purely speculative).

### [chapter_notes] Three Core Domains of AI
1. Data Science: Analyzes numerical and structured data for pattern finding, weather forecasting, financial modeling. 2. Computer Vision (CV): Enables computers to interpret digital images and video (face unlock, medical scan analysis, self-driving perception). 3. Natural Language Processing (NLP): Enables computers to comprehend, analyze, and generate human spoken/written languages (speech recognition, Google Translate, chatbots).

### [chapter_notes] Autonomous Driving Synergy
A self-driving car combines all three domains: Computer Vision processes camera feeds for lanes/pedestrians, Data Science analyzes vehicle speed/GPS data to plot optimal path, NLP listens to user destination commands.

### Q11. [ict_ch3_notes_long1] | Autonomous Vehicle Synergy
* **Source**: `chapter_notes` | **Type**: `long_answer` | **Difficulty**: `hard_hots` | **Marks**: 4M
* **Question**: Explain how all three domains of AI collaborate inside an autonomous self-driving car.
* **Answer**: 1. Computer Vision (CV): Processes continuous video streams from onboard cameras to detect lane boundaries, traffic lights, cyclists, and pedestrians.
2. Data Science: Aggregates radar, lidar, speed telemetry, and GPS data to calculate braking distance and plan optimal routes.
3. Natural Language Processing (NLP): Allows passengers to give vocal instructions ('Take me to the nearest hospital') and announces audio alerts.
* **Explanation**: Without all three domains working in unison, an autonomous vehicle cannot perceive, compute, and interact safely.

### Q14. [ict_ch3_enrich_short1] | Turing Test
* **Source**: `researched_enrichment` | **Type**: `short_answer` | **Difficulty**: `medium` | **Marks**: 2M
* **Question**: What is the Turing Test and what was its original purpose?
* **Answer**: Proposed by British mathematician Alan Turing in 1950, the Turing Test evaluates whether a computer's conversational ability is indistinguishable from a human being. If a human interrogator cannot reliably tell the machine apart from a human respondent, the machine passes the test.
* **Explanation**: Published in his foundational paper 'Computing Machinery and Intelligence'.
