## 2. Exhaustive Theoretical Framework

### 1. Definition, Objectives & Historical Milestones of AI

Artificial Intelligence (AI) broadly refers to any human-like behaviour displayed by a machine or system. In its fundamental form, computers are programmed to 'mimic' human behaviour using extensive datasets derived from past examples of similar behaviour.

Formal Definition:
AI is the development of computer systems capable of performing tasks that typically require human intelligence, such as decision making, object detection, solving complex problems, high-level computations, and pattern recognition with elevated accuracy and speed. Additionally, AI empowers machines to execute high-risk or hazardous operations that humans cannot or should not perform.

Historical Milestones:
• 1950 — The Turing Test: Proposed by British mathematician Alan Turing in his landmark paper 'Computing Machinery and Intelligence'. It is an empirical benchmark to determine whether a computer can exhibit intelligent behavior indistinguishable from that of a human.
• 1956 — The Dartmouth Conference: John McCarthy, widely revered as the 'Father of Artificial Intelligence', officially coined the term 'Artificial Intelligence'. He defined AI as: 'The science and engineering of making intelligent machines, especially intelligent computer programs.'
• 1997 — Deep Blue vs. Garry Kasparov: On May 11, 1997, IBM's supercomputer Deep Blue defeated the reigning world chess champion Garry Kasparov under tournament conditions, marking a watershed moment in machine intelligence.

### 2. Classification of AI by Capabilities (ANI, AGI, ASI)

Artificial Intelligence is categorized according to its intellectual capability into three distinct developmental stages:

1. Artificial Narrow Intelligence (ANI / Weak AI):
   • Scope: Involves a limited part of the mind. Trained and programmed to perform strictly ONE dedicated task within predefined boundaries.
   • Behavior: Highly proficient at its designated function, but completely fails if tasked with anything beyond its programmed parameters.
   • Examples: Virtual assistants (Apple Siri, Amazon Alexa, Microsoft Cortana, Google Assistant), facial recognition software, spam filters,      and autonomous driving cruise algorithms (which execute lane-keeping without general common sense).
   • Status: The ONLY stage of AI that currently exists in practical, real-world deployment.

2. Artificial General Intelligence (AGI / Strong AI / Deep AI):
   • Scope: The concept of a machine that possesses human-level cognitive ability across all intellectual domains.
   • Behavior: Capable of learning, thinking, reasoning, and applying intelligence to solve novel, unfamiliar problems indistinguishably from a human.
   • Status: Theoretical research stage; no true AGI machine exists today.

3. Artificial Super Intelligence (ASI):
   • Scope: A hypothetical future intelligence that vastly surpasses the collective cognitive capacity of all human minds.
   • Capabilities: Superhuman competence across scientific creativity, philosophical wisdom, general problem-solving, and emotional understanding.
   • Status: Purely hypothetical and theoretical.

### 3. Classification of AI by Functionality (Reactive, Limited Memory, Theory of Mind, Self-Awareness)

Based on how AI systems process information, memory, and environment, they are classified into four functional tiers:

1. Reactive Machines:
   • The most basic, primitive form of AI. They react only to current immediate stimuli based on hardcoded rules and logic.
   • Crucial Characteristic: They possess NO memory and cannot store past experiences to learn or influence future decisions.
   • Case Study — IBM Deep Blue (1997): Defeated world chess champion Garry Kasparov. Deep Blue calculated 200 million board positions per second      with a speed of 11 Gigaflops, powered by 480 VLSI chips running C on an AIX operating system. It made tactical decisions solely based on the opponent's      present board setup without learning from past games.

2. Limited Memory:
   • Machines capable of looking into the past by storing transitory data and recent observations for a short period of time to make informed decisions.
   • Case Study — Waymo Autonomous Vehicle: Developed from research initiated in 2007 and tested driverless on public roads in Texas. Waymo uses rooftop      LiDAR to project a 360-degree 3D map 200 meters around the car, combined with 6 external sensors, GPS, gyroscopes, tachometers, and altimeters.      It tracks the speed and trajectories of nearby vehicles, pedestrians, lane markings, traffic signals, and hand gestures of other commuters.

3. Theory of Mind:
   • Advanced prospective AI capable of understanding human social values, psychological beliefs, mental states, and emotions (happiness, sadness, anger, sarcasm).
   • Enables machines to engage in meaningful two-way social interaction. Currently under active laboratory research.

4. Self-Awareness:
   • The hypothetical ultimate frontier of AI development. Systems that possess their own consciousness, self-identity, feelings, and autonomous desires.
   • Such machines would be aware of their own internal states and smarter than the human mind. Remains purely theoretical.

### 4. Humanoids & Robotics in Contemporary Practice

Humanoids are anthropomorphic robots engineered with an external physical structure resembling the human body (head, torso, arms, legs):

Prominent Humanoid Robots in Modern AI:
• OceanOne (Stanford Robotics Lab): An underwater bimanual humanoid robot designed for coral reef and deep-sea shipwreck exploration.
• Atlas (Boston Dynamics): A high-mobility bipedal humanoid engineered to navigate rough outdoor terrain and execute search-and-rescue operations.
• Nao (Aldebaran Robotics / SoftBank): A programmable educational humanoid that competes in autonomous soccer matches in the international RoboCup Standard Platform League.
• Sophia (Hanson Robotics): An AI humanoid capable of facial expressions and conversational banter that gained global fame on talk shows and was granted citizenship by Saudi Arabia.
• Mitra (Invento Robotics, India): A self-autonomous service humanoid designed to welcome guests, provide customer assistance in banks, and assist patients in hospitals.

### 5. The Three Core Domains of AI: Data Science (DS)

An AI 'domain' refers to a broad area of application and foundational technique. The first major domain is Data Science:

Principles of Data Science (DS):
• Data Science is the interdisciplinary study that focuses on capturing, cleaning, analyzing, and extracting actionable meaning from large datasets.
• Learning Source: When machines exhibit learning, their fuel is Data. A 'DataSet' is a structured collection of related data points (e.g., likes, comments, shares, views on social media).
• Sources of Bulk Data: Social media feeds, web browsing logs, e-commerce cart histories, smartphone GPS coordinates, and IoT telemetry.
• High-Yield Applications:
  1. Predictive Analytics: Numerical weather forecasting, epidemic disease modeling, and stock market fluctuation prediction.
  2. Targeted Advertising: Recommending commercial products based on online behavioral profiles and search keyword matching, saving businesses advertising costs.

### 6. The Three Core Domains of AI: Computer Vision (CV)

Computer Vision (CV) is the domain of AI that enables machines to acquire, process, analyze, and comprehend digital images, video frames, and visual inputs:

The Three Structural Components of CV:
1. Optics: Hardware capture equipment including digital cameras, infrared sensors, and specialized optical lenses for continuous image feed recording.
2. Digital Image Processing: Analog-to-Digital Converters (ADC) and signal processing algorithms that transform raw visual pixels into machine-readable numerical matrices.    The accuracy of downstream machine learning is directly governed by the quality of image preprocessing.
3. Machine Learning: Pattern recognition algorithms trained on prepared visual datasets to recognize faces, objects, and anomalies.

Human Vision vs. Computer Vision Pipeline:
• Human Vision: Eye (Sensing Device) $\rightarrow$ Brain (Interpreting Device) $\rightarrow$ Cognitive Recognition (Output).
• Computer Vision: Digital Camera / Sensor (Sensing Device) $\rightarrow$ Computer / GPU (Interpreting Device) $\rightarrow$ Classification / Decision (Output).

Applications: Facial recognition unlocking, biometric fingerprint scanning, autonomous traffic light detection, optical character recognition (OCR), and medical imaging diagnostic tools.

### 7. The Three Core Domains of AI: Natural Language Processing (NLP) & Synergy

Natural Language Processing (NLP) is the branch of AI that enables computers to analyze, comprehend, infer meaning, and generate human spoken and written languages:

Key Applications of NLP:
1. Conversational Chatbots: Automated conversational agents programmed to interact with human users using natural conversational phrasing.
2. Search Engine Autocomplete: As a user types 2-3 letters in a search bar (e.g., 'world\'s fastest'), NLP algorithms analyze massive query datasets to predict the completed search intention.
3. Voice Assistants: Real-time speech-to-text conversion and intent extraction in Siri, Alexa, and Google Assistant.
4. Language Translation & Sentiment Analysis: Grammatical translation between global languages and detecting emotional sentiment (positive, negative, sarcastic) in customer reviews.

Multi-Domain Synergy in Autonomous Vehicles (Waymo):
A modern self-driving car operates at the intersection of all three AI domains:
• Computer Vision: Detects road boundaries, lane markers, pedestrian positions, traffic light colors, and commuter hand gestures via cameras and LiDAR.
• Natural Language Processing: Interprets verbal voice commands given by passengers ('Drive me to the library').
• Data Science: Evaluates real-time GPS coordinates, vehicle telemetry, speed logs, and historical traffic patterns to compute the safest, quickest route.
