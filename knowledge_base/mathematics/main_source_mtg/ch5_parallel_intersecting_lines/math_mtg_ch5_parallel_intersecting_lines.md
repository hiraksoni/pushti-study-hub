## 1. Chapter Overview & Core Theoretical Principles

### Points, Lines, Rays and Line Segments
A point has no length, breadth, or height ($0$-dimensional). A line has infinite length extending without end in both opposite directions ($1$-dimensional). A ray has one fixed origin endpoint and extends infinitely in the other direction. A line segment has two fixed endpoints with a finite, measurable length.

### Angle Classifications & Special Angle Pairs
An angle is formed when two rays originate from a common vertex. Complementary angles sum to $90^\circ$. Supplementary angles sum to $180^\circ$. Adjacent angles share a common vertex and a common interior arm, with non-overlapping interiors. A Linear Pair consists of two adjacent angles on a straight line whose sum is strictly $180^\circ$. Vertically opposite angles are formed across the intersection of two straight lines and are identically equal: $\angle 1 = \angle 3$ and $\angle 2 = \angle 4$.

### Parallel Lines & Perpendicular Lines
Two straight lines lying in the same plane that never intersect, regardless of how far extended, are parallel lines ($l \parallel m$). The perpendicular distance between parallel lines is constant everywhere. Perpendicular lines ($l \perp m$) intersect at exactly a $90^\circ$ right angle.

### Transversals & Angle Theorems
A transversal is a line that intersects two or more lines at distinct points. When transversal $t$ cuts parallel lines $l \parallel m$:
1. Corresponding Angles (F-shape) are equal: $\angle 1 = \angle 5$, $\angle 2 = \angle 6$, $\angle 4 = \angle 8$, $\angle 3 = \angle 7$.
2. Alternate Interior Angles (Z-shape) are equal: $\angle 3 = \angle 5$, $\angle 4 = \angle 6$.
3. Alternate Exterior Angles are equal: $\angle 1 = \angle 7$, $\angle 2 = \angle 8$.
4. Co-Interior Angles (C-shape) on the same side of the transversal are supplementary: $\angle 4 + \angle 5 = 180^\circ$, $\angle 3 + \angle 6 = 180^\circ$.

### Auxiliary Line Construction Technique
In multi-line bend problems or reflex angles (e.g., zig-zag transversals $M \to N \to O \to P$), construct an auxiliary line passing through the internal bend vertex parallel to the given parallel lines. This decomposes the unknown bend angle into two independent alternate interior or co-interior angle components.

### Complementary Angles

#### [MTG_MATH5_ILL_001] Illustration 1 (Textbook p. 107): An angle is double its complement. Find the angle.
- **Difficulty**: `easy` | **Marks**: `2` | **Topic**: `Complementary Angles` | **Source**: `textbook_illustration`
- **Answer**: **60°**
- **Mathematical Rationale & Step-by-Step Solution**: Let the angle be $x$. Its complement is $90^\circ - x$. Given $x = 2(90^\circ - x) \implies x = 180^\circ - 2x \implies 3x = 180^\circ \implies x = 60^\circ$. The angle is $60^\circ$ and its complement is $30^\circ$.
- **Formal Solution Steps**:
  1. Let the unknown angle be $x$.
  1. By definition, complementary angles sum to $90^\circ$, so the complement is $90^\circ - x$.
  1. Formulate equation: $x = 2(90^\circ - x)$.
  1. $x + 2x = 180^\circ \implies 3x = 180^\circ \implies x = 60^\circ$.
- **Marking Rubric**: 1 mark for equation setup; 1 mark for correct angle evaluation.


### Supplementary Angles

#### [MTG_MATH5_ILL_002] Illustration 2 (Textbook p. 107): Find an angle which is two-fifths of its supplement.
- **Difficulty**: `easy` | **Marks**: `2` | **Topic**: `Supplementary Angles` | **Source**: `textbook_illustration`
- **Answer**: **51 3/7° (360/7°)**
- **Mathematical Rationale & Step-by-Step Solution**: Let the angle be $x$. Its supplement is $180^\circ - x$. Given $x = \frac{2}{5}(180^\circ - x) \implies 5x = 360^\circ - 2x \implies 7x = 360^\circ \implies x = \frac{360^\circ}{7} = 51\frac{3}{7}^\circ$.
- **Formal Solution Steps**:
  1. Let the angle be $x$.
  1. Its supplement is $180^\circ - x$.
  1. Set up relation: $x = \frac{2}{5}(180^\circ - x)$.
  1. Multiply across by 5: $5x = 360^\circ - 2x \implies 7x = 360^\circ \implies x = \frac{360^\circ}{7}$.
- **Marking Rubric**: 1 mark for setting up fractional equation; 1 mark for final fraction or mixed number.


### Linear Pair

#### [MTG_MATH5_ILL_003] Illustration 3 (Textbook p. 107): In the given figure, $AOB$ is a straight line with ray $OC$ standing on it. If $\angle AOC = (x + 35)^\circ$ and $\angle BOC = (2x + 25)^\circ$, find (a) $\angle AOC$ (b) $\angle BOC$.

  1. $(x + 35) + (2x + 25) = 180^\circ$.
  1. $3x + 60 = 180 \implies 3x = 120 \implies x = 40^\circ$.
  1. Substitute $x=40^\circ$: $\angle AOC = 40 + 35 = 75^\circ$, $\angle BOC = 80 + 25 = 105^\circ$.
- **Marking Rubric**: 1 mark for finding $x = 40^\circ$; 1/2 mark for each sub-angle.

- **Marking Rubric**: 1 mark for vertically opposite deduction; 1 mark for straight line calculation.

### Parallel Line Construction

#### [MTG_MATH5_ILL_007] Illustration 7 (Textbook p. 109): In a given triangle $ABC$, describe how to draw a line through vertex $A$ that is parallel to side $BC$.
- **Difficulty**: `easy` | **Marks**: `2` | **Topic**: `Parallel Line Construction` | **Source**: `textbook_illustration`
- **Answer**: **Draw line through A making alternate interior angle equal to ∠ABC**
- **Mathematical Rationale & Step-by-Step Solution**: Measure $\angle ABC$. At vertex $A$, on the opposite side of transversal $AB$, construct an angle equal to $\angle ABC$. The resulting line is parallel to $BC$ by the converse of the alternate interior angles theorem.
- **Formal Solution Steps**:
  1. Treat line segment $AB$ as a transversal between line $BC$ and the required line through $A$.
  1. Measure $\angle ABC$.
  1. At vertex $A$, construct an alternate interior angle equal to $\angle ABC$.
  1. By converse of alternate interior angle theorem, the constructed line is parallel to $BC$.
- **Marking Rubric**: 1 mark for geometric principle (alternate angles); 1 mark for construction description.


### Dot Grid Geometry

#### [MTG_MATH5_ILL_008] Illustration 8 (Textbook p. 109): Can you draw parallel lines on dot paper that are neither horizontal, vertical, nor at $45^\circ$? How can you ensure they are strictly parallel?
- **Difficulty**: `medium` | **Marks**: `2` | **Topic**: `Dot Grid Geometry` | **Source**: `textbook_illustration`
- **Answer**: **Yes, by using equal rise-over-run grid step vectors (e.g. 3 units right, 2 units up)**
- **Mathematical Rationale & Step-by-Step Solution**: A line segment connecting grid dots has a vector slope $\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}$. If you move by the exact same step (e.g., 3 dots right, 2 dots up) from two different starting dots, the resulting segments have identical slopes and constant perpendicular distance, proving they are parallel.
- **Formal Solution Steps**:
  1. Pick a starting dot and define a step rule: e.g. move $p$ units right and $q$ units up to an endpoint.
  1. Pick a second starting dot on a different row/column.
  1. Apply the exact same step rule ($p$ units right, $q$ units up) to find its endpoint.
  1. The slopes are equal ($\frac{q}{p} = \text{constant}$), ensuring the lines are strictly parallel.
- **Marking Rubric**: 1 mark for step-count / slope concept; 1 mark for explanation.

### Auxiliary Line Method

#### [MTG_MATH5_ILL_012] Illustration 12 (Textbook p. 112): In the given figure, $AB \parallel CD$, $\angle CDM = 150^\circ$, $\angle BMD = 64^\circ$, and $\angle ABM = x$. Find the value of $x$.
- **Difficulty**: `hard` | **Marks**: `3` | **Topic**: `Auxiliary Line Method` | **Source**: `textbook_illustration`
- **Answer**: **x = 146°**
- **Mathematical Rationale & Step-by-Step Solution**: Through vertex $M$, draw auxiliary line $EMF \parallel AB \parallel CD$. Let $\angle EMB = y$. Then $\angle EMD = 64^\circ - y$. Since $EF \parallel CD$, co-interior angles sum to $180^\circ$: $150^\circ + (64^\circ - y) = 180^\circ \implies 214^\circ - y = 180^\circ \implies y = 34^\circ$. Now $AB \parallel EF$, so co-interior angles sum to $180^\circ$: $x + y = 180^\circ \implies x + 34^\circ = 180^\circ \implies x = 146^\circ$.
- **Formal Solution Steps**:
  1. Construct auxiliary line $EF \parallel AB \parallel CD$ passing through $M$.
  1. Split angle at $M$: let $\angle EMB = y$, then $\angle EMD = 64^\circ - y$.
  1. Apply co-interior rule on $EF \parallel CD$: $150^\circ + (64^\circ - y) = 180^\circ \implies y = 34^\circ$.
  1. Apply co-interior rule on $AB \parallel EF$: $x + 34^\circ = 180^\circ \implies x = 146^\circ$.
- **Marking Rubric**: 1 mark for auxiliary line construction; 1 mark for finding split angle y; 1 mark for evaluating x.


### Parallelism Verification

#### [MTG_MATH5_ILL_013] Illustration 13 (Textbook p. 113): In the given figure, transversal $GH$ intersects lines $AB$ and $CD$ such that $\angle BGH = 125^\circ$ and the vertically opposite angle to $\angle GHD$ is $55^\circ$. Check whether $AB \parallel CD$.
- **Difficulty**: `medium` | **Marks**: `2` | **Topic**: `Parallelism Verification` | **Source**: `textbook_illustration`
- **Answer**: **Yes, AB || CD (Co-interior angles sum to 180°)**
- **Mathematical Rationale & Step-by-Step Solution**: The vertically opposite angle gives $\angle GHD = 55^\circ$. Now inspect co-interior angles $\angle BGH$ and $\angle GHD$: $125^\circ + 55^\circ = 180^\circ$. Since the sum of consecutive interior angles on the same side of the transversal is exactly $180^\circ$, lines $AB$ and $CD$ are parallel.
- **Formal Solution Steps**:
  1. Find interior angle: $\angle GHD = 55^\circ$ (vertically opposite).
  1. Calculate sum of co-interior angles: $\angle BGH + \angle GHD = 125^\circ + 55^\circ = 180^\circ$.
  1. Conclude: Since co-interior angles are supplementary, $AB \parallel CD$.
- **Marking Rubric**: 1 mark for calculation of angle sum; 1 mark for geometric conclusion.

### Transitivity of Parallel Lines

#### [MTG_MATH5_ILL_015] Illustration 15 (Textbook p. 113): In the given figure, $\angle ABC = 70^\circ$, $\angle BCE = 30^\circ$, $\angle ECD = 40^\circ$, and $\angle FEC = 140^\circ$. Show that $AB \parallel EF$.
- **Difficulty**: `hard` | **Marks**: `3` | **Topic**: `Transitivity of Parallel Lines` | **Source**: `textbook_illustration`
- **Answer**: **Proved: AB || CD and CD || EF, hence AB || EF**
- **Mathematical Rationale & Step-by-Step Solution**: 1. $\angle BCD = \angle BCE + \angle ECD = 30^\circ + 40^\circ = 70^\circ$. Since $\angle ABC = \angle BCD = 70^\circ$, alternate interior angles are equal, so $AB \parallel CD$. 2. $\angle FEC + \angle ECD = 140^\circ + 40^\circ = 180^\circ$. Since co-interior angles sum to $180^\circ$, $EF \parallel CD$. 3. Lines parallel to the same line are parallel to each other: $AB \parallel EF$.
- **Formal Solution Steps**:
  1. Calculate $\angle BCD = 30^\circ + 40^\circ = 70^\circ$.
  1. Since $\angle ABC = \angle BCD = 70^\circ$, alternate interior angles are equal $\implies AB \parallel CD$.
  1. Check co-interior angles between $EF$ and $CD$: $140^\circ + 40^\circ = 180^\circ \implies EF \parallel CD$.
  1. By transitivity: $AB \parallel CD$ and $EF \parallel CD \implies AB \parallel EF$.
- **Marking Rubric**: 1 mark for proving $AB \parallel CD$; 1 mark for proving $CD \parallel EF$; 1 mark for transitivity conclusion.
