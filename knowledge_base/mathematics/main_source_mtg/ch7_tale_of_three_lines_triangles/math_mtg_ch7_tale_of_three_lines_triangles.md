## 1. Chapter Overview & Core Theoretical Principles

> **Syllabus Scope**: Discovery of triangles formed by three mutually non-concurrent intersecting lines, angle sum property ($180^\circ$), exterior angle property, and triangle inequality theorem.

### Angle Sum Property of a Triangle
The sum of all three interior angles of any triangle is always $180^\circ$: $\angle A + \angle B + \angle C = 180^\circ$.

### Exterior Angle Property
An exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of its two interior opposite angles: $\angle \text{ext} = \angle 1 + \angle 2$.

### Triangle Inequality Theorem
The sum of any two sides of a triangle is strictly greater than the third side: $a + b > c$.

#### [MTG_MATH7_MCQ_001] The three angles of a triangle are in the ratio $2 : 3 : 5$. What is the measure of the largest angle?
- **Difficulty**: `easy` | **Marks**: `1` | **Topic**: `Angle Sum Property` | **Source**: `textbook_exercise`
  - 90°
  - 100°
  - 75°
  - 60°
- **Answer**: **90°**
- **Mathematical Rationale & Step-by-Step Solution**: Let angles be $2x, 3x, 5x$. $2x + 3x + 5x = 180^\circ \implies 10x = 180^\circ \implies x = 18^\circ$. Largest angle $= 5 \times 18^\circ = 90^\circ$.

#### [MTG_MATH7_MCQ_002] An exterior angle of a triangle measures $115^\circ$ and one of its interior opposite angles is $45^\circ$. What is the other interior opposite angle?

  - 70°
  - 65°
  - 80°
  - 55°
- **Answer**: **70°**
- **Mathematical Rationale & Step-by-Step Solution**: Exterior angle = sum of interior opposite angles: $115^\circ = 45^\circ + x \implies x = 115^\circ - 45^\circ = 70^\circ$.

#### [MTG_MATH7_LA_001] $ABCD$ is a quadrilateral. Prove that $AB + BC + CD + DA > AC + BD$.
- **Difficulty**: `hard_hots` | **Marks**: `5` | **Topic**: `Triangle Inequality Proof` | **Source**: `chapter_notes`
- **Step-by-Step Model Solution**:
  • **Proof**:
  1. Consider $\triangle ABC$. By the triangle inequality theorem (sum of two sides > third side):
     $$AB + BC > AC \quad \text{--- (1)}$$
  2. In $\triangle ADC$:
     $$AD + CD > AC \quad \text{--- (2)}$$
  3. In $\triangle ABD$:
     $$AB + DA > BD \quad \text{--- (3)}$$
  4. In $\triangle BCD$:
     $$BC + CD > BD \quad \text{--- (4)}$$
  5. Adding inequalities (1), (2), (3), and (4):
     $$(AB + BC) + (AD + CD) + (AB + DA) + (BC + CD) > AC + AC + BD + BD$$
     $$2AB + 2BC + 2CD + 2DA > 2AC + 2BD$$
     $$2(AB + BC + CD + DA) > 2(AC + BD)$$
  6. Dividing both sides by 2:
     $$\mathbf{AB + BC + CD + DA > AC + BD}$$
  Hence proved.
- **Marking Rubric & Working Notes**: Classic quadrilateral perimeter versus diagonals inequality proof.
