## 1. Chapter Overview & Core Theoretical Principles

> **Syllabus Scope**: Mathematical games, parity invariance (odd/even sums), construction of 3x3 magic squares, Khajuraho Chautisa Yantra, Virāhaṅka numbers (Fibonacci sequence in Indian prosody), and cryptarithms.

### Parity Invariance Rules
Sum of an odd number of odd integers is always **Odd**. Parity invariance proves the impossibility of certain grid and coin games.

### 3x3 Magic Square & Khajuraho Chautisa
- $3\times 3$ grid using $1-9$: Magic constant $M = 15$. Center MUST be $5$.
- **Khajuraho Chautisa**: $4\times 4$ pan-diagonal magic square summing to $34$ across all rows, columns, diagonals, and $2\times 2$ subgrids.

### Virāhaṅka Sequence (Fibonacci)
Codified by Indian prosodist Virāhaṅka (c. 600 CE): $F_n = F_{n-1} + F_{n-2}$ with terms: $1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21, 34, \dots$.

### Cryptarithms (Alphametics)
Puzzles where letters represent unique digits ($0-9$). Leading digit $\neq 0$. Maximum carry from adding two single digits is $1$.

#### [MTG_MATH6_SA_001] Solve the cryptarithm:
$$\begin{array}{r@{\quad}l}
  & 4 A \\
+ & B 8 \\
\hline
  & 8 3
\end{array}$$
Find the values of digits $A$ and $B$.
- **Difficulty**: `medium` | **Marks**: `3` | **Topic**: `Solving Cryptarithm` | **Source**: `chapter_notes`
- **Step-by-Step Model Solution**:
  1. Look at the units column: $A + 8$ ends in $3$. Since $A$ is a single digit, $A + 8 = 13 \implies A = 5$ (carry 1 to tens column).
2. Look at the tens column: $1\text{ (carry)} + 4 + B = 8$.
   $$5 + B = 8 \implies B = 3$$
3. Check: $45 + 38 = 83$. This is correct.
**Result: $A = 5, B = 3$**.
- **Marking Rubric & Working Notes**: Columnar deduction in cryptarithms.

#### [MTG_MATH6_LA_001] There are 15 coins placed on a table, all showing heads. In each turn, you must flip exactly 2 coins simultaneously. Can you ever reach a state where all 15 coins show tails? Justify mathematically using parity invariance.
- **Difficulty**: `hard_hots` | **Marks**: `5` | **Topic**: `Parity Invariance Problem` | **Source**: `chapter_notes`
- **Step-by-Step Model Solution**:
  • **Mathematical Formulation**:
  - Let the number of tails be $T$.
  - Initially, all coins are heads, so $T = 0$ (an **even** number).
  - Goal: Reach all 15 coins showing tails, meaning $T = 15$ (an **odd** number).
• **Analyzing the Flipping Operation**:
  When you flip exactly 2 coins, three cases arise:
  1. Both were heads $\rightarrow$ both become tails: $T$ increases by 2 ($T + 2$).
  2. Both were tails $\rightarrow$ both become heads: $T$ decreases by 2 ($T - 2$).
  3. One was head and one was tail $\rightarrow$ they swap: $T$ remains unchanged ($T + 0$).
• **Parity Invariance**:
  In all three cases, the change in $T$ is $+2, -2,$ or $0$. Therefore, **the parity of $T$ (evenness or oddness) never changes**; it remains strictly invariant.
• **Conclusion**:
  Since $T$ starts as an even number ($0$), it can **never** become an odd number ($15$). Hence, it is impossible to reach all tails.
- **Marking Rubric & Working Notes**: Rigorous parity invariance proof standard in mathematical Olympiads and CBSE HOTS.
