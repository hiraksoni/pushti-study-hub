## 2. Core Conceptual Theory & Notes
### [chapter_notes] Chemical Definition & Properties of Acids
Acids are chemical substances characterized by a sour taste that release Hydrogen ions (H⁺) in aqueous solutions and turn blue litmus paper red. 1. Organic Acids (Naturally Occurring): Acetic acid (CH₃COOH in vinegar), Citric acid (citrus fruits like lemons/oranges), Lactic acid (curd and sour milk), Tartaric acid (tamarind, unripe grapes), Oxalic acid (spinach, tomatoes), Formic/Methanoic acid (ant sting), Ascorbic acid (Vitamin C in amla). 2. Mineral / Inorganic Acids (Laboratory Acids): Hydrochloric acid (HCl), Sulphuric acid (H₂SO₄ — known as the 'King of Chemicals'), and Nitric acid (HNO₃). Concentrated mineral acids are highly corrosive; always dilute by adding acid slowly into water with constant stirring, never water into acid.

### [chapter_notes] Chemical Definition & Properties of Bases
Bases are chemical substances with a bitter taste and a slippery, soapy touch that release Hydroxide ions (OH⁻) in aqueous solutions and turn red litmus paper blue. Bases soluble in water are termed Alkalis. Common industrial and domestic bases include: Sodium hydroxide (NaOH — Caustic Soda, used in soaps and detergents), Potassium hydroxide (KOH — Caustic Potash), Calcium hydroxide (Ca(OH)₂ — Slaked Lime, used in white washing and lime water), Magnesium hydroxide (Mg(OH)₂ — Milk of Magnesia, antacid), and Ammonium hydroxide (NH₄OH — window cleaners).

### [chapter_notes] Natural & Synthetic Acid-Base Indicators
Indicators are special chemical substances that exhibit distinct colour changes in acidic and basic solutions. 1. Litmus (Natural dye extracted from Lichens): In neutral distilled water it is mauve/purple; turns Red in acids and Blue in bases. Available as solution or red/blue strips. 2. Turmeric (Curcuma longa): Natural yellow indicator. Remains yellow in acids and neutral solutions; turns deep Red/Brown in basic solutions (e.g. soap on curry stain). 3. China Rose (Hibiscus Rosa-sinensis): Warm water petal extract is light pink; turns Dark Pink / Magenta in acids and Green in bases. 4. Phenolphthalein (Synthetic): Colourless in acids and neutral solutions; turns vivid Pink in basic solutions. 5. Methyl Orange: Red in acids, Yellow in bases.

### [chapter_notes] Neutralization Reaction & Salt Synthesis
Neutralization is an exothermic chemical reaction between an acid and a base to produce salt, water, and heat energy: Acid + Base → Salt + Water + Heat (Δ). Example: Hydrochloric acid reacts with Sodium hydroxide to form Sodium chloride and water: HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O + Heat. Salts can be: (a) Neutral Salt (pH = 7): Strong acid + Strong base (e.g. NaCl, Na₂SO₄); (b) Acidic Salt (pH < 7): Strong acid + Weak base (e.g. NH₄Cl); (c) Basic Salt (pH > 7): Weak acid + Strong base (e.g. Na₂CO₃, NaHCO₃).

### [chapter_notes] Neutralization in Everyday Life & Environmental Applications
1. Indigestion (Hyperacidity): Gastric glands secrete excess hydrochloric acid (HCl), causing heartburn. Neutralized by taking mild bases (antacids) like Milk of Magnesia [Mg(OH)₂] or Baking Soda [NaHCO₃]. 2. Ant & Wasp Stings: Ant bites inject Formic acid into skin, causing burning pain; neutralized by rubbing moist baking soda [NaHCO₃] or Calamine solution containing Zinc carbonate [ZnCO₃]. Wasp stings are alkaline, neutralized by mild vinegar (acetic acid). 3. Agricultural Soil Treatment: Overuse of chemical fertilizers makes soil overly acidic; treated with basic quicklime [CaO] or slaked lime [Ca(OH)₂]. If soil is too basic, organic manure (compost) is added, which releases natural acids upon decay. 4. Factory Waste Neutralization: Acidic industrial effluents are neutralized with basic slaked lime before discharging into water bodies to protect aquatic life.
