## 1. Chapter Overview & Core Theoretical Principles

> **Syllabus Scope**: Advanced integer arithmetic, rules of signs for multiplication and division, properties of integers (closure, commutativity, associativity, distributivity), additive inverse, and multi-step evaluation drills.

### Rules of Signs in Integer Operations
- **Multiplication & Division**: $(-) \times (-) = (+)$, $(+) \times (-) = (-)$.
- Product of an **odd number** of negative integers is negative; of an **even number** is positive.

### Distributive Property
$a \times (b + c) = (a \times b) + (a \times c)$. Example: $725 \times 102 = 725 \times (100 + 2) = 72,500 + 1,450 = 73,950$.

### Properties of Integers
Integers are closed under addition, subtraction, and multiplication. Division by zero is undefined.

#### [CORD_MATH1_SA_002] A submarine was situated 800 m below sea level. If it ascends 250 m and then dives 120 m, what is its new position relative to sea level?
- **Difficulty**: `medium` | **Marks**: `3` | **Topic**: `Integer Word Problem` | **Source**: `chapter_notes`
- **Step-by-Step Model Solution**:
  1. Initial position = $-800\text{ m}$.
2. Ascent (+250 m): $-800 + 250 = -550\text{ m}$.
3. Descent (-120 m): $-550 - 120 = -670\text{ m}$.
The submarine is at $-670\text{ m}$ (or $670\text{ m}$ below sea level).

#### [CORD_MATH1_LA_001] Verify the distributive law $a \times (b + c) = (a \times b) + (a \times c)$ for $a = -12$, $b = -8$, and $c = 15$.
- **Difficulty**: `hard_hots` | **Marks**: `5` | **Topic**: `Distributive & Associative Laws` | **Source**: `chapter_notes`
- **Step-by-Step Model Solution**:
  • **LHS Calculation**:
  $$b + c = (-8) + 15 = 7$$
  $$a \times (b + c) = (-12) \times 7 = -84$$
• **RHS Calculation**:
  $$a \times b = (-12) \times (-8) = +96$$
  $$a \times c = (-12) \times 15 = -180$$
  $$(a \times b) + (a \times c) = 96 + (-180) = -84$$
• **Conclusion**:
  $$\text{LHS} = \text{RHS} = -84$$
  Hence, the distributive law of multiplication over addition is verified.
- **Marking Rubric & Working Notes**: Formal algebraic verification of distributive property for integers.
