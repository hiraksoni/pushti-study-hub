## Core Conceptual Foundations
### 1. What is an Arithmetic Expression?
An arithmetic (or numerical) expression is a mathematical phrase formed by combining numbers (constants) with operation symbols ($+, -, \times, \div$) and grouping symbols (parentheses $( )$, curly braces $\{ \}$, square brackets $[ ]$). Unlike an equation, an expression contains no equals sign ($=$) or inequality symbols ($<, >$). When computed according to standard operator precedence, it evaluates to a single numerical quantity called its **value**.
**Key Principle**: A single numerical value can be expressed through infinitely many distinct arithmetic expressions: e.g., $22 = 24 - 2 = 11 \times 2 = 15 + 7 = 44 \div 2 = (5 \times 4) + 2$.

### 2. Simple vs. Complex Expressions & Meaning of Terms
- **Simple Expressions**: Involve only one arithmetic operation and no brackets (e.g. $2 + 5$, $6 \times 3$, $16 \div 4$).
- **Complex Expressions**: Involve multiple operations and grouping symbols (e.g. $12 - 4 \times 2$, $(8 + 4) \div 6$).
- **Terms in an Expression**: Terms are the individual components separated strictly by addition ($+$) or subtraction ($-$) signs. Products and quotients like $7 \times 8$ or $24 \div 6$ represent a single unified term until evaluated. E.g., $15 + 6 \div 3 - 2$ has 3 distinct terms: $15$, $(6 \div 3)$, and $-2$.

### 3. Order of Operations Hierarchy (BODMAS / PEMDAS)
To ensure every arithmetic expression evaluates to a unique, consistent value, operations follow strict precedence:
1. **Brackets / Grouping**: Evaluate innermost brackets first: Parentheses $( ) \to$ Curly Braces $\{ \} \to$ Square Brackets $[ ]$.
2. **Orders / Exponents**: Evaluate powers and square roots.
3. **Division & Multiplication**: Equal precedence; evaluate strictly from **left to right** as they appear.
4. **Addition & Subtraction**: Equal precedence; evaluate strictly from **left to right** as they appear.

### 4. Comparing Expressions: Direct vs. Logical Reasoning
Comparison can be done in two ways:
1. **By Direct Calculation**: Evaluate both expressions completely and insert $<, >, \text{ or } =$.
2. **By Structural Logical Reasoning (Without Full Calculation)**:
   - *Identical Base, Different Addends*: If $A + B$ vs $A + C$, and $B > C$, then $A + B > A + C$.
   - *Identical Base, Different Subtrahends (The Subtraction Inversion Trap)*: If $A - B$ vs $A - C$, and $B > C$, then $A - B < A - C$ because subtracting more leaves less!
   - *Net Difference Analysis*: In $245 + 289$ vs $246 + 285$: $245$ is 1 less than $246$, but $289$ is 4 more than $285$, giving a net gain of $+3 \implies 245 + 289 > 246 + 285$.

### 5. Commutative and Associative Properties
- **Commutative Property (Swapping)**: Changing the order of terms does not affect the result:
  - Addition: $a + b = b + a$
  - Multiplication: $a \times b = b \times a$
  - *Counterexample*: Subtraction and division are NOT commutative: $9 - 4 \ne 4 - 9$ and $12 \div 3 \ne 3 \div 12$.
- **Associative Property (Grouping)**: Regrouping terms with brackets preserves the result:
  - Addition: $(a + b) + c = a + (b + c)$
  - Multiplication: $(a \times b) \times c = a \times (b \times c)$
  - *Counterexample*: Subtraction and division are NOT associative: $(5 - 3) - 1 = 1 \ne 5 - (3 - 1) = 3$.

### 6. Distributive Property of Multiplication
Multiplying a sum or difference by a number equals multiplying each term separately and then adding or subtracting the products:
- $a \times (b + c) = a \times b + a \times c$
- $a \times (b - c) = a \times b - a \times c$
- Factorization form: $(a \times c) \pm (b \times c) = (a \pm b) \times c$
- Essential for rapid mental arithmetic: $18 \times 12 = 18 \times (10 + 2) = 180 + 36 = 216$.

### 7. Signs Preceding Brackets & Bracket Removal Rules
- **Plus Sign Outside ($+$)**: Removing brackets preserves all internal signs:
  $$a + (b + c) = a + b + c \quad \text{and} \quad a + (b - c) = a + b - c$$
- **Minus Sign Outside ($-$)**: Removing brackets inverts every internal sign:
  $$a - (b + c) = a - b - c \quad \text{and} \quad a - (b - c) = a - b + c$$
  *Why?* Subtracting a group is mathematically equivalent to subtracting each member individually.

### 8. Additive Inverse & Formal Subtraction Definition
- **Additive Inverse (Opposite)**: The unique number $-x$ that sums with $x$ to equal the additive identity $0$: $x + (-x) = 0$.
  - Inverse of positive $a$ is $-a$ (e.g. inverse of $36$ is $-36$).
  - Inverse of negative $-a$ is $+a$ (e.g. inverse of $-25$ is $25$).
  - Inverse of product: inverse of $36 \times 21$ is $-(36 \times 21)$.
- **Formal Subtraction Definition**: Subtraction is formally defined as adding the additive inverse: $a - b = a + (-b)$.

### 9. Tinker the Terms: Near-Product Decompositions
A mental mathematics technique of modifying an existing benchmark expression to solve adjacent calculations without starting from scratch:
- **In Addition/Subtraction**: If $a + b = c$, then $(a + 1) + b = c + 1$, and $(a - 1) + b = c - 1$.
  *Example*: Given $156 + (-42) = 114 \implies 155 + (-42) = 114 - 1 = 113$.
- **In Multiplication**: Given $k \times n = P$:
  - Adjacent increment: $(k + 1) \times n = P + n$
  - Adjacent decrement: $(k - 1) \times n = P - n$
  - Jump by multiple: $(k + m) \times n = P + m \times n$
  *Example*: Given $525 \times 16 = 8400 \implies 525 \times 17 = 8400 + 525 = 8925$, and $525 \times 15 = 8400 - 525 = 7875$.

### 10. Real-World Modeling with Arithmetic Expressions
Translating verbal and logistical situations into expressions:
- Savings & Gifts: $\text{Saved} \times \text{Weeks} - \text{Expense} + \text{Gift} = 50 \times 8 - 150 + 100 = 350$.
- Amusement Park Trips: Tickets + Food - Discount $= 3 \times 50 + 100 - 20 = 230$.
- Bus Seating Capacity: $\text{Available Seats} = \text{Total Capacity} - \text{Occupied} = (3 \times 42) - 99 = 126 - 99 = 27$.

**Question**: Illustration 1 (p. 2): Fill in the blanks to make the expressions equal: (i) $12 + 6 = \text{___} + 9$; (ii) $54 - 12 = 16 + \text{___}$; (iii) $55 \div \text{___} = 5 \times 1$.
**Answer**: (i) 9; (ii) 26; (iii) 11.

**Answer**: (i) $18 < 19$; (ii) $25 > 5$; (iii) $20 = 20$.
**Explanation**: (i) $15 + 3 = 18$ and $17 + 2 = 19 \implies 18 < 19$. (ii) $5 \times 5 = 25$ and $30 \div 6 = 5 \implies 25 > 5$. (iii) $25 - 5 = 20$ and $12 + 8 = 20 \implies 20 = 20$.

**Explanation**: (i) 654 is 1 more than 653, but 239 is 3 less than 242 (net loss of 2) $\implies <$. (ii) 412 is 1 more, but subtracts 3 more $\implies$ net loss $\implies <$. (iii) Both terms increase by 1, so difference remains invariant $\implies =$.
