## 1. Chapter Overview & Core Concepts

> **Syllabus Summary**: Study of geomorphic processes shaping Earth's surface, driven by internal Endogenic forces (diastrophic plate tectonics, sudden earthquakes, volcanoes) and external Exogenic forces (weathering, erosion, and deposition by running water, wind, glaciers, and marine waves).

### 1. Earth Movements & Plate Tectonics
Earth's crust consists of numerous large and small rigid slabs called **Lithospheric Plates**, which move continuously by a few millimeters each year driven by convective heat currents in the underlying mantle.
- **Endogenic Forces**: Act in the interior of the earth. Include sudden forces (earthquakes, volcanoes) causing catastrophic damage, and slow diastrophic forces (mountain building).
- **Exogenic Forces**: Act on the surface of the earth. Driven by atmospheric weathering, erosion, and deposition by rivers, winds, glaciers, and sea waves.

### 2. Volcanoes & Earthquakes
- **Volcano**: A circular vent or opening in Earth's crust through which molten magma, gases, and ash erupt violently from the subterranean magma chamber.
- **Earthquake**: Tremors generated when lithospheric plates slip. The point where vibrations originate underground is the **Focus**; the point on the surface directly above it is the **Epicentre**. Measured using a **Seismograph** and quantified on the logarithmic **Richter Scale** (magnitude 7.0+ classifies as major destructive shock).

### 3. Exogenic Landforms of Water, Wind, and Ice
- **River Landforms**: Waterfalls (vertical drop over hard rock), Meanders (river curves), Oxbow lakes (cut-off meander loops), Levees (raised natural banks), and Deltas (triangular silt deposits at river mouths).
- **Sea Wave Action**: Sea caves (hollows), Sea arches (openings through headlands), Sea stacks (isolated rock pillars), Sea cliffs (steep vertical coasts), and Beaches (sand deposits).
- **Glacial Action**: Glaciers carve steep **U-shaped valleys** and deposit unsorted rocky mounds called **Glacial Moraines**.
- **Wind Action in Deserts**: **Mushroom rocks** (narrow base, wide top caused by low-altitude wind abrasion), **Sand dunes** (wind-blown sand mounds), and **Loess** (extensive deposits of fine dust).

#### [GEO2_SA_EXT_001] Differentiate between the three types of earthquake waves.
- **Difficulty**: `medium` | **Marks**: `3` | **Topic**: `Seismic Waves` | **Source**: `textbook_exercise`
- **Model Answer**:
  1. **P-Waves (Primary / Longitudinal waves)**: Fastest waves that travel through solids, liquids, and gases via compressional push-pull action.
2. **S-Waves (Secondary / Transverse waves)**: Slower waves that travel only through solids, moving particles perpendicular to wave direction.
3. **L-Waves (Surface waves)**: Travel along Earth's surface and cause the greatest ground displacement and structural damage.
- **Marking Rubric & Notes**: Standard seismology classification recorded on a seismograph.

#### [GEO2_LA_EXT_001] Explain the major landforms produced by a river across its upper, middle, and lower course.
- **Difficulty**: `hard_hots` | **Marks**: `5` | **Topic**: `Gradational Work of a River` | **Source**: `textbook_exercise`
- **Model Answer**:
  • **Upper Course (Youth Stage)**: Steep gradient and dominant vertical erosion create deep **V-shaped valleys**, steep **gorges**, and dramatic **waterfalls** (e.g., Angel Falls, Victoria Falls).
• **Middle Course (Mature Stage)**: Lateral erosion dominates as the valley widens, causing the river to develop large winding curves called **meanders** and seasonal **floodplains**.
• **Lower Course (Old Stage)**: Gentle gradient causes extreme deposition. When the river breaches meander necks it forms **oxbow lakes**. Near the sea, the river splits into numerous distributaries, depositing silt to form a fertile fan-shaped **delta** (e.g., Ganga-Brahmaputra Delta).
- **Marking Rubric & Notes**: Complete fluvial geomorphology lifecycle from source to river mouth.
