## 1. Chapter Overview & Core Concepts

> **Syllabus Summary**: Impact of iron metallurgy, agricultural expansion, rise of post-Mauryan regional dynasties (Kushanas in the north, Satavahanas in the Deccan, Muvendar in the south), the Silk Route international trade networks, craft guilds (shrenis), and Sangam literature.

### 1. Agricultural Revolution & Urbanisation
From around 500 BCE onwards, the widespread use of iron tools (axes for clearing forests, ploughshares for turning fertile soils) combined with paddy transplantation and irrigation canals produced massive agricultural surpluses. This spurred the 'Second Urbanisation' characterized by bustling market towns, fortified capitals, and specialized craft centers.

### 2. Regional Dynasties: North, Deccan, and South
- **The Kushanas**: Ruled north-western India and Central Asia. They secured control over the overland Silk Route and were among the first subcontinental dynasties to issue fine gold coins. King **Kanishka** convened the Fourth Buddhist Council.
- **The Satavahanas**: Ruled western and central India (Maharashtra and Andhra). Their greatest ruler was **Gautamiputra Satakarni**, known as the 'Lord of the Dakshinapatha' (the southern trade highway).
- **The Muvendar**: Three southern ruling houses—**Cholas** (Kaveri delta, capital Uraiyur/Puhar), **Cheras** (Kerala coast), and **Pandyas** (capital Madurai, famous for pearl fisheries and patronizing Tamil Sangam literature).

### 3. Guilds, Trade, and External Connections
Craftspersons and merchants organized into powerful corporate bodies called **Shrenis**. They provided technical training, bulk purchased raw materials, regulated product quality, and operated as trust banks. External trade flourished with the Roman Empire through coastal ports like **Arikamedu** (Puducherry), where Roman pottery (amphorae), Arretine ware, and Roman coins have been excavated.

#### [HIS7_LA_EXT_001] How did the widespread adoption of iron technology and new irrigation techniques transform agriculture and society during this period?
- **Difficulty**: `hard_hots` | **Marks**: `5` | **Topic**: `Agricultural & Economic Growth in the Iron Age` | **Source**: `chapter_notes`
- **Model Answer**:
  • **Iron Ploughshares**: Replaced wooden ploughs, allowing heavy, clayey soil of the fertile Gangetic plain to be tilled deeply, drastically multiplying crop yields.
• **Iron Axes**: Facilitated systematic clearance of dense subtropical forests for expanding agricultural settlements.
• **Paddy Transplantation**: Instead of broadcasting seeds, farmers raised saplings in seedbeds and transplanted them into waterlogged fields, ensuring higher seedling survival rates.
• **State-Supported Irrigation**: Kings and villages constructed extensive networks of canals, wells, artificial irrigation tanks, and reservoirs, raising agricultural surpluses that supported flourishing urban craft centers.
- **Marking Rubric & Notes**: Foundation of the Second Urbanisation in the Indian subcontinent.
