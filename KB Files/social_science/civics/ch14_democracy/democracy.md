## 1. Chapter Overview & Core Concepts

> **Syllabus Summary**: Understanding democratic representation in India: Universal Adult Franchise, the independent role of the Election Commission of India, electoral process, political parties, election of MLAs, formation of the state executive (Governor, Chief Minister, Council of Ministers), and legislative debates in the Vidhan Sabha.

### 1. Universal Adult Franchise & The Election Commission
Democracy is grounded in the principle of **Universal Adult Franchise**: every citizen aged 18 years or older has one vote of equal value, without discrimination. Elections are conducted independently by the **Election Commission of India (ECI)**, an autonomous constitutional authority that enforces the Model Code of Conduct to ensure free and fair polling.

### 2. Who is an MLA & How Government is Formed
Every state is divided into territorial constituencies. Voters elect one representative called a **Member of Legislative Assembly (MLA)**. The political party whose MLAs win more than 50% of the total assembly seats achieves an absolute majority and becomes the **Ruling Party**. The MLAs select their leader, whom the **Governor** appoints as the **Chief Minister**. The Chief Minister then selects other MLAs to serve as Ministers heading administrative departments.

### 3. The Legislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha) & Accountability
The **Legislative Assembly** is the central forum where MLAs from both the ruling party and the opposition debate public issues, review government expenditures, and pass state laws. Ruling ministers must respond to questions posed by MLAs and defend policy actions through public press conferences and media briefings.

#### [CIV14_LA_EXT_001] Explain the step-by-step process of how a government is formed in an Indian state after assembly elections.
- **Difficulty**: `hard_hots` | **Marks**: `5` | **Topic**: `Formation of State Government` | **Source**: `textbook_exercise`
- **Model Answer**:
  • **Constituency Delimitation & Voting**: The state is divided into electoral constituencies. Citizens elect one MLA from each constituency.
• **Majority Threshold**: To form a government, a political party must secure more than half the total number of assembly seats (e.g., 35+ out of 68 seats).
• **Ruling Party & Chief Minister**: The majority party members select their legislative leader. The Governor officially appoints this leader as the **Chief Minister**.
• **Council of Ministers**: The Governor, on the advice of the Chief Minister, appoints other MLAs as ministers handling portfolios (Health, Education, Finance, Public Works).
• **Coalition Government**: If no single party wins an absolute majority, two or more parties join together to form a **Coalition Government**.
- **Marking Rubric & Notes**: Core constitutional mechanics of state executive and legislative governance.
