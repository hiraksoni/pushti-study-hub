## 1. Chapter Overview & Core Concepts

> **Syllabus Summary**: Comprehensive curriculum coverage of thermal physics: molecular definition of heat and temperature, thermometry scales (Celsius, Fahrenheit, Kelvin) with mathematical conversion formulas, clinical vs laboratory thermometer mechanics (constriction/kink), the three fundamental modes of heat transfer (Conduction in crystalline lattices, Convection fluid currents and coastal breezes, Radiation electromagnetic infrared waves), absorption/emission spectra of surfaces, and Dewar vacuum flask thermodynamics.

### Thermal Energy, Temperature & Thermometry Scales
Heat is a form of energy that flows spontaneously from a region of higher temperature to a region of lower temperature due to a thermal gradient. Temperature is an intensive physical property measuring the average kinetic energy of molecular motion. Standard Thermometric Scales: 1. Celsius Scale (°C): Lower Fixed Point (LFP, pure ice melting) = 0°C; Upper Fixed Point (UFP, pure steam boiling at 1 atm) = 100°C; divided into 100 equal degrees. 2. Fahrenheit Scale (°F): LFP = 32°F; UFP = 212°F; divided into 180 equal degrees. 3. Kelvin Scale (SI Unit, K): Absolute thermodynamic scale starting at Absolute Zero (0 K = −273.15°C, where all classical molecular motion ceases). Fundamental Conversion Formulas: T(°C) = 5/9 × (T(°F) − 32); T(°F) = (9/5 × T(°C)) + 32; T(K) = T(°C) + 273.15.

### Clinical vs Laboratory Thermometers & Liquid Selection
1. Clinical Thermometer: Specifically calibrated for human body temperature over the range 35°C to 42°C (or 94°F to 108°F). Normal healthy human body temperature is 37.0°C (98.6°F). Critical Design Feature: Contains a narrow constriction (kink) in the capillary bore just above the bulb. When removed from the patient's mouth/armpit, the mercury thread contracts and snaps at the kink, preventing mercury backflow and allowing accurate temperature reading at leisure. Must be shaken vigorously before reuse to reset mercury into the bulb. 2. Laboratory Thermometer: Calibrated for broad experimental ranges, typically −10°C to 110°C. Has NO constriction; must be read while immersed in the substance. 3. Thermometric Liquid: Mercury is preferred because: (a) uniform expansion across temperatures, (b) high boiling point (357°C) and low freezing point (−39°C), (c) opaque, lustrous silvery appearance, (d) does not wet or stick to glass capillary walls. Alcohol thermometers (dyed red) are used for sub-zero Arctic climates (freezing point −114°C).

### Modes of Heat Transfer: Conduction, Convection & Radiation
1. Conduction: Predominant in solids. Heat energy transfers from hot to cold regions through direct atomic/molecular vibrations and free electron collisions without any bulk displacement of matter. Good Conductors: Metals (Silver > Copper > Aluminium > Iron). Poor Conductors (Insulators): Wood, plastic, bakelite, rubber, glass, and trapped still air. 2. Convection: Occurs in fluids (liquids and gases). Heat transfers through the actual physical movement of heated molecules. Heated fluid expands, becomes less dense, and floats upward; colder, denser fluid sinks to replace it, establishing a continuous convection current. 3. Radiation: Transfer of heat via electromagnetic infrared waves requiring no material medium. Travels at the speed of light through empty vacuum (e.g. solar energy reaching Earth). All warm bodies radiate infrared heat.

### Atmospheric Convection Currents & The Vacuum Flask Triad
1. Coastal Breezes: (a) Sea Breeze (Daytime): Land heats up much faster than the high-specific-heat sea. Warm air over land expands and rises; cool, dense sea air rushes inland to take its place. (b) Land Breeze (Nighttime): Land cools down rapidly while sea retains heat. Warm air over the sea rises; cool air from land flows seaward. 2. Thermal Absorption & Emission: Dark matte surfaces are excellent absorbers and emitters of thermal radiation; light shiny surfaces reflect radiation. 3. Dewar Vacuum Flask (Thermos): Eliminates all 3 heat transfer modes simultaneously: (a) Vacuum between double glass walls completely stops Conduction and Convection. (b) Silvered mirror coatings on glass walls reflect infrared radiant waves back inside (stops Radiation). (c) Insulating plastic stopper and cork base prevent convective air exchange and conductive heat leakage.
