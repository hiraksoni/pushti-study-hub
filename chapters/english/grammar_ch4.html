Chapter 4: Case: Noun and Pronoun

  • Cases: Nouns and pronouns have different forms depending on their function in a sentence. There are three main cases: nominative (subjective), objective, and possessive.
  • Nominative (Subjective) Case: Used as the subject of a sentence. The subject is the person, place, thing or idea that performs the action or is described.
    Examples: He paints the portraits. The cat is sleeping on the couch.
  • Objective Case: Used as the direct object of a verb or the object of a preposition. The object receives the action of the verb or shows relationship.
    Note: The direct object is taken as the objective case, while the indirect object is taken as the dative case.
    Example: She gave me a gift. ("me" = dative case); I met your sister. ("your sister" = objective case)
  • Possessive Case: Shows ownership or possession over an object. Possessive nouns usually have an apostrophe (') and an s, while possessive pronouns do not.
    Examples: It is our idea. This is your pencil. Kriti's lunch is packed.

3. John went to the store.
4. We have a test tomorrow.
5. The teacher explained the lesson.

2. They
3. John
4. We

6. It
7. The children

2. me / him / her

7. her
