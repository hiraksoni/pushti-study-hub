The Mechanics of Homophones
Textbook Pages 149–150
Grammar Definition: Homophones are words that share the exact same or very similar pronunciation (sound), but have different spellings and completely different meanings.
Etymology Hook: Greek Homo (same) + Phone (sound/voice) = Same Sound, Different Spelling!
Authentic Spotlight Grammar Textbook Scan (Page 149)

Why Homophones Cause Illogical Sentences:

Because they sound identical in speech, writers frequently confuse them in writing, creating absurd mistakes:

Deer (noun): A graceful wild horned forest animal.
❌ "Anshu is deer to me." → Illogical! Anshu is not a four-legged woodland animal!
✔️ "I saw many deer in the forest sanctuary."
Dear (adjective): Beloved, highly cherished, or affectionate.
❌ "I saw a dear in the zoo." → Illogical spelling!
✔️ "Anshu is dear to me."

Exercise A (Textbook Page 150) — Fill in the blanks with the correct words 8 Authentic Questions
1. She gave him a __________ of her mind. (peace / piece)
Answer: piece • Explanation: The idiom "give someone a piece of your mind" means speaking angrily and candidly. "Peace" means tranquility.
2. He's the __________ to the throne. (hair / heir)
Answer: heir (pronounced 'air') • Explanation: An "heir" is a person legally entitled to property or royal rank after someone dies. "Hair" grows on the scalp.
3. Do you think I can __________ that stick in half? (break / brake)
Answer: break • Explanation: To "break" is to separate or smash into parts. "Brake" is the mechanical friction device that halts a vehicle.
4. What do you think an eagle __________ on? (prey / pray)
Answer: preys (prey) • Explanation: "Prey on" means to hunt down as food. "Pray" means addressing solemn petitions to God.
5. I could not __________ what he said. (here / hair / hear)
Answer: hear • Explanation: "Hear" is the auditory sense of perceiving sound with the ears. "Here" indicates this specific place.
6. Why do you look like a __________ caught in headlights? (deer / dear)
Answer: deer • Explanation: The idiom "deer caught in headlights" describes someone so surprised or terrified they freeze completely motionless.
7. I forgot to bring my __________ kit to school today. (stationary / stationery)
Answer: stationery • Explanation: Stationery with an 'e' refers to pens and envelopes. Stationary with an 'a' means standing still!
8. These pants are __________. (loose / lose)
Answer: loose • Explanation: "Loose" (rhymes with goose) means baggy or not fitting tight. "Lose" (rhymes with choose) is a verb meaning to misplace.

Etymology Hook: Greek Homo (same) + Onym (name/word) = Same Exact Word, Multiple Personalities!

Suffix Fundamentals & Master Catalog
Textbook Pages 152–154
Grammar Definition: Suffixes are letters or morphemes appended to the end of a root word to generate an entirely new word, often altering its grammatical category (e.g., changing a noun to an adjective, or a verb to an abstract noun).
Root Word + Suffix = Derivative Word (e.g., comfort + -able = comfortable; pain + -less = painless; bad + -ly = badly).

Common Suffixes & Word Families (Spotlight Grammar Page 153)

Suffix Grammatical Role Textbook Derivative Word Inventory
-able Forms adjectives denoting "capable of / worthy of" admirable, agreeable, approachable, avoidable, believable, breakable, comfortable
-en Forms verbs meaning "to make or become" blacken, brighten, cheapen, dampen, darken, fatten, flatten, freshen, gladden
-ful Forms adjectives meaning "full of / possessing" armful, careful, cheerful, colourful, delightful, fearful, forgetful, helpful, hopeful
-ion Forms abstract nouns of action, process, or state action, attraction, celebration, collection, collision, communication, confusion
-ly Forms adverbs of manner ("in such a way") badly, bravely, brightly, cleverly, fairly, foolishly, freely, gladly, greatly, happily
-less Forms adjectives meaning "without / devoid of" fruitless, priceless, speechless, sleepless, hapless, senseless, limitless, cordless
-er Forms nouns designating an agent or performer catcher, commander, dancer, defender, designer, dodger, driver, dryer, explorer

The 5 Golden Rules of Adding Suffixes (Pages 153–154)

Rule 1 Textbook Page 153

Direct Addition with -ness and -ly

When adding the suffixes -ness and -ly to a standard root word, the root spelling generally undergoes no alteration.

kind + -ness = kindness  |  love + -ly = lovely
Stay Ahead Exception: If a root word terminates in a consonant followed by -y, the -y changes into -i before adding -ness or -ly:
• ready + -ly = readily  • giddy + -ness = giddiness  • happy + -ness = happiness
Rule 2 Textbook Page 153

Vowel + -y Preservation Rule

When a word concludes with -y that is directly preceded by a vowel (a, e, i, o, u), keep the letter -y intact when adding any suffix.

enjoy + -ed = enjoyed  |  play + -ing = playing  |  buy + -er = buyer  |  joy + -ful = joyful
Rule 3 Textbook Page 153

Dropping Silent -e Before Vowel Suffixes

When a suffix beginning with a vowel (-ing, -y, -able, -ed) is joined to a root ending in a silent -e, the terminal -e is dropped.

care + -ing = caring  |  noise + -y = noisy  |  write + -ing = writing
Soft Sound Exception: When words end in -ce or -ge, the silent -e is retained if the suffix starts with a or o to preserve the soft sound (/s/ and /dʒ/):
• courage + -ous = courageous  • peace + -able = peaceable  • notice + -able = noticeable
Rule 4 Textbook Page 154

Doubling Final Consonant (Short Vowel Sound)

When a single-syllable root word concludes with a short vowel sound followed immediately by a single consonant, the final consonant must be doubled before attaching a suffix starting with a vowel.

flip + -ed = flipped  |  mud + -y = muddy  |  run + -ing = running  |  stop + -ed = stopped
Rule 5 Textbook Page 154

Multi-Syllable Doubling of Terminal -l

When a word contains more than one syllable and terminates in the consonant -l, you must double the l upon appending a suffix.

control + -ed = controlled  |  compel + -ing = compelling  |  travel + -er = traveller
Boundary Condition: This doubling rule does not apply to words terminating with -w, -x, or -y (e.g., box → boxing, grow → growing).

2. The failing company had to file for __________. (root: bankrupt)
Answer: bankruptcy • Suffix: -cy (converts adjective 'bankrupt' into abstract noun).
3. After she gave birth, the villagers thought that __________ suited her well. (root: mother)
Answer: motherhood • Suffix: -hood (denoting the state, condition, or period of being a mother).
4. He tried __________, but she wasn't impressed. (root: flatter)
Answer: flattery • Suffix: -y (transforms verb 'flatter' into the abstract noun of action).

Eliminating Tautologies & Redundancies
Textbook Pages 156–158
What is a Redundancy (Unnecessary Repetition)?
Certain expressions pair two words together even though one word already encompasses the full meaning of the other. In English writing, this generates superfluous, repetitive phrasing that weakens your prose.
The Golden Editing Rule: If a modifying word adds zero new information because the core noun/verb already implies it, cut it out!

Master Textbook Catalog of 22 Redundant Terms (Pages 157–158)

actual fact
fact
A fact is by definition a statement verified to be true. Adding "actual" is redundant.
added bonus
bonus
A bonus is already something added above the standard.
ATM machine
ATM (or automated teller machine)
The "M" in ATM stands for Machine; saying "ATM machine" says machine twice!
blatantly obvious
blatant (or obvious)
Things that are blatant are already conspicuous and obvious.
empty space
space
Space already signifies an unoccupied, available area.
false pretence
pretence
A pretence is by its very nature deceptive and false.
repay back
repay (or pay back)
"Re-" implies back; use either "repay" or "pay back", never both together.
repeat again
repeat (or say again)
Repeat means to say or do once more. "Again" is wholly unnecessary.
return back
return (or come back)
Return already denotes going or coming back.
revert back
revert (or reply)
Revert already carries the sense of responding or returning.
brief moment / brief summary
moment / summary
A moment is brief; a summary is inherently concise.
circulate around
circulate
To circulate means to move freely from person to person. "Around" is useless repetition.
close proximity
proximity (or closeness)
Proximity literally means nearness in space or time.
collaborate together
collaborate (or work together)
The prefix "co-" denotes togetherness; one cannot collaborate alone!
exact same
the same (or identical)
Same denotes not different. "Exact" contributes no fresh meaning.
two equal halves
two halves
A half mathematically represents an equal 50% division.
free gift
gift
A gift is, by ethical and linguistic definition, given without charge.
unexpected surprise
surprise
All genuine surprises are unexpected; an expected surprise is a contradiction.
9 am in the morning
9 am (or 9 in the morning)
a.m. (ante meridiem) already designates the morning period.
total destruction
destruction
Destruction already signifies ruining beyond repair.
temper tantrum
tantrum
A tantrum is already an outburst of bad temper.
absolutely certain / essential
certain / essential
Certainty and essentiality are absolute non-gradable qualities.

Exercise E (Textbook Page 158) — 5 Additional Redundant Phrases Analyzed Textbook Inquiry Task

Textbook Directive: Find five other unnecessary repetitive phrases and write them in the blanks provided. Also, make a sentence with the correct word or phrase.

1. Redundancy: past history → Correct: history
Rationale: History is by definition past events. "Past history" is redundant.
• Model Sentence: The museum curator shared the fascinating history of the ancient Harappan civilization.
2. Redundancy: end result → Correct: result (or outcome)
Rationale: A result occurs at the conclusion of a process; it is inherently at the end.
• Model Sentence: After weeks of diligent preparation, the final result exceeded all expectations.
3. Redundancy: revert back → Correct: revert (or reply)
Rationale: The prefix re- signifies back. "Revert back" is a double-backward tautology.
• Model Sentence: Please review the draft and revert to our team by Monday afternoon.
4. Redundancy: free gift → Correct: gift
Rationale: A gift is something presented without payment. If you pay for it, it is a purchase!
• Model Sentence: Each attending guest received a commemorative brass pen as a gift.
5. Redundancy: sum total → Correct: total (or sum)
Rationale: The sum and the total are synonymous mathematical aggregates.
• Model Sentence: The total cost of the school textbook bundle came to ₹1,450.
