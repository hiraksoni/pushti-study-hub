Passage 1: The Elderly Carpenter and the House

10 Marks
Read the passage given below and answer the questions that follow: An elderly carpenter was ready to retire. He told his employer of his plans to leave the house building business and live a more leisurely life with his family. He would miss the pay cheque, but he needed to retire. The employer was sorry to see his good worker go and asked if he could build just one more house as a personal favour. The carpenter said ‘yes’, but in time it was easy to see that his heart was not in his work. He resorted to shoddy workmanship and used inferior material. It was an unfortunate way to end his career. When the carpenter finished his work, his employer handed over the key of the house to the carpenter— “This is your house, my gift to you.” What a shock! What a shame! If he had only known he was building his own house, he would have done it all so differently! Now he had to live in a poorly built house. So it is with us. We build our lives, a day at a time, often putting less than our best into the building. Then, with a shock, we realize we have to live in the house we have built. If we could do it over, we would do it much differently. But, you cannot go back. You are the carpenter, and every day you hammer a nail, place a board, or erect a wall. Someone once said, your attitude, and the choices you make today, help build the “house” you will live in tomorrow. Therefore, build wisely! The carpenter stood before his shoddy creation, the heavy key weighing down his hand like a block of lead. He looked at the crooked doors and the weak foundation, weeping for the craftsmanship and integrity he had thrown away out of sheer impatience. He realized that the only way to find peace was to spend his retirement years rebuilding the structure, replacing every cheap board with strong oak, ensuring his legacy ended with pride rather than regret. Like that old man. We often forget that the foundation we lay today determines the shelter we enjoy tomorrow. Every kind word spoken, every honest hour worked and every difficult choice made with integrity is a sturdy beam in the architecture of our character. Do not wait for the final keys to be handed over to regret the shortcuts you took; build with your finest efforts right now, for you must live in the house of your own making.

View Answer & Grammar Rule
Answer: Cycling and dancing (Gerunds / Non-finite verbs)

Answer: Verb: 'invited' — Transitive Verb
