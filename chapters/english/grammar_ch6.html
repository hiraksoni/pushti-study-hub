Articles (a / an / the)

Textbook Definition: 'a' and 'an' (Indefinite) are used for single countable nouns. 'an' is used before vowel sounds; 'a' is used before consonant sounds. 'the' (Definite) is used before both singular and plural nouns to denote something particular.
Layman Terms: Use 'a' or 'an' when you are talking about any general thing (I want an apple). Use 'the' when you are talking about a specific thing both you and the listener know about (I want the red apple).
Indefinite (a / an)Definite (the)
a cat, a dog, a unicorn, a one-eyed pirate, an umbrella
an hour, an apple, an honest mistake, a user, an MP3 player		 the moon, the sun, the president, the oldest book
the boy we saw yesterday, the Pacific Ocean, the River Nile

Possessives & Demonstratives

Textbook Definition: Demonstratives tell relative position (this, that, these, those). Possessives show relation or possession (my, our, your, its, their, his, her).
Layman Terms: Demonstratives point at things: "this" for near, "that" for far, "these" for plural near, "those" for plural far. Possessives show who owns it.
DemonstrativesPossessives
this book, that car, these shoes, those mountains
this idea, that guy, these apples, those days		 my car, your house, his dog, her bag, its tail
our school, their team, my mistakes, our victory

Quantifiers

Textbook Definition: Tell us about number or quantity. Many/several/a few for countable nouns. Much/little for uncountable. Some/Any for positive/negative.
Layman Terms: Words that tell you 'how much' or 'how many' of something there is.
Countable (Many/several/a few)Uncountable & Mixed (Much/little/some/any)
many friends, several books, a few cars, many options
several times, a few errors, many challenges		 much water, little time, some sugar, any luck
much effort, little hope, some money, any milk

Distributives & Interrogatives

Textbook Definition: Distributives show distribution (either, neither, each, every). Interrogatives help ask questions (which, who, why, whose, what, how much, how many).
Layman Terms: Distributives refer to a group's members individually rather than the whole group together. Interrogatives are question words used before nouns.
DistributivesInterrogatives
each student, every day, each of the boys, every person
either side, neither way, either of them, neither answer		 which book, whose car, what time, how much water
how many apples, which color, whose turn, what day
