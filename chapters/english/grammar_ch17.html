Active vs Passive Voice

Textbook Definition: A verb is in the active voice when the subject does the action. The passive voice is the form of the verb which shows that the subject is the receiver of an action. The object of the active sentence becomes the subject of the passive sentence. The subject of the active sentence becomes the agent (usually preceded by 'by').
Layman Terms: Active voice means the subject is DOING the action. Passive voice means the action is happening TO the subject. We use passive when the action itself is more important than who did it.
Active VoicePassive Voice
Ronnie plays the guitar.The guitar is played by Ronnie.
The dog chases the cat.The cat is chased by the dog.
She writes a letter.A letter is written by her.
They eat apples.Apples are eaten by them.
He builds a house.A house is built by him.
I watch a movie.A movie is watched by me.
You read a book.A book is read by you.
We sing a song.A song is sung by us.

Pronoun Changes

Textbook Definition: The subject pronoun of the active sentence changes to an object pronoun when it becomes the agent in the passive sentence.
Layman Terms: When a person moves to the end of the sentence (after "by"), you have to use the object form of their pronoun, like changing "I" to "me".

Tense Changes (Active to Passive)

Textbook Definition: An appropriate form of the verb 'be' is added before the main verb, which changes to the past participle (3rd form). Only transitive verbs can be changed into passive.
Layman Terms: You have to match the timeline (past, present, future) by picking the right form of "be" (is, was, has been, etc.) and then slapping on the 3rd form (past participle) of the action word.

Exercise B: Transform these sentences by changing the voice

2. They are not organising the event. → The event is not being organised by them.
3. She has not completed the assignment. → The assignment has not been completed by her.
