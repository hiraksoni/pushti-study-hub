Personal Pronouns

Textbook Definition: Pronouns that replace the names of persons and things, playing the role of a subject or an object. They have first, second, and third person forms.
Layman Terms: Words used instead of names. Use them when you don't want to repeat someone's or something's name over and over again.
Subjective (Doing the action)Objective (Receiving the action)
I am going to the store.
We love playing football.
You are a great singer.
He forgot his backpack.
She is learning Spanish.
It rained all day yesterday.
They built a new house.
You all need to listen carefully.		 The teacher called me.
They invited us to the party.
I will send you the details.
The dog bit him.
We gave her a present.
I read it in one day.
The principal thanked them.
She warned you all.

Possessive & Demonstrative Pronouns

Textbook Definition: Possessive pronouns show ownership. Demonstrative pronouns indicate or point out specific nouns in a sentence based on distance.
Layman Terms: Words that show who owns what, or words used to point a finger at something near or far.
Possessive (Ownership)Demonstrative (Pointing out)

Is this seat yours?
The victory was ours.

That was a fantastic movie.
Those are very tall mountains.
Is this the right way?
Are these the keys you lost?
That looks like a huge storm.
Those were the best days.

Interrogative & Relative Pronouns

Textbook Definition: Interrogative pronouns are used to ask questions. Relative pronouns join a phrase or clause with another in a sentence.
Layman Terms: Question words (who, what, which) and connector words that refer back to something just mentioned (the man 'who' called).
Interrogative (Asking questions)Relative (Connecting clauses)

Whom did you invite?
Whose umbrella is this?

What are you doing?		 The girl who won the prize is happy.

The house that Jack built is huge.
I know the person who did this.
Here is the book that you lent me.
This is the artist whose work I admire.

Reflexive & Emphatic Pronouns

Textbook Definition: Reflexive pronouns refer back to the subject of the sentence. Emphatic pronouns are used merely to add emphasis to a noun or pronoun.
Layman Terms: Words ending in -self or -selves. Reflexive is when you do something to yourself. Emphatic is when you emphasize that YOU did it personally.
Reflexive (Action reflects on subject)Emphatic (Adding emphasis)
I cut myself while chopping.
You should take care of yourself.
He bought himself a watch.
She looked at herself in the mirror.
The cat washed itself.
We enjoyed ourselves at the park.
You must protect yourselves.
They hid themselves in the cave.		 I myself saw him do it.
You yourself must finish the job.
He himself wrote the letter.
She herself baked the cake.
The town itself is very small.
We ourselves are responsible.
You yourselves chose this path.
They themselves admitted their mistake.

Distributive, Reciprocal & Indefinite

Textbook Definition: Distributive denote things taken as single. Reciprocal show relationship between subjects. Indefinite refer to unspecified beings/objects.
Layman Terms: Words for dividing things up (each, neither), words for mutual actions (each other), and words for vague things (someone, nothing).
CategoryExamples
Distributive (One at a time)Each of the boys got a prize.
Either of you can go.
Neither of the answers is correct.
Every student must attend.
Both of them are talented.
All of the cake was eaten.
Each gave his opinion.
Neither road leads to the city.
Reciprocal (Mutual action)The two brothers love each other.
They fought with each other.
The team members supported one another.
All the students helped one another.
Romeo and Juliet loved each other.
They trust one another completely.
Indefinite (Unspecified)Someone is at the door.
Anyone can enter the contest.
Nobody knows the secret.
Everything is going fine.
Nothing can stop us now.
No one heard the loud noise.
Somebody left their jacket here.
I have nowhere to go.
Exercise A: Subjective vs Objective
Identify and write the pronouns in these sentences as subjective or objective.
1. We will join you at the party tomorrow. → We (subjective), you (objective)
2. Can you help us with our homework? → you (subjective), us (objective)
3. I saw him at the park yesterday. → I (subjective), him (objective)
4. He looked at me and smiled. → He (subjective), me (objective)
5. The teacher asked if he had completed his assignment. → he (subjective) ["his" is possessive adj]
6. You are an intelligent person. → You (subjective)
7. It was a beautiful day. → It (subjective)
8. They enjoyed at the party. → They (subjective)

✨ Beyond the Textbook

E1. She gave them the instructions clearly. → She (subjective), them (objective)
E2. They invited her to the concert. → They (subjective), her (objective)
E3. I told him the truth. → I (subjective), him (objective)
E4. We rely on you for support. → We (subjective), you (objective)
E5. He asked us to participate. → He (subjective), us (objective)
