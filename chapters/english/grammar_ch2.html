Chapter 2: Countable and Uncountable Nouns

  • Countable Nouns: Some nouns are treated as separate items which can be counted. (e.g. a woman, two sisters, emperors). They can be further classified into singular (one person/thing) and plural (more than one).
  • Uncountable Nouns: Some words are treated as whole and cannot be counted or separated. (e.g. furniture, knowledge, music).
    Note: Some nouns are not used with a/an or numbers and are not used in the plural (e.g. false information, listening to music). However, "some" and "any" can be used with both countable and uncountable nouns.
  • Rules of Forming Plural Nouns from Singular:
    • Nouns ending in -s, -ss, -sh, -ch, -x form plurals by adding -es. (ash->ashes, dish->dishes, batch->batches, fox->foxes, class->classes, gas->gases, box->boxes, match->matches)
    • Nouns ending in -y preceded by a consonant change -y to -ies. (army->armies, fairy->fairies, baby->babies, family->families)
    • Nouns ending in -y preceded by a vowel simply add -s. (bay->bays, key->keys, chimney->chimneys, storey->storeys)
    • Nouns ending in -o preceded by a consonant add -es. (buffalo->buffaloes, potato->potatoes, cargo->cargoes, torpedo->torpedoes)
    • Nouns ending in -f or -fe change into -ves. (calf->calves, scarf->scarves, half->halves, knife->knives). Exceptions (add -s): belief->beliefs, hoof->hoofs/hooves, chief->chiefs, proof->proofs, cliff->cliffs, roof->roofs, dwarf->dwarfs/dwarves.
    • Change of inside vowels. (foot->feet, man->men, fisherman->fishermen, milkman->milkmen, gentleman->gentlemen, mouse->mice, goose->geese, tooth->teeth)
    • Compound nouns change the principal word. (brother-in-law -> brothers-in-law, commander-in-chief -> commanders-in-chief, daughter-in-law -> daughters-in-law, maid-servant -> maid-servants, son-in-law -> sons-in-law, lady-doctor -> lady-doctors)
    • Letters, numbers, degrees, abbreviations add apostrophe 's. (A->A's, BA->BA's, P->P's, MA->MA's)
    • Foreign origin nouns ending in -us drop it and add -i. (cactus->cacti, fungus->fungi, focus->foci, syllabus->syllabi, nucleus->nuclei, stimulus->stimuli)
    • Nouns ending in -is change to -es. (axis->axes, crisis->crises, basis->bases, oasis->oases)
    • Nouns ending in -on drop it and add -a. (criterion->criteria, phenomenon->phenomena)
    • Nouns ending in -um drop it and add -a. (bacterium->bacteria, curriculum->curricula, addendum->addenda, memorandum->memoranda)
Exercise A: Underline the nouns and write whether the nouns in these sentences are countable or uncountable.

1. The children are playing in the garden.
2. I don't like milk.
3. I prefer tea.
4. Scientists say that the environment is threatened by pollution.
5. There are a lot of windows in our classroom.
6. We need some glue to fix this vase.
7. The waiters in this restaurant are very professional.
8. He drinks two big glasses of water every morning.
Answers:
1. children (countable), garden (countable)
2. milk (uncountable)
3. tea (uncountable)
4. Scientists (countable), environment (uncountable), pollution (uncountable)
5. windows (countable), classroom (countable)
6. glue (uncountable), vase (countable)
7. waiters (countable), restaurant (countable)
8. glasses (countable), water (uncountable), morning (countable)
