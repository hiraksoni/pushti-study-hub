Countable vs Uncountable Nouns

Textbook Definition: Countable nouns are treated as separate items which can be counted. Uncountable nouns are treated as whole and cannot be separated or counted.
Layman Terms: If you can put a number in front of it (1 apple, 2 apples) without it sounding weird, it is countable. If you cannot (1 water? 2 knowledges?), it is uncountable.

Basic Plural Rules

Textbook Definition: Rules for forming plurals by adding suffixes depending on the word ending (-s, -es, -ies, -ves).
Layman Terms: Standard patterns for making things plural based on their last few letters.
RuleSingularPlural
Most Nouns (add -s)book, table, pen, cat, window, gardenbooks, tables, pens, cats, windows, gardens
Ends in -s, -ss, -sh, -ch, -x (add -es)ash, batch, class, box, dish, fox, gas, matchashes, batches, classes, boxes, dishes, foxes, gases, matches
Ends in consonant + y (-ies)army, baby, fairy, family, city, party, storyarmies, babies, fairies, families, cities, parties, stories
Ends in vowel + y (add -s)bay, chimney, key, storey, day, toy, boybays, chimneys, keys, storeys, days, toys, boys
Ends in consonant + o (add -es)buffalo, cargo, potato, torpedo, hero, tomato, echobuffaloes, cargoes, potatoes, torpedoes, heroes, tomatoes, echoes
Ends in -f or -fe (-ves)calf, half, scarf, knife, leaf, wolf, life, thiefcalves, halves, scarves, knives, leaves, wolves, lives, thieves

Irregular Plurals & Foreign Origins

Textbook Definition: Some nouns form plurals by changing inside vowels, modifying the principal word in compounds, or using foreign origin suffixes.
Layman Terms: Words that totally change their spelling, words glued together where only one part changes, and weird science/latin words.

Special & Exceptional Cases (Stay Ahead)

Textbook Definition: Nouns that do not follow standard plural rules or have identical singular and plural forms.
Layman Terms: Rule-breakers. Some words look plural but are singular, some look singular but act plural, and some never change.
CategoryExamples & Rules
-f/-fe Exceptions (just add -s)belief → beliefs, chief → chiefs, cliff → cliffs, proof → proofs, roof → roofs
Two valid plural formsdwarf → dwarfs/dwarves, hoof → hoofs/hooves, scarf → scarfs/scarves
Same Singular and Pluralfish, sheep, deer, aircraft, species, salmon, spacecraft
Always used in Pluralspectacles, scissors, trousers, jeans, glasses, binoculars
Look singular, act pluralpeople, police, cattle, staff, poultry
Look plural, act singularmathematics, physics, news, gymnastics, billiards
Uncountable (No plural form)milk, ability, furniture, advice, luggage, information, homework
Exercise A: Identify Countable/Uncountable
Underline the nouns and write whether the nouns in these sentences are countable or uncountable.
1. The children are playing in the garden. → children (countable), garden (countable)
2. I don't like milk. → milk (uncountable)
3. I prefer tea. → tea (uncountable)
4. Scientists say that the environment is threatened by pollution. → Scientists (countable), environment (countable), pollution (uncountable)
5. There are a lot of windows in our classroom. → windows (countable), classroom (countable)
6. We need some glue to fix this vase. → glue (uncountable), vase (countable)
7. The waiters in this restaurant are very professional. → waiters (countable), restaurant (countable)
8. He drinks two big glasses of water every morning. → glasses (countable), water (uncountable), morning (countable)
