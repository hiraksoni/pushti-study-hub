What are Articles?

Textbook Definition: Words that point out the person or thing which is being referred to. Tells whether a noun is mentioned in a general or a particular sense.
Layman Terms: Little pointer words ('a', 'an', or 'the') placed in front of nouns to indicate whether you are talking about any general member of a group or one specific, known item.

Indefinite Articles (a, an)

Textbook Definition: Used before singular countable nouns. 'a' is used before words starting with a consonant sound. 'an' is used before words starting with a vowel sound or silent initial consonants.
Layman Terms: Sound is king! Pay attention to the FIRST SOUND of the word, not just its spelling. If it starts with a vowel sound (a, e, i, o, u), use 'an'. If it starts with a consonant sound, use 'a'. Watch out for silent 'h' and vowel letters sounding like /y/ or /w/!

Definite Article (the)

Textbook Definition: Used when referring to a particular person or thing, when a singular noun represents an entire class, before unique celestial/natural entities, before specific proper nouns (rivers, oceans, ranges, monuments, holy scriptures, famous trains), and with superlatives and ordinals.
Layman Terms: Use 'the' whenever both you and the listener know the exact specific item. Also mandatory for one-of-a-kind things in nature (the sun), the number one extreme (the tallest), and ordinal positions (the first, the third).

Omission of Articles (Zero Article)

Textbook Definition: Articles are omitted before proper nouns, material nouns used in a general sense, abstract nouns used in a general sense, names of arts and sciences, languages, games, meals, days, seasons, and diseases.
Layman Terms: Leave it out! Never put 'a', 'an', or 'the' before individual people's names, cities, general concepts, feelings, school subjects, sports, meals, or days of the week, unless referring to a specific instance.
