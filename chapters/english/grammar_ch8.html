Verbs: The Core of a Sentence

Textbook Definition: Essential components of sentences, indicating actions, occurrences, experiences or states of being.
Layman Terms: The "doing", "happening", or "being" words. Without a verb, an English sentence simply cannot exist!

Main vs Auxiliary Verbs

Textbook Definition: Main verbs indicate the action or state of the subject. Auxiliary verbs are helping verbs that indicate the tense, aspect, voice, or mood of the main verb.
Layman Terms: The main verb is the lead star of the sentence. Auxiliary verbs are the backup crew that tell us when and how the action occurs.

Regular vs Irregular Verbs

Textbook Definition: Regular verbs form simple past and past participle by adding -ed (or -d). Irregular verbs form past and past participles in other unpredictable ways.
Layman Terms: Regular verbs follow the simple "+ed" recipe. Irregular verbs rebel and change vowel sounds, change endings, or never change at all.

Transitive vs Intransitive Verbs

Textbook Definition: Transitive verbs require a direct object to complete their meaning (answering 'What?' or 'Whom?'). Intransitive verbs do not require an object.
Layman Terms: Transitive verbs transfer action directly onto a target. Intransitive verbs are self-contained and don't need a target object to make sense.

Direct vs Indirect Objects

Textbook Definition: Direct objects receive the action of the verb directly (answers 'What?' or 'Whom?'). Indirect objects receive the action indirectly, naming the person to whom or for whom the action is performed.
Layman Terms: The Direct Object is the actual item being passed or acted upon (WHAT). The Indirect Object is the lucky person receiving it (TO/FOR WHOM).

Complements: Subject & Object

Textbook Definition: Complements are words that complete the meaning of the predicate. A Subject Complement follows a linking verb and describes the subject. An Object Complement follows and modifies the direct object.
Layman Terms: Essential descriptors! A subject complement tells us more about the subject (doer). An object complement tells us more about the direct object (receiver).

Verbs of Incomplete Prediction

Textbook Definition: Verbs (especially intransitive or linking verbs) that require additional words (complements) to form a complete predicate and express a complete thought.
Layman Terms: Cliffhanger verbs! If you stop talking right after them ("He became..."), everyone is left waiting for you to finish ("...a doctor!").
