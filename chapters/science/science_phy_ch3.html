Q1 • Level 1: Foundation 1 Mark
Which of the following device was not used by our ancestors to measure time?
Scientific & Mathematical Rationale
Quartz clocks utilize modern electronic piezoelectric quartz crystal oscillations, unlike ancient sundials, water clocks (clepsydra), and sand clocks (hourglasses).
Q2 • Level 1: Foundation 1 Mark
Which of the following cannot be used for measurement of time?
Scientific & Mathematical Rationale
A blinking eye is not a regular periodic event and its frequency varies randomly, making it unsuitable for measuring time accurately.
Q4 • Level 1: Foundation 1 Mark
How many minutes are there in 6 hours?
Scientific & Mathematical Rationale
1 hour = 60 minutes. Therefore, 6 hours = 6 × 60 = 360 minutes.
Q5 • Level 1: Foundation 1 Mark
On Saturday night, Prachi spent 18 minutes on social science homework, 35 minutes on mathematics homework and 22 minutes on english homework. How much time did she spend on her homework?
Scientific & Mathematical Rationale
Total time spent = 18 min (Social Science) + 40 min (Maths) + 20 min (English) = 78 minutes = 1 hour 18 minutes.
Q6 • Level 1: Foundation 1 Mark
How many weeks are there in 56 days?
Scientific & Mathematical Rationale
1 week = 7 days. Number of weeks in 56 days = 56 / 7 = 8 weeks.
Q7 • Level 1: Foundation 1 Mark
What part of second is called nanosecond?
Scientific & Mathematical Rationale
1 nanosecond (ns) = 10⁻⁹ second = 1 / 1,000,000,000 of a second (one billionth of a second).
Q8 • Level 1: Foundation 1 Mark
How many minutes are there in 4.5 hours?
Scientific & Mathematical Rationale
4.5 hours = 4.5 × 60 = 270 minutes.

Q32 • Level 2: Application 1 Mark

Q33 • Level 2: Application 1 Mark
If a car covers 2/5th of the total distance with speed v₁ and the remaining 3/5th distance with speed v₂, then its average speed for the entire journey is:
Scientific & Mathematical Rationale
Let total distance be s. Time t₁ = (2s/5)/v₁ = 2s/(5v₁). Time t₂ = (3s/5)/v₂ = 3s/(5v₂). Total time t = s/(5v₁v₂) × (2v₂ + 3v₁). Average speed = s / t = 5v₁v₂ / (3v₁ + 2v₂).
Q34 • Level 2: Application 1 Mark
A body goes 30 km east then 20 km north then 80 km west and then 50 km east. Find the displacement of the body from the initial position.
Scientific & Mathematical Rationale
Net East-West displacement = 30 East - 80 West = -50 km (50 km West). Net North-South displacement = 20 North - 50 South = -30 km (30 km South). Magnitude = √(50² + 30²) = √(2500 + 900) = √3400 ≈ 58.3 km (or coordinates (-50, -30)).

Section 1 2 Marks (SA)
On what scientific principle are modern wristwatches and quartz clocks based?
Modern quartz clocks and watches work on the principle of the high-frequency periodic vibrations of a tiny quartz crystal driven by a miniature electric cell, offering extremely precise and stable timekeeping.
SUBJECTIVE SECTION 02

Section 2: Simple Pendulum, Periodic Motion & Isochronism

Conceptual mechanisms of oscillatory motion, time period calculations, and Galileo's cathedral observations.

4 Questions
Section 2 1 Mark (VSA)
Give the term used for the time taken by a pendulum to complete one oscillation.
It is termed as the Time Period ($T$) of the pendulum.
Section 2 3 Marks (SA)
Explain Galileo's discovery regarding the simple pendulum at Pisa Cathedral.
In 1583, Galileo Galilei observed a swinging chandelier in Pisa Cathedral and timed the oscillations against his own pulse. He discovered that a pendulum of a given length always takes the exact same time to complete one swing, regardless of amplitude. This property is known as isochronism and laid the foundation for pendulum clocks.
Section 2 3 Marks (SA / Numerical)
A simple pendulum takes 32 s to complete 20 oscillations. Calculate its time period and frequency.
  • Time Period: $T = \frac{\text{Total Time Taken}}{\text{Number of Oscillations}} = \frac{32\text{ s}}{20} = 1.6\text{ s}$.
  • Frequency: $f = \frac{1}{T} = \frac{1}{1.6} = 0.625\text{ Hz}$ (or oscillations per second).
Section 2 3 Marks (SA)
State the factors that affect the time period of a simple pendulum and two factors that do NOT affect it.
  • Factors that affect Time Period: (1) Effective length of pendulum ($T \propto \sqrt{L}$), (2) Acceleration due to gravity ($g$).
  • Factors that DO NOT affect Time Period: (1) Mass or material of the bob, (2) Amplitude of swing (for small angular displacements < 15°).
SUBJECTIVE SECTION 03

Section 3: Speed, Distance, Velocity & Kinematic Conversions

Formula application, vector vs scalar differentiation, and metric conversion calculations.

5 Questions
Section 3 1 Mark (VSA)
Define Speed. Write its formula and SI unit.
Speed is defined as the distance travelled by an object per unit of time: $$\text{Speed} = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Time}}$$. Its SI unit is metre per second (m/s).
Section 3 2 Marks (SA)
Convert 54 km/h into m/s and 25 m/s into km/h.
  • $54\text{ km/h} \times \frac{5}{18} = 3 \times 5 = 15\text{ m/s}$.
  • $25\text{ m/s} \times \frac{18}{5} = 5 \times 18 = 90\text{ km/h}$.

Section 3 2 Marks (Numerical)
The distance between two stations is 240 km. A train takes 4 hours to cover this distance. Calculate the speed of the train in km/h and m/s.
  • $\text{Speed in km/h} = \frac{240\text{ km}}{4\text{ h}} = 60\text{ km/h}$.
  • $\text{Speed in m/s} = 60 \times \frac{5}{18} = 16.67\text{ m/s}$.
Section 3 3 Marks (Numerical)
The odometer of a car reads 57321.0 km at 08:30 AM. At 08:50 AM, the odometer reads 57336.0 km. Find speed in km/min and km/h.
  • Distance covered $\Delta s = 57336.0 - 57321.0 = 15.0\text{ km}$.
  • Time taken $\Delta t = 20\text{ min} = \frac{1}{3}\text{ h}$.
  • $\text{Speed in km/min} = \frac{15\text{ km}}{20\text{ min}} = 0.75\text{ km/min}$.
  • $\text{Speed in km/h} = \frac{15\text{ km}}{1/3\text{ h}} = 45\text{ km/h}$.
SUBJECTIVE SECTION 04

Section 4: Distance-Time Graphs & Motion Curves

Geometric slope interpretation, uniform speed lines, stationary states, and non-uniform curved graphs.

3 Questions
Section 4 1 Mark (VSA)
What is the shape of distance-time graph for a body moving with uniform speed?
A straight line inclined to the time axis. The constant slope directly represents the constant speed.
Section 4 2 Marks (SA)
How does a distance-time graph show that a body is at rest?
When a body is at rest (stationary), its distance from the origin does not change as time elapses. Hence, the graph is a horizontal straight line parallel to the time axis (Slope = 0).
Section 4 2 Marks (SA)
Interpret the physical meaning of the slope of a Distance-Time graph.
The slope (gradient) of a Distance-Time graph is given by: $$\text{Slope} = \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x} = \frac{\text{Change in Distance}}{\text{Change in Time}} = \text{Speed}$$. A steeper slope represents higher speed.
SUBJECTIVE SECTION 05

Section 5: NCERT & HOTS Multi-Step Numerical Problems

High Order Thinking Skills (HOTS) numericals requiring multi-stage speed-distance calculations and average speed derivations.

4 Questions
Section 5 2 Marks (NCERT)
NCERT Q1: Calculate speed of car that travels 150 metres in 10 seconds. Express answer in km/h.
  • Speed in m/s = $\frac{150\text{ m}}{10\text{ s}} = 15\text{ m/s}$.
  • Speed in km/h = $15 \times \frac{18}{5} = 54\text{ km/h}$.
Section 5 3 Marks (NCERT)
NCERT Q2: A train travels 180 km in 3 hours. Find its speed in (i) km/h, (ii) m/s, and (iii) distance covered in 4 hours at constant speed.
  • (i) Speed in km/h = $\frac{180}{3} = 60\text{ km/h}$.
  • (ii) Speed in m/s = $60 \times \frac{5}{18} = 16.67\text{ m/s}$.
  • (iii) Distance in 4 hours = $60\text{ km/h} \times 4\text{ h} = 240\text{ km}$.
Section 5 4 Marks (HOTS / Olympiad)
HOTS Q3: A vehicle travels along a straight line covering 2 km. First 500 m at 10 m/s, next 500 m at 5 m/s. What speed is needed for the remaining 1000 m so that total journey takes 200 s? What is average speed?
  • Total distance $s = 2000\text{ m}$, Total time $t = 200\text{ s}$.
  • Time for first 500 m: $t_1 = \frac{500}{10} = 50\text{ s}$.
  • Time for second 500 m: $t_2 = \frac{500}{5} = 100\text{ s}$.
  • Remaining time available: $t_3 = 200 - (50 + 100) = 50\text{ s}$.
  • Remaining distance: $2000 - 1000 = 1000\text{ m}$.
  • Required speed $v_3 = \frac{1000\text{ m}}{50\text{ s}} = 20\text{ m/s}$.
  • Average speed = $\frac{2000\text{ m}}{200\text{ s}} = 10\text{ m/s}$ (or $36\text{ km/h}$).
Section 5 3 Marks (HOTS)
HOTS Q4: A car covers 30 km at a uniform speed of 60 km/h and the next 30 km at a uniform speed of 30 km/h. Find the average speed of the car for the complete 60 km trip.
  • Total distance $d = 30 + 30 = 60\text{ km}$.
  • Time for first part: $t_1 = \frac{30\text{ km}}{60\text{ km/h}} = 0.5\text{ h} = 30\text{ min}$.
  • Time for second part: $t_2 = \frac{30\text{ km}}{30\text{ km/h}} = 1.0\text{ h} = 60\text{ min}$.
  • Total time $t = t_1 + t_2 = 1.5\text{ h}$.
  • $$\text{Average Speed} = \frac{\text{Total Distance}}{\text{Total Time}} = \frac{60\text{ km}}{1.5\text{ h}} = 40\text{ km/h}$$.
  • Note: Common student trap is taking the arithmetic mean $(60 + 30)/2 = 45\text{ km/h}$, which is incorrect because time spent at each speed is different!

VISUAL SYNTHESIS

4 Core Concept Pillars

Foundational physics principles underlying kinematics, oscillation, and graph models.

4 Pillars

Pillar 1: Isochronism of the Pendulum

The revolutionary discovery by Galileo that the period of oscillation is purely a function of length ($T \propto \sqrt{L}$), completely unaffected by the mass of the bob or amplitude.

Key Formula: $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{L}{g}}$ • $f = \frac{1}{T}$

Pillar 2: Distance vs. Displacement

The critical distinction between scalar total ground covered vs. the straight-line vector from origin to terminus. In closed circuits, net displacement is identically zero!

Golden Rule: $\text{Distance} \ge |\text{Displacement}|$

Pillar 3: Kinematic Formulations

Speed calculates rate of motion ($s/t$). Average speed accounts for non-uniform phases. The conversion trick ($5/18$ and $18/5$) enables swift metric conversions.

Conversion: $1\text{ km/h} = \frac{5}{18}\text{ m/s}$ • $1\text{ m/s} = 3.6\text{ km/h}$

Pillar 4: Graphical Kinematics

In distance-time graphs, slope equals speed ($m = \Delta y / \Delta x$). Horizontal line means object at rest. In velocity-time graphs, area under curve represents distance covered.

Slope Rule: $\text{Slope} = \text{Speed}$ • $\text{Area} = \text{Distance}$

Physics Master Key Vault

Crucial examiner caveats, rapid unit conversion mnemonics, and common numerical pitfalls.

4 Keys

Trap 1: The Average Speed Fallacy

Never average the speeds directly! If a car travels equal distances at $v_1$ and $v_2$, average speed is the harmonic mean: $v_{\text{avg}} = \frac{2 v_1 v_2}{v_1 + v_2}$, NOT $\frac{v_1 + v_2}{2}$. Direct averaging is only valid if time intervals are equal!

Trap 2: Pendulum Amplitude & Mass

Examiners love testing if heavier bobs swing slower. Remind yourself: Mass has ZERO effect on time period! A lead bob and a wooden bob of identical string length swing at the identical period: $T = 2\pi\sqrt{L/g}$.

The 5/18 Conversion Magic

To convert from $\text{km/h}$ to $\text{m/s}$, multiply by $\frac{5}{18}$. To convert from $\text{m/s}$ to $\text{km/h}$, multiply by $\frac{18}{5}$. Quick reference: $18\text{ km/h} = 5\text{ m/s}$, $36\text{ km/h} = 10\text{ m/s}$, $54\text{ km/h} = 15\text{ m/s}$, $72\text{ km/h} = 20\text{ m/s}$.

Trap 4: Reading Graph Axes

Always verify whether the graph is Distance vs. Time or Velocity vs. Time! A horizontal line on a Distance-Time graph means the object is at rest ($v = 0$). A horizontal line on a Velocity-Time graph means the object is moving at constant uniform speed ($a = 0$).
