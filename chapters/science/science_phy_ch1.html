Q1 • Level 1: Basic 1 Mark
The torch bulb is connected to the cell with the help of:
Scientific Rationale

To form a complete, closed circuit allowing current to flow from one terminal of the cell, through the bulb filament, and back to the other terminal, two connecting wires are required.
Q2 • Level 1: Basic 1 Mark
What type of energy conversion happens in a cell when it powers a torch?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
An electric cell contains chemicals that undergo redox reactions, transforming chemical potential energy directly into electrical energy.
Q3 • Level 1: Basic 1 Mark
What is the source of electricity in a typical portable torch?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option C
The electric cells (or battery of cells) act as the chemical source of electromotive force (voltage) driving the current in a portable torch.
Q9 • Level 1: Basic 1 Mark
If a torch does not work even when switched on, which of the following could be the reason?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option D
Any discontinuity or reversal of cells prevents a closed circuit, causing the torch to fail to light.
Q10 • Level 1: Basic 1 Mark
Sometimes an electric bulb does not glow even when connected to a cell without any visible gap. This can occur due to:
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option D
A broken filament breaks the internal path; an exhausted cell provides no voltage; loose connections cause infinite resistance.
Q11 • Level 1: Basic 1 Mark
What is the function of a switch in an electric circuit?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option C
A switch is a control device designed to manually close (complete) or open (break) the conductive path of current.
Q12 • Level 1: Basic 1 Mark
Which of the following components is used to store electrical energy chemically?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option A
A battery or accumulator stores energy in chemical form and releases it as electrical current during discharge.
Q13 • Level 1: Basic 1 Mark

Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
In the OFF position, the switch creates an air gap, leaving the circuit open so zero current can flow.
Q14 • Level 1: Basic 1 Mark
A key or switch in an electric circuit can be placed:
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option C
Current flows through a continuous loop. Breaking the loop at any position stops the current throughout the entire series circuit.
Q15 • Level 1: Basic 1 Mark
Which of the following represents the standard circuit symbol of an electric bulb?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option A
The standard schematic symbol for an incandescent lamp/bulb is a circle with a loop or cross representing the filament.
Q16 • Level 1: Basic 1 Mark
The shorter, thicker vertical line in the symbol of an electric cell represents:
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
In standard schematics, the long thin line represents the positive terminal (+), while the short thick line represents the negative terminal (-).
Q17 • Level 1: Basic 1 Mark
Which switch, if opened in a main supply branch, will cause all light bulbs in that circuit branch to stop glowing?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option A
A master switch placed in the main line controls the entire circuit loop, interrupting the current to all downstream loads.
Q24 • Level 1: Basic 1 Mark
The SI unit of electric charge is the:
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option A
Charge is measured in Coulombs ($Q = I \times t$). Ampere is current, Joule is energy, and Watt is power.
Q25 • Level 1: Basic 1 Mark
Which of the following is NOT a physical circuit component (element)?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option A
Potential difference is a physical quantity (measured in volts), whereas resistors, voltmeters, and batteries are physical components/instruments.
Q28 • Level 1: Basic 1 Mark
Which of the following is NOT a source of electric current?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option D
An electromagnet is a load that consumes electrical energy to produce magnetic force; it does not generate electric current.
Q33 • Level 1: Basic 1 Mark
Which of the following elements has the highest electrical conductivity at room temperature?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option D
Silver has the highest electrical and thermal conductivity of all known metals, followed closely by copper and gold.
Q34 • Level 1: Basic 1 Mark
Why is copper wire universally preferred for domestic electrical wiring?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option A
Copper combines high electrical conductivity, excellent mechanical flexibility (ductility), and cost-effectiveness.
Q35 • Level 1: Basic 1 Mark

Scientific Rationale

Conductors have abundant free electrons that drift readily under an applied electric field, allowing current to flow.
Q36 • Level 1: Basic 1 Mark
Among the following metals, which is the best conductor of electricity?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option A
Silver has the lowest resistivity ($\approx 1.59 \times 10^{-8}\ \Omega\cdot\text{m}$), making it the best conductor.
Q37 • Level 1: Basic 1 Mark
Why is domestic wiring made of copper rather than silver?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option C
Silver is a precious metal and prohibitively expensive for large-scale wiring, whereas copper is economical and widely available.
Q38 • Level 1: Basic 1 Mark
Which of the following does NOT belong to the group formed by the others?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option C
A wooden ruler is an electrical insulator, whereas copper coins, steel spoons, and iron nails are electrical conductors.
Q39 • Level 1: Basic 1 Mark
Why are copper electric wires covered with an outer layer of PVC plastic or rubber?
Scientific Rationale

Plastic and rubber are excellent insulators that protect humans from lethal electric shocks and prevent bare wires from touching.
Q40 • Level 1: Basic 1 Mark
Which among the following materials is an electrical insulator?
Scientific Rationale

Both wool and plastic are non-conductors (insulators) because they lack free mobile charge carriers.
Q41 • Level 2: Intermediate 1 Mark
In which of the following circuits will identical bulbs glow the brightest?

Connecting two cells in series doubles the voltage ($V = 2E$), driving twice the current through a single bulb ($P = V^2/R$), yielding maximum brightness.
Q44 • Level 2: Intermediate 1 Mark

Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
A rubber eraser is an electrical insulator. It creates an open circuit, preventing current from reaching the bulb.
Q45 • Level 2: Intermediate 1 Mark
A student connected two bulbs in series with two cells. How can he make the bulbs glow dimmer?
Scientific Rationale

Removing one cell halves the source electromotive force, reducing current and causing the bulbs to glow dimmer.
Q56 • Level 2: Intermediate 1 Mark

An LED is a polar semiconductor device. The longer lead is the positive anode and the shorter lead is the negative cathode.
Q58 • Level 2: Intermediate 1 Mark
In an electrical circuit, what physically happens when the switch is moved to the 'OFF' position?

Air has high resistance ($> 10^{14}\ \Omega$). Opening the switch creates an air gap that breaks continuity, stopping current.
Q59 • Level 2: Intermediate 1 Mark
Why are copper and aluminium metals universally chosen for transmission cables and household wiring?
Scientific Rationale

Copper and aluminium have low electrical resistance, minimizing energy loss ($I^2R$) during transmission.
Q60 • Level 2: Intermediate 1 Mark

Scientific Rationale

Insulating handles prevent electric current from flowing through the user's body if the tool touches a live conductor.
Q61 • Level 2: Intermediate 1 Mark
Which of the following correctly describes the operation of a switch?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
A switch simply bridges or severs the electrical contacts, permitting or preventing the flow of charges.
Q64 • Level 2: Intermediate 1 Mark
The work done in moving a 10 C charge between two points in a circuit is 350 J. What is the potential difference between the points?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
Potential difference $V = W/Q = 350\text{ J} / 10\text{ C} = 35\text{ V}$.
Q65 • Level 2: Intermediate 1 Mark
Given that the elementary charge of an electron is $1.6 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}$, how many electrons pass through a cross-section of wire per second when it carries 1 Ampere?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
$I = Q/t = n e / t \implies n = 1\text{ A} \times 1\text{ s} / (1.6 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}) = 6.25 \times 10^{18}\text{ electrons}$.
Q69 • Level 2: Intermediate 1 Mark
To connect multiple cells in series to form a battery, how must their terminals be arranged?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option A
In series aiding connection, the positive terminal of each cell connects to the negative terminal of the following cell so their voltages add together.
Q70 • Level 2: Intermediate 1 Mark
Statement 1: In an electric cell, chemical reactions produce potential difference between terminals. Statement 2: Two or more cells connected properly form a battery.
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option A
A cell converts chemical energy into electrical energy, and a combination of two or more cells connected in series or parallel is defined as a battery.

Incandescent lamps waste more than 90% of electrical energy as heat rather than visible light, giving them low energy efficiency and shorter operating lifespans.

Q8 • Level 1: Basic 1 Mark
An LED lamp works on the principle of converting electrical energy into:
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
Light Emitting Diodes (LEDs) emit light via electroluminescence at a p-n semiconductor junction, converting electricity directly to light with negligible heat loss.
Q18 • Level 1: Basic 1 Mark
The bulb in an electric circuit glows primarily because of the:
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
Electric current encounters high resistance in the tungsten filament, dissipating energy as heat ($H = I^2Rt$) until it reaches incandescence and emits light.
Q19 • Level 1: Basic 1 Mark
An electric room heater is used to convert electrical energy primarily into:
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option A
Electric room heaters use nichrome coils possessing high resistivity to convert electrical energy directly into thermal/heat energy.
Q48 • Level 2: Intermediate 1 Mark
Statement 1: An electric bulb works on the heating effect of current. Statement 2: When current passes through the thin tungsten filament, it reaches high temperature and emits white light.
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option A
The filament's high resistance dissipates electrical energy into thermal energy ($I^2Rt$), heating it to ~2500 °C where it glows incandescently.
Q49 • Level 2: Intermediate 1 Mark
In which of the following conditions will the amount of heat produced in a current-carrying wire increase? (i) Increasing current, (ii) Increasing wire resistance, (iii) Passing current for longer time.
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option D
By Joule's Law of Heating, $H = I^2 R t$. Heat is directly proportional to the square of current, resistance, and time of flow.
Q50 • Level 2: Intermediate 1 Mark
How does the electrical resistance of an ideal metallic (ohmic) conductor depend on the applied voltage at a constant temperature?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option C
By Ohm's law, resistance $R = V/I$ is a constant property of the conductor's geometry and material, independent of applied voltage.
Q55 • Level 2: Intermediate 1 Mark
As the operating temperature of an incandescent bulb's tungsten filament increases, what happens to its visible light emission?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option C
By Planck and Stefan-Boltzmann radiation laws, total radiant power scales as $T^4$, rapidly increasing the proportion of visible light emitted.
Q57 • Level 2: Intermediate 1 Mark
Why does an LED bulb consume substantially less power than an incandescent bulb of equal luminosity?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
LEDs convert over 80% of electrical energy directly into light without needing to heat a wire to white-hot incandescence.
Q62 • Level 2: Intermediate 1 Mark
A heating wire is glowing red hot. To further increase its temperature, what adjustment should be made?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option A
Rate of heat dissipation is $H = I^2Rt$. Increasing the current dramatically increases thermal energy generation.

Scientific Rationale

Passing current through a solenoid wrapped on a soft iron core creates a temporary, strong magnet called an electromagnet.

Q46 • Level 2: Intermediate 1 Mark

Q47 • Level 2: Intermediate 1 Mark

Scientific Rationale

Foundational questions on circuit continuity, switch positions, and battery polarities.

2 Questions
Section 1 3 Marks (NCERT)
Draw in your notebook the symbols to represent the following components of electrical circuits: connecting wires, switch in the 'OFF' position, bulb, cell, switch in the 'ON' position, and battery.

Standard Schematic Symbols:

  • Connecting wire: A straight plain continuous line.
  • Switch in OFF position: An open gap line with an inclined bar.
  • Bulb: A circle enclosing a looped filament coil.
  • Electric cell: One long thin vertical line (positive terminal) paired with a parallel short thick vertical line (negative terminal).
  • Switch in ON position: Continuous straight line with dots bridged.
  • Battery: Combination of alternating long thin and short thick lines connected in series (+ to -).
Section 1 2 Marks (Conceptual)
Why is a switch placed anywhere in a circuit able to control the bulb?

An electric current requires an unbroken, closed conducting loop to flow from the positive terminal to the negative terminal of the cell. If the switch is opened (OFF) at any point in the circuit, the conductive loop is broken (open circuit), halting the flow of electrons through all components immediately.

SUBJECTIVE SECTION 02

Section 2: Heating Effect of Current & Joule's Law

Mechanism of electrical resistance, Joule heating ($H = I^2Rt$), and appliance heating elements.

2 Questions
Section 2 3 Marks (NCERT Exemplar)
Explain why the filament of an electric bulb glows white-hot while the connecting wires remain cool.

The filament is fabricated from tungsten, which possesses extremely high electrical resistance and an ultra-high melting point (approx. $3422^\circ\text{C}$). When current passes through, the high resistance causes intense Joule heating ($H = I^2Rt$), heating it to white incandescence ($~2500^\circ\text{C}$) to emit visible light.

In contrast, connecting wires are composed of thick copper or aluminium with negligible electrical resistance, so heat dissipation is imperceptibly small.

Section 2 2 Marks (Application)
Name two devices that deliberately utilize the heating effect of electric current, and state the material used for their heating elements.

Devices: Electric iron, electric toaster, geyser, room heater.
Material: Nichrome (an alloy of nickel, chromium, and iron) because of its exceptionally high resistivity and high resistance to oxidation (does not burn or melt easily at high temperatures).

SUBJECTIVE SECTION 03

Section 3: Safety Devices (Electric Fuse, MCBs & Earthing)

Overcurrent protection, short circuit hazards, fuse wire metallurgy, and modern electromagnetic MCBs.

2 Questions
Section 3 3 Marks (NCERT)
Explain the working principle and construction of an electric fuse. Why should a thick copper wire never be used as a fuse wire?

An electric fuse is a critical safety device designed to break the circuit in the event of excessive current (overloading or short-circuiting). It consists of a thin alloy wire (typically tin-lead alloy) with a low melting point.

When current exceeds the safe limit, the Joule heat generated ($I^2Rt$) rapidly raises the temperature past the alloy's melting point, causing the wire to melt and break the circuit safely.

Why copper wire must NOT be used: Copper has very low resistance and a very high melting point ($1085^\circ\text{C}$). It will not melt during dangerous current spikes, allowing the excessive current to overheat house wiring and cause catastrophic electrical fires.

Section 3 2 Marks (Modern Tech)
What is a Miniature Circuit Breaker (MCB), and what advantage does it offer over traditional wire fuses?

An MCB is an automatic electromagnetic switch that automatically trips into the 'OFF' position whenever current exceeds safe thresholds. Unlike traditional fuses that require manual replacement of melted wires, an MCB can be simply reset back to the 'ON' position once the electrical fault is corrected.

SUBJECTIVE SECTION 04

Section 4: Magnetic Effect of Current & Electromagnets

Oersted's discovery, solenoid construction, electromagnet core material, and electric bell action.

2 Questions
Section 4 3 Marks (NCERT)
Describe Oersted's experiment and state its fundamental scientific conclusion.

In 1820, Danish scientist Hans Christian Oersted placed a magnetic compass needle parallel and close to a straight conducting wire. When electric current was switched ON, the compass needle immediately deflected from its North-South alignment. When the current was reversed, the needle deflected in the opposite direction. When current was switched OFF, the needle returned to magnetic North.

Conclusion: An electric current flowing through a conductor generates a surrounding magnetic field.

Section 4 4 Marks (Comprehensive)
Explain the working mechanism of an electric bell with a circuit schematic description.

An electric bell operates via an electromagnet and a make-and-break contact mechanism:

  1. When the bell switch is pressed, current flows through the electromagnet coil, magnetizing its soft iron core.
  2. The energized electromagnet attracts the soft iron armature attached to a spring strip.
  3. As the armature rushes toward the electromagnet, the striker hits the metallic gong, producing a loud ring.
  4. However, this forward motion simultaneously pulls the contact screw away from the contact strip, breaking the circuit.
  5. With the circuit broken, current stops, the electromagnet de-energizes, and the spring pulls the armature back.
  6. As the armature springs back, it touches the contact screw again, completing the circuit once more. This rapid make-and-break cycle repeats continuously as long as the switch is held down.
SUBJECTIVE SECTION 05

Section 5: Advanced Circuit HOTS & Olympiad Problems

Complex fault-finding, multi-bulb series/parallel configurations, and LED polarity rules.

2 Questions
Section 5 4 Marks (HOTS / Olympiad)
HOTS Q1: Four identical bulbs A, B, C, and D are connected in a circuit. Bulbs A and B are in series, and this combination is connected in parallel with bulb C. Bulb D is connected in the main line. If bulb C's filament breaks, explain what happens to the brightness of bulbs A, B, and D.

Circuit Analysis:

  • Total circuit current flows through main-line bulb D before splitting into parallel branches $(A+B)$ and $C$.
  • When bulb C burns out, branch C becomes an open circuit.
  • The total equivalent resistance of the entire circuit increases because the parallel path has been removed.
  • Due to the increased total resistance, the total current drawn from the battery decreases. Hence, bulb D in the main line dims slightly.
  • However, the voltage across the surviving branch $(A+B)$ increases because the voltage drop across D has decreased. Therefore, bulbs A and B actually glow brighter than before!
Section 5 2 Marks (HOTS / Modern Physics)
HOTS Q2: Why are LEDs (Light Emitting Diodes) increasingly replacing incandescent bulbs and CFLs? State two major technical advantages.

Advantages of LEDs:

  • High Energy Efficiency: LEDs produce light via electroluminescence (electron-hole recombination in semiconductors) without wasting massive energy as heat ($>80\%$ less energy consumed than incandescent bulbs).
  • Long Lifespan & Durability: LEDs have no fragile filament to break or burn out, operating for 25,000 to 50,000 hours with negligible heat dissipation.

VISUAL SYNTHESIS

4 Core Concept Pillars

Four fundamental pillars governing electric circuits, thermal dissipation, magnetic fields, and safety systems.

4 Pillars

Pillar 1: Circuit Continuity & Topology

An electric current requires an unbroken conducting loop from positive to negative terminal. A single opened switch breaks continuity across all series elements.

Rule: Conventional current flows from Positive (+) to Negative (-) externally.

Pillar 2: Joule Heating Effect ($I^2Rt$)

Electrical resistance transforms electrical energy into thermal energy. High resistance nichrome wires glow hot in heaters, while low resistance copper wires transport current safely.

Key Formula: $H = I^2Rt$ • Tungsten melting point is $~3422^\circ\text{C}$.

Pillar 3: Safety & Overcurrent Protection

Electric fuses protect circuits via intentional sacrificial melting. Modern MCBs protect via electromagnetic tripping, preventing electrical fires during short circuits.

Safety Standard: Always look for the official ISI mark on electrical appliances.

Pillar 4: Oersted's Electromagnetism

Current flowing through a coiled wire creates an artificial magnetic field that can be turned ON and OFF at will. Soft iron cores concentrate field lines into powerful lifting electromagnets.

Rule: Electromagnet strength $\propto$ current ($I$) and number of coil turns ($N$).

4 Keys

Oersted's Serendipitous Lecture (1820)

Hans Christian Oersted discovered electromagnetism completely by accident during an undergraduate physics lecture in Copenhagen while setting up a demonstration with a voltaic pile!

Edison's 6,000 Filament Trials

Thomas Edison tested over 6,000 organic materials — including cedar, coconut hair, and carbonized bamboo — before achieving 1,200 hours of continuous incandescent light!

Fuse vs MCB Distinction

A fuse melts irreversibly and requires rewiring with a rated alloy wire. An MCB works via an electromagnetic coil and bimetallic strip, snapping open automatically and resettable with a flick of a lever.

Sturgeon's Horseshoe Electromagnet (1824)

William Sturgeon wrapped 18 turns of bare copper wire around a varnished soft iron bar, creating an electromagnet that lifted 9 pounds — 20 times its own weight!
