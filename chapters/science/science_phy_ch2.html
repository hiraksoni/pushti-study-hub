SECTION 01

Section 1: Heat, Temperature & Thermometry

Thermal equilibrium, Celsius & Fahrenheit scales, clinical vs laboratory thermometers, constriction (kink), and digital sensors.

16 Questions
Q1 • Level 1: Foundation 1 Mark
The measure of the degree of hotness or coldness of an object is called its:
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
Temperature is the quantitative, objective physical measure of the degree of hotness or coldness of a body. Heat is the form of energy transferred due to temperature difference.
Q2 • Level 1: Foundation 1 Mark
The normal temperature of a healthy human body in Celsius and Fahrenheit scales is:
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option A
The normal average physiological body temperature of a healthy human being is 37°C, which precisely corresponds to 98.6°F on the Fahrenheit scale.
Q3 • Level 1: Foundation 1 Mark
What is the standard measurement range of a doctor's clinical thermometer?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option C
A clinical thermometer is designed exclusively for the human body. Human body temperature never falls below 35°C or rises above 42°C (94°F to 108°F), hence its compact range.
Q4 • Level 1: Foundation 1 Mark
The standard temperature measuring range of a laboratory thermometer is generally:
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
Laboratory thermometers are used for diverse scientific experiments and chemical solutions. Their standard calibrated range extends from -10°C up to 110°C.
Q5 • Level 1: Foundation 1 Mark
What is the primary function of the tiny kink (constriction) present in a clinical thermometer?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
The kink or constriction in the capillary bore prevents the mercury thread from falling back into the bulb once removed from the patient's mouth or armpit, allowing an accurate reading.
Q6 • Level 1: Foundation 1 Mark
A liquid-in-glass thermometer works on the fundamental physical principle of:
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option C
Liquid-in-glass thermometers rely on the uniform, proportional volumetric expansion of a thermometric liquid (mercury or alcohol) when thermal energy is absorbed.
Q7 • Level 1: Foundation 1 Mark
Which temperature scale has its Lower Fixed Point (LFP) at 0° and Upper Fixed Point (UFP) at 100°?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
On the Celsius scale, the freezing point of pure water at standard atmospheric pressure is taken as 0°C (LFP), and the boiling point is taken as 100°C (UFP), divided into 100 equal divisions.
Q8 • Level 1: Foundation 1 Mark
Why are modern digital thermometers increasingly preferred over traditional glass mercury thermometers?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
Mercury is a toxic heavy metal that is hazardous to dispose of if a glass thermometer breaks. Digital thermometers use electronic thermistors with clear LCD displays and zero mercury.
Q9 • Level 1: Foundation 1 Mark
Paheli measures her body temperature as 98.6°F, while Boojho measures his as 37°C. Which conclusion is correct?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option C
37°C and 98.6°F are exact conversions of each other: $F = \frac{9}{5}C + 32 = \frac{9}{5}(37) + 32 = 66.6 + 32 = 98.6^\circ\text{F}$. Both students have normal body temperature.
Q10 • Level 2: Application 1 Mark
At what specific temperature do the Celsius and Fahrenheit scales read the exact same numerical value?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
Setting $C = F = x$: $x = \frac{9}{5}x + 32 \implies x - \frac{9}{5}x = 32 \implies -\frac{4}{5}x = 32 \implies x = -40$. Hence at -40°, both thermometers read identical values (-40°C = -40°F).
Q11 • Level 2: Application 1 Mark
A weather report states the ambient temperature as 95°F. Express this reading in degrees Celsius (°C):
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option C
Using the conversion formula: $C = \frac{5}{9}(F - 32) = \frac{5}{9}(95 - 32) = \frac{5}{9}(63) = 5 \times 7 = 35^\circ\text{C}$.
Q12 • Level 2: Application 1 Mark
Why must a clinical thermometer NEVER be sterilized by dipping it in boiling water?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option C
Water boils at 100°C, while a clinical thermometer is calibrated only up to 42°C. The huge thermal expansion of mercury will exert immense hydraulic pressure and burst the glass tube.
Q13 • Level 2: Application 1 Mark
Which property makes mercury an exceptionally superior thermometric fluid compared to water?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
Mercury does not wet/stick to glass, has high silvery opacity for easy reading in thin capillaries, expands uniformly across a wide liquid range (-39°C to 357°C), and is a good thermal conductor.
Q14 • Level 2: Application 1 Mark
What is the lowest possible theoretical temperature in the universe (known as Absolute Zero)?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option C
Absolute Zero is 0 Kelvin ($0\text{ K} = -273.15^\circ\text{C}$), the fundamental limit where all classical molecular kinetic motion ceases completely.
Q15 • Level 2: Application 1 Mark
Raghav's body temperature is measured as 40°C. What does this indicate and what should be done?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
Normal body temperature is 37°C (98.6°F). A reading of 40°C translates to $104^\circ\text{F}$, indicating severe fever that demands immediate medical consultation.
Q16 • Level 2: Application 1 Mark
Which specialized thermometer is employed by meteorological departments to record daily weather extremes?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
A Maximum-Minimum thermometer (Six's thermometer) uses dumbbell-shaped metal indicators within a U-shaped tube to automatically log the highest daytime and lowest nighttime temperatures.
SECTION 02

Section 2: Conduction & Thermal Conductivity

Mechanism of solid conduction, metallic conductors, thermal insulators, double-walled ice boxes, and winter insulation.

16 Questions
Q17 • Level 1: Foundation 1 Mark
The process of heat transfer in solids from the hotter end to the colder end without any bulk motion of particles is:
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option C
Conduction is the primary mode of heat transfer in solids. Thermal energy is passed from molecule to neighbouring molecule via direct collisions and lattice vibrations without displacement.
Q18 • Level 1: Foundation 1 Mark
Which of the following metals is the best conductor of heat?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option C
Silver has the highest thermal and electrical conductivity among all metals, closely followed by copper and gold.
Q19 • Level 1: Foundation 1 Mark
The handles of cooking pans and pressure cookers are manufactured from Bakelite or wood because:
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
Bakelite, hard plastic, and wood are poor thermal conductors (insulators). They prevent the intense heat of the cooking vessel from transferring to the cook's hand.
Q20 • Level 1: Foundation 1 Mark
Why are copper bottoms frequently bonded to stainless steel cooking utensils?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option C
Copper has roughly 10 times higher thermal conductivity than stainless steel. A copper bottom spreads heat evenly across the base, eliminating localized hot spots that burn food.
Q21 • Level 1: Foundation 1 Mark
Pins fixed with wax at equal intervals along an iron rod heated at one end fall off progressively. Why?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
This classic experiment demonstrates conduction: thermal energy travels sequentially down the solid rod, melting wax and dropping pins in order of proximity to the flame.
Q22 • Level 1: Foundation 1 Mark
An ice-box is engineered with double walls and insulating material (thermocol or cork) packed between them to:
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
The insulating layer between the double walls provides high thermal resistance, drastically slowing down conductive heat transfer from the surrounding air to the melting ice.
Q23 • Level 2: Application 1 Mark
Why do two thin woollen blankets joined together keep you significantly warmer than a single thick blanket of equal total thickness?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
Still, non-circulating air is one of the best thermal insulators. The air trapped between the two thin blankets prevents body heat from conducting into the cold bedroom air.
Q24 • Level 1: Foundation 1 Mark
In thermal conduction, heat energy spontaneously transfers:
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
According to the Second Law of Thermodynamics, heat spontaneously flows down a thermal gradient from a region of higher temperature to a region of lower temperature until thermal equilibrium is attained.
Q25 • Level 1: Foundation 1 Mark
Which of the following substances is classified as a poor conductor of heat (insulator)?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option D
Distilled water, still air, and dry wood/cork all have very low thermal conductivities and are categorized as thermal insulators.
Q26 • Level 2: Application 1 Mark
Why do birds fluff their feathers noticeably during harsh winter mornings?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
Fluffing feathers increases the volume of air trapped adjacent to their skin. Because air is a poor conductor of heat, it creates an efficient insulating envelope that conserves avian body heat.
Q27 • Level 2: Application 1 Mark
On a cold winter morning, a marble tile floor feels much colder to bare feet than a wooden floor in the same room because:
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
Both floors are at identical room temperature! However, marble has high thermal conductivity and swiftly drains heat away from your $37^\circ\text{C}$ foot. Wood is an insulator and draws very little heat.
Q28 • Level 2: Application 1 Mark
Eskimos construct igloos out of compressed snow blocks to stay warm in the Arctic because:
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
Compacted snow is porous and contains up to 90% trapped air. The trapped air prevents conductive heat loss, keeping the interior of the igloo well above freezing even when outside air is -40°C.
Q29 • Level 2: Application 1 Mark
False ceilings constructed with plaster of Paris or thermocol sheets in modern buildings help to:
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
A false ceiling creates a dead air cavity beneath the primary concrete roof slab. This trapped air buffer impedes heat ingress during scorching summers and prevents heat leakage in winter.
Q30 • Level 2: Application 1 Mark
Which of the following liquids is an exception and acts as a remarkably GOOD conductor of heat?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option C
Most liquids are poor thermal conductors. Mercury is an exception because it is a liquid metal with free delocalized electrons that conduct heat with high efficiency.
Q31 • Level 2: Application 1 Mark
When heating an iron rod in a blacksmith's forge, thermal conduction transfers energy via:
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
In metals, thermal conduction occurs through dual mechanisms: lattice vibrational phonons and rapid collision/diffusion of free conduction electrons.
Q32 • Level 2: Application 1 Mark
According to Fourier's law of heat conduction, if the thickness of an insulating wall is doubled, the rate of heat conduction will be:
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
The rate of conductive heat transfer is inversely proportional to thickness: $\frac{Q}{t} = \frac{k A \Delta T}{d}$. Doubling thickness ($d$) halves the conductive heat flow rate.
SECTION 03

Section 3: Convection & Coastal Atmospheric Currents

Fluid density cycles, upward buoyant currents, smoke alarms, ventilation, and coastal Sea & Land Breezes.

16 Questions
Q33 • Level 1: Foundation 1 Mark
The mode of heat transfer in liquids and gases that occurs via the actual bulk displacement of heated fluid particles is:
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
Convection occurs exclusively in fluids (liquids and gases). When heated, fluid particles expand, become less dense, and rise upwards, carrying thermal energy bodily.
Q34 • Level 1: Foundation 1 Mark
Why is thermal convection physically impossible in solid substances?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
Convection requires the physical bodily migration of molecules. In solids, atoms are tightly locked into crystalline lattice positions and can only vibrate in place.
Q35 • Level 1: Foundation 1 Mark
In coastal regions, the refreshing 'Sea Breeze' blows during:
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
During the day, land heats up faster than the sea. Hot air over land rises, and cool dense air over the sea rushes inland to take its place, creating the daytime Sea Breeze.
Q36 • Level 1: Foundation 1 Mark
In coastal regions, the 'Land Breeze' blows during:
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option A
At night, land cools down much faster than the sea. The warmer air over the sea rises, and cool air from the land moves toward the sea, creating the nighttime Land Breeze.
Q37 • Level 1: Foundation 1 Mark
Where is the most scientifically sound location to install a residential smoke detector or fire alarm in a room?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option C
Hot air and smoke from a fire expand, become less dense, and rise rapidly toward the ceiling due to strong convection currents. Mounting on the ceiling ensures earliest detection.
Q38 • Level 1: Foundation 1 Mark
Why are room heaters placed near floor level, whereas air conditioner units are mounted near ceilings?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
Heaters heat bottom air which rises, circulating warm convection currents throughout the room. ACs cool ceiling air which becomes dense and sinks, establishing cooling convection cycles.
Q39 • Level 1: Foundation 1 Mark
When water in a round-bottom flask containing potassium permanganate crystals is heated from the bottom, colored trails show:
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
Potassium permanganate crystals visually trace fluid flow: warm water rises centrally above the flame, and cooler, denser surface water sinks along the perimeter, completing a convection cell.
Q40 • Level 1: Foundation 1 Mark
Why can thermal convection NEVER occur in the outer space vacuum between Sun and Earth?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
Convection requires fluid particles to physically move and carry thermal energy. A vacuum is empty space devoid of matter, making convection impossible.
Q41 • Level 2: Application 1 Mark
Why are ventilation openings and exhaust fans positioned near the top of walls or ceiling roofs?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
Warm air from breathing and room heating expands and rises toward the ceiling. High exhaust vents facilitate the evacuation of warm stale air, drawing fresh cool air through windows.
Q42 • Level 2: Application 1 Mark
If you try to heat water in a test tube by applying a flame exclusively at the top brim, what happens?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
Warm water is less dense and stays at the top. Since water is a poor conductor of heat and convection cannot carry heat downwards, bottom water remains cold even as top water boils.
Q43 • Level 2: Application 1 Mark
What happens to a parcel of air as its temperature increases?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
Heating air increases molecular kinetic energy, causing gas molecules to push apart. The gas expands, reducing its density ($D = M/V$). The buoyant, lighter warm air rises upward.
Q44 • Level 2: Application 1 Mark
Why does dry land heat up and cool down much more rapidly than ocean water?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
Water has an exceptionally high specific heat capacity ($~4184\text{ J/kg}\cdot^\circ\text{C}$) compared to dry sand/soil ($~800\text{ J/kg}\cdot^\circ\text{C}$). Water requires far more heat to change temperature.
Q45 • Level 2: Application 1 Mark
If you place your hand above a candle flame, you feel intensely hot, but if you place your hand beside the flame at equal distance, it feels only mildly warm. Why?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option A
Above the flame, buoyant hot air rises directly onto your hand via vigorous convection. To the sides, convection does not operate; you feel only moderate radiant heat and negligible conduction.
Q46 • Level 2: Application 1 Mark
Global atmospheric trade winds and equatorial monsoon systems are immense natural convection currents driven by:
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
The sun heats the Equator far more intensely than the poles. Warm equatorial air rises, and cool polar air sweeps in along the surface, driving global planetary convection wind belts.
Q47 • Level 2: Application 1 Mark
Why are tall chimneys erected over thermal power stations and brick kilns?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
A tall column of hot, low-density gas inside the chimney creates a strong pressure differential (chimney effect/draft) that draws fresh combustion air into the furnace and exhausts smoke high up.
Q48 • Level 2: Application 1 Mark
In a physics laboratory, ice is wrapped in wire mesh and sunk to the bottom of a water-filled test tube. Heating the top causes boiling while the ice stays frozen. This proves that:
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
Since warm water floats on cold water, convection cannot operate downwards. Furthermore, water is a poor conductor of heat, allowing ice at the bottom to remain frozen as top water boils.
SECTION 04

Section 4: Radiation & Thermal Absorption

Infrared electromagnetic propagation across vacuum, matte black absorption, white reflective clothes, and solar cookers.

16 Questions
Q49 • Level 1: Foundation 1 Mark
The mode of heat transfer that does NOT require any physical material medium to propagate is:
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option C
Radiation transfers energy via electromagnetic infrared waves. It travels freely through a complete vacuum without requiring any atoms or molecules.
Q50 • Level 1: Foundation 1 Mark
How does the massive thermal energy generated by the Sun reach the Earth across 150 million km of empty space?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option C
Interplanetary space is a near-total vacuum where conduction and convection cannot operate. Solar heat travels entirely as electromagnetic radiation (infrared, visible, UV rays).
Q51 • Level 1: Foundation 1 Mark
Which surfaces are the BEST absorbers and also the BEST emitters of radiant thermal energy?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
Dark, rough, matte black surfaces absorb virtually all incident radiant heat. According to Kirchhoff's radiation law, good absorbers are also the most powerful emitters of thermal radiation.
Q52 • Level 1: Foundation 1 Mark
Which surfaces are the BEST reflectors of radiant thermal energy?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
Bright, shiny, polished white or silvered surfaces reflect almost all incident electromagnetic radiation, absorbing and emitting negligible amounts.
Q53 • Level 1: Foundation 1 Mark
Why do people overwhelmingly prefer wearing light-coloured or white cotton clothes during hot summer months?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
Light-coloured and white fabrics reflect incident solar radiant energy instead of absorbing it, significantly minimizing heat absorption during hot summer days.
Q54 • Level 1: Foundation 1 Mark
Why are dark-coloured and black woollen clothes preferred during chilly winter months?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
Dark-coloured fabrics absorb a substantially higher percentage of ambient and solar radiant energy, helping keep the body comfortable and warm in winter.
Q55 • Level 2: Application 1 Mark
The bottom of stainless steel cooking pots is frequently blackened, while the outer walls are polished shiny. Why?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
A blackened base absorbs maximum radiant heat from the burner. Polished reflective outer walls are poor emitters, conserving thermal energy inside the pot.
Q56 • Level 2: Application 1 Mark
Why is the internal cooking chamber of a solar cooker coated in matte black paint?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
Matte black paint absorbs over 95% of incoming solar radiation, heating the interior cavity to high temperatures ($100^\circ\text{C}$ to $140^\circ\text{C}$) to cook food.
Q57 • Level 2: Application 1 Mark
At what velocity does thermal radiant heat propagate through empty space?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
Thermal radiation consists of electromagnetic infrared waves. Like all electromagnetic radiation, it travels through a vacuum at the speed of light ($c \approx 300,000\text{ km/s}$).
Q58 • Level 2: Application 1 Mark
Equal volumes of boiling water at 90°C are poured into two identical cans: Can A painted matte black, and Can B painted shiny silver. After 20 minutes:
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option A
A matte black surface is not only a superior absorber, but also an exceptionally efficient radiator/emitter of heat. Can A emits radiation rapidly and cools much faster than Can B.
Q59 • Level 2: Application 1 Mark
Why are heavily overcast, cloudy nights substantially warmer than crisp, cloudless nights in winter?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
On clear nights, Earth's surface rapidly radiates thermal energy straight out into outer space. Clouds act as an infrared barrier, absorbing and re-radiating heat back down to the ground.
Q60 • Level 2: Application 1 Mark
When standing several metres sideways from a blazing bonfire, by which primary mechanism do your face and hands receive heat?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option C
Convection currents carry heated air straight upward above the fire. Air is an insulator, so conduction is negligible. Thermal energy travels sideways to you predominantly via radiation.
Q61 • Level 2: Application 1 Mark
Infrared thermal imaging cameras can accurately photograph living animals and humans in pitch-black night because:
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
Every physical body with temperature above 0 K emits electromagnetic thermal radiation. Warm-blooded animals ($~37^\circ\text{C}$) emit peak infrared radiation that thermal sensors detect.
Q62 • Level 2: Application 1 Mark
Which fundamental radiation law establishes that 'a good absorber of radiation at a given wavelength is also an equally good emitter'?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
Kirchhoff's Law of Thermal Radiation states that for an arbitrary body emitting and absorbing thermal radiation in thermodynamic equilibrium, the emissivity equals the absorptivity ($e = a$).
Q63 • Level 2: Application 1 Mark
Why does hot tea served in a highly polished, mirror-finish silver teapot stay warm significantly longer than in a rough dark teapot?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
Polished silver surfaces have very low emissivity ($e \approx 0.02$). They emit almost no radiant heat compared to dull earthenware or dark teapots ($e \approx 0.90$).
Q64 • Level 2: Application 1 Mark
To maximize thermal protection from blistering sun and heat, a summer umbrella should ideally have:
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
A reflective white or silver outer surface reflects harsh incoming solar radiation, while a dark inner lining prevents scattered stray rays from reflecting back onto your face.
SECTION 05

Section 5: Advanced Heat Transfer, HOTS & Thermos Flask Mechanics

16 Olympiad HOTS questions: Thermos flask multi-barrier design, bimetallic strips, anomalous water expansion, and mixture thermal balance.

16 Questions
Q65 • Level 3: HOTS 1 Mark
In a Thermos flask (vacuum flask), heat transfer by CONDUCTION and CONVECTION between the walls is eliminated by:
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
Both conduction and convection require a material medium (atoms/molecules) to transport heat. By pulling a vacuum between the double glass walls, both modes are completely blocked.
Q66 • Level 3: HOTS 1 Mark
In a Thermos flask, heat transfer by RADIATION is blocked primarily by:
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option C
Radiation can travel across a vacuum. To block radiation, the glass surfaces facing the vacuum are coated with silver. The inner mirror reflects heat back into hot liquid, and outer mirror reflects exterior radiation away.
Q67 • Level 3: HOTS 1 Mark
In a Thermos flask, heat loss through the mouth opening is minimized by using a stopper manufactured from:
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
Cork, silicone, and hollow plastic are excellent thermal insulators that prevent heat from conducting upward or setting up convection loops through the bottle neck.
Q68 • Level 3: HOTS 1 Mark
Three liquids X, Y, Z have the following freezing/boiling points: Mercury (-39°C to 357°C), Alcohol (-112°C to 78°C), and Pentane (-180°C to 36°C). Which liquid can measure an Arctic winter temperature of -50°C and a hot water bath of 50°C?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
At -50°C, mercury is frozen solid (freezes at -39°C). At 50°C, pentane has boiled into gas (boils at 36°C). Only alcohol stays liquid from -112°C up to 78°C, spanning both test temperatures.
Q69 • Level 3: HOTS 1 Mark
Why does a thin paper cup filled with water placed directly over a Bunsen burner flame NOT catch fire?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
Water has high heat capacity. Heat conducting through the paper is absorbed by the water. The paper's temperature cannot rise above the boiling point of water ($100^\circ\text{C}$), well below paper's ignition point ($~233^\circ\text{C}$).
Q70 • Level 3: HOTS 1 Mark
A bimetallic strip composed of brass and iron strips firmly riveted together is heated evenly. How does it behave?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
Brass has a higher coefficient of thermal expansion than iron. On heating, brass expands more than iron, forcing the composite strip to curve with brass forming the longer outer convex arc.
Q71 • Level 3: HOTS 1 Mark
A solid iron sphere at 50°C is dropped into a thermally insulated container filled with water at exactly 50°C. Net heat flow will:
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option C
Heat transfer strictly requires a temperature difference ($\Delta T > 0$). Because both the iron sphere and water are at identical temperatures ($50^\circ\text{C}$), they are in thermal equilibrium and net heat transfer is zero.
Q72 • Level 3: HOTS 1 Mark
A dry wooden spoon is dipped into a steaming cup of hot tomato soup. Its other end will:
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option C
Wood is an organic polymer with very poor thermal conductivity ($k \approx 0.15\text{ W/m}\cdot\text{K}$). Heat does not conduct through the spoon handle to any perceptible degree.
Q73 • Level 3: HOTS 1 Mark
1 litre of water at 30°C is mixed thoroughly with 1 litre of water at 50°C in an insulated flask. What is the final equilibrium temperature?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option D
Equal masses of identical liquid mix according to heat balance: $m c (T_f - 30) = m c (50 - T_f) \implies 2 T_f = 80 \implies T_f = 40^\circ\text{C}$. Common student trap is adding temperatures ($80^\circ\text{C}$)!
Q74 • Level 3: HOTS 1 Mark
A circular metal ring has an inner radius $r_1$ and outer radius $r_2$. When the ring is uniformly heated in an oven, what happens to $r_1$ and $r_2$?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
Thermal expansion acts like a photographic enlargement in all directions. The inner hole expands outward just as if it were filled with metal; hence both inner radius $r_1$ and outer radius $r_2$ increase.
Q75 • Level 3: HOTS 1 Mark
A man stands beneath a concrete shelter on a blazing summer day. Which sequence correctly represents how solar heat energy reaches the man?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option A
Sun's heat reaches shelter roof via Radiation. Heat conducts through solid concrete via Conduction. The hot bottom ceiling then emits Infrared Radiation and warms air that circulates to the man via Convection.
Q76 • Level 3: HOTS 1 Mark
Four identical beakers containing water at 80°C are tested: Beaker 1 painted matte black, Beaker 2 painted shiny white, Beaker 3 wrapped in wool, Beaker 4 unpainted glass. In which beaker does water cool fastest?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option A
A matte black surface is the most efficient emitter of infrared radiation (emissivity $e \approx 1.0$). It radiates thermal energy at the highest rate, cooling fastest.
Q77 • Level 3: HOTS 1 Mark
At what Celsius temperature is the numerical value on the Fahrenheit scale exactly twice that of the Celsius scale ($F = 2C$)?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option A
Using $F = \frac{9}{5}C + 32$ and substituting $F = 2C$: $2C = \frac{9}{5}C + 32 \implies 2C - 1.8C = 32 \implies 0.2C = 32 \implies C = \frac{32}{0.2} = 160^\circ\text{C}$ (Fahrenheit value is $320^\circ\text{F}$).
Q78 • Level 3: HOTS 1 Mark
What unique phenomenon occurs when water is heated from 0°C to 4°C (the anomalous expansion of water)?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
Unlike virtually all other liquids which expand when heated, water contracts between 0°C and 4°C due to collapsing hydrogen-bonded ice lattices. Water reaches its maximum density at exactly 4°C.
Q79 • Level 3: HOTS 1 Mark
Why do civil engineers leave intentional expansion gaps between successive steel railway rail joints?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
Steel rails expand significantly in summer ($L = L_0(1 + \alpha \Delta T)$). If rails were laid end-to-end without gaps, enormous compressive thermal stress would buckle and distort the track.
Q80 • Level 3: HOTS 1 Mark
Paheli takes her body temperature under her tongue, while Boojho takes his under his armpit (axilla) with identical calibrated thermometers. Why is Boojho's reading slightly lower by approx. 0.5°C?
Scientific Rationale
Correct Answer: Option B
Oral sublingual measurement captures core vascular temperature near the carotid artery, whereas axillary measurement reflects superficial skin temperature which is typically $0.3^\circ\text{C}$ to $0.6^\circ\text{C}$ ($~1^\circ\text{F}$) cooler.

10 Items
FIB Q1 1 Mark
The difference between lower fixed point (0°C) and upper fixed point (100°C) is divided into _______ equal divisions on the Celsius scale.
Explanation
Correct Answer: 100. 100 equal degrees between freezing and boiling points of water.
FIB Q2 1 Mark
In solid substances, heat energy is primarily transferred by the process of _______ .
Explanation
Correct Answer: conduction. Heat transfers through lattice vibrations and atomic collisions without bulk movement.
FIB Q3 1 Mark
The normal boiling point of pure water on the Fahrenheit scale is _______ °F.
Explanation
Correct Answer: 212. Freezing is 32°F and boiling is 212°F (180 division interval).
FIB Q4 1 Mark
In liquids and gases, heat is transferred through bulk fluid movement via _______ .
Explanation
Correct Answer: convection. Warm buoyant fluid rises while cold dense fluid sinks.
FIB Q5 1 Mark
In a complete vacuum, heat energy travels exclusively by the process of _______ .
Explanation
Correct Answer: radiation. Infrared electromagnetic waves do not require any physical material medium.
FIB Q6 1 Mark
Matte black colour is an exceptionally good _______ of radiant heat energy.
Explanation
Correct Answer: absorber. Dark matte surfaces absorb over 95% of incident thermal radiation.
FIB Q7 1 Mark
The handles of cooking utensils are manufactured from thermal _______ .
Explanation

Section 1 3 Marks (Numerical)
Convert $95^\circ\text{F}$ into degrees Celsius, and convert standard body temperature $37^\circ\text{C}$ into degrees Fahrenheit.
  • $95^\circ\text{F}$ to $^\circ\text{C}$: $$C = \frac{5}{9}(F - 32) = \frac{5}{9}(95 - 32) = \frac{5}{9}(63) = 35^\circ\text{C}$$.
  • $37^\circ\text{C}$ to $^\circ\text{F}$: $$F = \frac{9}{5}C + 32 = \frac{9}{5}(37) + 32 = 66.6 + 32 = 98.6^\circ\text{F}$$.
Section 1 2 Marks (Reasoning)
Why can a clinical thermometer not be used to measure the temperature of boiling milk or water?

Water and milk boil around $100^\circ\text{C}$, whereas a clinical thermometer is calibrated with a strict upper limit of $42^\circ\text{C}$. The massive thermal expansion of mercury would exceed the capillary volume, exerting huge hydraulic pressure and shattering the glass bulb dangerously.

Section 1 3 Marks (SA)
Why is mercury preferred over water as a thermometric liquid in liquid-in-glass thermometers?
  • Mercury expands uniformly across an extensive liquid range ($-39^\circ\text{C}$ to $357^\circ\text{C}$).
  • It does not wet or stick to the inner capillary walls of the glass tube.
  • It is an opaque, silvery liquid metal, making it distinct and easy to read accurately.
  • It has high thermal conductivity and low specific heat, responding swiftly to small temperature fluctuations.
SUBJECTIVE SECTION 02

Section 2: Conduction & Thermal Conductivity

Molecular lattice vibrations, good conductors vs thermal insulators, and everyday household thermal design.

4 Questions
Section 2 2 Marks (NCERT)
Why are the handles of cooking utensils made of wood or plastic, while their bodies are made of metal?

Metals (aluminium, stainless steel, copper) are excellent thermal conductors that transfer flame heat quickly and evenly to cook the food. Conversely, wood and Bakelite plastic are thermal insulators (poor conductors); their handles remain cool to touch, protecting the cook from severe burns.

Section 2 3 Marks (SA)
Explain why an ice box is constructed with double walls and the space between them is packed with cork or thermocol.

Ice melts when it absorbs heat from the surrounding environment. An ice box utilizes a double-wall design packed with insulating materials (cork, glass wool, or thermocol) containing trapped air pockets. Because air and cork are poor thermal conductors, this barrier drastically retards the flow of external heat into the box, preserving the ice for extended periods.

Section 2 3 Marks (Conceptual)
Explain why wearing two thin woollen sweaters in winter keeps you warmer than wearing one thick sweater.

When two thin sweaters are worn, an invisible cushion of still air is trapped in the gap between them, in addition to the air trapped within the wool fibres. Since still air is one of the poorest thermal conductors in nature, this double-layer insulation barrier significantly reduces the conductive escape of body heat.

Section 2 2 Marks (Reasoning)
On a cold morning, a metal chair and a wooden chair are in the same room. Why does the metal chair feel much colder when touched?

Both chairs are in thermal equilibrium at the exact same room temperature. However, metal has high thermal conductivity and draws heat rapidly away from your warm finger ($37^\circ\text{C}$). Wood has very low thermal conductivity and conducts heat away very slowly; hence your skin senses metal as colder.

SUBJECTIVE SECTION 03

Section 3: Convection & Coastal Atmospheric Currents

Convection currents, fluid buoyancy, coastal Sea Breeze and Land Breeze diurnal cycles, and room ventilation.

3 Questions
Section 3 5 Marks (NCERT Long Answer)
Explain in detail the formation of Sea Breeze and Land Breeze in coastal regions with the help of scientific principles.

1. Sea Breeze (Daytime): During the day, land heats up much faster than sea water due to its lower specific heat capacity. The air over the land warms, expands, becomes less dense, and rises upward, creating a low-pressure area. The cooler, denser air over the sea rushes inland to take its place, creating the Sea Breeze.

2. Land Breeze (Nighttime): At night, land loses heat rapidly by radiation, while the sea cools slowly. The air above the warm sea rises, and cooler, denser air from the land sweeps outward toward the sea, creating the Land Breeze.

Section 3 3 Marks (Application)
Why are room heaters placed near the floor, while air conditioners and ventilation grilles are installed near the ceiling?

Heat transfer in room air occurs via convection currents:

  • Heater on the floor: Heats the dense cold air at ground level. This air expands, becomes buoyant, and rises, while cooler ceiling air sinks to be heated, setting up warm circulation throughout the room.
  • Air Conditioner near ceiling: Chills warm air at the top. The chilled air becomes denser and sinks downward, forcing warm bottom air upward into the AC intake, cooling the room uniformly.
Section 3 2 Marks (Reasoning)
Why are smoke detectors mounted on the ceiling rather than on the wall or floor?

Smoke, fumes, and hot combustion gases from a fire expand and become much less dense than surrounding ambient air. Buoyant convection currents carry them directly upward to accumulate beneath the ceiling, triggering the smoke alarm in the earliest stages of a fire.

SUBJECTIVE SECTION 04

Section 4: Radiation & Thermal Absorption

Electromagnetic infrared radiation, absorption vs reflection, dark vs light clothes, and solar cooker cavity design.

3 Questions
Section 4 3 Marks (NCERT)
Explain why we prefer to wear white or light-coloured clothes in summer and dark-coloured clothes in winter.

Thermal radiation does not require a medium and is absorbed or reflected depending on surface coloration:

  • Summer: Light-coloured and white surfaces reflect most of the incident solar radiant heat and absorb very little, keeping our bodies cool and comfortable.
  • Winter: Dark-coloured and black surfaces absorb the maximum amount of radiant heat from the sun and ambient surroundings, helping maintain cozy body warmth.
Section 4 2 Marks (Conceptual)
How does heat from the Sun reach the Earth across interplanetary space?

Between the Sun and Earth's atmosphere lies a vast vacuum spanning approximately 150 million kilometres. Neither conduction nor convection can operate without a material medium. Heat travels across this void as thermal radiation (electromagnetic infrared waves) propagating at the speed of light ($3 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}$).

Section 4 3 Marks (Application)
Describe the construction of a simple box-type solar cooker and explain why its interior is painted matte black.

A box solar cooker consists of an insulated wooden box enclosing an inner metallic tray covered with a clear glass sheet and an adjustable top mirror. The interior tray is painted matte black because black surfaces absorb over $95\%$ of incident solar radiation, converting radiant photon energy into trapped thermal energy to cook food.

SUBJECTIVE SECTION 05

Section 5: Advanced Heat Transfer, HOTS & Thermos Flask Mechanics

Multi-mode heat loss barriers, vacuum flask architecture, bimetallic strips, and anomalous water expansion.

3 Questions
Section 5 5 Marks (HOTS / Master Level)
Explain the complete construction and working of a Thermos flask (vacuum flask), showing how all three modes of heat transfer are minimized.

A Thermos flask maintains hot liquids hot and cold liquids cold by establishing barriers against all three modes of heat transfer:

  1. Conduction & Convection Blocked: The flask consists of a double-walled glass or stainless steel vessel. The air in the space between the double walls is completely evacuated to create a high vacuum. Since conduction and convection require matter, neither mode can cross this vacuum barrier.
  2. Radiation Blocked: The glass walls facing the vacuum are coated with reflective silvering (mirrors). The inner shiny wall reflects infrared radiation from the hot liquid back inside, while the outer silvered wall reflects external ambient radiation away.
  3. Neck & Stopper Insulation: The mouth is sealed with a tight-fitting stopper made of cork, silicone, or hollow plastic (poor conductors), eliminating conductive heat loss through the neck and preventing evaporation.
Section 5 4 Marks (HOTS / Environmental)
What is anomalous expansion of water? Explain how this unique phenomenon preserves aquatic life during freezing winter.

Most liquids contract continuously as they cool. Water behaves abnormally: between $4^\circ\text{C}$ and $0^\circ\text{C}$, water actually expands as it cools, reaching its maximum density at exactly $4^\circ\text{C}$.

Aquatic Preservation: In winter, as surface water cools to $4^\circ\text{C}$, it becomes densest and sinks to the bottom of the lake. When the surface further cools below $4^\circ\text{C}$, it becomes lighter and remains at the top, eventually freezing into an ice crust at $0^\circ\text{C}$. Since ice is a poor conductor of heat, it insulates the water underneath, maintaining a liquid bottom layer at $4^\circ\text{C}$ where fish and plants survive comfortably.

Section 5 3 Marks (HOTS / Experiment)
Why can a thin paper cup containing water be held directly over a burner flame without catching fire?

Paper has an ignition temperature of approximately $233^\circ\text{C}$. When water is in the paper cup, the heat supplied by the flame is rapidly conducted through the thin paper to the water due to water's immense heat capacity. The temperature of the paper never exceeds the boiling point of water ($100^\circ\text{C}$); because this is far below the paper's ignition point, the paper cannot catch fire as long as water remains.

Physics Master Key Vault

Examiner traps, temperature vs heat distinctions, and key conceptual shortcuts.

4 Keys

Trap 1: Clinical Thermometer in Boiling Water

Examiners love asking if a clinical thermometer can be used for boiling liquids. Never say yes! Boiling water is $100^\circ\text{C}$; the clinical scale caps out at $42^\circ\text{C}$. Liquid expansion will blow the glass bulb into dangerous shards.

Trap 2: Heat vs Temperature Distinction

Heat is the total thermal energy (extensive property measured in Joules), while temperature is the average kinetic energy (intensive property measured in °C). An iceberg at $0^\circ\text{C}$ contains vastly more heat energy than a boiling cup of tea at $100^\circ\text{C}$!

Trap 3: The Two-Blanket Paradox

Two thin blankets are always warmer than one thick blanket of identical material and total mass. Why? The dead air pocket trapped between the layers has near-zero thermal conductivity, forming a superior thermal barrier.

Trap 4: Coastal Breeze Orientation

Always name a breeze by where it comes from: Sea Breeze comes FROM the sea (daytime cool relief). Land Breeze comes FROM the land (nighttime cool drainage). Land always experiences faster temperature swings due to its low specific heat capacity.
