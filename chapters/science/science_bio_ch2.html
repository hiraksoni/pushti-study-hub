Adolescence vs. Puberty

Adolescence (Period: 11 to 19 Years)

The transitional developmental period of life leading to reproductive maturity. Derived from the Latin verb adolescere ("to grow up"). Also known as the "teen" years.

Puberty (The Process of Sexual Maturation)

The sequence of physical and physiological changes by which a child's body matures into an adult body capable of sexual reproduction. Puberty ends when reproductive maturity is attained.

Height Growth Spurt Equation

The sudden rapid increase in height during puberty is caused by the elongation of the long bones of the arms and legs.

Calculation of Full Projected Height:
Projected Height (cm) = [ Current Height (cm) ÷ % of Full Height at this Age ] × 100
Girls typically achieve their maximum height faster (~18 yrs), though by age 18 both boys and girls reach their genetically determined maximum adult height.

Pimples & Acne Origin:

Sweat glands and sebaceous (oil) glands become hyperactive during puberty. Excess sebum oil clogs dermal pores, trapping airborne bacteria that cause localized pustules.

The Endocrine System & Hormonal Feedback

Ductless chemical messengers, the master pituitary orchestrator, and gland deficiency disorders

Endocrine Glands (Ductless Glands)

Secrete chemical messengers called hormones directly into circulating blood without ducts. The bloodstream transports hormones to target organs equipped with specific chemical receptors.

Exocrine Glands (Duct-Bearing Glands)

Release secretions through distinct physical ducts/tubes onto body surfaces or into cavities (e.g., sweat glands, salivary glands, tear glands, liver bile duct).

Reproductive Phases & Menstrual Hygiene

Menarche to menopause, 28-day cyclic endometrial timeline, and public health hygiene

Menarche (The Beginning) Age 10–12

The first occurrence of menstruation at puberty in females, marking the biological commencement of the fertile reproductive lifespan.

Menopause (The Conclusion) Age 45–50

The permanent natural cessation of the menstrual cycle and ovarian ovulation, signifying the completion of the reproductive phase.

The Four Sequential Phases of the 28–30 Day Cycle

Phase 1 • Days 1–5 Menses
Menstruation

If fertilization does not occur, the unfertilized ovum dies. The thickened vascular uterine lining (endometrium) sheds with blood and mucosal fluid through the vagina (3–7 days).

Phase 2 • Days 6–13 Follicle
Follicular Phase

Pituitary FSH triggers an ovarian follicle to mature. Secreted estrogen causes the endometrium to regrow, thicken, and become sponge-like in preparation for pregnancy.

Phase 3 • Day 14 Peak LH
Ovulation

A surge of Luteinizing Hormone (LH) causes the mature ovarian follicle to rupture, releasing the viable ovum into the fallopian tube (oviduct).

Phase 4 • Days 15–28 Luteal
Luteal Phase

Ruptured follicle becomes the corpus luteum, secreting progesterone to sustain the uterine bed. If unfertilized, hormone levels plummet, triggering the next cycle.

Metamorphosis & Iodine in Water

Metamorphosis is the drastic biological transformation of an aquatic larva into a terrestrial adult (e.g. tadpole into adult frog).

The Iodine Dependency Chain:

Thyroid Gland → Secretes Thyroxine (Requires Dissolved Iodine from Pond Water) → Triggers Metamorphosis.

⚠️ Olympiad Trap: If pond water lacks iodine, tadpoles CANNOT synthesize thyroxine and remain permanently in the aquatic larval stage without maturing into frogs!

Menstruation is the monthly cyclic discharge of blood, mucosal tissue, and the unfertilized ovum through the vagina in adolescent and adult human females.

Q46 • Long Answer Female Reproductive Cycle
hard 5 Marks
Elaborate on the reproductive cycle occurring in human females with its key phases, hormonal controls, and physiological changes.
Model Examination Answer
The female reproductive cycle (Menstrual Cycle) recurs approximately every 28 to 30 days throughout the reproductive phase (from menarche to menopause):
1. Follicular Phase (Days 1–13):
* Under the influence of pituitary Follicle Stimulating Hormone (FSH), an ovarian follicle matures in one of the ovaries.
* The maturing follicle secretes estrogen, which causes the inner uterine lining (endometrium) to proliferate, thicken, and become richly supplied with blood vessels.
2. Ovulation (Around Day 14):
* A surge in Luteinizing Hormone (LH) causes the mature ovarian follicle to rupture, releasing a mature ovum into the fallopian tube (oviduct).
3. Luteal Phase (Days 15–28):
* The ruptured follicle transforms into the corpus luteum, secreting progesterone to maintain the spongy, receptive endometrial lining for embryo implantation.
4. Menstruation (Days 1–5 of next cycle):
* If the egg is not fertilized by a sperm, the unfertilized ovum degenerates, and hormone levels decline.
* The thick vascular uterine lining breaks down and sheds along with blood, mucous, and cellular debris through the vagina, completing the cycle.
