SECTION 01

Section 1: Adolescence & Pubertal Transformations

Physical growth spurts, bodily proportions, Adam's apple, voice change, and sweat/sebaceous gland activity.

10 Questions
Q1 Assertion & Reason (2 Marks)
Assertion (A): Adolescence is a critical phase of human development.
Reason (R): Adolescence is accompanied by marked physical, psychological, and behavioural transformations.

Choose the correct option:
(a) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A.
(b) Both A and R are true but R is NOT the correct explanation of A.
(c) A is true but R is false.
(d) A is false but R is true.
Model Textbook Solution & Explanation: Correct Option: (a) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A.

Scientific Rationale: Adolescence is deemed a critical phase precisely because the individual undergoes profound somatic growth, endocrine restructuring, and cognitive developments that bridge the gap between childhood dependence and adult independence.
Q2 Assertion & Reason (2 Marks)
Assertion (A): Puberty is the age at which an individual attains reproductive maturity.
Reason (R): In girls, puberty typically occurs at a younger age than in boys.

Choose the correct option:
(a) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A.
(b) Both A and R are true but R is NOT the correct explanation of A.
(c) A is true but R is false.
(d) A is false but R is true.
Model Textbook Solution & Explanation: Correct Option: (b) Both A and R are true but R is NOT the correct explanation of A.

Scientific Rationale: Both statements are biologically factual. However, the earlier onset of puberty in girls is a separate developmental timing phenomenon and does not explain the physiological definition of puberty itself.
Q3 Short Answer (2 Marks)
When do sudden and noticeable physical changes begin to appear in human children?
Model Textbook Solution & Explanation: Sudden and noticeable physical changes begin to appear at the onset of puberty, typically between 10 to 11 years in girls and 11 to 12 years in boys. These changes are initiated when the pituitary gland stimulates the sex organs to produce reproductive hormones.
Q4 Definition (2 Marks)
What is puberty? Define the term precisely.
Model Textbook Solution & Explanation: Puberty is the physiological stage of physical and sexual maturation during which a boy or girl attains reproductive capability. It ends when an adolescent reaches full sexual and physical maturity.
Q5 Short Answer (2 Marks)
Do adolescent boys and girls grow at the exact same rate? Explain.
Model Textbook Solution & Explanation: No. Initially, between ages 10 and 13, girls grow faster and reach their growth spurt earlier than boys. However, around age 14 to 18, boys accelerate in height and muscular mass, generally catching up with and surpassing girls in stature by age 18.
Q6 Short Answer (2 Marks)
Explain the changes occurring in the voice of boys and girls during puberty.
Model Textbook Solution & Explanation:
  • In Boys: The voice box (larynx) enlarges significantly under testosterone, protruding as the Adam's apple. The vocal cords become thicker and longer, causing the voice to deepen and produce a low-pitched tone (sometimes cracking temporarily during growth).
  • In Girls: The larynx remains comparatively small and does not protrude, resulting in a distinct, high-pitched voice.
Q7 Short Answer (2 Marks)
What are the characteristic changes in the body shape of boys and girls during adolescence?
Model Textbook Solution & Explanation:
  • In Boys: Shoulders become broader, chest widens, and muscles grow more prominently due to testosterone.
  • In Girls: The region below the waist (pelvic girdle) widens to facilitate future pregnancy, and breast development commences.
Q8 Short Answer (2 Marks)
What is the function of sweat glands and sebaceous glands during puberty?
Model Textbook Solution & Explanation: Sweat glands secrete sweat to help regulate body temperature through evaporative cooling. Sebaceous glands secrete sebum (oil) to lubricate the skin and hair. At puberty, both glands become hyperactive due to hormonal surges, which frequently clogs hair follicles and causes acne and pimples on the face.
Q9 Comprehensive Long Answer (4 Marks)
Describe in detail the various mental, intellectual, and emotional changes taking place during adolescence.
Model Textbook Solution & Explanation: Adolescence represents an intense period of cognitive and psychological reorganization:

1. Intellectual and Cognitive Development: Adolescents develop the capacity for abstract logical thinking, deductive reasoning, and future planning. In fact, a person's brain has the greatest capacity for learning and neuroplasticity during this stage.

2. Search for Identity & Independence: Adolescents become self-conscious about their appearance and place greater value on peer relationships, seeking personal identity distinct from family figures.

3. Emotional Vulnerability & Mood Swings: Rapid hormonal fluctuations combined with social expectations often cause sudden shifts in mood, restlessness, and feelings of insecurity. These emotional swings are a completely normal biological consequence of developmental maturation.
Q10 Case Study & Passage Evaluation (4 Marks)
Case Study Passage:
'Period X' is the biological stage of human life when the body undergoes rapid changes leading to reproductive maturity. It begins around the age of 11 and lasts up to 18 or 19 years. Since this period spans the 'teens', adolescents are also called 'teenagers'. In girls, 'Period X' may begin a year or two earlier than in boys. The rate and timing of 'Period X' varies from person to person. The most critical milestone of 'Period X' is that boys and girls become capable of sexual reproduction.

Questions:
1. Identify the developmental phase represented by 'Period X'.
2. State two visible secondary sexual characteristics associated with Period X in boys.
3. Explain why the onset of Period X occurs earlier in females than males.
Model Textbook Solution & Explanation: 1. Identification: 'Period X' represents Adolescence / Puberty.
2. Secondary Sexual Traits in Boys: Growth of facial hair (beard and moustache) and prominence of the voice box (Adam's apple with voice deepening).
3. Earlier Onset in Females: In females, the hypothalamic-pituitary-ovarian axis activates approximately 1 to 2 years earlier due to genetic and neuroendocrine programming, initiating estrogen secretion and somatic growth sooner than in boys.

3 Questions
Q11 Definition (2 Marks)
What are hormones? How do they reach their target organs?
Model Textbook Solution & Explanation: Hormones are biochemical messengers synthesized and secreted in microscopic amounts by ductless endocrine glands. They are released directly into the bloodstream and travel throughout the body to reach specific target sites/organs that possess corresponding cellular receptors.
Q12 Short Answer (2 Marks)
Name the male and female sex hormones and identify the endocrine organs that secrete them.
Model Textbook Solution & Explanation:
  • Male Hormone: Testosterone — secreted by the testes.
  • Female Hormone: Estrogen (and Progesterone) — secreted by the ovaries.
Both are under the primary regulatory control of hormones released by the pituitary gland.
Q13 Short Answer (2 Marks)
Explain the development of primary sex organs in males and females at puberty.
Model Textbook Solution & Explanation:
  • In Males: The testes and penis develop completely. The testes descend and begin spermatogenesis (production of viable male gametes or sperms).
  • In Females: The ovaries enlarge and reach maturity, and ovarian follicles begin to release one mature ovum (egg) every month alternately from either ovary.
SECTION 03

Section 3: Reproductive Phase of Life & Menstrual Cycle

Monthly 28-day cycle, ovulation, uterine thickening, menstruation, menarche, menopause, and menstrual hygiene.

4 Questions
Q14 Definition (2 Marks)
Define the menstrual cycle in human females.
Model Textbook Solution & Explanation: The menstrual cycle is a rhythmic, approximately 28-day reproductive cycle in human females involving the maturation of an ovum, preparation and vascularization of the uterine lining, ovulation, and the shedding of this lining (menstruation) if fertilization does not occur.
Q15 Definition (2 Marks)
What is menopause? How is it different from menarche?
Model Textbook Solution & Explanation:
  • Menarche: The very first menstrual cycle in a female's life, signaling the onset of reproductive capability at puberty (~10-12 years).
  • Menopause: The permanent cessation of menstruation and ovulation due to natural ovarian depletion in late adulthood (~45-55 years), marking the close of the reproductive phase.
Q16 Short Answer (2 Marks)
Why are sanitary pads strongly preferred over reusable cotton cloths during menstruation?
Model Textbook Solution & Explanation: Commercially sterilized sanitary pads are absorbent, dry, and hygienic. Reused, improperly washed, or damp cotton cloths harbor dangerous pathogenic bacteria and fungi, predisposing adolescent girls to severe reproductive and urinary tract infections.
Q17 Comprehensive Long Answer (4 Marks)
Elaborate in detail on the reproductive cycle occurring in human females, explaining all four major phases.
Model Textbook Solution & Explanation: The human female reproductive cycle spans approximately 28 to 30 days and comprises four distinct phases:

1. Menstrual Phase (Days 1–5): In the absence of fertilization, the unfertilized egg and the breakdown products of the thickened, blood-rich uterine endometrium are discharged through the vagina as menstrual blood.

2. Follicular / Proliferative Phase (Days 6–13): Under the influence of FSH from the pituitary, a new ovarian follicle matures into a Graafian follicle, and rising estrogen prompts the uterine wall to rebuild and become thick and spongy with new blood capillaries.

3. Ovulatory Phase (Around Day 14): A rapid surge of Luteinizing Hormone (LH) triggers ovulation—the release of the mature ovum from the ovary into the fallopian tube (oviduct).

4. Luteal / Secretory Phase (Days 15–28): The empty ovarian follicle transforms into the corpus luteum, secreting progesterone to maintain the uterine lining ready for implantation. If fertilization does not occur, the corpus luteum degenerates, progesterone drops sharply, and the next menstrual cycle begins.
SECTION 04

Section 4: Sex Determination in Humans

Chromosomes (23 pairs), sex chromosomes (X and Y), gamete inheritance, and zygote genetic determination.

1 Questions
Q18 Short Answer (3 Marks)
With the help of a genetic cross explanation, describe how the sex of an unborn baby is determined in humans.
Model Textbook Solution & Explanation: Inside the nucleus of human cells are 23 pairs of chromosomes. One pair represents the sex chromosomes (XX in females, XY in males):

  • The mother produces ova that always carry an X chromosome.
  • The father produces two types of sperms in equal 50:50 proportions: half carry an X chromosome, and half carry a Y chromosome.
If an X-sperm fertilises the ovum (X + X), the zygote becomes XX (Girl).
If a Y-sperm fertilises the ovum (X + Y), the zygote becomes XY (Boy).
Hence, sex is determined strictly at fertilization by the paternal sperm, rendering the traditional blaming of mothers completely scientifically false.

Q20 Short Answer (3 Marks)
Explain the role of thyroxine and dissolved iodine in the metamorphosis of frogs.
Model Textbook Solution & Explanation: Metamorphosis is the drastic developmental transformation of an aquatic, gill-breathing tadpole into a terrestrial, air-breathing adult frog.

  • This process is biochemically stimulated by the hormone thyroxine, synthesized by the tadpole's thyroid gland.
  • Thyroxine synthesis strictly depends on the presence of dissolved iodine in the pond water.
  • If the water is iodine-deficient, the tadpoles cannot produce thyroxine and cannot transform into adult frogs, remaining permanently in their larval tadpole state.

Section 6: Reproductive Health, Adolescent Nutrition & Well-being

5 Questions
Q21 Definition (2 Marks)
What is the full form of WHO, and what do you understand by a balanced diet?
Model Textbook Solution & Explanation: WHO: World Health Organization.
A balanced diet is a diet that contains adequate amounts of all essential nutrients—carbohydrates, proteins, fats, vitamins, minerals, water, and roughage—in proper proportions required for normal growth, cellular repair, and disease resistance.
Q22 Short Answer (2 Marks)
Why are adolescent girls at a particularly high risk of developing anaemia?
Model Textbook Solution & Explanation: Adolescent girls experience monthly blood loss through menstruation while simultaneously undergoing rapid somatic growth and blood volume expansion. If dietary iron intake is insufficient, hemoglobin synthesis drops, resulting in iron-deficiency anaemia.
Q23 Short Answer (2 Marks)
Who was Dorothy Hodgkin, and what was her scientific contribution?
Model Textbook Solution & Explanation: Professor Dorothy Crowfoot Hodgkin was a British Nobel Prize-winning chemist (1964) who pioneered X-ray crystallography to decipher the three-dimensional molecular structures of vital biochemicals, including Vitamin B12 and penicillin, proving essential for medical treatments of pernicious anaemia.
Q24 Short Answer (2 Marks)
What are the key food sources that promote strong bone growth in teenagers?
Model Textbook Solution & Explanation: Strong bone mineral density requires calcium, phosphorus, and Vitamin D. Primary dietary sources include cow's milk, curd, paneer (cheese), ragi (finger millet), green leafy vegetables, and exposure to early morning sunlight.
Q25 Short Answer (2 Marks)
What do you understand by the term substance abuse, and why must adolescents firmly say 'NO' to drugs?
Model Textbook Solution & Explanation: Substance abuse is the non-medical, hazardous intake of psychoactive chemicals (such as tobacco, alcohol, or narcotics) that induce artificial euphoria and physical dependence. Drugs are severely addictive; taking them even once tempts adolescents to repeat use, ultimately ruining physical health, neural cognition, academic prospects, and family happiness.

4 Core Concept Pillars (CBSE Class 7)

Comprehensive visual breakdown of pubertal transformations, master endocrine glands, reproductive cycles, and adolescent nutrition.

4 Pillars
Pillar 1: Pubertal Transformation & Physical Growth
Sudden Height Spurt

Long bones of limbs elongate rapidly between 10-14 years. Girls initially grow faster around 11-12 years, but boys catch up and surpass them by 18.

Voice Change & Adam's Apple

The larynx (voice box) enlarges in boys under testosterone, protruding noticeably as the Adam's apple, resulting in a deeper resonance.

Cutaneous Gland Secretions

Sweat (sudoriferous) and oil (sebaceous) glands become hyperactive on the face and body, often producing transient acne and pimples.

Pillar 2: Endocrine Switchboard & Master Hormones
Pituitary Master Gland

Situated at the brain base, it secretes stimulating hormones that trigger sex organs to release their respective hormones.

Testosterone (Testes)

Secreted by male gonads, promoting facial hair, muscle mass expansion, and reproductive organ maturation.

Estrogen (Ovaries)

Primary female sex hormone driving mammary gland development, pelvic widening, and onset of menstrual cycles.

Pillar 3: The 28-Day Reproductive Phase & Hygiene
Ovulation (~Day 14)

Every 28 to 30 days, one mature ovum is released alternately by an ovary into the fallopian tube.

Endometrial Preparation

The uterine wall thickens with vascular spongy tissue to nurture a potentially fertilised egg.

Menstrual Flow & Hygiene

If fertilisation does not occur, the unfertilised ovum and uterine lining shed for 3-7 days. Frequent changing and safe pad disposal prevent infections.

Pillar 4: Adolescent Health, Nutrition & Well-Being
High-Protein Balanced Diet

Rapid cellular mitosis demands adequate proteins, carbohydrates, and minerals like calcium for maximum adult bone mass.

Iron & Haemoglobin

Essential for preventing nutritional anaemia (especially in menstruating girls); rich sources include spinach, jaggery (gur), and citrus fruits.

Saying NO to Substance Abuse

Addictive drugs and tobacco harm developing neurological pathways. Adolescents must assertively refuse peer pressure.

Biology Vault & Deep-Dive Trivia

Fascinating biological phenomena, biophysics of voice changes, hormone triggers, and frog metamorphosis.

4 Cards
Neurobiology

What Neurobiological Trigger in the Brain Starts Puberty?

How the brain knows exactly when to begin the transition from child to adult.
The hypothalamus in the brain acts as the master biological alarm clock. When a child reaches a critical body weight and developmental stage, the hypothalamus begins the pulsatile release of GnRH (Gonadotropin-Releasing Hormone). GnRH directly signals the pituitary gland to secrete LH and FSH, which in turn awaken the dormant ovaries and testes to produce sex hormones!
Biophysics & Sound

Why Does the Adam's Apple Deepen the Male Voice?

How testosterone physically alters acoustic resonance like guitar strings.
Under testosterone stimulation, the thyroid cartilage of the larynx expands rapidly, protruding as the prominent Adam's apple. Inside, the vocal cords grow significantly thicker and longer. In acoustic physics, thicker and longer vibrating cords produce a lower fundamental resonant frequency—just like the thick lower bass strings on an acoustic guitar—creating the characteristic deep male voice.
Developmental Biology

The Tadpole's Magic Mineral: Why Frogs Need Iodine

Can a tadpole remain a tadpole forever without metamorphosis?
Yes, experimentally! Metamorphosis of an aquatic, gill-breathing tadpole into an air-breathing adult frog strictly requires the hormone thyroxine. Thyroxine synthesis requires iodine dissolved in the pond water. If tadpoles are raised in iodine-deficient water, they cannot synthesize thyroxine and remain trapped in the tadpole larval stage indefinitely, growing into giant perpetual tadpoles!
Nutritional Science

Anaemia Mukt Bharat & The Superfood Jaggery (Gur)

Why iron + vitamin C is vital for adolescent blood and energy.
During puberty, blood volume expands rapidly and adolescent girls lose blood monthly during menstruation. Iron is the core mineral required to build haemoglobin in red blood cells. Traditional Indian jaggery (gur), green leafy spinach, and raisins provide rich non-heme dietary iron. When eaten with vitamin C (amla, lemons), iron absorption increases by up to 300%, preventing nutritional anaemia!
