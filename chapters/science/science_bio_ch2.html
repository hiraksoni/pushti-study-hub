1. Introduction to Adolescence PUBERTY

The life of a human is divided into infancy, childhood, adolescence, adulthood, and old age. Adolescence is the transitional stage of human development from childhood to adulthood. During this period, physical, psychological, and social transitions occur.

  • WHO Definition: The period of life between 10 to 19 years of age.
  • Puberty: The stage during which an adolescent attains sexual maturity (9-12 years in girls, 10-14 years in boys).

2. Physical Changes During Puberty GROWTH

Height & Body Shape

  • Sudden increase in height due to growth of leg and arm bones. Girls initially grow faster than boys.
  • In boys: Shoulders broaden and chest becomes wide. Muscles grow prominent.
  • In girls: Pelvic region widens, breasts develop (mammary glands enlarge).

Voice Box & Glands

  • Adam's Apple: Protruding voice box in boys causing a deep, hoarse voice. Girls have a high-pitched voice.
  • Increased activity of sweat and sebaceous (oil) glands, leading to acne and pimples.

Secondary Sexual Characteristics

  • Growth of facial hair (moustache, beard) in boys.
  • Growth of hair in armpits and pubic region in both boys and girls.
  • Controlled by Testosterone (males) and Estrogen/Progesterone (females).

3. Reproductive Capability & Menstrual Cycle BIOLOGY

In females, reproductive age starts at puberty and continues till 45-55 years. The release of an ovum (egg) by an ovary is called ovulation.

Menstrual Cycle (28-30 days):

  • Menstrual phase (1st-5th day): Uterus wall breaks down, bleeding occurs (menstruation).
  • Proliferative phase (6th-13th day): Uterine wall thickens.
  • Ovulatory phase (14th day): Ovulation occurs.
  • Secretory phase (15th-28th day): Uterine glands produce mucus for implantation.

Note: The first menstrual flow is called menarche. The stoppage of menstruation at 45-50 years is called menopause.

4. Making Adolescence Joyful LIFESTYLE

Health is a state of complete physical, mental, and social well-being.

  • Balanced Diet: Crucial for rapid growth. Includes proteins, carbs, fats, vitamins. Lack of iron/B12 causes Anaemia. Government schemes like WIFS (Weekly Iron and Folic Acid Supplementation) help prevent this.
  • Personal Hygiene: Daily bathing prevents infections. Menstrual hygiene is critical to avoid RTIs.
  • Physical Exercise: Keeps body fit, boosts mood.
  • Say NO to Harmful Substances: Avoid drugs, alcohol, tobacco. Government initiatives like Nasha Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan aim to spread awareness.

1. What is the protruding voice box found in boys called?
Correct! The voice box (larynx) grows larger in boys during puberty and protrudes out, known as Adam's apple.
2. The stoppage of menstruation at 45 to 55 years of age is termed as:
Correct! Menarche is the first flow, while menopause is the permanent stoppage.
3. Which hormone is responsible for pubertal changes in males?
Correct! Testes secrete testosterone which regulates male secondary sexual characteristics.
4. The primary cause of acne and pimples during adolescence is the increased activity of:
Correct! Sebaceous (oil) glands become highly active during puberty.
Q1. Why do acne and pimples appear during the adolescent period?
During the adolescent period, both sweat glands and sebaceous (oil) glands become more active, especially on the face. This increased secretion often clogs skin pores and attracts bacteria, leading to the appearance of acne and pimples. Maintaining facial hygiene is crucial.
Q2. Differentiate between menarche and menopause.
Menarche: The very first menstrual flow that begins at puberty, marking the onset of female reproductive capability.
Menopause: The permanent stoppage of menstruation, typically occurring between 45 to 55 years of age, marking the end of the reproductive phase.
Q3. Define cyberbullying. What are its effects?
Cyberbullying involves the use of digital devices and online platforms to harass others by sending misleading messages, spreading false rumours, or sharing personal information without consent. It causes severe emotional distress, anxiety, and social withdrawal in adolescents.
Q4. Why is a balanced diet critical during adolescence? Give examples of nutrient deficiencies.
Adolescence is a stage of rapid physical growth requiring ample nutrients. A balanced diet provides proteins for tissue building, calcium for bones, and vitamins/minerals for overall health. A common deficiency is lack of Iron or Vitamin B12, which leads to Anaemia, causing fatigue and paleness.
