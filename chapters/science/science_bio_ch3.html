1. Introduction to Animal Nutrition DIGESTION

Animals are heterotrophic and cannot make their own food. The process of obtaining nutrition involves ingestion, digestion, absorption, assimilation, and egestion.

  • Digestion: The breakdown of complex food substances (carbohydrates, proteins, fats) into simple soluble forms inside a long tube called the alimentary canal.

2. Human Digestive System ANATOMY

Mouth & Buccal Cavity

  • Food is ingested here. Teeth chew food (mastication).
  • Salivary glands secrete saliva containing salivary amylase, which breaks down starch into sugars.
  • Tongue mixes saliva with food.

Stomach

  • J-shaped, thick-walled bag.
  • Inner lining secretes mucus, Hydrochloric Acid (HCl) (kills bacteria), and digestive juices (pepsin breaks down proteins).

Small & Large Intestine

  • Small Intestine: ~7.5m long. Highly coiled. Site of complete digestion and absorption via finger-like projections called villi. Receives bile from liver and pancreatic juice from pancreas.
  • Large Intestine: Absorbs water and salts. Waste moves to rectum and exits via anus (egestion).

3. Digestion in Other Animals ADAPTATIONS

Ruminants (Cow, Buffalo, Sheep):

  • Stomach has 4 chambers: Rumen, Reticulum, Omasum, Abomasum.
  • They quickly swallow grass, storing it in the rumen where bacteria partially digest cellulose, forming cud.
  • The cud returns to the mouth in small lumps to be thoroughly chewed (rumination).

Birds:

  • Lack teeth; use beaks. Food is stored in the Crop, passes to the Proventriculus (juices added), and then ground up in the muscular Gizzard (often using swallowed stones/grit).

4. Respiration in Animals RESPIRATION

Respiration is the biochemical breakdown of food (glucose) to release energy.

  • Aerobic Respiration: Uses oxygen. Releases CO2, Water, and Energy.
  • Anaerobic Respiration: Occurs without oxygen (e.g., in yeast, producing alcohol and CO2).

Human Respiratory System:

  • Path: Nostrils → Nasal Cavity → Pharynx → Trachea (windpipe) → Bronchi → Lungs → Alveoli.
  • Inhalation: Ribs move upward/outward, diaphragm moves down (flattens), volume increases, air rushes in.
  • Exhalation: Ribs move downward/inward, diaphragm moves up (dome-shaped), volume decreases, air pushed out.

Note: Fish use Gills. Frogs use Skin and Lungs. Earthworms use moist skin. Insects use spiracles and tracheae.

1. The largest gland in the human body is:
Correct! The liver is a reddish-brown gland situated in the upper part of the abdomen and is the largest gland. It secretes bile juice.
2. During inhalation, what happens to the diaphragm?
Correct! The diaphragm moves downward (flattening) to increase the volume of the chest cavity.
3. Which part of a ruminant's stomach is responsible for storing partially chewed food?
Correct! The rumen stores food and houses bacteria that help ferment and digest cellulose.
4. The tiny finger-like projections in the small intestine that increase surface area for absorption are called:
Correct! Villi significantly increase the surface area of the small intestine to absorb digested food into the bloodstream.
Q1. What is the role of HCl secreted by the inner lining of the stomach?
Hydrochloric Acid (HCl) serves two main purposes: 1) It kills harmful bacteria that enter the stomach along with food. 2) It makes the medium in the stomach acidic, which is necessary for digestive enzymes (like pepsin) to act and break down proteins.
Q2. Cows and buffaloes are usually seen chewing continuously even when they are not grazing. Explain.
Cows and buffaloes are ruminants. While grazing, they quickly swallow grass and store it in a stomach chamber called the rumen. Here it is partially digested to form 'cud'. Later, while resting, this cud returns to the mouth in small lumps so the animal can chew it thoroughly. This process is called rumination.
Q3. Explain the mechanism of breathing in humans during inhalation.
During inhalation (breathing in), two main muscle movements occur: the ribs move upward and outward, and the diaphragm moves downward. This combined movement increases the volume of the thoracic cavity, causing a decrease in air pressure inside the lungs. Air from the atmosphere rushes in to fill this space.
Q4. How does the digestion of food take place in birds?
Birds don't have teeth. They swallow food whole using their beaks. Food passes down the oesophagus into the Crop for storage. From there, it enters the stomach, which has two parts: the Proventriculus (where digestive juices are added) and the Gizzard (a muscular organ that grinds the food, often aided by swallowed stones/grit).
