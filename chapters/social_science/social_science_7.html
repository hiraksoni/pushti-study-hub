NCERT History · Chapter 7

India in the Iron Age

Second Urbanisation, Iron Metallurgy, Post-Mauryan Kingdoms (Kushanas, Satavahanas, Muvendar) & Silk Route Trade

Iron AgeUrbanisationKushanasSatavahanasSangamSilk Route 20 Verified Qs

1. The Iron Revolution & Agricultural Expansion

Around 500 BCE, heavy iron axes cleared dense tropical Gangetic forests, while heavy iron ploughshares turned stiff river soils. Coupled with paddy transplantation and irrigation, food yields exploded.

2. The Second Urbanisation

Surplus agriculture propelled urban clustering: fortified capital cities, thriving trade marts, the luxury Northern Black Polished Ware (NBPW) ceramic culture, and sanitary terracotta ring wells.

3. The Kushanas & Trans-Eurasian Commerce

Originating from Central Asia, the Kushana Empire (under King Kanishka) controlled Northwest India and the strategic Silk Route, issued pure gold coins, and convened the Fourth Buddhist Council.

4. The Satavahanas: Lords of the Deccan

Ruling from Pratishthana (Paithan) across Maharashtra and Andhra Pradesh, rulers like Gautamiputra Satakarni dominated the Dakshinapatha trade route linking North and South India.

5. The Muvendar: Three Crowned Kings of Tamilakam

The Cholas (Uraiyur/Puhar), Cheras (Vanji/Muziris), and Pandyas (Madurai) controlled coastal ports, naval spice fleets, and rich agricultural deltas in South India.

6. Sangam Literature & Roman Trade Networks

Great poetic assemblies (*Sangams*) in Madurai composed thousands of verses depicting heroic kings, trade marts, and heavy exports of Indian black pepper ('black gold'), pearls, and beryl to the Roman Empire.

Chapter Terminology & Epigraphy Guide

Second Urbanisation

The revival of major Indian cities, trade guilds, and monetary economies during the Iron Age (~500 BCE) following the Bronze Age Harappan collapse.

Paddy Transplantation

The agricultural technique of sprouting paddy seedlings in nurseries before transplanting into flooded fields, drastically multiplying harvest yields.

NBPW

Northern Black Polished Ware; ultra-fine luxury ceramic pottery with a glossy black metallic sheen produced during the Iron Age.

Ring Wells

Vertical ceramic rings stacked in underground shafts used in Iron Age towns as drainage pits, soakaway latrines, or wells.

Shrenis

Autonomous guilds formed by craftspersons and merchants for mutual economic cooperation, quality control, and banking.

Dakshinapatha

The great trans-Indian southern trade route connecting northern plains through the Deccan plateau to the southern kingdoms.

Muvendar

A Tamil term meaning 'Three Great Chiefs' or 'Three Crowned Kings', referring to the Cholas, Cheras, and Pandyas of early South India.

Sangam Literature

The corpus of ancient Tamil literature compiled at royal literary assemblies (*Sangams*) convened in Madurai under Pandyan kings.

Silk Route

The vast trans-continental network of overland and maritime trade arteries linking China, India, Persia, and the Roman Empire.

Punch-Marked Coins

Early Indian coins minted from sheets of silver or copper stamped with symbolic dies rather than struck with inscriptions.

Black Gold

The nickname given to Indian black pepper by Roman traders due to its immense value in European culinary luxury markets.

Mahayana Buddhism

The 'Greater Vehicle' Buddhist tradition that popularized the veneration of Bodhisattvas, deity worship of the Buddha, and devotional rituals.

Specialized Applied Analysis

Economic Networks, Shrenis & Cultural Diffusion

Master Hub Verified

✦ The Silk Route Geopolitical Nexus

Kushana rulers collected tolls and offered military escorts to international silk, glassware, and spice caravans traveling between China, India, and the Mediterranean.

✦ Craft Guilds (Shrenis)

Organized associations of artisans and merchants that functioned as technical training academies, raw material procurement syndicates, quality controllers, and trusted commercial banks.

✦ Punch-Marked Coinage System

Earliest Indian silver and copper currency punched with distinct royal symbols (Sun, Crescent, Chaitya, Animals), enabling trans-regional monetization.

Detailed Answer · #2 his7_la_ext_001

How did the widespread adoption of iron technology and new irrigation techniques transform agriculture and society during this period?

• **Iron Ploughshares**: Replaced wooden ploughs, allowing heavy, clayey soil of the fertile Gangetic plain to be tilled deeply, drastically multiplying crop yields. • **Iron Axes**: Facilitated systematic clearance of dense subtropical forests for expanding agricultural settlements. • **Paddy Transplantation**: Instead of broadcasting seeds, farmers raised saplings in seedbeds and transplanted them into waterlogged fields, ensuring higher seedling survival rates. • **State-Supported Irrigation**: Kings and villages constructed extensive networks of canals, wells, artificial irrigation tanks, and reservoirs, raising agricultural surpluses that supported flourishing urban craft centers.
Evaluation Criteria: Foundation of the Second Urbanisation in the Indian subcontinent.
