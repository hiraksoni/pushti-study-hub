The Crust (Lithosphere)

Thickness / Depth 5 km to 70 km
Temperature Range 0°C to 700°C
Chemical Makeup SIAL & SIMA
State of Matter Solid Brittle Rock

The Crust is the outermost and thinnest layer of the Earth. It constitutes less than 1% of the Earth's total volume.

Continental Crust (SIAL): Composed mainly of Silica and Aluminium. Averages 35 km to 70 km in thickness (especially under mountain ranges like the Himalayas).

Oceanic Crust (SIMA): Composed of Silica and Magnesium. Much denser and only about 5 km to 8 km thick beneath ocean basins.

Did You Know?

The deepest gold mine in the world (Mponeng in South Africa) is only ~4 km deep. Engineers cannot go deeper due to intense 60°C+ rock temperatures!

The Three Great Rock Families & The Perpetual Rock Cycle

A rock is an aggregate of one or more naturally occurring minerals. Explore how rocks are born, transformed, and continuously recycled.

Primary Rocks

Igneous Rocks

Derived from Latin 'Ignis' (fire). Formed when fiery molten magma or lava cools and solidifies. All other rocks originate from them!

Intrusive (Plutonic): Cool slowly inside crust → large crystal grains. E.g., Granite, Diorite.
Extrusive (Volcanic): Cool rapidly on surface → fine/glassy grain. E.g., Basalt (Deccan Plateau), Obsidian.
No fossils (destroyed by heat)
Layered / Stratified Rocks

Sedimentary Rocks

From Latin 'Sedimentum' (settling down). Formed by the deposition, compaction, and cementation of rock fragments, mineral precipitates, and organic debris over epochs.

5-Step Sequential Formation: Weathering → Erosion → Deposition → Compaction → Cementation.
Classic Examples: Sandstone (Red Fort), Limestone, Shale, Conglomerate, Gypsum, Coal.
Contains ancient plant & animal fossils!
Changed Form

Metamorphic Rocks

From Greek 'Metamorphose' (change of form). Existing igneous or sedimentary rocks subjected to colossal heat and crushing pressure undergo physical and chemical recrystallization.

Key Transformations: Limestone → Marble (Taj Mahal)
Clay / Shale → Slate
Sandstone → Quartzite
Granite → Gneiss
Extremely hard, crystalline & durable

Origin in the Subterranean Hearth

Deep within the lower crust and upper mantle, intense heat melts rocks into glowing, pressurized liquid called magma. When this magma rises toward cooler zones or erupts from volcanoes as lava, cooling triggers solidification into Igneous Rocks.

Minerals: Chemistry & Everyday Utility

Minerals are naturally occurring inorganic substances having a definite chemical composition and physical properties.

Metallic Minerals

Contain metals in raw form; ductile, malleable, and conduct heat/electricity.

  • Iron: Bridges, steel framing, automobiles, railway tracks.
  • Copper: Electrical cables, household wiring, plumbing.
  • Gold & Silver: Jewelry, dentistry, aerospace electronics.
  • Aluminium (Bauxite): Aircraft bodies, soda cans, kitchen utensils.

Non-Metallic Minerals & Fuels

Do not contain extractable metals; non-conductive and softer.

  • Coal & Petroleum: Primary fossil fuels powering electricity & transport.
  • Limestone & Gypsum: Cement manufacturing & building plaster.
  • Graphite: Pencil leads and battery electrodes.
  • Talc: Talcum powder, ceramics, paper making.

Fill in the Blanks

1. The earth's crust is divided into the [___________] crust and the [___________] crust.

Answer: continental crust and oceanic crust.

2. The outer core is mainly made up of [___________] and [___________].

Answer: nickel and iron (NIFE).

3. Igneous rocks are also called [___________] rocks.

Answer: primary rocks (because all other rocks directly or indirectly originate from them).

4. [___________] rocks are formed when magma cools down and solidifies inside the earth.

Answer: Intrusive igneous rocks (e.g. Granite).

5. [___________] igneous rocks have a grainy texture with small crystals due to rapid cooling on the surface.

Answer: Extrusive igneous rocks (e.g. Basalt).

6. The weight of the sediments compresses the bottom layers of the sediments to form [___________] rocks.

Answer: sedimentary rocks (from process of lithification).

7. The cyclic process in which rocks constantly transform from one type to another type under certain conditions is called the [___________].

Answer: rock cycle.

8. The minerals that do not contain metals are called [___________] minerals.

Answer: non-metallic minerals (e.g. Limestone, Gypsum, Coal).

Multiple Choice Questions

1. Which is the thinnest layer of the earth?

(a) Mantle (b) Crust (c) Inner core (d) Outer core
Correct: (b) Crust — Constitutes only 0.5% to 1% of the Earth's radius (averaging 35 km continental and 5 km oceanic).

2. Which of the following is NOT an example of metamorphic rocks?

(a) Marble (b) Slate (c) Gneiss (d) Shale
Correct: (d) Shale — Shale is a sedimentary rock formed of compacted silt/clay. (When shale is subjected to heat/pressure, it metamorphoses into slate!).

3. When do metamorphic rocks become sedimentary rocks?

(a) When sedimentary rocks are broken down by wind, water, or ice (b) When igneous rocks are under heat and pressure (c) When metamorphic rocks are broken down by wind, water, or ice into sediments (d) When magma or lava cools down
Correct: (c) — Any rock type, once weathered into sediments and compacted, transforms into sedimentary rock.

4. Which of the following is NOT an example of a metallic mineral?

(a) Iron (b) Tin (c) Potash (d) Copper
Correct: (c) Potash — Potash is a non-metallic potassium compound widely used in agricultural fertilizers.

Short Answer Questions

1. Name the three concentric layers of the Earth.

The three main concentric layers of the Earth are:

  1. Crust: Outermost solid layer (composed of SIAL and SIMA).
  2. Mantle: Thick middle semi-molten/solid layer (~2,900 km).
  3. Core: Innermost metallic layer subdivided into Liquid Outer Core and Solid Inner Core.

2. What is a rock?

Any natural mass of mineral matter that makes up the Earth's crust is called a rock. Rocks can have different textures, grain sizes, mineral compositions, and colors.

3. How are extrusive and intrusive igneous rocks formed? Give one example of each.

• Intrusive Igneous Rocks: Formed when molten magma cools deep inside the Earth's crust slowly, allowing large crystals to grow. E.g., Granite.

• Extrusive Igneous Rocks: Formed when molten lava reaches the Earth's surface and cools rapidly, resulting in fine-grained crystal structures. E.g., Basalt (Deccan plateau).

4. What do you mean by a 'Rock Cycle'?

The continuous cyclic process by which rocks transform from one type to another under certain geological conditions (heat, pressure, weathering, melting) in a cyclic manner is known as the Rock Cycle.

Long Answer Detailed Explanations

1. Describe the three layers of the Earth with their thickness, composition, and characteristics.

1. The Crust:

The outermost brittle layer. It covers only ~1% of Earth's volume. Its thickness varies from 5 km under the ocean floor to 35-70 km under continental masses. Continental crust is primarily made of Silica and Aluminium (SIAL), while oceanic crust consists of Silica and Magnesium (SIMA).

2. The Mantle:

Just below the crust lies the mantle extending up to a depth of 2,900 km. It comprises ~84% of Earth's volume. It consists of the upper semi-molten layer called the asthenosphere (where convection currents occur) and the dense lower mantle consisting of iron and magnesium silicates. Temperatures range from 870°C to 4,400°C.

3. The Core:

The innermost layer has a radius of approximately 3,500 km. It accounts for 15% of Earth's volume. It is composed of Nickel and Iron (NIFE). It has two parts: the Liquid Outer Core (2,300 km thick), whose swirling motions generate Earth's magnetic field, and the Solid Inner Core (1,200 km radius), kept solid despite 6,000°C temperatures due to immense gravitational pressure.

2. What are the different uses of rocks and minerals in human civilization?

Rocks and minerals are indispensable to our daily lives:

  • Construction: Hard rocks like granite, basalt, sandstone, and marble are used to build monuments (Red Fort, Taj Mahal), houses, and bridges.
  • Energy Resources: Minerals like coal, petroleum, and natural gas provide fuel for transport and generate electricity.
  • Industrial Manufacturing: Iron, bauxite (aluminium), and copper are critical for machinery, automobiles, aircraft, and electronics.
  • Agriculture: Minerals like potash, phosphate, and gypsum are used in chemical fertilizers and soil conditioners.
  • Medicines & Gems: Diamonds, rubies, and gold are prized in jewelry; minerals like talc and zinc are used in pharmaceuticals and cosmetics.

Give Reasons & High Order Thinking Skills (HOTS)

1. Give Reason: We cannot go to the center of the Earth.

Reason: To reach the center of the Earth, one would have to dig a hole 6,371 km deep through the ocean floor. This is impossible because the temperature reaches approximately 6,000°C and the pressure is millions of times higher than surface atmospheric pressure, which would crush and vaporize any drill or human vessel.

2. Give Reason: Sedimentary rocks are formed from sediments.

Reason: Rocks continuously crack, roll down, and hit each other, breaking into tiny fragments called sediments. These loose sediments are transported by wind and flowing water into depressions. Over millions of years, the immense weight of overlying layers compresses and hardens these loose sediments into stratified sedimentary rocks.

3. Give Reason: Limestone changes into marble.

Reason: When sedimentary limestone (composed of calcite/calcium carbonate) is buried deep inside the Earth and subjected to tremendous subterranean heat and pressure during tectonic movements, its minerals recrystallize into a much denser, harder crystalline rock known as marble.
