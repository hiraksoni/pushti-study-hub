Cosmic Genesis · 4.6 Billion Years Ago Nebula Theory

How Did the Earth's Layers Form?

Over 4.6 billion years ago, a giant rotating cloud of cosmic gas and dust called a 'nebula' collapsed under its own gravity. As the newborn molten Earth cooled over millions of years, gravity performed a natural density separation: the heaviest metals (iron and nickel) sank to form the super-dense central Core, while the lighter silicate rocks floated upward to solidify into the outer Crust.

6 Core Definitions
Gravity:

A strong, invisible attractive force that pulls matter together into planets.

Molten:

Heated to an extremely high temperature until it turns into liquefied rock.

Texture:

The size, shape, and arrangement of mineral crystals or grains in a rock.

Solidifies:

The physical change when liquid molten rock cools into hard solid rock.

Preserved petrified remains or impressions of prehistoric plants and animals.

The Continuous Rock Cycle

Primary Rocks

Igneous Rocks

Born directly from molten fiery rock (magma or lava) cooling and solidifying.

Intrusive Igneous: Cools slowly deep inside the crust. Forms large crystals (e.g. Granite, Diorite).
Extrusive Igneous: Pours out as lava and cools rapidly on the surface. Fine, grainy texture with tiny crystals (e.g. Basalt, Andesite).
Indian Context: Deccan Plateau (Maharashtra) is built of basalt sheets!
Layered Rocks

Sedimentary Rocks

Formed when weathered rock fragments (sediments) are transported, deposited in layers, and compacted.

Fossil Storage: The only rocks containing trapped fossils of prehistoric marine life, plants, and dinosaur bones.
Common Examples: Sandstone (compacted sand), Shale (compacted mud/clay), Limestone, and Gypsum.
Indian Monument: Delhi's famous Red Fort is built entirely of red sandstone!
Changed Rocks

Metamorphic Rocks

From Greek meta (change) + morphe (form). Altered by extreme tectonic heat and crushing pressure.

Metamorphic Transitions: • Limestone $\rightarrow$ Marble
• Clay / Shale $\rightarrow$ Slate
• Granite $\rightarrow$ Gneiss
Distinctive Features: Crystals re-align under directional pressure, creating banded foliage textures.
Indian Wonder: The Taj Mahal in Agra is carved from white Makrana marble!

Minerals: Classification & Vital Applications

Metallic Minerals

Contain metals in chemical combination; malleable, ductile, lustrous, and conduct electricity.

Iron (Fe) Copper (Cu) Tin (Sn) Manganese (Mn) Gold & Silver

Non-Metallic Minerals

Do NOT contain metals; break or fracture under pressure; vital for plant growth, agriculture, and construction.

Potash (Plant Fertilizer) Gypsum (Plaster of Paris) Limestone (Cement) Mica (Electrical Insulator) Diamond (Hardest Mineral)
Botanical Significance (Gemini Question B.5): Plants specifically utilize Potassium and Magnesium minerals from the soil to synthesize chlorophyll, build structural cell walls, and fuel photosynthesis!
