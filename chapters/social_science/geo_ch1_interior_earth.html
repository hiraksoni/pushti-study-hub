Cosmic Origin: How Planetary Density Differentiation Created Earth's Layers

The Earth originated approximately 4.6 billion years ago from the gravitational collapse of a giant swirling cosmic dust cloud called a nebula. In its primordial infancy, Earth was a completely molten, fiery ball bombarded by planetesimals. As gravitational energy converted to extreme heat, density differentiation occurred:

1. Heavy Core Sink

Dense metallic elements—chiefly Iron (Fe) and Nickel (Ni)—gravitationally sank to the very centre, creating the molten and solid core.

2. Silicate Mantle Slurry

Medium-density silicate rocks rich in iron and magnesium formed the 2,900 km thick mantle, which still churns with convection currents.

3. Light Solid Crust

The lightest silicates (silicon, aluminum) floated to the surface like slag on molten steel, cooling and crystallizing into our paper-thin solid crust.

Crustal Composition Breakdown

SIAL (Continental) vs. SIMA (Oceanic)

Total Volume: ~1%
Continental Crust 35 to 70 km Thick

SIAL Layer

Forms the massive landmasses, mountain ranges, and continents. Composed predominantly of light, low-density granitic rocks.

Main Elements: Silica (Si) + Alumina (Al)
Average Density: ~2.7 g/cm³
Dominant Rock: Granite
Oceanic Crust 5 to 8 km Thick

SIMA Layer

Forms the floor beneath the great oceans. Much thinner than continental crust, but significantly heavier and denser due to dark volcanic minerals.

Main Elements: Silica (Si) + Magnesium (Ma)
Average Density: ~3.0 g/cm³ (Denser!)
Dominant Rock: Basalt

Key Geological Terminology Glossary

Claude Integration
1. Gravity

A strong, invisible attractive force that pulls objects together and concentrated dense iron at Earth's core.

2. Molten

Heated to extreme temperatures until solid rock liquefies into subterranean magma or surface lava.

3. Texture

The tactile size, shape, and arrangement of interlocking mineral crystals or grains across a rock specimen.

4. Solidifies

The thermal freezing process where liquid magma or lava cools and freezes into rigid crystalline igneous rock.

5. Fossils

Petrified remains, shells, carbonized imprints, or tracks of prehistoric plants and animals preserved in sedimentary strata.

6. Cyclic

A continuous circular process that repeats perpetually through geological time without a definitive beginning or end.
