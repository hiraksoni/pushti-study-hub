1. Explain the administrative system of the Mauryan Empire. 4 Marks
Model Answer & Notes Citation
Model 4-Pillar Answer:
  • 1. Central Administration: The Emperor was the supreme head of the army, judiciary, and state. The area around the capital, Pataliputra, was under the direct supervision of the emperor and his council of ministers (Mantriparishad).
  • 2. Provincial Government: The empire was divided into provinces ruled by royal governors called Kumaras (princes). Important provincial capitals included Taxila (gateway to the northwest) and Ujjain (trade route from north to south).
  • 3. Taxation & Revenue System: Royal officials were appointed to collect taxes from farmers, herders, craftspeople, and traders. Farmers typically gave 1/6th of their produce (Bhaga) to the state.
  • 4. Espionage & Dhamma Officers: A wide network of spies and royal inspectors (spashas) kept the emperor informed about governors and officials. Emperor Ashoka also created special officials called Dhamma Mahamattas to spread moral teachings and ensure public welfare.
Teacher Marking Scheme (4M): 1 Mark for Central rule at Pataliputra; 1 Mark for Provincial Kumaras (Taxila/Ujjain); 1 Mark for Tax collection/Revenue; 1 Mark for Spies and Dhamma Mahamattas.

2. Discuss the technological advancements made during the Iron Age besides agriculture. 4 Marks
Model Answer & Notes Citation
Model 4-Pillar Answer:
  • 1. Iron Metallurgy & Tool Manufacturing: Iron technology allowed craftsmen to manufacture hardened iron axes, chisels, saws, hammers, and weapons (swords, daggers, spears, iron arrowheads) that were far sharper and more durable than bronze or copper.
  • 2. Fine Pottery Production (NBPW): The Iron Age saw the development of Northern Black Polished Ware (NBPW), a distinctive, glossy, lustrous black pottery used as luxury tableware by elites and royalty.
  • 3. Coinage & Monetisation: The introduction of punch-marked coins made of silver and copper revolutionized trade, replacing the barter system and allowing precise standard pricing across kingdoms.
  • 4. Construction & Sanitation (Ring Wells): Builders developed ring wells (terracotta rings stacked vertically) that served as domestic wells, soak-pits, and drainage systems, along with kilned bricks for permanent town structures.

Q.3 Detail Answers (Answer any 2 out of 4)

Ch 1 & 2 · All 4 Fully Solved
(i) What are tectonic plates? Explain their movements. 3 Marks
Model Answer & Notes Citation
Model Answer:

Definition: The Earth's lithosphere is broken down into numerous massive, irregularly shaped rigid slabs of solid rock called Lithospheric (Tectonic) plates. These plates float slowly on top of the semi-molten asthenosphere (moved by convection currents in the mantle).

Three Types of Plate Movements:

  • 1. Convergent Boundary (Colliding): When two plates move towards each other, they collide. One plate is forced under the other (subduction) or buckles upwards to form fold mountains (e.g. formation of the Himalayas by Indo-Australian and Eurasian plate collision).
  • 2. Divergent Boundary (Spreading): When two plates pull apart from each other, magma rises from the mantle to fill the gap, creating rift valleys on land and mid-ocean ridges under the sea (e.g. East African Rift).
  • 3. Transform Boundary (Sliding): When two plates slide past each other horizontally. Stress builds up along fault lines, and sudden release generates violent earthquakes (e.g. San Andreas Fault).
Refer: Geography Ch 2 · Plate Tectonics & Boundaries
(ii) Define a volcano. Mention its three types and explain the process of a volcanic eruption. 3 Marks
Model Answer & Notes Citation
Model Answer:

Definition: A volcano is a vent or opening in the Earth’s crust through which molten magma, volcanic ash, steam, and gases erupt suddenly from deep inside the interior.

Three Types of Volcanoes:

  • 1. Active Volcanoes: Erupt frequently or have erupted recently (e.g. Mt. Etna in Italy, Barren Island in India, Mt. Fuji).
  • 2. Dormant Volcanoes: "Sleeping" volcanoes that have not erupted for a long time but show signs that they may erupt in the future (e.g. Mt. Vesuvius).
  • 3. Extinct Volcanoes: "Dead" volcanoes that have stopped erupting completely with no historical eruption and no likelihood of future eruption (e.g. Mt. Kilimanjaro in Africa).

Process of Eruption: Due to extreme heat and pressure, magma collects in a magma chamber in the upper mantle. Convection forces the magma upwards through a narrow vertical pipe called a conduit/vent. When the confining rock pressure is overcome, magma bursts through the bowl-shaped crater at the summit as glowing molten lava, releasing ash, cinders, and volcanic gases.

Refer: Geography Ch 1 · Volcanoes: Structure & Types
(iii) Explain the Rock Cycle with the help of a neat labeled diagram. 3 Marks
Model Answer & SVG Diagram
Model Answer:

Rock Cycle Concept: One type of rock changes into another type under certain conditions in a cyclic manner. This endless process of transformation of rocks is called the Rock Cycle.

  • Step 1 (Igneous): Molten magma cools and solidifies to form primary Igneous rocks.
  • Step 2 (Sedimentary): Weathering and erosion break igneous rocks into small fragments (sediments) transported by wind/water, which get compacted and cemented to form Sedimentary rocks.
  • Step 3 (Metamorphic): When igneous and sedimentary rocks are subjected to intense heat and pressure, they transform into Metamorphic rocks (e.g. limestone into marble).
  • Step 4 (Melting back to Magma): Under even greater heat and pressure deep within the crust, metamorphic rocks melt down to form molten magma once again, restarting the cycle.

(iv) How are Igneous rocks formed? Explain their two types with examples. 3 Marks
Model Answer & Notes Citation
Model Answer:

Formation: The word igneous is derived from the Latin word Ignis, meaning fire. When molten magma cools and solidifies, igneous rocks are formed. They are also known as primary rocks because all other rocks originate from them.

Two Types of Igneous Rocks:

  • 1. Intrusive Igneous Rocks (Plutonic):
    • Formed when magma cools slowly deep inside the Earth's crust.
    • Because they cool slowly over thousands of years, they form large, coarse-grained crystals.
    • Examples: Granite (used for grinding stones), Diorite, and Gabbro.
  • 2. Extrusive Igneous Rocks (Volcanic):
    • Formed when molten lava erupts onto Earth’s surface and cools rapidly.
    • Because of rapid cooling in air/water, crystals do not grow large, resulting in a very fine-grained or glassy texture.
    • Examples: Basalt (the entire Deccan Plateau of India is made of basalt), Obsidian, and Pumice.
Refer: Geography Ch 1 · Intrusive vs Extrusive Rocks

Draw and label the concentric layers of the Earth:

Teacher Rubric & Key Layer Facts
  • Crust: Outermost, thinnest solid layer. Continental crust (~35 km) is made of Silica and Alumina (SIAL). Oceanic crust (~5 km) is made of Silica and Magnesium (SIMA).
  • Mantle: Extends up to a depth of 2,900 km beneath the crust. Comprises molten asthenosphere where convection magma currents originate.
  • Outer Core: Liquid layer composed primarily of molten iron and nickel under extreme temperatures.
  • Inner Core: Solid metallic center composed of Nickel and Iron (NIFE) under immense pressure, radius of ~3,500 km.

Q.3 Reading Comprehension: Democracy, Rule of Law & Powers

Ch 14 · Comprehension Analysis
"In a democratic system, the ultimate sovereign power resides with the citizens of the nation. Democracy is governed by the fundamental principle of the 'Rule of Law', which ensures that every citizen—from a poor agricultural labourer to the President or Prime Minister—is equal before the law, and no individual is above the law. To safeguard democracy and prevent tyranny, power is divided among three separate organs: the Legislature (makes laws), the Executive (enforces and implements laws), and the Judiciary (interprets laws and administers justice). 3. Describe the role of three different types of people involved in buying and selling in a market chain. 4 Marks
Model Answer & Notes Citation
Model 3-Role Answer:
  • 1. The Producers / Growers:
    • These are farmers, weavers, or factory manufacturers who invest initial capital, raw materials, and intense manual labour to produce goods (e.g., small cotton farmers in Kurnool or garment factory owners).
    • They sell their bulk produce at the local mandi or to traders, often earning the smallest profit margin in the entire chain.
  • 2. The Wholesalers / Bulk Traders:
    • Wholesale traders operate in large central wholesale markets (Mandis). They purchase goods in massive quantities directly from producers.
    • They have storage godowns, transport fleets, and large working capital. They break bulk quantities and sell to secondary city traders and retail merchants.
  • 3. The Retailers / Shopkeepers / Hawkers:
    • Retailers are neighbourhood grocers, weekly market vendors, or shopping complex merchants who buy smaller lots from wholesalers.
    • They deal directly with the end consumer, selling goods in small individual quantities according to family needs.

5. How does the inequality in the market chain affect: (a) Producers (Farmers / Weavers) (b) Consumers 4 Marks
Model Answer & Notes Citation
Model 2-Part Answer:
  • (a) Impact on Producers (Small Farmers / Weavers):
    • Smallest Share of Profit: Despite bearing the entire production risk (drought, pests, hard physical labour), small producers earn a tiny fraction of the final product price (e.g. a cotton farmer earns only ~₹25-30 per kg while a finished shirt sells for ₹1200+).
    • Middlemen Exploitation & Debt: Because poor farmers lack storage godowns and working capital, they take loans from local money-lenders/traders at exorbitant interest rates. In return, they are forced to sell their crops at distress prices dictated by middlemen.
    • Under the Putting-out System: Weavers depend entirely on merchants for yarn supply and market orders, resulting in low wages and severe economic dependency.
  • (b) Impact on Consumers:
    • Heavily Inflated Retail Prices: As goods pass through multiple intermediaries (village traders → wholesale mandi commission agents → secondary distributors → retail mall brand owners), each stage adds heavy transportation, storage, marketing, and profit margins.
    • Bearing the Middlemen Markup: The end consumer ends up paying many times higher than the actual farm/factory production cost, reducing purchasing power and hurting low-income families.
Teacher Marking Scheme (4M): 2 Marks for detailed analysis of producers (distress sale, debt trap, low share); 2 Marks for consumer impact (inflated price, multi-tier middleman markups).

Top 5 Common Student Mistakes in This Paper:
  • Mistake 1 (Chalukya Capital): Confusing Chalukyas with Pallavas (Pallava capital was Kanchipuram; Chalukya capital was Aihole/Badami).
  • Mistake 2 (Harsha's Biographer): Confusing Banabhatt (wrote Harshacharita) with Harisena (wrote Prayaga Prashasti).
  • Mistake 3 (Extrusive vs Intrusive): Forgetting that rapid cooling on surface produces small crystals (extrusive basalt), whereas slow cooling deep underground produces large crystals (intrusive granite).
