E. Answer the following questions in brief

1. What do you understand by the term gender equality? Answer in about 20-30 words.
Gender equality means that every human being, regardless of their gender, should have equal rights to think, express, work, and access opportunities without discrimination.
2. What are gender stereotypes? How are gender stereotypes strengthened?
Gender stereotypes are fixed beliefs about how boys and girls should behave and their roles in society. They are strengthened by families, schools, and society treating children differently based on gender.
3. Explain why gender equality is important for us.
Gender equality is important because it allows individuals to realise their full potential, live a life of dignity, and contribute equally to society, free from limitations set by gender roles.
4. Do you think it is only the government's responsibility to ensure gender equality? Why/why not?
No, it is a shared responsibility. While the government creates laws and policies, individuals, families, and schools must change their mindsets and actions to practice equality in everyday life.
5. List any three ways in which we can play a positive role in reducing gender inequality.
1. Treat everyone equally regardless of gender. 2. Break gender stereotypes by encouraging boys and girls to pursue diverse interests. 3. Share household chores equally.

F. Answer the following questions in detail

Try to answer these descriptive questions in your notebook, then check the key points below.

1. Using an example, explain how gender stereotypes lead to gender inequality.
If society believes the stereotype that women are only meant for household work, it limits their opportunities for education and employment. This unequal access to opportunities leads to gender inequality.
2. Describe any two factors that lead to gender inequality in not more than 80 words.
1. Preconceived Stereotypes: Beliefs that boys and girls should have different roles limit their potential. 2. Unequal burden of household work: The expectation that only women must manage the household prevents them from seeking outside employment.
3. How do women get affected by the unpaid work they do?
Women performing unpaid household work are often not recognized for their efforts. This devalues their labor, restricts their financial independence, and limits their time to pursue careers or further education, reinforcing their lower economic status.
4. Explain the historical evolution of the women's movement in India in about 100 words.
Historically, women faced social evils like sati and denial of education. Social reformers like Raja Rammohun Roy and Savitribai Phule championed women's rights and education. Over time, the movement gained momentum, and post-independence, the Constitution granted equal rights. Women continue to advocate for equality today.
5. Describe any two laws that the government has passed to promote gender equality.
1. Right to Equality: Prohibits discrimination on the basis of gender and guarantees equal opportunities. 2. Laws against Dowry and Domestic Violence: Protects women from violence and abuse, making such acts punishable offenses.
