NCERT History · Chapter 8

India from 4th to 7th Century CE

The Classical Gupta Empire, Harshavardhana of Kannauj, Chalukyas of Vatapi & Pallavas of Kanchipuram

GuptasSamudraguptaHarshaChalukyasPallavasClassical Age 37 Verified Qs

1. Genesis of the Gupta Golden Age (320 CE)

Emerging from Magadha, Chandragupta I forged a powerful marriage alliance with the Lichchhavis, assumed the exalted title *Maharajadhiraja*, and inaugurated the Gupta Era in 320 CE.

2. Samudragupta & The Prayaga Prashasti

Court poet Harishena engraved the famous *Prashasti* on the Ashokan pillar at Allahabad, detailing Samudragupta's brilliant military campaigns across 4 distinct geopolitical zones.

3. Chandragupta II Vikramaditya & Fa-Xian's Accounts

Conquering the Western Sakas (Kshatrapas), Chandragupta II patronized the Nine Gems (*Navaratnas*) including Kalidasa and Varahamihira. Chinese pilgrim Fa-Xian recorded a prosperous, peaceful realm with mild taxes.

4. Harshavardhana & The Kannauj Empire

Following Gupta decline, Harsha reunited North India from Kannauj (606–647 CE). Court biographer Banabhatta wrote *Harshacharita*, and pilgrim Xuanzang documented Harsha's religious assemblies.

5. The Chalukyas of Vatapi & Battle of Narmada

In the Deccan, Pulakeshin II defeated Harsha's southward expansion at the Narmada River, as celebrated in court poet Ravikirti's famous Aihole Inscription.

6. The Pallavas of Kanchipuram & Dravidian Architecture

In the Far South, rulers like Mahendravarman I and Narasimhavarman I (Mamalla) established great rock-cut monuments, the monolithic Rathas, and the Shore Temple at Mahabalipuram.

Chapter Terminology & Epigraphy Guide

Prashasti

A Sanskrit poetic eulogy or inscription composed by royal court scholars praising the genealogy, valor, and conquests of an emperor.

Maharajadhiraja

An imperial Sanskrit title meaning 'King of Kings' or 'Supreme Emperor', first adopted by Chandragupta I.

Samanta

A feudal landlord or military chieftain who provided soldiers, tribute, and military assistance to the sovereign emperor.

Harshacharita

The classical Sanskrit biography of King Harshavardhana composed by his celebrated court poet Banabhatta.

Aihole Inscription

A famous poetic eulogy inscribed at the Meguti Jain temple composed by Ravikirti praising Chalukya King Pulakeshin II.

Sabha

An assembly of Brahmin landholders in South Indian kingdoms that functioned through specialized sub-committees for irrigation, roads, and temples.

Ur

A village assembly found in southern districts where the landowners were not primarily Brahmins.

Nagaram

An organized administrative assembly or council of merchants and traders in ancient South Indian commercial towns.

Navaratnas

The legendary 'Nine Gems'—a circle of nine brilliant scholars, poets, and scientists patronized at the court of Chandragupta II Vikramaditya.

Nalanda Mahavihara

A globally renowned ancient Buddhist monastic university established in Bihar during the Gupta era.

Rock-Cut Architecture

The ancient architectural practice of excavating natural solid rock cliffs to construct temples, caves, and monasteries.

Classical Age

A historical epoch characterized by extraordinary peaks of artistic perfection, philosophical maturity, scientific advancement, and literature.

Master Hub Verified

✦ The Samanta System & Hereditary Bureaucracy

Gupta administration relied on feudatory military chiefs (*Samantas*) who provided soldiers during wartime. Key administrative posts became strictly hereditary (e.g., Harishena held multiple high offices).

✦ South Indian Village Democracy

The Pallava kingdom operated through distinct local councils: *Sabha* (assembly of Brahmin landholders), *Ur* (general non-Brahmin village assembly), and *Nagaram* (merchant guild committee).

✦ The Nalanda Mahavihara Intellectual Hub

The world's premier residential university in Bihar hosted over 10,000 scholars and 2,000 teachers from China, Korea, and Tibet, specializing in Buddhist philosophy, logic, medicine, and astronomy.

MCQ · #1 his8_mcq_001

Who captured nine kingdoms in Aryavarta and brought them under direct rule?

Explanation: Samudragupta directly annexed 9 kingdoms in Aryavarta. Chandragupta I founded the empire, while Chandragupta II conquered western Shaka territories.
MCQ · #2 his8_mcq_002

Which strategic trade city became Harshavardhana's new capital after Thaneshwar?

Explanation: Harsha shifted his capital to Kanauj to better control northern trade routes. Ujjain and Pataliputra were Gupta capitals.
MCQ · #3 his8_mcq_003

Which Chalukya ruler defeated Harshavardhana at the Narmada River?

Explanation: Pulakesin II defeated Harsha on the banks of the Narmada, stopping his southward march.
MCQ · #4 his8_mcq_004

What were governors of provinces (Bhuktis) called in the Gupta Empire?

Explanation: Provinces were called Bhuktis (ruled by Uparikas). Districts were Vishyas (ruled by Vishyapatis).
MCQ · #5 his8_mcq_005

Who authored the biography Harshacharita and the novel Kadambari?

Explanation: Court poet Banabhatta authored Harshacharita and Kadambari. Harisena wrote the Prayagraj Prashasti.
MCQ · #6 his8_mcq_006

Which Pallava ruler took the title 'Mamalla' and constructed Mamallapuram?

Explanation: Narasimhavarman I was nicknamed Mamalla ("Great Wrestler") and founded Mamallapuram.
MCQ · #7 his8_mcq_007

What was the assembly in non-Brahmana villages under the Pallava kingdom?

Explanation: Ur was for non-Brahmana villages, Sabha was for Brahmana villages, and Nagaram was for market towns.
MCQ · #8 his8_mcq_008

What was a 'Brahmadeya' land grant in the Pallava kingdom?

Explanation: Brahmadeya were special tax-free village grants given to Brahmanas who collected taxes from residents.
MCQ · #9 his8_mcq_009

Who were the 'Samantas' in the Gupta administration?

Explanation: Samantas were army chiefs who received land grants instead of cash salaries and commanded troops.
MCQ · #10 his8_mcq_010

Which Central Asian warrior tribe repeatedly attacked and ended the Gupta Empire?

Explanation: The Huns from Central Asia invaded repeatedly around 550 CE, destroying Gupta authority.
MCQ · #11 his8_mcq_011

Who composed the Aihole Prashasti describing the achievements of Pulakesin II?

Explanation: Court poet Ravikirti composed the Aihole Prashasti for Pulakesin II.
MCQ · #12 his8_mcq_012

What was the primary source of income for ancient empires in this period?

Explanation: Land tax (agricultural land, grazing land, wasteland) was the biggest source of royal income.
MCQ · #13 his8_mcq_013

Chandragupta II assumed the famous title ________.

Explanation: Students confuse grandfather and grandson! Chandragupta I and Samudragupta took Maharajadhiraja, but Chandragupta II specifically took Vikramaditya.
MCQ · #14 his8_mcq_014

The Prayagraj Prashasti in praise of Samudragupta was composed by ________.

Explanation: Both were famous court poets who wrote prashastis! Harisena wrote for Samudragupta at Prayagraj, whereas Ravikirti wrote the Aihole Prashasti for Pulakesin II.
MCQ · #15 his8_mcq_015

Chinese traveler Xuanzang visited India during the reign of ________.

Explanation: Two Chinese travelers visited during different eras! Xuanzang visited during Harsha's rule, whereas Fa Xian visited during the Gupta rule.
MCQ · #16 his8_mcq_016

District-level administrative units in the Gupta Empire were called ________.

Explanation: Bhuktis were larger provinces, while Vishyas were the smaller districts inside those provinces.
MCQ · #17 his8_mcq_017

Samudragupta allowed defeated rulers in South India to continue ruling in exchange for ________.

Explanation: Samudragupta directly annexed northern Aryavarta kings, but used indirect rule (annual tributes) for distant southern kings.
MCQ · #18 his8_mcq_018

In the Pallava kingdom, the village assembly for Brahmana villages was called ________.

Explanation: Sabha was for Brahmana landholders, while Ur was for general non-Brahmana villages.
MCQ · #19 his8_mcq_019

King Harshavardhana shifted his capital from Thaneshwar to ________.

Explanation: Pataliputra was the historic capital of the Guptas. Harsha shifted his capital to Kanauj.
MCQ · #20 his8_mcq_020

The Chalukya capital city Vatapi is known today as ________.

Explanation: Kanchipuram was the Pallava capital, while Badami was the Chalukya capital.
MCQ · #21 his8_mcq_ext_001

The famous Allahabad Pillar Inscription (Prayag Prashasti) eulogizing Samudragupta was composed by his court poet:

Explanation: Harishena, court poet and minister of Samudragupta, composed the Prayag Prashasti in classical Sanskrit poetry praising Samudragupta as a warrior king of hundred battles.
MCQ · #22 his8_mcq_ext_002

The biography 'Harshacharita' recounting the life and accession of King Harshavardhana was authored by:

Explanation: Banabhatta was Harshavardhana's celebrated court poet who wrote the biography Harshacharita in Sanskrit.
True or False · #1 his8_tf_001

Samudragupta defeated 12 kingdoms in the Deccan and South India.

Fact Check: TRUE. He defeated 12 kings in the south (Dakshinapatha) but reinstated them as tributary rulers.
True or False · #2 his8_tf_002

Chandragupta II took the title of Maharajadhiraja.

Fact Check: FALSE. Chandragupta II took the title Vikramaditya. Chandragupta I and Samudragupta were the ones who took Maharajadhiraja.
True or False · #3 his8_tf_003

The Narmada River was the southern boundary of Harsha's empire.

Fact Check: TRUE. Pulakesin II defeated Harsha at the Narmada River, making it the permanent southern border of Harsha's state.
True or False · #4 his8_tf_004

The Gupta Empire's biggest source of income was trade tax.

Fact Check: FALSE. The largest source of revenue was taxes collected on land (farming, grazing, forests).
True or False · #5 his8_tf_005

Pallava kings gave Brahmanas special tax-free land grants called Brahmadeya.

Fact Check: TRUE. Pallava kings granted tax-exempt villages to Brahmanas, who collected revenue from local inhabitants.
True or False · #6 his8_tf_006

Prashastis written by court poets give a completely balanced view of kings.

Fact Check: FALSE. Prashastis are biased eulogies praising only victories and virtues while omitting failures.

Short Answer · #5 his8_sa_ext_001

Why is the Gupta period frequently described as the 'Golden Age of Ancient India'?

1. **Literature**: Extraordinary poetic and dramatic masterpieces in Sanskrit by Kalidasa (e.g., *Abhijnanashakuntalam*, *Meghaduta*). 2. **Science & Mathematics**: Aryabhata formulated the heliocentric concept, calculated the value of Pi, explained eclipses, and introduced the zero decimal place-value system. 3. **Art & Metallurgy**: Superb bronze/stone sculptures of Buddha at Sarnath and the rust-free Iron Pillar of Mehrauli (Delhi) demonstrating advanced metallurgy.
Marking Rubric: Flourishing of classical Indian arts, literature, and empirical sciences under royal patronage.

Detailed Answer · #4 his8_la_ext_001

Discuss Harshavardhana's reign, his governance, religious patronage, and his military confrontation with Pulakeshin II.

• **Accession & Expansion**: Harshavardhana ascended the throne of Thanesar in 606 CE and united it with Kannauj after his brother and brother-in-law were killed. He conquered northern India across Punjab, Bengal, and Odisha. • **Deccan Campaign Defeat**: When Harsha attempted to march into the Deccan across the Narmada River, he was decisively repulsed by the Western Chalukya king **Pulakeshin II** (recorded in Ravikirti's Aihole inscription). • **Patronage & Chinese Pilgrims**: Harsha was an enlightened ruler and playwright who authored *Ratnavali*, *Priyadarsika*, and *Nagananda*. He warmly patronized the Chinese Buddhist pilgrim **Xuanzang (Hiuen Tsang)** and organized grand religious assemblies at Kannauj and Prayag.
