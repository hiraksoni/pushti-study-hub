NCERT Civics · Chapter 14

How Does Democracy Work?

Universal Adult Suffrage, The Election Commission of India, 8-Step Election Lifecycle, EVM/VVPAT & State Assembly

DemocracyElectionsECI & EVMVVPATMLAGovernance 15 Verified Qs

1. Fundamental Precepts of Democratic Governance

Democracy is government of the people, by the people, and for the people. Its bedrock is Universal Adult Suffrage: every citizen aged 18 years or older possesses exactly one vote of equal value, without discrimination.

2. The Autonomous Election Commission of India (ECI)

Enshrined in Article 324, the ECI is an independent constitutional body led by the Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) empowered to conduct free, fair, and transparent parliamentary and assembly elections.

3. The 8-Step Electoral Lifecycle

1. Delimitation of Constituencies | 2. Preparation of Electoral Roll | 3. Election Schedule Notification | 4. Filing of Nominations | 5. Campaigning & Manifestos | 6. Polling Day (Secret Ballot) | 7. Counting of Votes | 8. Government Formation.

4. Voting Technology: EVMs & VVPAT

Electronic Voting Machines (EVM) revolutionized Indian polling by eliminating paper ballot tampering. The Voter Verifiable Paper Audit Trail (VVPAT) prints a 7-second slip confirming candidate choice for physical verification.

5. Political Party Typologies & Systems

Single-Party (authoritarian rule like China), Bi-Party (two dominant alternating parties like USA/UK), and Multi-Party systems (multiple diverse parties competing, common in India, fostering inclusive coalition governments).

6. The Legislative Assembly & Role of MLA

Members of the Legislative Assembly (MLAs) represent local constituencies in the state Vidhan Sabha. They debate public grievances, question Ministers during Question Hour, pass state bills, and approve budgetary appropriations.

Chapter Terminology & Epigraphy Guide

Universal Adult Suffrage

The democratic principle granting every citizen aged 18 years and above the right to vote without any discrimination.

Constituency

A distinct geographical voting area whose eligible electorate chooses one representative to a legislative body.

Electoral Roll

The official public register of all eligible voters residing within each territorial constituency.

EPIC

Elector's Photo Identity Card; the official photographic identity card issued by the Election Commission of India to eligible voters.

Model Code of Conduct

A strict set of regulatory guidelines formulated to govern the conduct of political parties and candidates during elections.

Secret Ballot

A voting method in which a voter's choices are confidential and completely anonymous to eliminate intimidation or coercion.

EVM

Electronic Voting Machine; an electronic device used to securely record votes in Indian national and state elections.

VVPAT

Voter Verifiable Paper Audit Trail; an independent printer attached to EVMs that allows voters to verify their vote via a printed paper slip.

Ruling Party

The political party or alliance that wins the absolute majority of seats in an election and forms the government.

Coalition Government

A government formed by an alliance of multiple political parties when no single party secures an outright parliamentary majority.

Opposition Party

Elected legislators who do not belong to the ruling party; their role is to question policies, check power, and demand accountability.

MLA

Member of the Legislative Assembly; an elected representative chosen by voters of a constituency to sit in the state legislature (Vidhan Sabha).

Electoral Mechanics & Legislative Accountability

Master Hub Verified

✦ The Model Code of Conduct (MCC)

A set of legally binding norms enforced by the ECI prohibiting hate speech, use of official government machinery for campaigns, bribery of voters, and policy announcements once elections are notified.

✦ Coalition Governments vs Ruling Majority

When no single political party wins an absolute majority (50% + 1 seats in Lok Sabha or Vidhan Sabha), multiple parties form a post-poll coalition based on a Common Minimum Programme.

✦ Anatomy of an Assembly Debate

How an MLA tables a calling attention motion during Question Hour regarding clean water or hospital staff, compelling the concerned state Cabinet Minister to deliver an official on-the-record response.

MCQ · #2 civ14_mcq_002

Which of the following is NOT a role performed by the Election Commission of India?

Explanation: Campaigning is done by candidates and political parties, NOT the ECI.
MCQ · #3 civ14_mcq_003

Which political system exists in countries like North Korea, Cuba, and China?

Explanation: China, Cuba, and North Korea operate under a single-party political system.
MCQ · #4 civ14_mcq_004

In India, the country is divided into smaller geographical voting units called ________.

Explanation: Electoral voting divisions for Lok Sabha/Assembly are called constituencies.
MCQ · #5 civ14_mcq_005

The party that wins the highest number of ________ forms the government.

Explanation: Government formation depends on winning majority *seats*, not just total raw vote count.
MCQ · #6 civ14_mcq_ext_001

What is the minimum voting age for an Indian citizen under Universal Adult Franchise?

Explanation: Every Indian citizen aged 18 years or above has the constitutional right to vote in elections regardless of caste, religion, gender, literacy, or wealth.
MCQ · #7 civ14_mcq_ext_002

Who appoints the Chief Minister of an Indian State?

Explanation: The Governor appoints the leader of the majority party or coalition in the State Legislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha) as the Chief Minister.

What is the role and importance of the Opposition party in a democratic legislature?

1. **Accountability**: Questions the government on policy lapses, corruption, and administrative failures during legislative sessions. 2. **Public Voice**: Highlights citizens' grievances and alternative policy viewpoints through debates. 3. **Prevents Authoritarianism**: Ensures the ruling party does not misuse power or enact arbitrary laws.

Detailed Answer · #2 civ14_la_ext_001

Explain the step-by-step process of how a government is formed in an Indian state after assembly elections.

• **Constituency Delimitation & Voting**: The state is divided into electoral constituencies. Citizens elect one MLA from each constituency. • **Majority Threshold**: To form a government, a political party must secure more than half the total number of assembly seats (e.g., 35+ out of 68 seats). • **Ruling Party & Chief Minister**: The majority party members select their legislative leader. The Governor officially appoints this leader as the **Chief Minister**. • **Council of Ministers**: The Governor, on the advice of the Chief Minister, appoints other MLAs as ministers handling portfolios (Health, Education, Finance, Public Works). • **Coalition Government**: If no single party wins an absolute majority, two or more parties join together to form a **Coalition Government**.
Evaluation Criteria: Core constitutional mechanics of state executive and legislative governance.
