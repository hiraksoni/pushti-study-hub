D. Answer the following questions in brief:

1. Why do historians divide history into different periods?

To organise history into structured blocks based on significant changes in beliefs, social structures, and ruling powers, making it easier to study.

2. List any two differences between a kingdom and an empire.

1. An empire is much larger than a kingdom. 2. A kingdom is ruled by a king/queen, whereas an empire is ruled by an emperor/empress.

3. Describe the events that led to the rise of the Mauryan Empire.

Chanakya wanted to overthrow the unpopular Nanda king, Dhana Nanda. He trained Chandragupta Maurya in warfare, who then defeated Dhana Nanda and became ruler of Magadha in 321 BCE, establishing the Mauryan Empire.

4. Explain the term 'dynasty' using the example of the Mauryan Empire.

A dynasty is a family of rulers ruling one after another. In the Mauryan Empire, Chandragupta Maurya was succeeded by his son Bindusara, who was then succeeded by his son Ashoka.

5. 'Pataliputra, Taxila, and Ujjain were important cities in the Mauryan Empire.' Explain why.

Pataliputra was the capital city where the emperor and officials lived. Taxila and Ujjain were important cities on major trade routes, acting as vital centres of trade.

6. Who paid taxes in the Mauryan Empire? What were these taxes used for?

Farmers, herders, traders, and craftspeople paid taxes. These were used to pay for the administration of the empire and provide for the needs of the people.

E. Answer the following questions in detail:

1. What were some teachings of Ashoka's dhamma? Why did he introduce them?

Teachings included compassion, non-violence towards humans and animals, and not making sacrifices. He introduced them because he saw people arguing over religion, sacrificing animals, and mistreating servants, and wanted his subjects to follow a peaceful and moral way of life.

2. Explain how Ashoka spread his message of dhamma.

He appointed officials called 'dhamma mahamattas' to teach the people. He sent messengers to places like Syria, Egypt, Greece, and Sri Lanka. He also inscribed his teachings on rocks and pillars for people to read, known as Ashokan edicts.

3. 'The Mauryan Empire was so vast that different parts had to be ruled differently.' Explain how the Mauryan Empire was governed.

The emperor directly controlled the area around the capital, Pataliputra, with help from a mantrin parishad (council of ministers). The rest of the empire was divided into provinces with capitals like Taxila and Ujjain, ruled by royal princes as governors. Provinces were further divided into districts and villages managed by local officials.

4. Explain each of the following aspects of life in the Mauryan Empire:
(a) Role of shrenis
(b) Farming in the empire
(c) The varna system

(a) Shrenis were guilds for craftspeople that supported, protected, and solved problems for them.
(b) Farming was supported by the government by clearing forests and building water tanks and dams for irrigation.
(c) The varna system was strictly followed in society by most people who adhered to the Vedic religion.

5. Explain the reasons for the decline of the Mauryan Empire.

1. Weak rulers after Ashoka could not maintain unity. 2. The empire was huge and required vast resources that weak rulers couldn't manage. 3. External invasions from foreign groups like the Sakas weakened military strength.
