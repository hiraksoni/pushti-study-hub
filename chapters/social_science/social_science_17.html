NCERT Civics · Chapter 17

Markets Around Us

Retail Taxonomy (Weekly Markets, Neighbourhood Shops, Malls, E-Commerce), Supply Chains, Cooperatives & Mindful Buying

MarketsSupply ChainRetailCooperativesEconomicsMindful Buying 65 Verified Qs

1. The Fivefold Taxonomy of Retail Markets

Weekly Markets (temporary stalls, low overheads, bargaining), Neighbourhood Kiranas (daily convenience, credit, proximity), Shopping Malls (air-conditioned, branded goods, high overheads), Wholesale Mandis (bulk trading), and Quick/E-Commerce (app-based digital dark stores).

2. Economic Anatomy of Overhead Costs

A garment costing ₹300 to manufacture sells for ₹500 in a weekly market but ₹2,500 in a mall. Why? Malls pay astronomical commercial rent, electricity for AC, security, interior displays, and staff salaries.

3. The Chain of Markets & Price Escalation

Goods travel from primary producers (farmers/factory workers) through wholesale traders, regional distributors, and local shopkeepers before reaching consumers, accumulating markups at every step.

4. Structural Inequality in the Supply Chain

Primary producers endure the hardest physical toil and bear the risk of crop failures, yet receive the slimmest profit margin, while urban intermediaries and corporate retailers reap massive financial windfalls.

5. Producer Cooperatives: Countering Exploitation

Cooperatives pool the resources of small producers (e.g., weavers, dairy farmers) to eliminate predatory middlemen, negotiate directly with bulk buyers, and distribute profits democratically ('one member, one vote').

6. Price Determination & Mindful Buying

Market prices fluctuate through the law of supply and demand. High demand + low supply = price surges (e.g., tomato/onion shocks). Mindful buying encourages ethical, need-based consumerism over impulsive commercialism.

Chapter Terminology & Epigraphy Guide

Producer

A person, farmer, or enterprise that grows, manufactures, or creates goods and services for commercial consumption.

Consumer

An individual who purchases and utilizes goods or services for personal and household satisfaction.

Weekly Market

A traditional market held on a specific day of the week at a designated public location with non-permanent stalls.

Kirana Store

A permanent neighborhood general store offering everyday household groceries, personal care items, and informal credit.

Wholesale Market (Mandi)

A centralized market where commodities are bought and sold in massive bulk quantities between producers and merchants.

Retailer

A merchant who purchases goods in intermediate bulk from wholesalers and sells them in small quantities to consumers.

Chain of Markets

The series of connected intermediaries and commercial conduits linking the original producer of a product to the final end-user.

Middleman / Intermediary

An agent or wholesale trader in the distribution chain who facilitates transactions between producers and retailers.

Overhead Costs

The ongoing operational expenses of operating a permanent business, including commercial rent, electricity, wages, and advertising.

Cooperative Society

A voluntary business organization owned and democratically operated by its members to share resources and profits equally.

Law of Supply and Demand

An economic principle stating that high consumer demand with limited supply elevates prices, while surplus supply dampens prices.

Mindful Buying

The conscious practice of purchasing goods based on genuine utility, financial prudence, and ethical sustainability.

Master Hub Verified

✦ The Amul White Revolution Case Study

Founded in Anand, Gujarat in 1946 by Tribhuvandas Patel and Dr. Verghese Kurien, the cooperative linked millions of small dairy farmers directly to consumers, bypassing milk cartels and making India the world's top milk producer.

✦ The Quick Commerce & Dark Store Disruption

Modern 10-minute grocery apps operate through micro-warehouses ('dark stores') scattered across cities. While offering extreme speed, they exert fierce competitive pressure on local family-owned kirana stores.

✦ The Mindful Buying Triad

Before making any non-essential purchase, evaluate: 1. Do I genuinely need this item? | 2. Can I afford it without borrowing? | 3. Was this product created ethically and sustainably?

Detailed Answer · #2 civ17_la_007

Explain why the price of an item increases as it moves through the chain of markets.

As an item moves from the producer to the final consumer, it passes through various middlemen (wholesalers, distributors, retailers). Each middleman incurs overhead costs like transportation, storage, shop rent, and salaries. To cover these costs and earn a profit, each middleman adds a markup to the price of the item. As a result, the final price paid by the consumer is significantly higher than the price at which the producer sold it.
Evaluation Criteria: Comprehensive multi-point textbook model answer.
Detailed Answer · #3 civ17_la_008

Explain how inequalities in the chain of markets affect: (a) producers (b) consumers

(a) Producers: They often earn a very small share of the final selling price despite doing the most physically demanding work. Since they cannot sell directly to consumers in small quantities or store their goods for long, they are forced to sell to middlemen at low prices, keeping them in poverty. (b) Consumers: They are affected because middlemen can drive up prices by hoarding goods or adding high markups. As a result, consumers end up paying a high price, and sometimes lower-income consumers cannot afford necessities.

Detailed Answer · #4 civ17_la_009

How does a cooperative reduce inequality in the chain of markets? Explain with the help of an example.

Cooperatives reduce inequality by eliminating middlemen. When producers form a cooperative, they combine their resources to process, transport, and sell their goods directly in the market. Example: If a group of farmers forms a cooperative, they can pool their mango harvest, rent a shared truck, and sell directly to consumers or large buyers. By doing so, they keep the profit that would have otherwise gone to wholesalers and retailers, thereby earning a fair price for their labor.

Detailed Answer · #8 civ17_la_ext_001

Compare weekly market traders with shop owners in urban shopping complexes. How does this demonstrate economic inequality?

• **Capital Investment**: Weekly market vendors invest very small sums of money (often assisted by family members with no rent or permanent electricity bills). Mall shop owners invest huge capital in air-conditioned premises, branding, advertising, and staff salaries. • **Pricing & Profits**: Weekly market sellers sell unbranded goods at cheap, negotiable rates with modest profit margins. Mall outlets sell expensive branded luxury products with high markups. • **Customer Base**: Weekly markets serve ordinary workers, daily-wage laborers, and rural households looking for budget items. Shopping malls cater primarily to affluent urban upper-middle-class consumers. • **Inequality**: Both participate in markets, but their financial gains, risk resilience, and social leverage are vastly unequal.
Evaluation Criteria: Critical comparative analysis required by CBSE curriculum.
Detailed Answer · #9 civ17_claude_la_001

Examine how cooperative societies operate, explaining why they serve as a powerful economic tool against middleman exploitation for small farmers and artisans.

1. **Problem of Middlemen**: Isolated small farmers or weavers possess negligible bargaining power, lack cold storage, and are forced to sell immediately at exploitative rates to local money-lending traders. 2. **Pooling Resources**: By forming a cooperative, small producers combine their agricultural crops or woven textiles into significant bulk quantities, commanding favorable wholesale prices. 3. **Direct Market Access**: Cooperatives sell directly to government agencies, wholesale processing factories, or consumer retail outlets, bypassing private intermediaries and securing maximum revenue for members. 4. **Democratic Parity**: Managed on the 'One Member, One Vote' principle, ensuring equitable distribution of profits and democratic representation. 5. **Government Assistance**: State governments support cooperatives with subsidized fertilizers, low-interest working capital loans, and warehousing infrastructure.
