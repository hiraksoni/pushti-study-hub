1. Cosmic Origin of Earth: The Nebular Hypothesis

  • The Solar Nebula: Over 4.6 billion years ago, a massive rotating cloud of hot interstellar gas and cosmic dust called a nebula collapsed under its own gravitational attraction to form the Sun and the protoplanets of our solar system.
  • Gravitational Density Differentiation: During Earth's initial molten state, dense metallic elements—primarily Iron (Fe) and Nickel (Ni)—sank toward the gravitational center to forge the metallic core. Simultaneously, lighter silicate minerals floated upward to crystallize into the solid rocky crust.
  • Subterranean Thermal Gradient: Temperature increases progressively from the surface downward:
    • Crust: 0°C at surface to ~700°C at its base.
    • Mantle: Ranging from 870°C in the upper asthenosphere to 4,400°C near the core boundary.
    • Core: Reaches 4,400°C in the liquid outer core up to a scorching 6,000°C in the solid inner core (comparable to the Sun's surface).

2. Concentric Spherical Layers: The "Onion" Analogy

Just like an onion consists of multiple concentric nested layers, Earth is structured in three primary physical shells:

1. The Crust (Skin)

The outermost, thinnest solid rocky shell. Accounts for less than 1% of Earth's planetary volume. Divided into Continental Crust (~35 km) and Oceanic Crust (~5 km).

2. The Mantle (Body)

The thickest intermediate layer extending to a depth of 2,900 km. Accounts for 84% of planetary volume. Contains the plastic, semi-molten Asthenosphere that drives plate tectonics.

3. The Core (Center)

The central metallic sphere with a radius of 3,500 km (comprising 15% volume). Divided into a liquid Outer Core (2,300 km) and a solid Inner Core (1,200 km).

4. Petrological Classification: The Three Rock Families

1. Igneous Rocks (Primary)

Formed directly from cooling and solidification of molten magma or lava. All other rocks derive from them.
• Intrusive (Plutonic): Magma cools slowly deep inside crust; coarse, large crystals (e.g., Granite, Diorite, Gabbro).
• Extrusive (Volcanic): Lava pours onto surface, cooling rapidly; fine-grained or glassy (e.g., Basalt, Andesite, Obsidian, Pumice).

2. Sedimentary Rocks (Secondary)

Formed from mechanical weathering, transport, and stratification of rock fragments (lithification).
• Deposited in distinct horizontal layers (strata).
• Only rock family containing Fossils—remains of prehistoric flora and fauna.
• Examples: Sandstone, Limestone, Shale, Conglomerate, Coal.

3. Metamorphic Rocks (Tertiary)

Formed when pre-existing igneous or sedimentary rocks undergo profound recrystallization under extreme subterranean heat and pressure without melting.
• Limestone → Marble
• Shale / Clay → Slate
• Granite → Gneiss
• Sandstone → Quartzite

7. Master Geological Terminology Glossary

Term Authoritative Definition & Exam Context
Crust The thin, solid outermost layer of Earth, averaging 5 km under oceans (SIMA) and 35 km under continents (SIAL).
Mantle The intermediate 2,900 km thick layer comprising 84% of planetary volume, containing the convective semi-fluid asthenosphere.
Core (NIFE) The dense metallic center of Earth (3,500 km radius) composed of Nickel (Ni) and Iron (Fe), split into liquid outer and solid inner core.
SIAL The continental crust rich in Silica (Si) and Aluminium (Al), with an average density of ~2.7 g/cm³.
SIMA The oceanic crust rich in Silica (Si) and Magnesium (Ma), with an average density of ~3.0 g/cm³.
Magma vs. Lava Magma is molten rock under high pressure inside the crust; Lava is molten rock that breaches the surface via volcanic vents.
Igneous Rocks Primary crystalline rocks formed directly from cooling magma/lava (Intrusive Granite vs Extrusive Basalt).
Sedimentary Rocks Secondary layered rocks formed through deposition, compaction, and lithification of mineral and organic fragments; contains fossils.
Metamorphic Rocks Tertiary altered rocks created when existing rocks undergo structural change under severe subterranean heat and pressure (e.g. Marble).
Fossils The preserved petrified remains, imprints, or traces of ancient animals and plants locked exclusively inside sedimentary strata.
Rock Cycle The continuous, non-linear geological process through which rocks are continually transformed between igneous, sedimentary, and metamorphic forms.
Nebular Hypothesis The scientific cosmological model describing how Earth and the solar system condensed from a swirling interstellar nebula 4.6 billion years ago.
