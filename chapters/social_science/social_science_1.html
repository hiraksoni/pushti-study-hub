E. Answer the following questions in brief

1. List any three features of the mantle.
1. It is the thickest layer of the earth (about 2,900 km).
2. It is divided into the upper mantle and the lower mantle.
3. It is made up of semi-liquid or molten rocks containing silicon, iron, and magnesium.
2. Why is the inner core solid?
The inner core is solid because of high pressure, even though its temperature is as high as 6000°C.
3. What are rocks? List three uses of rocks.
Rocks are naturally occurring substances that form a part of the earth's outermost layer. Uses: making kitchen countertops and floors (granite), making pavements, statues, and monuments (basalt), and making cement, tiles, bricks, and pottery (sedimentary rocks).
4. Why are igneous rocks called primary rocks?
Igneous rocks are also called primary rocks since these were the first rocks to be formed. All other types of rocks are directly or indirectly formed from igneous rocks.
5. What are minerals?
Minerals are naturally occurring substances that contain one or more elements. Different minerals have different chemical and physical properties.

F. Answer the following questions in detail

1. Differentiate between the following:
(a) The inner core and the outer core
(b) Intrusive igneous rocks and extrusive igneous rocks
(a) The outer core is the liquid part of the core, about 2,300 km thick, made up of nickel and iron. The inner core is the solid part, about 1,200 km thick, and is solid due to high pressure.

(b) Intrusive igneous rocks form when magma cools and solidifies inside the earth's crust slowly, developing large crystals (e.g., Granite). Extrusive igneous rocks form when magma pours out as lava and solidifies on the earth's surface quickly, having a grainy texture with small crystals (e.g., Basalt).
2. Describe the structure of the earth.
The structure of the earth consists of three layers:
1. Crust: The outermost and thinnest layer made of solid rocks (continental and oceanic).
2. Mantle: The second and thickest layer made up of semi-liquid rocks.
3. Core: The innermost layer divided into a liquid outer core and a solid inner core made primarily of nickel and iron (nife).
3. Explain the formation of igneous rocks.
Due to high heat, rocks melt inside the earth and mix with gases to form magma. During volcanic eruptions, magma is pushed towards the surface. The magma that flows onto the surface is called lava. On the surface or sometimes within the crust, this molten material cools down, solidifies, and forms igneous rocks.
4. Explain the rock cycle with the help of a labelled diagram.
The rock cycle is the continuous process in which rocks transform from one type to another. Magma cools to form igneous rocks. These break down into sediments which compress to form sedimentary rocks. Under extreme heat and pressure, igneous and sedimentary rocks change into metamorphic rocks. Metamorphic rocks can melt back into magma, continuing the cycle.
5. Define the different types of minerals.
Minerals are classified into two types:
1. Metallic minerals: Minerals that contain metals (e.g., iron, copper, tin, manganese).
2. Non-metallic minerals: Minerals that do not contain metals (e.g., potash, gypsum, limestone, mica).
