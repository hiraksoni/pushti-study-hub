Expressions using Letter-Numbers

Variables, algebraic expressions, terms, coefficients, arithmetic properties in algebra, and mathematical modeling of patterns.

What is a Letter-Number (Variable)?

In mathematics, a letter-number (commonly called a variable) is a letter such as $x, y, n, a, b$ used to represent an unknown quantity or a number that can take different values in different situations.

For example, if Nakul's age is 5 years more than Ankit's age, and Ankit's age is $n$ years, then Nakul's age can be neatly written as: $$a = n + 5$$ Here, $n$ can be 10, 12, or 25; for any value of $n$, Nakul's age is automatically determined.

Constant vs Variable
• Constant: A fixed numerical value that never changes (e.g. $5, -12, 0, \frac{3}{4}$).
• Variable (Letter-Number): A letter representing numbers that can vary (e.g. $x, t, w$).
Algebraic Expression
A mathematical phrase composed of numbers, letter-numbers, and operational symbols ($+, -, \times, \div$).
Examples: $2x + 3$, $4y - 7$, $12ab + 5$.

Anatomy of an Algebraic Term

An algebraic expression is made up of terms separated by plus ($+$) or minus ($-$) signs.

Consider the expression: $$4x^2 - 3xy + 7$$ • The terms are: $4x^2$, $-3xy$, and $7$.

Factors of a Term
A term can be written as a product of its factors:
• In term $24x$: Factors are $2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times x$.
• Factors containing variables are called algebraic factors ($x, y$).
• Factors that are numbers are called numerical factors ($24$).
Coefficients
The coefficient is usually the numerical factor of a term:
• In $7x$, the coefficient of $x$ is $7$.
• In $-5y$, the coefficient of $y$ is $-5$.
• In $24ay$, the numerical coefficient is $24$, while the coefficient of $y$ is $24a$.

Extending Arithmetic Rules to Letter-Numbers

All fundamental laws of arithmetic apply directly to algebraic expressions because letter-numbers simply represent numbers.

1. Commutative Property of Addition
Changing the order of terms does not change their sum:
$$\mathbf{x + y = y + x}$$ Example: $2x + 3y = 3y + 2x$.
2. Associative Property of Addition
Grouping terms does not change the result:
$$\mathbf{(x + y) + z = x + (y + z)}$$ Example: $(3a + 2b) + 5c = 3a + (2b + 5c)$.
3. Distributive Property
Multiplication distributes over addition and subtraction:
$$\mathbf{a(b + c) = ab + ac}$$ $$\mathbf{a(b - c) = ab - ac}$$ Example: $4(x + 6) = 4x + 24$.
4. Brackets with a Minus Sign
A minus sign outside reverses every sign inside:
$$\mathbf{-(a + b) = -a - b}$$ $$\mathbf{-(a - b) = -a + b}$$ Example: $9a - (3a + 4) = 9a - 3a - 4 = 6a - 4$.
Omission of the Multiplication Symbol ('$\times$')

In algebra, the multiplication symbol '$\times$' is intentionally omitted between numbers and variables, and between two variables:

• $7 \times k$ is written as $\mathbf{7k}$.
• $6 \times a + b$ is written as $\mathbf{6a + b}$.
• $x \times y$ is written as $\mathbf{xy}$.
Why? Because the symbol '$\times$' looks identical to the letter '$x$', causing severe confusion in written mathematics.

What are Like and Unlike Terms?

• Like Terms: Terms that have the exact same algebraic factors (same variables with same powers). Their numerical coefficients can be different.
Examples: $3x$ and $-7x$; $4ab$ and $9ba$; $5x^2y$ and $-2x^2y$.

• Unlike Terms: Terms that have different variables or different powers of the same variable.
Examples: $3x$ and $3y$; $5x^2$ and $5x$; $4ab$ and $4a^2b$.

The Golden Rule of Algebraic Addition & Subtraction

Only LIKE terms can be added or subtracted together. Unlike terms can NEVER be combined!

When combining like terms, simply add or subtract their numerical coefficients and keep the algebraic factor unchanged:

$$5x + 3x = (5 + 3)x = \mathbf{8x}$$ $$9ab - 4ab = (9 - 4)ab = \mathbf{5ab}$$ $$3x + 4y = \mathbf{3x + 4y} \quad \text{(cannot be simplified further)}$$
Horizontal Method
Write expressions side-by-side, group like terms using brackets, and combine:
$$\begin{aligned} &(3x + 4y) + (5x - 2y) \\ &= (3x + 5x) + (4y - 2y) \\ &= \mathbf{8x + 2y} \end{aligned}$$
Column Method
Write expressions in rows with like terms placed directly under each other:
$$\begin{array}{rr} 3x & + 4y \\ +\, 5x & - 2y \\ \hline \mathbf{8x} & \mathbf{+\, 2y} \end{array}$$
Pitfall 1: Partial Distribution Trap

Wrong: $5(2a + 3) = 10a + 3$  

Why it's wrong: The outside multiplier was only multiplied with the first term, leaving the second term untouched.

Correct: Multiply 5 by every term inside: $5(2a) + 5(3) = \mathbf{10a + 15}$  

Pitfall 2: Minus Sign Outside Bracket (The Classic Trap)

Wrong: $x - (2y - x) = x - 2y - x = -2y$  

Why it's wrong: When removing a bracket preceded by a minus sign, all signs inside must be flipped (negative times negative is positive!).

Correct: $x - 2y - (-x) = x - 2y + x = \mathbf{2x - 2y}$  

Pitfall 3: Combining Unlike Terms

Wrong: $3x + 2y = 5xy$  

Why it's wrong: 3 apples plus 2 oranges cannot become 5 "apple-oranges"! Since $x$ and $y$ have different variable bases, they cannot be added into a single term.

Correct: $3x + 2y$ remains $\mathbf{3x + 2y}$.

Pitfall 4: Negative Substitution Error

If $a = 2$ and $b = -3$, find the value of $2a - 3b$:

Wrong: $2(2) - (3 \times (-3)) = 4 - 9 = -5$  

Correct: $2(2) - 3(-3) = 4 - (-9) = 4 + 9 = \mathbf{13}$

Mental Math Example 7

Grid Grouping and Faster Simplification

Status:
Combine the terms from the two rows: Row 1: $[12, -5x, 3x, 4y]$ and Row 2: $[12, 7y, 5x, 6x]$. Try grouping terms in different ways to check if the result is identical. Which way is fastest?

Method 1: Row-by-Row Sum: Row 1: $12 + (-5x + 3x) + 4y = 12 - 2x + 4y$
Row 2: $12 + 7y + (5x + 6x) = 12 + 11x + 7y$
Total Sum: $(12 + 12) + (-2x + 11x) + (4y + 7y) = \mathbf{24 + 9x + 11y}$

Terms in $y$: $4y + 7y = 11y$

Conclusion: Both methods yield identical results. Direct like-term grouping is significantly faster because additive inverses like $-5x$ and $+5x$ eliminate each other immediately.

1. Core Definitions
• Letter-Number (Variable): A symbol ($x, y$) that can take multiple values.
• Constant: A fixed numerical value ($7, -5$).
• Term: A product of factors separated by $+$ or $-$.
• Numerical Coefficient: The number multiplied with variables.
2. Arithmetic Laws
• Commutative: $x + y = y + x$
• Associative: $(x + y) + z = x + (y + z)$
• Distributive: $a(b + c) = ab + ac$
• Distributive: $a(b - c) = ab - ac$
3. Bracket Removal Rules
• $+(a + b) = a + b$
• $-(a + b) = -a - b$
• $-(a - b) = -a + b$
• $m(a - b) = ma - mb$
4. Terms Combination Rules
• Like Terms: Same variables and powers (e.g. $4x, -9x$). Combine coefficients: $4x - 9x = -5x$.
• Unlike Terms: Different variables or powers (e.g. $4x, 4y$). CANNOT be combined: $4x + 4y$ stays as is!
5. Grid Patterns
• $2 \times 2$ Calendar: Diagonal sums are equal: $a + (a + 8) = (a + 1) + (a + 7) = 2a + 8$.
• $3 \times 3$ Calendar: Sum of all 9 numbers is $9 \times \text{Centre Date}$.
• Plus Shape (5 dates): Sum is $5 \times \text{Centre Date}$.
6. Matchstick Chains
• Squares in a row: $\mathbf{3n + 1}$
• Triangles in a row: $\mathbf{2n + 1}$
• Hexagons in a row: $\mathbf{5n + 1}$

Nested Brackets Simplification: (), {}, []

Simplify the algebraic expression by removing grouping symbols from the inside out:
$$4x - [3y - \{2x - (y - 2x)\}]$$
Step 1: Innermost Parentheses $( )$

$$\begin{aligned} &4x - [4y - 4x] \\ &= 4x - 4y + 4x = \mathbf{8x - 4y} \end{aligned}$$

Algebraic Proof: Sum of three consecutive odd numbers

Write an algebraic expression representing three consecutive odd integers. Prove that their sum is always divisible by 3.
Part 1: Algebraic Modeling
Let the first odd integer be $2n + 1$ (where $n$ is any integer).
Next two odd integers: $(2n + 3)$ and $(2n + 5)$.
Part 2: Sum & Factoring
Sum $= (2n + 1) + (2n + 3) + (2n + 5) = 6n + 9$
Factoring out 3: $$\mathbf{6n + 9 = 3(2n + 3)}$$
Since $2n+3$ is an integer, the sum is always a multiple of 3. $\mathbf{Q.E.D.}$
Answer: Sum $= 6n + 9 = 3(2n + 3)$, which is unconditionally divisible by $3$.
HOTS • Level 3 Q24

Real-World Motion Modeling: Track Runner

A runner jogs along a circular track at a speed of $2x$ meters per second for 3 seconds to complete one quarter of a lap.
