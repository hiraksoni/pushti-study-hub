Hierarchy

Visual Concept Map

1. Intersecting Lines

Two lines crossing at exactly one common vertex point $O$.
• Vertically Opposite Angles: Equal ($\angle 1 = \angle 3, \angle 2 = \angle 4$).
• Linear Pairs: Adjacent angles adding to $180^\circ$.

2. Parallel Lines ($l \parallel m$)

Coplanar lines that never meet, no matter how far extended.
• Perpendicular distance $d$ remains constant everywhere.
• Notated with arrowheads ($>$ or $>>$).

3. Perpendicular Lines ($l \perp m$)

Two lines intersecting at a right angle ($90^\circ$).
• All four angles around the intersection are $90^\circ$.
• Symbol: $\perp$ (Right-angle corner square $\llcorner$).

4. Transversal Systems

A line cutting two or more lines at distinct points.
• Corresponding Angles ($F$-Rule): Equal.
• Alternate Interior ($Z$-Rule): Equal.
• Co-Interior ($C$-Rule): Supplementary ($180^\circ$).

Core Principles

The Four Fundamental Geometric Theorems

Theorem 1: Vertically Opposite Angles

If two straight lines intersect, vertically opposite angles are always equal.

$$\angle 1 + \angle 2 = 180^\circ,\; \angle 2 + \angle 3 = 180^\circ \implies \angle 1 = \angle 3$$
Theorem 2: Linear Pair Axiom

If a ray stands on a straight line, the sum of two adjacent angles formed is $180^\circ$.

$$\angle AOC + \angle BOC = 180^\circ$$
Theorem 3: Parallel Transversal Alternation

If two parallel lines are cut by a transversal, alternate interior angles are equal.

$$l \parallel m \implies \angle 3 = \angle 5,\quad \angle 4 = \angle 6$$
Theorem 4: Co-Interior Supplementation

Interior angles on the same side of a transversal cutting parallel lines sum to $180^\circ$.

$$l \parallel m \implies \angle 4 + \angle 5 = 180^\circ$$

Geometry Core

Vertically Opposite Angles (Theorem & Proof)

When two straight lines $AB$ and $CD$ intersect at point $O$, four angles are formed. Non-adjacent angles facing each other are called vertically opposite angles.

Figure 5.1: Textbook Intersecting Lines: $\angle 1 = \angle 3$ and $\angle 2 = \angle 4$
Mathematical Proof

1. Since $CD$ is a straight line and ray $OA$ stands on it: $\angle 1 + \angle 4 = 180^\circ$ (Linear pair).
2. Since $AB$ is a straight line and ray $OD$ stands on it: $\angle 1 + \angle 2 = 180^\circ$ (Linear pair).
3. Equating both sums: $\angle 1 + \angle 4 = \angle 1 + \angle 2 \implies \mathbf{\angle 4 = \angle 2}$.
Similarly, $\mathbf{\angle 1 = \angle 3}$.

Linear Pair Axiom

Linear Pairs of Angles

A linear pair is a pair of adjacent angles whose non-common arms form opposite rays (a straight line). The sum of angles in a linear pair is strictly equal to $180^\circ$.

Figure 5.2: Linear Pair on Straight Line $AOB$: $\angle 1 + \angle 2 = 180^\circ$
Angle Pairs

Complementary & Supplementary Angles

Complementary Angles

Two angles are complementary if their sum is exactly $90^\circ$. Each angle is called the complement of the other.

$$\angle A + \angle B = 90^\circ \implies \text{Complement of } x = 90^\circ - x$$

Example: Complement of $35^\circ = 90^\circ - 35^\circ = 55^\circ$.

Supplementary Angles

Two angles are supplementary if their sum is exactly $180^\circ$. Each angle is called the supplement of the other.

$$\angle A + \angle B = 180^\circ \implies \text{Supplement of } x = 180^\circ - x$$

Example: Supplement of $110^\circ = 180^\circ - 110^\circ = 70^\circ$.

Linear Pair Equation on Line AOB

Problem: In the given figure, $AOB$ is a straight line. Find (a) $\angle AOC$, (b) $\angle BOC$.

Figure 5.4: Textbook Illustration 3: Linear pair on straight line $AOB$
View Step-by-Step Textbook Solution

✓ Final Answer: (a) $\angle AOC = 75^\circ$, (b) $\angle BOC = 105^\circ$. Check: $75^\circ + 105^\circ = 180^\circ$.

Three Straight Lines Intersecting at O

Problem: In the given figure, $AB$, $CD$, and $EF$ are three straight lines intersecting each other at a point $O$. If $\angle COE = 45^\circ$ and $\angle AOF = 90^\circ$, find $\angle BOD$.

Figure 5.6: Textbook Illustration 5: Three lines intersecting at $O$
View Step-by-Step Textbook Solution
1. Since $\angle COE$ and $\angle DOF$ are vertically opposite angles formed by lines $CD$ and $EF$:
$$\angle DOF = \angle COE = 45^\circ$$
2. Now, $AB$ is a straight line. The sum of angles on straight line $AB$ on one side equals $180^\circ$:
$$\begin{aligned} \angle AOF + \angle DOF + \angle BOD &= 180^\circ \\ 90^\circ + 45^\circ + x &= 180^\circ \\ 135^\circ + x &= 180^\circ \\ x &= 180^\circ - 135^\circ = \mathbf{45^\circ} \end{aligned}$$
✓ Final Answer: $\angle BOD = 45^\circ$.
Concept

Parallel Lines ($l \parallel m$) & Equidistance

Two lines in the same plane are parallel if they never intersect, regardless of how far they are extended in both directions. The perpendicular distance $d$ between them is constant everywhere.

Figure 5.7: Parallel Lines: Perpendicular distance $d$ remains constant ($l \parallel m$)
Concept

Perpendicular Lines ($l \perp m$)

Two lines are perpendicular when they intersect at right angles ($90^\circ$). If two lines are perpendicular, all four angles formed at the intersection are equal to $90^\circ$.

Figure 5.8: Perpendicular Lines ($l \perp m$): Right-angle intersection ($90^\circ$)
Hands-on Lab

Constructions & Paper Folding

Ruler and Set Square Method

To draw a line parallel to line $l$ passing through an external point $A$:

  1. Align one edge of a set square along line $l$.
  2. Place a ruler firmly along another edge of the set square.
  3. Slide the set square along the ruler until its edge touches point $A$.
  4. Draw line $m$ along the edge through $A$. Line $m \parallel l$.
Paper Folding Geometry

Fold a sheet in half to create crease 1. Fold perpendicular to crease 1 to create crease 2. Fold crease 2 onto itself to create crease 3. Crease 1 and Crease 3 are parallel!

Key Rule: Two lines perpendicular to the same line are parallel to each other.

Parallel Sets System: AE || GF || BD

Problem: In the given figure, $AE \parallel GF \parallel BD$, $AB \parallel CG \parallel DF$ and $\angle CHE = 120^\circ$. Find $\angle ABC$ and $\angle CDE$.

Figure 5.9: Textbook Illustration 10: $AE \parallel GF \parallel BD$ and $AB \parallel CG \parallel DF$
View Textbook Solution

$$\angle HCB = \angle CHE = 120^\circ \quad \text{(Alternate interior angles)}$$
2. Since $CH \parallel DF$ and $CD$ is a transversal:
$$\angle CDE = \angle HCB = \mathbf{120^\circ} \quad \text{(Corresponding angles)}$$
3. Since $AB \parallel DF$ and $BD$ is a transversal:
$$\begin{aligned} \angle ABC + \angle CDE &= 180^\circ \quad \text{(Co-interior angles)} \\ \angle ABC + 120^\circ &= 180^\circ \implies \mathbf{\angle ABC = 60^\circ} \end{aligned}$$

Master System

Transversals Cutting Parallel Lines ($l \parallel m$)

A transversal is a line that intersects two or more lines in a plane at distinct points. When a transversal $t$ cuts two parallel lines $l$ and $m$, exactly eight angles are formed.

Figure 5.11: Master Transversal System: Transversal $t$ cutting parallel lines $l \parallel m$
Angle Rule

Corresponding Angles (The F-Rule)

Angles in matching positions relative to the transversal and parallel lines are corresponding angles. When lines are parallel, corresponding angles are strictly equal.

Four Corresponding Pairs
  • $\angle 1 = \angle 5$ (Top-Left)
  • $\angle 2 = \angle 6$ (Top-Right)
  • $\angle 3 = \angle 7$ (Bottom-Left)
  • $\angle 4 = \angle 8$ (Bottom-Right)
The Visual F-Shape

Look for the letter F (can be upright, inverted, or backward). The two arms of the F lie along the parallel lines, and the angles in the arm corners are equal!

Angle Rule

Alternate Angles (The Z-Rule)

Angles on opposite (alternate) sides of the transversal between the two parallel lines are alternate interior angles. When lines are parallel, alternate interior angles are strictly equal.

Alternate Interior Pairs
  • $\mathbf{\angle 3 = \angle 5}$ (Z-corner pair)
  • $\mathbf{\angle 4 = \angle 6}$ (Inverted Z-corner pair)
Alternate Exterior Pairs
  • $\mathbf{\angle 1 = \angle 7}$
  • $\mathbf{\angle 2 = \angle 8}$

Zig-Zag Angle: AB || CD with BMD = 64°

Problem: In the given figure, $AB \parallel CD$, $\angle CDM = 150^\circ$, $\angle BMD = 64^\circ$ and $\angle ABM = x$. Find the value of $x$.

Figure 5.12: Textbook Illustration 12: Auxiliary parallel construction $EMF \parallel CD \parallel AB$
View Step-by-Step Textbook Solution
1. Through $M$, draw auxiliary line $EMF \parallel CD \parallel AB$.
2. Let $\angle EMB = y$. Then $\angle EMD = 64^\circ - y$.
3. Since $EF \parallel CD$ and $MD$ is a transversal, co-interior angles sum to $180^\circ$:
$$\begin{aligned} 150^\circ + (64^\circ - y) &= 180^\circ \\ 214^\circ - y &= 180^\circ \implies y = 214^\circ - 180^\circ = 34^\circ \end{aligned}$$
4. Since $AB \parallel EF$ and $BM$ is a transversal, co-interior angles sum to $180^\circ$:
$$\begin{aligned} x + y &= 180^\circ \\ x + 34^\circ &= 180^\circ \implies x = 180^\circ - 34^\circ = \mathbf{146^\circ} \end{aligned}$$
✓ Final Answer: $x = 146^\circ$.

Parallel Proof: Show that AB || EF

Problem: In the given figure, show that $AB \parallel EF$.

Figure 5.13: Textbook Illustration 15: Proving $AB \parallel EF$ via transversal $CE$
View Proof
1. $\angle BCD = 30^\circ + 40^\circ = 70^\circ$.

Question 2(ii): Two Horizontal Parallel Lines Crossed by Two Parallel Transversals

In the textbook figure below, two horizontal parallel lines ($\rightarrow$) are intersected by two slanted transversal lines marked with double arrows ($\twoheadrightarrow$), indicating that the transversals are also parallel to each other. Given angle $62^\circ$, find angle $a^\circ$.

Textbook Figure (p. 123 Q2 ii): Two horizontal parallel lines intersected by two parallel transversals with double arrows.

Step-by-Step Derivation:

  1. Let the top and bottom horizontal lines be $l_1 \parallel l_2$, and the two slanted lines be $t_1 \parallel t_2$.
  2. At line $t_2$ and $l_1$, the angle given is $62^\circ$ in the interior region.
  3. Because $t_1 \parallel t_2$, the corresponding angle at line $t_1$ and $l_1$ is also $62^\circ$.
  4. Because $l_1 \parallel l_2$, the alternate interior angle at line $t_1$ and $l_2$ is $62^\circ$.
  5. Angle $a^\circ$ is vertically opposite to this $62^\circ$ interior angle. Therefore:
$$\mathbf{a = 62^\circ}$$

8.2 Assertion & Reasoning (Textbook p. 123)

Evaluate mathematical rigor through two-statement logic prompts directly from the textbook.

Textbook p. 123: Item 1

Assertion (A): If two lines intersect at a point, then the vertically opposite angles are always equal.

Reason (R): The sum of angles in a linear pair is $180^\circ$, and subtracting the common adjacent angle from $180^\circ$ proves the opposite angles are equal.

Evaluation: Both (A) and (R) are true, and (R) is the correct mathematical explanation of (A).

Proof: Let lines $AB$ and $CD$ intersect at $O$. $\angle 1 + \angle 2 = 180^\circ$ (linear pair on $CD$), and $\angle 2 + \angle 3 = 180^\circ$ (linear pair on $AB$). Equating both: $\angle 1 + \angle 2 = \angle 2 + \angle 3 \implies \angle 1 = \angle 3$.

Textbook p. 123: Item 2

Assertion (A): The complement of $35^\circ$ is $55^\circ$.

Reason (R): If two angles are complementary, then their sum is $90^\circ$.

Evaluation: Both (A) and (R) are true, and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).

Calculation: $35^\circ + 55^\circ = 90^\circ$. Since Reason gives the exact definition of complementary angles, (R) correctly explains (A).

16. Two obtuse angles form a pair of complementary angles. FALSE

An obtuse angle is $> 90^\circ$. Two obtuse angles sum to $> 180^\circ$, which can never equal $90^\circ$.

Theorem: When two lines intersect, the vertically opposite angles formed are always equal.

Two right angles ($90^\circ + 90^\circ = 180^\circ$) are supplementary and neither is obtuse.

The sum of two acute angles is strictly $< 180^\circ$, so they can never form a supplementary pair.

Linear pairs must be adjacent sharing a common ray; vertically opposite angles share only a vertex, not a common arm.

HOTS 2

Sum of $\angle a$ and $\angle b$ when $\angle a = \angle b$ and $\angle c = \angle d$

Look at the figure given alongside. What can you conclude about the sum of $\angle a$ and $\angle b$ if $\angle a = \angle b$ and $\angle c = \angle d$? Justify your answer.

View Solution & Justification

Angles on a straight line sum to $180^\circ$:

$$\angle a + \angle b + \angle c + \angle d = 180^\circ$$

Since $\angle a = \angle b$ and $\angle c = \angle d$:

$$2\angle a + 2\angle c = 180^\circ \implies 2(\angle a + \angle c) = 180^\circ \implies \mathbf{\angle a + \angle c = 90^\circ}$$

Also, since $\angle a = \angle b$, $\mathbf{\angle a + \angle b = 2\angle a}$. If $\angle a = \angle c = 45^\circ$, then $\angle a + \angle b = 90^\circ$ (perpendicular normal bisector, matching optical reflection).

HOTS 3

Two angles supplementary to the same angle

If two angles are supplementary to the same angle, how are the measures of the two angles related?

View Solution & Congruence Proof

Let the two angles be $\angle 1$ and $\angle 2$, and let the third common angle be $\angle 3$.

$$\angle 1 + \angle 3 = 180^\circ \implies \angle 1 = 180^\circ - \angle 3$$ $$\angle 2 + \angle 3 = 180^\circ \implies \angle 2 = 180^\circ - \angle 3$$

Since both right-hand sides are identical:

$$\mathbf{\angle 1 = \angle 2}$$
✅ Conclusion: The two angles are equal in measure (Congruent Supplements Theorem).
