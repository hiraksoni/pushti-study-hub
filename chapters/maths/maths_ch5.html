Master Concept Map: Parallel and Intersecting Lines

Explore the logical hierarchy connecting angles, pairs, and parallel line transversal theorems.

1. Intersecting Lines
  • Meet at a unique single point.
  • Vertically Opposite Angles: Non-adjacent opposite angles are equal ($\angle 1 = \angle 3, \angle 2 = \angle 4$).
  • Linear Pair: Adjacent angles on a straight line add to $180^\circ$.
  • Perpendicular ($\perp$): Special intersection where all 4 angles equal $90^\circ$.
2. Parallel Lines ($l \parallel m$)
  • Lie in the same plane and never meet, no matter how far extended.
  • The perpendicular distance between them is constant everywhere.
  • If two lines are both perpendicular to the same third line, they are parallel to each other.
3. Transversal Theorems
  • Corresponding ($F$-Shape): Lie in matching positions $\implies$ equal.
  • Alternate Interior ($Z$-Shape): Opposite sides between lines $\implies$ equal.
  • Alternate Exterior: Opposite sides outside lines $\implies$ equal.
  • Consecutive Interior ($C$-Shape): Same side between lines $\implies$ Supplementary ($180^\circ$).
Topic 5.1

Angles, Vertex & Intersecting Lines

Verified: 13 Sep 2026

An angle is the geometric figure formed when two rays originate from a common starting point called the vertex. When two straight lines in a plane cross each other, they meet at a single common point called the point of intersection.

Vertically Opposite Angles
When two lines $AB$ and $CD$ intersect at point $O$, four angles are created. Pairs of angles that do not share a common arm are called vertically opposite angles.
Figure 5.1: Vertically opposite angles: ∠1 = ∠3 and ∠2 = ∠4
Key Theorem: Vertically opposite angles are always equal in measure: $$\begin{aligned} \angle AOC &= \angle BOD \\ \angle AOD &= \angle BOC \end{aligned}$$
Linear Pairs of Angles
A linear pair is a pair of adjacent angles whose non-common arms form opposite rays (i.e. a straight line).
Figure 5.2: Linear pair: ∠1 + ∠2 = 180° along straight line QR
Axiom: The angles in a linear pair are supplementary (sum to $180^\circ$): $$\angle 1 + \angle 2 = 180^\circ$$
Figure 5.3: Complementary Angles (Sum = 90°) & Supplementary Angles (Sum = 180°)
Complementary Angles

Two angles are said to be complementary if the sum of their measures is $90^\circ$.

Each angle is called the complement of the other. Example: $35^\circ$ and $55^\circ$.

Supplementary Angles

Two angles are said to be supplementary if the sum of their measures is $180^\circ$.

Each angle is called the supplement of the other. Example: $110^\circ$ and $70^\circ$.

Illustration 1 (Page 3) Complementary
An angle is double its complement. Find the angle.
View Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Define variables: Let the angle be $x$.

Step 2: Its complement is double the angle $\implies 2x$.

Step 3: Apply Complementary Sum Rule:

$$x + 2x = 90^\circ \implies 3x = 90^\circ \implies x = \frac{90^\circ}{3} = 30^\circ$$
The angle is $60^\circ$ and its complement is $30^\circ$ (or $30^\circ$ if double refers to the given angle).
Illustration 2 (Page 3) Supplementary
Find an angle which is two-fifth of its supplement.
View Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Let the angle be $x$. Its supplement is $(180^\circ - x)$.

Step 2: Setup Equation:

$$x = \frac{2}{5}(180^\circ - x) \implies 5x = 360^\circ - 2x \implies 7x = 360^\circ \implies x = \left(\frac{360}{7}\right)^\circ = 51\frac{3}{7}^\circ$$
Required angle = $\frac{360^\circ}{7} \approx 51.43^\circ$.
Illustration 3 (Page 3) Linear Pair
In the given figure, $AOB$ is a straight line. Angle $\angle AOC = x + 35^\circ$ and $\angle BOC = 2x + 25^\circ$. Find (a) $\angle AOC$ and (b) $\angle BOC$.
Figure 5.4: Illustration 3: Linear pair on line AOB
View Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Since $AOB$ is a straight line, the angles form a linear pair:

$$(x + 35^\circ) + (2x + 25^\circ) = 180^\circ$$ $$3x + 60^\circ = 180^\circ \implies 3x = 120^\circ \implies x = 40^\circ$$

Step 2: Calculate specific angles:

(a) $\angle AOC = x + 35^\circ = 40^\circ + 35^\circ = 75^\circ$

(b) $\angle BOC = 2x + 25^\circ = 2(40^\circ) + 25^\circ = 80^\circ + 25^\circ = 105^\circ$

$\angle AOC = 75^\circ$, $\angle BOC = 105^\circ$ (Check: $75^\circ + 105^\circ = 180^\circ$).
Illustration 4 (Page 3) Identification
Identify pairs of adjacent angles, vertically opposite angles, and linear pairs when two lines $XY$ and $PQ$ intersect at $O$.
Figure 5.5: Illustration 4: Intersecting lines XY and PQ at O
View Step-by-Step Solution

1. Adjacent Angles: Share a common vertex and common arm:

$(\angle POY, \angle YOQ)$, $(\angle YOQ, \angle QOX)$, $(\angle QOX, \angle XOP)$, $(\angle XOP, \angle POY)$.

2. Vertically Opposite Angles: Non-adjacent opposite angles:

$\angle POY$ and $\angle QOX$; $\angle POX$ and $\angle QOY$.

3. Linear Pairs: Adjacent angles lying on a straight line:

$(\angle QOX, \angle XOP)$ along line $QP$; $(\angle XOP, \angle POY)$ along line $XY$.

Illustration 5 (Page 3) 3 Lines Intersection
In the given figure, straight lines $AB, CD$ and $EF$ intersect at $O$. If $\angle COE = 45^\circ$ and $\angle AOF = 90^\circ$, find $\angle BOD$.
Figure 5.6: Illustration 5: Three lines intersecting at O
View Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Identify vertically opposite angle:

$$\angle FOD = \angle COE = 45^\circ \quad \text{(Vertically opposite angles)}$$

Step 2: Linear pair on straight line $AB$:

Ray $OF$ and ray $OD$ stand on line $AB$, so angles on straight line $AB$ add to $180^\circ$:

$$\begin{aligned} \angle AOF + \angle FOD + \angle BOD &= 180^\circ \\ 90^\circ + 45^\circ + \angle BOD &= 180^\circ \\ 135^\circ + \angle BOD &= 180^\circ \\ \angle BOD &= 180^\circ - 135^\circ = \mathbf{45^\circ} \end{aligned}$$
$\angle BOD = 45^\circ$.
Topic 5.2

Parallel & Perpendicular Lines

Verified: 13 Sep 2026
Parallel Lines ($l \parallel m$)
Two lines in the same plane that do not intersect, no matter how far they are extended in either direction, are called parallel lines.
Figure 5.7: Parallel Lines: Perpendicular distance d remains constant
  • Symbol: $l \parallel m$ (read as "$l$ is parallel to $m$").
  • Perpendicular distance between parallel lines is constant everywhere.
  • Real-world examples: Railway tracks, opposite edges of a door or textbook, zebra crossings.
Perpendicular Lines ($l \perp m$)
When two intersecting lines form right angles ($90^\circ$) at their point of intersection, they are called perpendicular lines.
Figure 5.8: Perpendicular Lines: Four 90° right angles (l ⊥ m)
  • Symbol: $l \perp m$ (read as "$l$ is perpendicular to $m$").
  • When two lines are perpendicular, all four angles formed at the junction are $90^\circ$.
  • Real-world examples: Corners of a wall, window crossbars, coordinate axes.
Figure 5.9: Paper Folding Geometry: Four distinct crease patterns
Constructing Parallel Lines using Ruler & Set Square
Figure 5.10: Construction of parallel lines using Ruler and Set Square

How to draw a line parallel to a given line $l$ passing through a given external point $P$:

Step 1: Align Ruler

Draw line $l_1$. Place one straight edge of a set square along line $l_1$.

Step 2: Place Guide Ruler

Place a ruler firmly against the other edge of the set square so it forms a sliding track.

Step 3: Slide & Draw

Slide the set square along the ruler until its drawing edge touches point $P$. Draw line $l_2$. Then $l_1 \parallel l_2$!

Crucial Theorem (Page 8): If two lines in the same plane are both perpendicular to the same given line, then they are parallel to each other: $$l_1 \perp t \quad \text{and} \quad l_2 \perp t \implies l_1 \parallel l_2$$

Topic 5.3

Transversals & Angle Relationships

Verified: 13 Sep 2026

A line that intersects two or more lines at distinct points is called a transversal. When a transversal cuts two parallel lines, it forms 8 distinct angles that exhibit fundamental geometric relationships.

Figure 5.11: Master Transversal System: 8 angles formed by transversal t cutting lines l ∥ m

Angle Category Shape Recognition Pairs (where line $l \parallel m$) Mathematical Property
Corresponding Angles F-Shape $(\angle 1, \angle 5)$, $(\angle 2, \angle 6)$, $(\angle 3, \angle 7)$, $(\angle 4, \angle 8)$ Equal: $\angle 1 = \angle 5$, $\angle 2 = \angle 6$
Alternate Interior Angles Z-Shape $(\angle 3, \angle 5)$ and $(\angle 4, \angle 6)$ Equal: $\angle 3 = \angle 5$, $\angle 4 = \angle 6$
Alternate Exterior Angles External Z $(\angle 1, \angle 7)$ and $(\angle 2, \angle 8)$ Equal: $\angle 1 = \angle 7$, $\angle 2 = \angle 8$
Consecutive Interior (Co-interior) C-Shape / U-Shape $(\angle 3, \angle 6)$ and $(\angle 4, \angle 5)$ Supplementary: $\angle 3 + \angle 6 = 180^\circ$
Figure 5.12: The F-Rule: Corresponding Angles are Equal
Figure 5.13: The Z-Rule: Alternate Interior Angles are Equal
Figure 5.14: The C-Rule: Co-Interior Angles Sum to 180°
Tests for Checking if Two Lines are Parallel

When a transversal intersects two lines, the lines are guaranteed parallel if any one of the following conditions holds true:

  1. A pair of corresponding angles is equal.
  2. A pair of alternate interior angles is equal.
  3. A pair of consecutive interior angles on the same side of the transversal is supplementary (sum equals $180^\circ$).
Textbook Solved Examples (10)
Example 1

Complete Angle at a Point

Confidence:

In the given figure, four angles meet at a common vertex point $O$: $x$, $45^\circ$, $45^\circ$, and $200^\circ$. Find the value of $x$.

Figure 5.20: Complete Angle at Common Vertex O (x + 45° + 45° + 200° = 360°)

View Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Apply Complete Angle Theorem:

The sum of all angles around a single point equals $360^\circ$ (a complete angle).

$$x + 45^\circ + 45^\circ + 200^\circ = 360^\circ$$ $$x + 290^\circ = 360^\circ \implies x = 360^\circ - 290^\circ = 70^\circ$$
Value of $x = 70^\circ$.
Example 2

Angles on a Straight Line

Confidence:

Three angles $x$, $120^\circ$, and $45^\circ$ lie on a straight line. Find the value of $x$.

Figure 5.21: Three Adjacent Angles on Straight Line AOB (x + 120° + 45° = 180°)

View Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Straight line angle property:

The sum of all angles forming a straight angle on a line is $180^\circ$:

$$x + 120^\circ + 45^\circ = 180^\circ \implies x + 165^\circ = 180^\circ$$ $$x = 180^\circ - 165^\circ = 15^\circ$$
Value of $x = 15^\circ$.
Example 3

Transversal with Stepped Angles

Confidence:

Given $l \parallel m$, and a transversal cuts both lines. An exterior/interior angle combination gives $140^\circ = 90^\circ + x$ via alternate interior angles. Find the measure of $x$.

Figure 5.22: Transversal Stepped Angles between Parallel Lines l and m (x = 50°)
View Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Since lines $l$ and $m$ are parallel:

The alternate interior angles are equal:

$$140^\circ = 90^\circ + x \implies x = 140^\circ - 90^\circ = 50^\circ$$
$x = 50^\circ$.
Example 4

Testing for Parallelism (Is $AB \parallel CD$?)

Confidence:

Transversal line $AD$ cuts two lines $AB$ and $CD$. The alternate interior angle $\angle BAD = 50^\circ$ while $\angle CDA = 40^\circ$. Are lines $AB$ and $CD$ parallel to each other?

Figure 5.23: Testing for Parallelism — Unequal Alternate Interior Angles (50° ≠ 40° ⟹ AB ∦ CD)
View Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Check alternate interior angle criterion:

For two lines to be parallel, their alternate interior angles must be equal.

$$\angle BAD = 50^\circ \quad \text{and} \quad \angle CDA = 40^\circ$$

Since $\angle BAD \neq \angle CDA$ ($50^\circ \neq 40^\circ$), the alternate interior angles are not equal.

Conclusion: Lines $AB$ and $CD$ are not parallel.
Example 5

Multi-Line Parallel Transversal System

Confidence:

In the figure, $AB \parallel CD$ and $EA \parallel DF$. If $\angle GAB = 75^\circ$, $\angle ADF = 80^\circ$, $\angle EAB = x$, and $\angle CDF = y$. Find the values of $x$ and $y$.

Figure 5.24: Multi-Line Parallel Transversal System (AB || CD and EA || DF)

View Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Use $EA \parallel DF$ with transversal $AD$:

$$\angle EAD = \angle ADF = 80^\circ \quad \text{(Alternate interior angles)}$$

Step 2: Linear pair along straight line at point $A$:

$$\angle GAB + \angle EAB + \angle EAD = 180^\circ \implies 75^\circ + x + 80^\circ = 180^\circ$$ $$x + 155^\circ = 180^\circ \implies x = 25^\circ$$

Step 3: Calculate $\angle BAD$ and $y$:

$$\angle BAD = x + 80^\circ = 25^\circ + 80^\circ = 105^\circ$$

Since $AB \parallel CD$ with transversal $AD$:

$$\angle ADC = \angle BAD = 105^\circ \quad \text{(Alternate interior angles)}$$ $$\angle ADC = 80^\circ + y = 105^\circ \implies y = 105^\circ - 80^\circ = 25^\circ$$
Values: $x = 25^\circ$ and $y = 25^\circ$.
Example 6

Finding $x$, $y$, and $z$ from Intersecting Lines

Confidence:

In the given figure, straight lines intersect at $O$. Given $\angle AOC = 40^\circ$, find $x$, $y$, and $z$ where $z = \angle AOC$ is vertically opposite, $x + 65^\circ = 180^\circ$, and $y + z = 180^\circ$.

Figure 5.25: Intersecting Lines: Linear Pairs and Vertically Opposite Angles (z = 40°, x = y = 140°)
View Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Vertically opposite angles:

$$z = \angle AOC = 40^\circ$$

Step 2: Linear pair:

$$y + z = 180^\circ \implies y + 40^\circ = 180^\circ \implies y = 140^\circ$$

Step 3: Angle $x$:

$$x + 65^\circ = 180^\circ \implies x = 180^\circ - 65^\circ = 115^\circ$$
Results: $x = 115^\circ$, $y = 140^\circ$, $z = 40^\circ$.
Example 7

Four Angles of Two Intersecting Lines

Confidence:

Lines $AB$ and $CD$ intersect at point $O$. If $\angle 3 = 70^\circ$, find the measures of all other angles ($\angle 1$, $\angle 2$, and $\angle 4$).

Figure 5.26: Four Angles Formed by Two Intersecting Lines (∠1 = ∠3 = 70°, ∠2 = ∠4 = 110°)

View Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Vertically opposite angle:

$$\angle 1 = \angle 3 = 70^\circ$$

Step 2: Linear pair along line $AB$:

$$\angle 2 + \angle 3 = 180^\circ \implies \angle 2 + 70^\circ = 180^\circ \implies \angle 2 = 110^\circ$$

Step 3: Vertically opposite to $\angle 2$:

$$\angle 4 = \angle 2 = 110^\circ$$
All angles: $\angle 1 = 70^\circ$, $\angle 2 = 110^\circ$, $\angle 3 = 70^\circ$, $\angle 4 = 110^\circ$.
Example 8

Zig-Zag Construction with Auxiliary Parallel Line

Confidence:

In the given figure, $AB \parallel CD$. An intermediate vertex $M$ forms reflex or interior angles $\angle EMB$ and $\angle EMD$. Through $M$, an auxiliary line $EMF \parallel CD \parallel AB$ is drawn. Let $\angle EMB = y$ and $\angle EMD = 64^\circ - y$. Find the measure of the vertex angle.

Figure 5.27: Auxiliary Parallel Construction Through Zig-Zag Vertex M (∠BMD = 64°)
View Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Auxiliary Line Construction:

Draw line $EMF$ through $M$ parallel to both $AB$ and $CD$.

Step 2: Alternate Interior Angles:

$$\angle ABM = \angle EMB = y \quad \text{and} \quad \angle CDM = \angle EMD = 64^\circ - y$$

Sum of angles at vertex $M = y + (64^\circ - y) = 64^\circ$.

Vertex angle $\angle BMD = 64^\circ$.
Example 9

Linear Pair and Transversal Angle Chain

Confidence:

Line $CD$ is a straight line. Angles $\angle 2$ and $\angle 3$ form a linear pair ($\angle 2 + \angle 3 = 180^\circ$). Given $\angle BMS = 120^\circ$, find $\angle LMS$.

Figure 5.28: Linear Pair and Transversal Angle Chain (120° ⟶ 60° ⟶ 60°)
View Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Linear pair reasoning:

$$\angle BMS + \angle LMS = 180^\circ \implies 120^\circ + \angle LMS = 180^\circ$$ $$\angle LMS = 180^\circ - 120^\circ = 60^\circ$$
$\angle LMS = 60^\circ$.
Example 10

Angle Bisectors of Corresponding Angles

Confidence:

Prove that if two parallel lines are cut by a transversal, the bisectors of any pair of corresponding angles are parallel to each other.

Figure 5.29: Proof: Bisectors GL and HM of Corresponding Angles are Parallel (GL || HM)

View Step-by-Step Solution

Given: $AB \parallel CD$ and transversal $EF$ intersects $AB$ at $G$ and $CD$ at $H$. Ray $GL$ bisects $\angle EGB$ and ray $HM$ bisects $\angle EHD$.

To Prove: $GL \parallel HM$.

Proof:

Since $AB \parallel CD$ and $EF$ is a transversal:

$$\angle EGB = \angle EHD \quad \text{(Corresponding angles)}$$

Multiplying both sides by $\frac{1}{2}$:

$$\frac{1}{2}\angle EGB = \frac{1}{2}\angle EHD \implies \angle EGL = \angle GHM$$

These two equal angles are corresponding angles for lines $GL$ and $HM$ with transversal $EF$.

Since the corresponding angles are equal, the lines must be parallel.

Hence Proved: $GL \parallel HM$.

Figure 5.17: Drill 6 (Q5): Three parallel lines with EA ⊥ AB and ∠BEF = 55°

Drill 7 (Q6): Multi-Step Stepped Zig-Zag Angle ∠NOP

What is the measure of angle $\angle NOP$ in the figure, where line $LM$ is parallel to line $PQ$ ($LM \parallel PQ$), $\angle LMN = 36^\circ$, $\angle MNO = 96^\circ$, and $\angle OPQ = 40^\circ$?

Figure 5.34: Drill 7 (Q6) — Auxiliary Parallel Lines at Vertices N and O (∠NOP = 100°)
Step 1 Auxiliary Line $NN'$ at Vertex $N$

Through vertex $N$, draw an auxiliary line $NN'$ parallel to $LM$ and $PQ$ ($NN' \parallel LM \parallel PQ$).

Since alternate interior angles between parallel lines are equal:

$$\angle MNN' = \angle LMN = 36^\circ$$

Step 2 Calculate Remaining Angle at Vertex $N$

The total given angle at vertex $N$ is $\angle MNO = 96^\circ$. Subtract the upper alternate angle:

$$\angle ONN' = \angle MNO - \angle MNN' = 96^\circ - 36^\circ = 60^\circ$$

Step 3 Auxiliary Line $OO'$ at Vertex $O$ & Alternate Angles

Through vertex $O$, draw auxiliary line $OO'$ parallel to $NN'$ and $PQ$ ($OO' \parallel NN' \parallel PQ$).

Between parallel lines $NN'$ and $OO'$ with transversal $NO$:

$$\angle NOO' = \angle ONN' = 60^\circ \quad \text{(Alternate Interior Angles)}$$

Step 4 Lower Alternate Angle from Line $PQ$

Between parallel lines $OO'$ and $PQ$ with transversal $OP$:

$$\angle POO' = \angle OPQ = 40^\circ \quad \text{(Alternate Interior Angles)}$$

Final Step Combine Angles at Vertex $O$

The total measure of the stepped angle $\angle NOP$ is the sum of both components:

$$\angle NOP = \angle NOO' + \angle POO' = 60^\circ + 40^\circ = 100^\circ$$

Practice Bank: Multiple Choice Questions
Q1 • Level 1 Complementary Angles

If two angles are complementary, then the sum of their measures is:

Explanation:
By definition, two angles whose sum equals $90^\circ$ are called complementary angles.
Q2 • Level 1 Vertically Opposite

In the given figure, two straight lines $AB$ and $CD$ intersect at $O$. If $\angle AOC = 40^\circ$, what is the measure of $\angle BOD$?

Figure 5.35: MCQ Q2 — Intersecting Lines AB and CD with ∠AOC = 40°

Explanation:
$\angle AOC$ and $\angle BOD$ are vertically opposite angles. Since vertically opposite angles are equal, $\angle BOD = \angle AOC = 40^\circ$.
Q3 • Level 1 Linear Pair

One angle of a linear pair is $65^\circ$. What is the measure of the other angle?

Explanation:
Angles in a linear pair add up to $180^\circ$. Other angle $= 180^\circ - 65^\circ = 115^\circ$.
Q4 • Level 1 Parallel Transversal

If two parallel lines are cut by a transversal, each pair of alternate interior angles are:

Explanation:
Alternate interior angles ($Z$-shape) are always equal when the two intersected lines are parallel.
Q5 • Level 2 Clock Angles

If the angle formed by the hour hand and a third pointer is $65^\circ$, and the hour and minute hands form a straight line ($180^\circ$), what is the angle between the minute hand and the third pointer?

Explanation:
Since the hour and minute hands lie on a straight line, they form a linear pair around the pointer: $180^\circ - 65^\circ = 115^\circ$.
Q6 • Level 2 Algebraic Angles

Two supplementary angles are in the ratio $3 : 2$. What is the measure of the smaller angle?

Explanation:
$3x + 2x = 180^\circ \implies 5x = 180^\circ \implies x = 36^\circ$. Smaller angle $= 2(36^\circ) = 72^\circ$.
Q7 • Level 3 HOTS Three Parallel Lines Zig-Zag

In the given figure, $AB \parallel CD$, $\angle ABE = 120^\circ$, and $\angle DCE = 110^\circ$. What is the measure of reflex or reflex angle $\angle BEC = x$?

Figure 5.36: MCQ Q7 — Auxiliary Parallel Construction Through Point E
Explanation:
Draw line $EF \parallel AB \parallel CD$ through $E$. Co-interior angle with $AB$: $180^\circ - 120^\circ = 60^\circ$. Co-interior angle with $CD$: $180^\circ - 110^\circ = 70^\circ$. So interior $\angle BEC = 180^\circ - (60^\circ + 70^\circ)$ or $x = 70^\circ$ depending on orientation.

Supplementary Angle Difference

Two supplementary angles $A$ and $B$ are given by $A = (3x - 25)^\circ$ and $B = (2x + 5)^\circ$. Find the absolute difference in degrees $|A - B|$.

View Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: $(3x - 25)^\circ + (2x + 5)^\circ = 180^\circ \implies 5x - 20^\circ = 180^\circ \implies 5x = 200^\circ \implies x = 40^\circ$.

Step 2: $A = 3(40^\circ) - 25^\circ = 120^\circ - 25^\circ = 95^\circ$.

Step 3: $B = 2(40^\circ) + 5^\circ = 80^\circ + 5^\circ = 85^\circ$.

Step 4: Difference $= 95^\circ - 85^\circ = 10^\circ$.

Exact Integer Value = 10.

Theorem Proof (Page 24)

Two Lines Perpendicular to Same Line are Parallel

Theorem: Prove that two lines in a plane that are both perpendicular to the same given line in the plane are parallel to each other.

View Formal Geometric Proof

Given: Let $m \perp t$ and $n \perp t$, where $t$ is a transversal intersecting line $m$ at $P$ and line $n$ at $Q$.

To Prove: $m \parallel n$.

Proof:

1. Since line $m \perp t$, the angle formed $\angle 1 = 90^\circ$.

2. Since line $n \perp t$, the corresponding angle formed $\angle 2 = 90^\circ$.

3. Thus, $\angle 1 = \angle 2 = 90^\circ$.

4. Since $\angle 1$ and $\angle 2$ are a pair of corresponding angles and they are equal, lines $m$ and $n$ must be parallel.

Hence Proved: $m \parallel n$.

Common Pitfalls

Top 4 Geometry Traps to Avoid in Exams

Trap 1: Assuming Alternate Angles are Equal on ANY Two Lines

Alternate interior angles ($Z$-shape) are ONLY equal if the two lines are parallel! If lines are not parallel, alternate angles exist but are unequal. Always verify line parallelism first.

Trap 2: Confusing Supplementary Angles with Linear Pairs

All linear pairs are supplementary (sum = $180^\circ$), but not all supplementary angles form a linear pair. Supplementary angles can be separate angles anywhere in a diagram, whereas a linear pair must share a common arm and vertex on a straight line.

Trap 3: Adding Non-Adjacent Angles to $90^\circ$ for Complementary

Complementary refers strictly to angle measures ($a + b = 90^\circ$). They do not need to be adjacent.
