View Summary of Main Points (10 Core Principles from Worksheet)
1. Arithmetic Expressions: A mathematical phrase formed using numbers and operations ($+, -, \times, \div$). Every expression has a single definite value.
2. Equality & Equivalence: Different expressions can have the same value. The equality sign ($=$) asserts that two expressions are equivalent.
3. Comparing Expressions: Compare expressions using $<, >, =$ by reasoning about how their parts differ, without fully calculating both values.
4. Brackets Control Meaning: Brackets show which part must be treated together. E.g. $5 \times (8 + 2) = 50$ is fundamentally different from $5 \times 8 + 2 = 42$.
5. Terms: Terms are parts separated by addition signs. Subtraction can be rewritten as addition of the additive inverse: $18 - 7 = 18 + (-7)$.
6. Swapping & Grouping: When written as a sum of terms, terms may be swapped or regrouped without changing value (Commutative & Associative properties).
7. Evaluating Complex Expressions: Evaluate inside brackets first. Products and quotients that form a compound term are evaluated before terms are added together.
8. Removing Brackets: If preceded by $+$, signs inside stay the same. If preceded by $-$, signs of terms inside reverse when brackets are removed.
9. Distributive Property: A multiple of a sum or difference can be distributed: $a \times (b + c) = a \times b + a \times c$ and $a \times (b - c) = a \times b - a \times c$.
10. Efficient Calculation: Equivalent expressions make arithmetic easier, e.g. $98 \times 25 = (100 - 2) \times 25 = 2500 - 50 = 2450$.

Section A: Foundations and Equivalent Expressions

Questions 1 – 8
Question 1 Concept: Expression Value
Write two different arithmetic expressions whose value is $18$.
Step-by-Step Mathematical Working

An arithmetic expression is a valid combination of numbers and operations evaluating to a single value.

Expression 1: $3 \times 6$ or $2 \times (5 + 4) = 18$
Expression 2: $30 - 12$ or $90 \div 5 = 18$
Key Insight: Different expressions can share the exact same value. The equality sign ($=$) can connect them into an equation: $3 \times 6 = 30 - 12$.
Question 2 Compensation & Equality
Fill in the blank:
$27 + 8 = \underline{\hspace{1.5cm}} + 10$
Step-by-Step Mathematical Working

Method 1 (Full Evaluation):

$$\text{LHS} = 27 + 8 = 35$$ $$\text{Blank} + 10 = 35 \implies \text{Blank} = 35 - 10 = \mathbf{25}$$

Method 2 (Algebraic Compensation — Smart Thinking):

Notice that on the RHS, $10$ is $2$ greater than $8$. To keep the sum equal, the first term must be $2$ smaller than $27$:

$$27 - 2 = \mathbf{25}$$
Check: $27 + 8 = 35$ and $25 + 10 = 35$. Both sides are equal!
Question 3 Subtraction Relationship
Fill in the blank:
$54 - \underline{\hspace{1.5cm}} = 39$
Step-by-Step Mathematical Working

In subtraction: $\text{Minuend} - \text{Subtrahend} = \text{Difference}$.

$$\text{Subtrahend} = \text{Minuend} - \text{Difference} = 54 - 39$$ $$\text{Blank} = \mathbf{15}$$
Verification: $54 - 15 = (54 - 10) - 5 = 44 - 5 = 39$.
Question 4 Balanced Multi-Operation
Fill in the blank:
$7 \times \underline{\hspace{1.5cm}} = 84 \div 2$
Step-by-Step Mathematical Working

First evaluate the RHS:

$$\text{RHS} = 84 \div 2 = 42$$

Now set $\text{LHS} = \text{RHS}$:

$$7 \times \text{Blank} = 42 \implies \text{Blank} = \dfrac{42}{7} = \mathbf{6}$$
Check: $7 \times 6 = 42$ and $84 \div 2 = 42$.
Question 5 Modeling Expression
Six notebooks cost $\text{₹}28$ each. Write an arithmetic expression for the total cost and find its value.
Step-by-Step Mathematical Working

1. Arithmetic Expression:

$$\mathbf{6 \times 28} \quad \text{or} \quad \mathbf{6 \times \text{₹}28}$$

2. Smart Mental Calculation:

$$\begin{aligned} 6 \times 28 &= 6 \times (30 - 2) \\ &= 6 \times 30 - 6 \times 2 \\ &= 180 - 12 = \mathbf{\text{₹}168} \end{aligned}$$

Answer: The total cost of the six notebooks is $\mathbf{\text{₹}168}$.

Question 6 Brackets in Real Context
A customer pays $\text{₹}500$ for items costing $\text{₹}135$ and $\text{₹}86$. Write one expression using brackets for the change received, then find the change.
Step-by-Step Mathematical Working

1. Expression using brackets:

$$\mathbf{500 - (135 + 86)}$$

2. Evaluation:

$$\text{Step 1 (Add inside brackets): } 135 + 86 = 221$$ $$\text{Step 2 (Subtract from 500): } 500 - 221 = \mathbf{\text{₹}279}$$
Common Error: Writing $500 - 135 + 86$. Without brackets, $86$ would be added to the result rather than subtracted, giving an erroneous $451$!

Explain the difference between the expression $18 + 7 \times 3$ and the equation $18 + 7 \times 3 = 39$.
Step-by-Step Mathematical Working
Arithmetic Expression ($18 + 7 \times 3$) Equation ($18 + 7 \times 3 = 39$)

Has no '$=$' sign; it can be evaluated to find its value ($39$). States that two expressions on either side of '$=$' are equal.
Question 8 Ascending Order
Arrange these expressions in ascending order of value:
$72 - 19$
$50 + 6$
$7 \times 8$
$162 \div 3$
Step-by-Step Mathematical Working

Step 1: Evaluate each expression:

  • $72 - 19 = \mathbf{53}$
  • $50 + 6 = \mathbf{56}$
  • $7 \times 8 = \mathbf{56}$
  • $162 \div 3 = \mathbf{54}$

Step 2: Order the numerical values:

$$53 < 54 < 56 = 56$$

Ascending order of expressions:

$$\mathbf{(72 - 19) < (162 \div 3) < (50 + 6) = (7 \times 8)}$$

Section B: Compare by Reasoning (Without Full Calculation)

Questions 9 – 14
Question 9 Reasoning: Sums
Insert $<, >,$ or $=$ without fully evaluating both sides:
$435 + 198 \;\underline{\hspace{1.5cm}}\; 436 + 196$
Step-by-Step Mathematical Working

Compare term by term:

  • First term: $436$ is $1$ more than $435$ ($+1$ for RHS).
  • Second term: $196$ is $2$ less than $198$ ($-2$ for RHS).
  • Net effect on RHS: $+1 - 2 = -1$.

Since the RHS is $1$ less than LHS, LHS is greater:

$$\mathbf{435 + 198 > 436 + 196}$$
Verification: $435 + 198 = 633$ and $436 + 196 = 632$. Correct!
Question 10 Reasoning: Differences
Insert $<, >,$ or $=$:
$723 - 158 \;\underline{\hspace{1.5cm}}\; 724 - 159$
Step-by-Step Mathematical Working

Constant Difference Property:

Notice that $724 = 723 + 1$ and $159 = 158 + 1$.

When the same number ($1$) is added to both the minuend and the subtrahend, the difference remains unchanged:

$$(a + 1) - (b + 1) = a - b$$ $$\mathbf{723 - 158 = 724 - 159}$$
Verification: Both differences equal $565$.
Question 11 Reasoning: Sums
Insert $<, >,$ or $=$:
$307 + 489 \;\underline{\hspace{1.5cm}}\; 309 + 486$
Step-by-Step Mathematical Working

Compare components:

  • $309 = 307 + 2$ (RHS gains $2$)
  • $486 = 489 - 3$ (RHS loses $3$)
  • Net change on RHS: $+2 - 3 = -1$.

The RHS is $1$ less than LHS:

$$\mathbf{307 + 489 > 309 + 486}$$
Verification: $307 + 489 = 796$, while $309 + 486 = 795$.
Question 12 Reasoning: Subtraction
Insert $<, >,$ or $=$:
$620 - 247 \;\underline{\hspace{1.5cm}}\; 618 - 244$
Step-by-Step Mathematical Working

Component analysis:

  • $618$ is $2$ less than $620$ (decreases value by $2$).
  • $244$ is $3$ less than $247$ (subtracting $3$ less increases value by $3$).
  • Net change on RHS: $-2 + 3 = +1$.

The RHS is $1$ greater than the LHS:

$$\mathbf{620 - 247 < 618 - 244}$$
Verification: $620 - 247 = 373$ and $618 - 244 = 374$.
Question 13 Reasoning: Multipliers
Insert $<, >,$ or $=$ and explain briefly:
$25 \times 19 \;\underline{\hspace{1.5cm}}\; 25 \times 20$
Step-by-Step Mathematical Working

Reasoning:

Both expressions share the common factor $25$. The second factor determines the size:

$$19 < 20 \implies 25 \times 19 < 25 \times 20$$

Specifically, $25 \times 19$ is smaller by exactly one group of $25$ ($500 - 25 = 475$).

$$\mathbf{25 \times 19 < 25 \times 20}$$
Question 14 Reasoning: Grouped Factors
Compare by reasoning:
$36 \times (18 - 7) \;\underline{\hspace{1.5cm}}\; 36 \times (17 - 6)$
Step-by-Step Mathematical Working

Reasoning:

First factor on both sides is identical: $36$.

Examine the bracketed expressions:

$$\text{LHS bracket: } 18 - 7 = 11$$ $$\text{RHS bracket: } 17 - 6 = 11$$

Since both bracketed factors evaluate to $11$, both expressions represent $36 \times 11$:

$$\mathbf{36 \times (18 - 7) = 36 \times (17 - 6)}$$

Section C: Terms and Evaluating Expressions

Questions 15 – 20
Question 15 Identify Terms
Identify the terms in: $38 - 7 + 4 \times 6$
Step-by-Step Mathematical Working

Rule: Terms are separated by addition signs. Products and quotients form single compound terms.

Rewriting with addition: $38 + (-7) + (4 \times 6)$.

The $3$ terms are:

Term 1: $38$ Term 2: $-7$ Term 3: $4 \times 6$ (or $24$)
Question 16 Compound Terms
Identify the terms in: $45 - 3 \times 8 + 20 \div 5$
Step-by-Step Mathematical Working

Rewriting as a sum of terms: $45 + (-3 \times 8) + (20 \div 5)$.

The $3$ terms are:

Term 1: $45$ Term 2: $-(3 \times 8)$ (value: $-24$) Term 3: $20 \div 5$ (value: $+4$)
Question 17 Additive Inverses
Rewrite using addition of additive inverses: $62 - 19 + 7 - 12$
Step-by-Step Mathematical Working

Subtracting a number is mathematically identical to adding its additive inverse (negative):

$$\text{Subtracting } 19 \implies +(-19)$$ $$\text{Subtracting } 12 \implies +(-12)$$

Rewritten expression:

$$\mathbf{62 + (-19) + 7 + (-12)}$$
Benefit: Once written as addition of terms, we can swap and group positive terms and negative terms freely: $(62 + 7) + [(-19) + (-12)] = 69 - 31 = 38$.
Question 18 Thinking in Terms
Find the value by thinking in terms: $28 - 7 + 8$
Step-by-Step Mathematical Working

The terms are $28$, $-7$, and $+8$.

Strategy 1 (Combine positives first):

$$(28 + 8) - 7 = 36 - 7 = \mathbf{29}$$

Strategy 2 (Combine $-7 + 8$ first):

$$28 + (-7 + 8) = 28 + 1 = \mathbf{29}$$
Result: $\mathbf{29}$. Thinking in terms prevents the common mistake of calculating $28 - (7 + 8) = 13$!
Question 19 Evaluation
Find the value: $39 - 2 \times 6 + 11$
Step-by-Step Mathematical Working

Step 1: Identify compound product term $2 \times 6 = 12$.

Step 2: Substitute back into expression:

$$39 - 12 + 11$$

Step 3: Group terms:

$$(39 + 11) - 12 = 50 - 12 = \mathbf{38}$$
Question 20 Multi-term Evaluation
Find the value: $48 - 10 \times 2 + 16 \div 2$
Step-by-Step Mathematical Working

Step 1: Evaluate product and quotient terms:

$$10 \times 2 = 20 \quad \text{and} \quad 16 \div 2 = 8$$

Step 2: Replace into expression:

$$48 - 20 + 8$$

Step 3: Evaluate left to right or group positives:

$$(48 + 8) - 20 = 56 - 20 = \mathbf{36}$$

Section D: Brackets and Sign Changes

Questions 21 – 27
Question 21 Without Brackets
Find the value of $30 + 5 \times 4$ by first identifying its terms.
Step-by-Step Mathematical Working

Step 1: Identify terms:

The terms are $30$ and the product $(5 \times 4)$.

Step 2: Evaluate the product term:

$$5 \times 4 = 20$$

Step 3: Add the terms:

$$30 + 20 = \mathbf{50}$$
Question 22 With Brackets
Find the value of $(30 + 5) \times 4$. Explain in one sentence why it differs from Question 21.
Step-by-Step Mathematical Working

1. Evaluation:

$$(30 + 5) \times 4 = 35 \times 4 = \mathbf{140}$$

2. Explanation of Difference:

In Question 22, the brackets group $30 + 5$ into a single quantity first, meaning that $4$ scales both $30$ and $5$ (yielding $120 + 20 = 140$), whereas in Question 21, $4$ multiplies only $5$.

Question 23 Remove Brackets (+)
Remove the brackets without changing the value: $75 + (18 - 9)$
Step-by-Step Mathematical Working

Rule: When a bracket is preceded by a plus sign ($+$), the signs inside remain unchanged.

$$\mathbf{75 + 18 - 9}$$
Check: Both forms equal $84$.
Question 24 Remove Brackets (-)
Remove the brackets without changing the value: $120 - (35 + 12)$
Step-by-Step Mathematical Working

Rule: When a bracket is preceded by a minus sign ($-$), each sign inside flips upon removal.

$$\mathbf{120 - 35 - 12}$$
Check: $120 - 47 = 73$, and $120 - 35 - 12 = 85 - 12 = 73$.
Question 25 Sign Flip with Minus
Remove the brackets without changing the value: $200 - (70 - 25)$
Step-by-Step Mathematical Working

Distribute the minus sign: $-(+70) = -70$ and $-(-25) = +25$.

$$\mathbf{200 - 70 + 25}$$
Check: $200 - 45 = 155$, and $200 - 70 + 25 = 130 + 25 = 155$.
Question 26 Place Brackets
Add brackets to make the statement true:
$48 - 12 + 6 = 30$
Step-by-Step Mathematical Working

Without brackets: $48 - 12 + 6 = 36 + 6 = 42 \ne 30$.

To reach $30$, we need to subtract $18$ from $48$ ($48 - 18 = 30$).

Since $12 + 6 = 18$, we group $(12 + 6)$:

$$\mathbf{48 - (12 + 6) = 30}$$
Verification: $48 - 18 = 30$. Statement is true!
Question 27 Place Brackets
Add brackets to make the statement true:
$60 - 20 - 10 = 50$
Step-by-Step Mathematical Working

Without brackets: $60 - 20 - 10 = 40 - 10 = 30 \ne 50$.

To obtain $50$, we need $60 - 10 = 50$.

Notice that $20 - 10 = 10$. Enclose $(20 - 10)$ in brackets:

$$\mathbf{60 - (20 - 10) = 50}$$
Verification: $60 - 10 = 50$. Statement is true!

Section E: Distributive Property and Smart Calculation

Questions 28 – 33
Question 28 Distributive Expansion
Expand using the distributive property: $7 \times (20 + 3)$
Step-by-Step Mathematical Working

Distributive Property Formula: $a \times (b + c) = a \times b + a \times c$.

$$\begin{aligned} 7 \times (20 + 3) &= \mathbf{7 \times 20 + 7 \times 3} \\ &= 140 + 21 = \mathbf{161} \end{aligned}$$
Question 29 Factoring Common Term
Write as a single product by taking out the common factor: $9 \times 17 + 9 \times 3$
Step-by-Step Mathematical Working

Both terms contain $9$ as a common factor:

$$9 \times 17 + 9 \times 3 = \mathbf{9 \times (17 + 3)}$$

Evaluating the single product:

$$9 \times 20 = \mathbf{180}$$
Question 30 Round Number Strategy
Evaluate efficiently using a nearby round number: $98 \times 24$
Step-by-Step Mathematical Working

Express $98$ as $(100 - 2)$:

$$\begin{aligned} 98 \times 24 &= (100 - 2) \times 24 \\ &= 100 \times 24 - 2 \times 24 \\ &= 2400 - 48 = \mathbf{2352} \end{aligned}$$
Question 31 Smart Multiplication
Evaluate efficiently: $103 \times 17$
Step-by-Step Mathematical Working

Express $103$ as $(100 + 3)$:

$$\begin{aligned} 103 \times 17 &= (100 + 3) \times 17 \\ &= 100 \times 17 + 3 \times 17 \\ &= 1700 + 51 = \mathbf{1751} \end{aligned}$$
Question 32 Benchmark Base 50
Evaluate efficiently: $49 \times 36$
Step-by-Step Mathematical Working

Express $49$ as $(50 - 1)$:

$$\begin{aligned} 49 \times 36 &= (50 - 1) \times 36 \\ &= 50 \times 36 - 1 \times 36 \\ &= 1800 - 36 = \mathbf{1764} \end{aligned}$$
Mental Trick for $50 \times 36$: $50 \times 36 = 100 \times (36 \div 2) = 100 \times 18 = 1800$.
Question 33 Incremental Factor
Given that $53 \times 18 = 954$, find $63 \times 18$ without starting the multiplication from the beginning.
Step-by-Step Mathematical Working

Observe that $63 = 53 + 10$:

$$\begin{aligned} 63 \times 18 &= (53 + 10) \times 18 \\ &= (53 \times 18) + (10 \times 18) \\ &= 954 + 180 = \mathbf{1134} \end{aligned}$$
Result: $\mathbf{1134}$ obtained in seconds without long multiplication!

Five friends buy one sandwich each at $\text{₹}42$ and together leave a $\text{₹}20$ tip. Write an expression for the total amount, identify its terms, and find the value.

Question 35 Compound Real-Life Model
A museum ticket costs $\text{₹}50$ for an adult and $\text{₹}30$ for a child. Write and evaluate an expression for the cost of tickets for $3$ adults and $4$ children.

$$\mathbf{3 \times 50 + 4 \times 30}$$

2. Evaluation:

$$\begin{aligned} \text{Adults: } 3 \times 50 &= 150 \\ \text{Children: } 4 \times 30 &= 120 \\ \text{Total Cost: } 150 + 120 &= \mathbf{\text{₹}270} \end{aligned}$$

$$\mathbf{8 \times 14 + 6}$$

$$8 \times 14 + 6 = 112 + 6 = \mathbf{118\text{ chairs}}$$

Section G: Reasoning Challenges

Questions 39 – 40
Question 39 HOTS Challenge
Without fully calculating each expression, identify all expressions equal to $84 - (29 + 11)$:
(A) $84 - 29 - 11$
(B) $84 - 29 + 11$
(C) $84 + (-29) + (-11)$
(D) $84 - (29 - 11)$
Step-by-Step Mathematical Working

Analysis:

  1. (A) $84 - 29 - 11$: Removing brackets preceded by minus flips $+11$ to $-11$. EQUAL (Correct).
  2. (B) $84 - 29 + 11$: Erroneously retains $+11$. Equals $66 \ne 44$. NOT Equal.
  3. (C) $84 + (-29) + (-11)$: Written as addition of additive inverses of $29$ and $11$. EQUAL (Correct).
  4. (D) $84 - (29 - 11)$: Changes the internal operation to subtraction ($84 - 18 = 66 \ne 44$). NOT Equal.

Correct Options: (A) and (C).

Question 40 HOTS Creative Modeling
Start with the number $12$. One student writes $(12 + 8) \times 3$; another writes $12 \times 3 + 8$. Find both values, explain why they are different, and write a real-life situation that could match each expression.
Step-by-Step Mathematical Working

1. Numerical Evaluation:

Student 1: $(12 + 8) \times 3 = 20 \times 3 = \mathbf{60}$
Student 2: $12 \times 3 + 8 = 36 + 8 = \mathbf{44}$

2. Why they differ:

In $(12 + 8) \times 3$, brackets mandate that $8$ is added to $12$ before multiplying, so the multiplier $3$ scales both amounts. In $12 \times 3 + 8$, only $12$ is multiplied by $3$, and $8$ is added once as a separate independent term.

3. Real-Life Contexts:

Scenario for $(12 + 8) \times 3 = 60$:
"Pushti prepares 3 birthday gift hampers. Each hamper contains 12 sketch pens and 8 gel pens. What is the total number of pens in all 3 hampers combined?"
$\implies 3 \times (12 + 8) = 60\text{ pens}$.

Scenario for $12 \times 3 + 8 = 44$:
"Pushti buys 3 notebooks at ₹12 each, and also buys a single ₹8 ruler. How much money does she spend in total?"
$\implies 3 \times 12 + 8 = 36 + 8 = \text{₹}44$.
