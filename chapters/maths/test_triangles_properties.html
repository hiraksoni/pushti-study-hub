Ex 12.1 Q1

Identify the types of triangles in each case

Classify triangles based on sides and angles for each specification:

(a) $AB = 3 ext{ cm}, BC = 3 ext{ cm}, CA = 3 ext{ cm}$
(b) $AB = 7 ext{ cm}, BC = 5 ext{ cm}, CA = 11 ext{ cm}$
(c) $AB = 4 ext{ cm}, BC = 5 ext{ cm}, CA = 4 ext{ cm}$
(d) $ngle A = 40^\circ, ngle B = 50^\circ, ngle C = 90^\circ$
(e) $ngle A = 60^\circ, ngle B = 60^\circ, ngle C = 60^\circ$
(f) $ngle A = 60^\circ, ngle B = 70^\circ, ngle C = 50^\circ$
(g) $AB = AC = 7 ext{ cm}, ngle A = 90^\circ$
View Detailed Classification & Reasons
  • (a) All 3 sides are equal ($3 ext{ cm} = 3 ext{ cm} = 3 ext{ cm}$) → Equilateral Triangle.
  • (b) All 3 sides are unequal ($7 eq 5 eq 11$) → Scalene Triangle.
  • (c) Two sides are equal ($AB = CA = 4 ext{ cm} eq 5 ext{ cm}$) → Isosceles Triangle.
  • (d) One angle is $90^\circ$ and all angles differ → Right-Angled Scalene Triangle.
  • (e) All three angles are equal ($60^\circ$) → Equiangular / Equilateral Triangle.
  • (f) All angles are acute ($< 90^\circ$) and distinct → Acute-Angled Scalene Triangle.
  • (g) Two equal sides with a $90^\circ$ angle → Right-Angled Isosceles Triangle.
Ex 12.1 Q2

Identify medians and altitudes; in which cases do they overlap?

(a) In $ riangle XYZ$, identify the median and altitude.
(b) In $ riangle ABC$, identify the median and altitude.
Core Question: In which cases do the medians and altitudes overlap?

View Solution & Geometrical Rules

Definitions:
• Median: Line segment connecting a vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side.
• Altitude: Perpendicular line segment dropped from a vertex to the line containing the opposite side.

When do they overlap (coincide)?
1. In an Equilateral Triangle, all three medians coincide with their respective altitudes.
2. In an Isosceles Triangle, the median and altitude drawn from the vertex angle between the two equal sides to the base overlap exactly!

Ex 12.1 Q3

Altitudes lying inside, on the side, and outside the triangle

Draw three triangles in which the altitudes lie inside, on the side, and outside the triangles.

View Solution & Descriptions
  • 1. Inside the triangle: In an Acute-Angled Triangle (e.g. all angles $< 90^\circ$), all three altitudes lie entirely inside the triangle, intersecting at an interior orthocenter.
  • 2. On the side of the triangle: In a Right-Angled Triangle, the two perpendicular legs forming the right angle are themselves the altitudes! They lie on the sides, meeting at the right-angle vertex.
  • 3. Outside the triangle: In an Obtuse-Angled Triangle (having one angle $> 90^\circ$), the altitudes drawn from the two acute vertices drop onto the extended base lines, thus lying strictly outside the triangle.

Ex 12.2 Q1

Isosceles triangle base angles given vertex angle $80^\circ$

If the vertex angle of an isosceles triangle is of measure $80^\circ$, find the measures of base angles.

View Solution

In an isosceles triangle, the base angles are equal. Let each base angle be $b$.

$$80^\circ + b + b = 180^\circ \implies 2b = 180^\circ - 80^\circ = 100^\circ \implies \mathbf{b = 50^\circ}$$
✅ Final Answer: The base angles are each $\mathbf{50^\circ}$ (i.e. $50^\circ$ and $50^\circ$).
Ex 12.2 Q2

Triangle angles in ratio $2 : 5 : 3$

If the angles of a triangle are in the ratio of $2 : 5 : 3$, find the measures of the angles.

View Solution

Let the three angles be $2x, 5x,$ and $3x$.

$$2x + 5x + 3x = 180^\circ \implies 10x = 180^\circ \implies x = 18^\circ$$
  • First angle $= 2 imes 18^\circ = \mathbf{36^\circ}$
  • Second angle $= 5 imes 18^\circ = \mathbf{90^\circ}$
  • Third angle $= 3 imes 18^\circ = \mathbf{54^\circ}$
✅ Final Answer: $\mathbf{36^\circ, 90^\circ, 54^\circ}$ (a right-angled scalene triangle).
Ex 12.2 Q3

Find $z$ for angles $8z, 5z,$ and $5z$

Find the value of $z$ if the three angles of a triangle are $8z, 5z,$ and $5z$.

View Solution
$$8z + 5z + 5z = 180^\circ \implies 18z = 180^\circ \implies \mathbf{z = 10^\circ}$$

The angles are $8(10^\circ) = 80^\circ$, $5(10^\circ) = 50^\circ$, $5(10^\circ) = 50^\circ$.

✅ Final Answer: $\mathbf{z = 10}$.

Ex 12.2 Q5

$ngle P : ngle Q = 3 : 7$ with exterior angle $100^\circ$

In $ riangle PQR$, the measures of $ngle P$ and $ngle Q$ are in the ratio of $3 : 7$. If the exterior angle $ngle PRM$ of the $ riangle PQR$ is of measure $100^\circ$, find the measures of all the angles of the triangle.

View Solution

Let $ngle P = 3k$ and $ngle Q = 7k$.

$$ngle P + ngle Q = ngle PRM \implies 3k + 7k = 100^\circ \implies 10k = 100^\circ \implies k = 10^\circ$$

  • $\mathbf{ngle Q = 7 imes 10^\circ = 70^\circ}$
  • $\mathbf{ngle PRQ = 180^\circ - 100^\circ = 80^\circ}$ (Linear pair with exterior angle)
✅ Final Answer: $\mathbf{ngle P = 30^\circ, ngle Q = 70^\circ, ngle PRQ = 80^\circ}$.
Ex 12.2 Q6

Find values of $p$ and $q$ from triangle figure

In the figure, top angle is $70^\circ$, base interior angles are $p$ and $q$, and adjacent exterior angle is $120^\circ$. Find $p$ and $q$.

View Solution

Ex 12.2 Q7

Find measures of $ngle ABC$ and $ngle ACB$

In the figure, vertex angle $ngle BAC = 65^\circ$, and exterior angle at vertex $C$ is $125^\circ$. Find $ngle ABC$ and $ngle ACB$.

View Solution

• By linear pair with exterior angle $125^\circ$: $$\mathbf{ngle ACB = 180^\circ - 125^\circ = 55^\circ}$$

• By exterior angle property: $$ngle BAC + ngle ABC = 125^\circ \implies 65^\circ + ngle ABC = 125^\circ \implies \mathbf{ngle ABC = 60^\circ}$$

Ex 12.2 Q8

Exterior Angle and Ratio of Interior Opposite Angles

One side of a triangle is extended and an exterior angle of measure $130^\circ$ is formed. If the measures of interior opposite angles are in the ratio $6:7$, find the measures of all the angles of the triangle.

View Step-by-Step Working

1. Exterior Angle Property:

$$\text{Exterior Angle} = \text{Sum of Interior Opposite Angles}$$

Let the two interior opposite angles be $6x$ and $7x$.

$$6x + 7x = 130^\circ \implies 13x = 130^\circ \implies x = 10^\circ$$

Therefore, the two interior opposite angles are:

$$6x = 6 \times 10^\circ = \mathbf{60^\circ}, \quad 7x = 7 \times 10^\circ = \mathbf{70^\circ}$$

2. Third Angle (Adjacent Interior Angle):

$$\text{Third Angle} = 180^\circ - 130^\circ = \mathbf{50^\circ} \quad (\text{Linear pair with exterior angle})$$

Answer: The three angles of the triangle are $\mathbf{50^\circ, 60^\circ, \text{ and } 70^\circ}$.

Ex 12.3 Q1

Which of the following measures can form a triangle?

Check using the Triangle Inequality Theorem (the sum of lengths of any two sides must be strictly greater than the third side):

(a) $6\text{ cm}, 8\text{ cm}, 10\text{ cm}$
(b) $5\text{ cm}, 10\text{ cm}, 15\text{ cm}$
(c) $8\text{ cm}, 6\text{ cm}, 13\text{ cm}$
(d) $7\text{ cm}, 7\text{ cm}, 7\text{ cm}$
View Step-by-Step Verification
  • (a) $6, 8, 10$: $6 + 8 = 14 > 10$. Can form a triangle (Yes).
  • (b) $5, 10, 15$: $5 + 10 = 15 \not> 15$ (sum equals third side, points are collinear). Cannot form a triangle (No).
  • (c) $8, 6, 13$: $6 + 8 = 14 > 13$. Can form a triangle (Yes).
  • (d) $7, 7, 7$: $7 + 7 = 14 > 7$. Can form a triangle (Yes — Equilateral).
Ex 12.3 Q2

Check if the following set of numbers form Pythagorean triplets:

Check if $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$ holds for each triplet (where $c$ is the largest number):

(a) $1.4, 2.3, 3.4$
(b) $7, 24, 25$
(c) $8, 14, 16$
(d) $9, 40, 41$
View Step-by-Step Verification
  • (a) $1.4, 2.3, 3.4$: $1.4^2 + 2.3^2 = 1.96 + 5.29 = 7.25 \ne 3.4^2 (11.56)$. Not a Pythagorean triplet.
  • (b) $7, 24, 25$: $7^2 + 24^2 = 49 + 576 = 625 = 25^2$. Forms a Pythagorean triplet (Yes).
  • (c) $8, 14, 16$: $8^2 + 14^2 = 64 + 196 = 260 \ne 16^2 (256)$. Not a Pythagorean triplet.
  • (d) $9, 40, 41$: $9^2 + 40^2 = 81 + 1600 = 1681 = 41^2$. Forms a Pythagorean triplet (Yes).
Ex 12.3 Q3

Find the unknown lengths in the given figures:

(a) Right Triangle: Hypotenuse $AC = 29\text{ cm}$, Base $BC = 21\text{ cm}$, Perpendicular $AB = x$.
(b) Isosceles Triangle: $AC = AB = 13\text{ cm}$, Base $BC = 24\text{ cm}$, Altitude $y$, Half-base $x$.
View Step-by-Step Working

Part (a): Right Triangle $\triangle ABC$ ($\angle B = 90^\circ$):

$$\begin{aligned} AC^2 &= AB^2 + BC^2 \\ 29^2 &= x^2 + 21^2 \\ x^2 &= 29^2 - 21^2 = (29 - 21)(29 + 21) = 8 \times 50 = 400 \\ x &= \sqrt{400} = \mathbf{20\text{ cm}} \end{aligned}$$

Part (b): Isosceles Triangle $\triangle ABC$ with Altitude from $A$:

The altitude to the base of an isosceles triangle bisects the base:

$$x = \dfrac{24}{2} = \mathbf{12\text{ cm}}$$

In the right-angled half-triangle:

$$\begin{aligned} y^2 + x^2 &= 13^2 \\ y^2 + 12^2 &= 169 \\ y^2 &= 169 - 144 = 25 \implies y = \sqrt{25} = \mathbf{5\text{ cm}} \end{aligned}$$

Answer: (a) $x = \mathbf{20\text{ cm}}$, (b) $x = \mathbf{12\text{ cm}}, y = \mathbf{5\text{ cm}}$.

Ex 12.3 Q4

Look at the quadrilateral ABCD with diagonals intersecting at O. Which statements are true?

(a) $DC + CB > BD$
(b) $AB + 2BC > AC$
(c) $AB + BC + CD + AD > 2AC$
(d) $BD + AB < AD$
View Geometric Proofs
  • (a) $DC + CB > BD$: TRUE. In $\triangle BCD$, the sum of any two sides is greater than the third side ($DC + CB > BD$).
  • (b) $AB + 2BC > AC$: TRUE. In $\triangle ABC$, $AB + BC > AC$. Since $BC > 0$, adding $BC$ gives $AB + 2BC > AC$.
  • (c) $AB + BC + CD + AD > 2AC$: TRUE. In $\triangle ABC$: $AB + BC > AC$. In $\triangle ADC$: $AD + CD > AC$. Adding the two inequalities gives $(AB + BC + CD + AD) > 2AC$.
  • (d) $BD + AB < AD$: FALSE. In $\triangle ABD$, $BD + AB > AD$ by triangle inequality.

True Statements: (a), (b), and (c).

Ex 12.3 Q5

Look at triangle PQR with altitude PS = 12 cm, PQ = 20 cm, base QR = 25 cm.

(a) Length of side $PR$
(b) Area of $\triangle PQR$
View Step-by-Step Working

Part (a): Find length of $PR$:

In right-angled $\triangle PSQ$ ($\angle PSQ = 90^\circ$):

$$QS^2 = PQ^2 - PS^2 = 20^2 - 12^2 = 400 - 144 = 256 \implies QS = 16\text{ cm}$$

Since $QR = 25\text{ cm}$, the remaining base segment $SR$ is:

$$SR = QR - QS = 25 - 16 = 9\text{ cm}$$

In right-angled $\triangle PSR$ ($\angle PSR = 90^\circ$):

$$\begin{aligned} PR^2 &= PS^2 + SR^2 \\ PR^2 &= 12^2 + 9^2 = 144 + 81 = 225 \\ PR &= \sqrt{225} = \mathbf{15\text{ cm}} \end{aligned}$$

Part (b): Find Area of $\triangle PQR$:

$$\text{Area} = \dfrac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height} = \dfrac{1}{2} \times 25 \times 12 = 25 \times 6 = \mathbf{150\text{ cm}^2}$$
Ex 12.3 Q6

Ladder Leaning Against Building

A ladder leans against a wall of a building. The length of the ladder is $5\text{ m}$ and its base is $3\text{ m}$ away from the building. Find the height of the wall reached by the ladder.

View Step-by-Step Working

The wall, ground, and ladder form a right-angled triangle where:

$$\text{Hypotenuse (ladder)} = 5\text{ m}, \quad \text{Base (distance from wall)} = 3\text{ m}$$ $$\begin{aligned} \text{Height}^2 &= \text{Hypotenuse}^2 - \text{Base}^2 \\ h^2 &= 5^2 - 3^2 = 25 - 9 = 16 \\ h &= \sqrt{16} = \mathbf{4\text{ m}} \end{aligned}$$

Answer: The ladder reaches a height of $\mathbf{4\text{ m}}$ on the wall.

Ex 12.3 Q7

Playground Diagonal Distance

A playground is of length $144\text{ m}$ and breadth $17\text{ m}$. If the coach asked the players to run from one corner to the other corner diagonally across the playground, what distance did each player run?

View Step-by-Step Working

By Pythagoras' Theorem on the rectangular diagonal:

$$\begin{aligned} d^2 &= \text{length}^2 + \text{breadth}^2 \\ d^2 &= 144^2 + 17^2 = 20736 + 289 = 21025 \\ d &= \sqrt{21025} = \mathbf{145\text{ m}} \end{aligned}$$

Answer: Each player ran a distance of $\mathbf{145\text{ m}}$.

Ex 12.3 Q8

Distance Between Bases of Two Towers

The heights of two towers are $15\text{ m}$ and $10\text{ m}$. If the distance between the top of the two towers is $13\text{ m}$, what is the distance between the bases of the two towers?

View Step-by-Step Working

Draw a horizontal line from the top of the shorter tower ($10\text{ m}$) to the taller tower ($15\text{ m}$):

  • Vertical leg of right triangle: $15 - 10 = \mathbf{5\text{ m}}$.
  • Hypotenuse (distance between tops): $\mathbf{13\text{ m}}$.
  • Horizontal leg (distance between bases): $b$.
$$\begin{aligned} b^2 &= 13^2 - 5^2 = 169 - 25 = 144 \\ b &= \sqrt{144} = \mathbf{12\text{ m}} \end{aligned}$$

Answer: The distance between the bases of the two towers is $\mathbf{12\text{ m}}$.

HOTS 1

Babita's Claim: Equilateral Triangle Medians and Altitudes

Babita says that in an equilateral triangle, medians and altitudes are the same. Is she correct? Justify your answer.

View Mathematical Justification

Answer: Yes, Babita is completely correct.

Justification:

Let $\triangle ABC$ be an equilateral triangle where $AB = BC = CA$. Draw altitude $AD \perp BC$ with $D$ on $BC$.

  • In right $\triangle ADB$ and right $\triangle ADC$:
  • Hypotenuse $AB = AC$ (equilateral sides).
  • Side $AD = AD$ (common altitude).
  • $\angle ADB = \angle ADC = 90^\circ$.
  • By RHS Congruence Criterion, $\triangle ADB \cong \triangle ADC$.
  • Therefore, by CPCTC, $BD = DC$.

Since $D$ is the midpoint of $BC$, $AD$ is also a median. By symmetry, the same holds for all three altitudes and medians!

HOTS 2

Squares on Triangle Sides: Triangle Type Determination

If a square has been drawn on each side of a triangle, how can you tell whether the triangle is obtuse, acute, or right-angled?

View Area Comparison Principle

Method: Compare the areas of the three squares!

Let the side lengths be $a, b, c$, with $c$ being the longest side. The areas of the three squares are $a^2, b^2,$ and $c^2$.

  • 1. Right-Angled Triangle: If $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$, the sum of the areas of the two smaller squares strictly equals the area of the largest square (Pythagoras' Theorem).
  • 2. Acute-Angled Triangle: If $a^2 + b^2 > c^2$, the sum of the two smaller squares is greater than the area of the largest square.
  • 3. Obtuse-Angled Triangle: If $a^2 + b^2 < c^2$, the sum of the two smaller squares is less than the area of the largest square.
HOTS 3

Six-Pointed Star Angle Sum Proof

Look at the six-pointed star figure with vertices $A, B, C, D, E, F$ and prove that:

$$\angle A + \angle B + \angle C + \angle D + \angle E + \angle F = 360^\circ$$
View Elegant Two-Triangle Proof

Proof:

Notice that the 6-pointed star is composed of two overlapping large triangles: $\triangle ACE$ and $\triangle BDF$.

Step 1: Apply the Angle Sum Property to $\triangle ACE$:

$$\angle A + \angle C + \angle E = 180^\circ \quad \text{--- (Equation 1)}$$

Step 2: Apply the Angle Sum Property to $\triangle BDF$:

$$\angle B + \angle D + \angle F = 180^\circ \quad \text{--- (Equation 2)}$$

Step 3: Add Equation 1 and Equation 2:

$$(\angle A + \angle C + \angle E) + (\angle B + \angle D + \angle F) = 180^\circ + 180^\circ$$ $$\mathbf{\angle A + \angle B + \angle C + \angle D + \angle E + \angle F = 360^\circ}$$

Hence Proved! (Q.E.D.)
