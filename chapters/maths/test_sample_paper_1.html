Q2–Q7 • 6 × 2 Marks
VSA • 2 Marks Q2

Insert brackets to make the statements true

(a) $45 - 12 + 8 = 25$     (b) $64 - 16 - 6 = 54$

View Solution
  • (a) Without brackets: $45 - 12 + 8 = 41$. Group 12 and 8 first: $$45 - (12 + 8) = 45 - 20 = 25\ ✓$$
  • (b) Without brackets: $64 - 16 - 6 = 42$. Subtract the difference instead: $$64 - (16 - 6) = 64 - 10 = 54\ ✓$$
✅ Answers: (a) $45 - (12 + 8) = 25$   (b) $64 - (16 - 6) = 54$
VSA • 2 Marks Q3

Add and subtract mixed fractions

(a) Ravi walks $2\frac{8}{10}$ m and then $3\frac{7}{10}$ m. What is the total distance walked?
(b) A pencil is $5\frac{4}{10}$ cm long and a crayon is $2\frac{9}{10}$ cm long. How much longer is the pencil?

View Solution

(a) Add whole parts and fractional parts:

$$2\frac{8}{10} + 3\frac{7}{10} = 5\frac{15}{10} = 6\frac{5}{10} = 6\frac{1}{2}\ \text{m}$$

(b) Subtract, regrouping 1 tenth from the whole part:

$$5\frac{4}{10} - 2\frac{9}{10} = 5\frac{14}{10} - 2\frac{9}{10} = 2\frac{5}{10} = 2.5\ \text{cm}$$
✅ Answers: (a) $6\frac{1}{2}$ m   (b) $2\frac{5}{10}$ cm (2.5 cm)
VSA • 2 Marks Q4

Write an algebraic expression for the total cost

A notebook costs ₹35 and a gift cover costs ₹80. Write an algebraic expression for the total cost of $n$ notebooks and $g$ gift covers.

View Solution

Cost of $n$ notebooks $= 35 \times n = 35n$; cost of $g$ gift covers $= 80 \times g = 80g$.

$$\text{Total cost} = 35n + 80g$$
✅ Answer: $35n + 80g$ (in rupees)
VSA • 2 Marks Q5

Find the value of angle $a$

In the figure, $POQ$ is a straight line and $\angle POR = 124^\circ$. Find $a$ (the angle adjacent to it on the line).

View Solution

Angles on a straight line form a linear pair (sum $180^\circ$):

$$a + 124^\circ = 180^\circ \implies a = 180^\circ - 124^\circ = 56^\circ$$
✅ Answer: $a = 56^\circ$
VSA • 2 Marks Q6

Compare the decimals: 6.50, 6.500 and 06.5

Are 6.50, 6.500 and 06.5 equal? Arrange them and give a reason.

View Solution

Trailing zeros after the decimal point do not change a decimal's value, and a zero before the number (06.5) does not change it either:

$$6.50 = 6.500 = 06.5 = 6.5$$
✅ Answer: All three are equal; each equals 6.5
VSA • 2 Marks Q7

Find the complementary and supplementary angles

(a) Find the complement of $25^\circ$.
(b) Find the supplement of $150^\circ$.

View Solution
  • (a) Complementary angles add to $90^\circ$: $$90^\circ - 25^\circ = 65^\circ$$
  • (b) Supplementary angles add to $180^\circ$: $$180^\circ - 150^\circ = 30^\circ$$
✅ Answers: (a) 65°   (b) 30°

Q8–Q10 • 3 × 3 Marks
SA • 3 Marks Q8

Compare the pairs of expressions using $>$, $<$ or $=$

(a) $72 \times 31 - 9$ $\square$ $72 \times 30 - 9$
(b) $25 \times (16 + 12)$ $\square$ $25 \times (17 + 13)$
(c) $(63 - 27) \times 15$ $\square$ $63 \times 15 - 27$

View Solution
  • (a) Same subtraction on both sides, and $31 > 30$ so $72 \times 31 > 72 \times 30$:   $\mathbf{>}$ ($72 \times 31 - 9 = 2223$, $72 \times 30 - 9 = 2151$)
  • (b) $25 \times 28 = 700$ and $25 \times 30 = 750$, and $700 < 750$:   $\mathbf{<}$
  • (c) $(63 - 27) \times 15 = 36 \times 15 = 540$; $63 \times 15 - 27 = 945 - 27 = 918$, and $540 < 918$:   $\mathbf{<}$
✅ Answers: (a) >   (b) <   (c) <
SA • 3 Marks Q9

View Solution

SA • 3 Marks Q10

Attempt either (a) or (b) — Angles with lines

(a) Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal. One of the marked angles measures $68^\circ$. Find $x$, the angle corresponding to it, and $y$, the angle co-interior (same-side interior) with it.
(b) In the figure two lines cross, forming an angle of $42^\circ$ with the transversal at which $p$ is marked, and an angle of $76^\circ$ at which $q$ is marked. Find $p$ and $q$.

View Solution

(a) Corresponding angles are equal; co-interior angles are supplementary:

$$x = 68^\circ \qquad y = 180^\circ - 68^\circ = 112^\circ$$

(b) $p$ forms a linear pair with the $42^\circ$ angle on the straight line, and $q$ corresponds to the $76^\circ$ angle:

$$p = 180^\circ - 42^\circ = 138^\circ \qquad q = 76^\circ$$
✅ Answers: (a) $x = 68^\circ$, $y = 112^\circ$   (b) $p = 138^\circ$, $q = 76^\circ$

Q11–Q13 • 3 × 5 Marks
LA • 5 Marks Q11

Attempt either (a) or (b) — Uniform speed (Unitary method)

(a) A van covered 500 km in 20 hours at a uniform speed.
(i) Find the distance covered in 5 hours.   (ii) Find the distance covered in 12 hours.   (iii) How many hours does it take to cover 125 km?

(b) A truck covered 637 km in 7 hours, and a van covered 480 km in 8 hours.
(i) Find the distance the truck covers in 1 hour.   (ii) Find the distance the truck covers in 20 hours.   (iii) Find the distance the van covers in 10 hours.

View Solution

(a) Speed of the van $= 500 \div 20 = 25$ km per hour.

  • (i) In 5 hours: $25 \times 5 = \mathbf{125}$ km
  • (ii) In 12 hours: $25 \times 12 = \mathbf{300}$ km
  • (iii) Time for 125 km: $125 \div 25 = \mathbf{5}$ hours

(b) Truck's speed $= 637 \div 7 = 91$ km per hour; van's speed $= 480 \div 8 = 60$ km per hour.

  • (i) Truck in 1 hour: $\mathbf{91}$ km
  • (ii) Truck in 20 hours: $91 \times 20 = \mathbf{1820}$ km
  • (iii) Van in 10 hours: $60 \times 10 = \mathbf{600}$ km
✅ Answers: (a) 125 km; 300 km; 5 hours  |  (b) 91 km; 1820 km; 600 km
LA • 5 Marks Q12

Extend the Fibonacci (Virahānka) sequence

Two consecutive terms of a Fibonacci-like sequence are 233 and 377 (each term is the sum of the two terms before it).
(i) Write the next three terms.   (ii) Write the previous two terms.

View Solution

(i) Next terms — keep adding the last two terms:

$$233 + 377 = 610, \quad 377 + 610 = 987, \quad 610 + 987 = 1597$$

(ii) Previous terms — subtract backwards:

$$377 - 233 = 144, \quad 233 - 144 = 89$$

Check: $89 + 144 = 233$ ✓ and $144 + 233 = 377$ ✓

✅ Answers: (i) next three: 610, 987, 1597   (ii) previous two: 144, 89
LA • 5 Marks Q13

Attempt either (a) or (b) — Construction of triangles

(a) Construct $\triangle ABC$ with $AB = 6$ cm, $BC = 7$ cm and $CA = 5$ cm, and draw the altitude from $A$ to side $BC$.
(b) Construct $\triangle PQR$ with $PQ = 6$ cm, $PR = 5$ cm and $\angle QPR = 60^\circ$, and draw the altitude from $R$ to side $PQ$.

View Solution

(a) Steps (SSS construction):

  1. Draw $BC = 7$ cm.
  2. With $B$ as centre and radius 6 cm, draw an arc.
  3. With $C$ as centre and radius 5 cm, draw another arc cutting the first arc at $A$.
  4. Join $AB$ and $AC$ → $\triangle ABC$ is ready.
  5. Altitude: With $A$ as centre, draw arcs cutting line $BC$ at two points; from those points draw two equal-radius arcs on the opposite side to meet at a point; join $A$ to that point — the perpendicular meets $BC$ at its foot.

(b) Steps (SAS construction):

  1. Draw $PQ = 6$ cm.
  2. At $P$, use a protractor to draw $\angle QPR = 60^\circ$.
  3. With $P$ as centre and radius 5 cm, cut the ray at $R$.
  4. Join $RQ$ → $\triangle PQR$ is ready.
  5. Altitude: From $R$, draw a perpendicular to line $PQ$ (arcs on either side of $PQ$ from $R$, then equal-radius arcs from those two points) — it meets $PQ$ at its foot.
✅ Answer: Both triangles constructed (SSS / SAS) with the required altitudes drawn as perpendiculars from the vertex to the opposite side.

Case Study • 4 Marks Q14

Case: Robotics Store

Case Data: At the school robotics store, 5 gear wheels cost ₹175. Aluminium tracks are cut from 2 m bars, and 76 cm of a bar is used for the mount of each track. A Maker Kit is priced at ₹291.

(i) Find the price of one gear wheel.
(ii) What length of track is left from a 2 m bar after the 76 cm mount is cut off?
(iii) (a) Find the cost of 3 Maker Kits.   (b) Riya buys 3 Maker Kits and pays ₹1000. How much change does she get?

View Solution
  • (i) Price of one wheel $= 175 \div 5 = \mathbf{₹35}$
  • (ii) $2\ \text{m} = 200$ cm; length left $= 200 - 76 = \mathbf{124}$ cm
  • (iii)(a) Cost $= 3 \times 291 = \mathbf{₹873}$
  • (iii)(b) Change $= 1000 - 873 = \mathbf{₹127}$
✅ Answers: (i) ₹35   (ii) 124 cm   (iii)(a) ₹873   (b) ₹127
Case Study • 4 Marks Q15

Case: Seating in the School Hall

Case Data: In the school hall each table seats 2 students, and 2 extra chairs are placed at the two ends of every row of tables.

(i) Write an algebraic expression for the number of chairs needed for $t$ tables.
(ii) How many chairs are needed for 18 tables?
(iii) (a) If 50 chairs have been set out, how many tables are there?   (b) Two blocks have 20 chairs and 30 chairs respectively. Find the total number of chairs.

View Solution
  • (i) 2 chairs per table + 2 extra chairs: $$2t + 2$$
  • (ii) For $t = 18$: $2 \times 18 + 2 = 36 + 2 = \mathbf{38}$ chairs
  • (iii)(a) $2t + 2 = 50 \implies 2t = 48 \implies t = \mathbf{24}$ tables
  • (iii)(b) $20 + 30 = \mathbf{50}$ chairs
✅ Answers: (i) 2t + 2   (ii) 38   (iii)(a) 24 tables   (b) 50 chairs
Case Study • 4 Marks Q16

Case: The School Garden

Case Data: The school garden is rectangular, 15 m long and 10 m wide. The gardener plants 8 saplings in each of its 6 rows. A flower bed occupies 15 m² of the garden and a vegetable patch occupies 12 m².

(i) How many saplings are planted in the garden?
(ii) What is the area of the garden?
(iii) (a) What fraction of the garden is the flower bed?   (b) What fraction of the garden is the vegetable patch?

View Solution
  • (i) $6 \text{ rows} \times 8 = \mathbf{48}$ saplings
  • (ii) Area $= 15 \times 10 = \mathbf{150}$ m²
  • (iii)(a) $\dfrac{15}{150} = \dfrac{1}{10}$
  • (iii)(b) $\dfrac{12}{150} = \dfrac{2}{25}$ (dividing numerator and denominator by 6)
✅ Answers: (i) 48 saplings   (ii) 150 m²   (iii)(a) 1/10   (b) 2/25
