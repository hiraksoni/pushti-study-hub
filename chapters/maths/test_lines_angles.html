Ex 11.1 Q3

Linear pair angle type deduction

(a) In a linear pair, if one of the angles is a right angle, what kind of angle will the other one be?
(b) In a linear pair, if one of the angles is an acute angle, what kind of angle will the other one be?

View Solution

(a) Right angle: Since the sum is $180^\circ$, $180^\circ - 90^\circ = \mathbf{90^\circ ext{ (Right angle)}}$.

(b) Obtuse angle: Since the acute angle is $< 90^\circ$, the other angle must be $> 90^\circ$ ($180^\circ - ext{acute} > 90^\circ$). Hence, it is an $\mathbf{obtuse ext{ angle}}$.

Ex 11.1 Q4

Two supplementary angles where one is $ rac{1}{5}$ of the other

Two supplementary angles are such that one is $ rac{1}{5}$ of the other. Find the measures of both the angles.

View Solution

Let the larger angle be $x$. Then the other angle is $ rac{1}{5}x$.

$$x + rac{1}{5}x = 180^\circ \implies rac{6}{5}x = 180^\circ \implies x = 180^\circ imes rac{5}{6} = \mathbf{150^\circ}$$

The smaller angle is $ rac{1}{5} imes 150^\circ = \mathbf{30^\circ}$.

✅ Final Answer: $150^\circ$ and $30^\circ$.

Ex 11.1 Q7

Supplementary angles with difference of $32^\circ$

In a pair of supplementary angles, one angle is $32^\circ$ less than the other. Find the measures of both the angles.

View Solution

Let the larger angle be $x$. The smaller angle is $x - 32^\circ$.

$$x + (x - 32^\circ) = 180^\circ \implies 2x = 212^\circ \implies x = \mathbf{106^\circ}$$

Smaller angle $= 106^\circ - 32^\circ = \mathbf{74^\circ}$.

✅ Final Answer: $106^\circ$ and $74^\circ$.
Ex 11.1 Q8

Complementary angles with difference of $45^\circ$

The difference between a pair of complementary angles is $45^\circ$. Find the measures of both the angles.

View Solution

Let the two angles be $a$ and $b$ with $a - b = 45^\circ$ and $a + b = 90^\circ$.

$$2a = 135^\circ \implies a = \mathbf{67.5^\circ} ext{ (or } 67^\circ 30' ext{)}$$ $$b = 90^\circ - 67.5^\circ = \mathbf{22.5^\circ} ext{ (or } 22^\circ 30' ext{)}$$
✅ Final Answer: $67.5^\circ$ and $22.5^\circ$.

Ex 11.2 Q1

State the property used in each statement

(a) If $l \parallel m$, then $ngle 1 = ngle 5$.
(b) If $ngle 4 = ngle 6$, then $l \parallel m$.
(c) If $ngle 4 + ngle 5 = 180^\circ$, then $l \parallel m$.

View Solution
  • (a): Corresponding Angles Property — If two parallel lines are cut by a transversal, each pair of corresponding angles are equal.
  • (b): Converse of Alternate Interior Angles Theorem — When a transversal cuts two lines such that alternate interior angles are equal, the lines are parallel.
  • (c): Converse of Consecutive Interior Angles Theorem — If interior angles on the same side of the transversal sum to $180^\circ$, the lines are parallel.

Examine the four configurations of intersecting lines and rays:

(b) Non-parallel lines with transversal
(c) Vertically opposite angles $\angle 1 = \angle 2$

Correct Answer: Option (c)

Geometric Rationale:

  • In figure (c), line $XZ$ and line $WV$ intersect each other at vertex $Y$.
  • Angles $\angle 1$ and $\angle 2$ lie opposite to each other across the common vertex $Y$, making them vertically opposite angles.

MCQ Q2

Two angles $x$ and $90^\circ - x$ are:

(a) a linear pair of angles
(b) vertically opposite angles
(c) complementary angles
(d) supplementary angles
View Mathematical Solution & Explanation

Correct Answer: Option (c) complementary angles

Step-by-step verification:

$$\text{Sum of the two angles} = x + (90^\circ - x) = 90^\circ$$

By definition, two angles whose measures add up to $90^\circ$ are called complementary angles.

MCQ Q3

The angles $y + 15^\circ$ and $190^\circ + y$:

(a) are interior angles on same side
(b) form a linear pair
(c) are complementary angles
(d) none of these
View Mathematical Solution & Explanation

Correct Answer: Option (d) none of these

Step-by-step verification:

$$\text{Sum} = (y + 15^\circ) + (190^\circ + y) = 2y + 205^\circ$$

Since the sum exceeds $180^\circ$ for any non-negative $y$, they cannot be complementary ($90^\circ$), supplementary ($180^\circ$), or form a linear pair.

MCQ Q4

If an angle is $30^\circ$ less than two times its complement, what is the measure of the greater angle?

(a) $40^\circ$
(b) $50^\circ$
(c) $60^\circ$
(d) $70^\circ$
View Mathematical Solution & Explanation

Correct Answer: Option (b) $50^\circ$

Mathematical Derivation:

Let the angle be $A$. Its complement is $90^\circ - A$.

According to the given condition:

$$\begin{aligned} A &= 2(90^\circ - A) - 30^\circ \\ A &= 180^\circ - 2A - 30^\circ \\ A &= 150^\circ - 2A \\ A + 2A &= 150^\circ \\ 3A &= 150^\circ \implies A = \mathbf{50^\circ} \end{aligned}$$

Its complement is $90^\circ - 50^\circ = 40^\circ$.

The question asks for the greater angle: $\max(50^\circ, 40^\circ) = \mathbf{50^\circ}$.

MCQ Q5

Which of the following angles is complementary to $\angle SOR$? (From Page 36 Figure)

In the given figure on line $PQ$, $\angle POT = 60^\circ$, $\angle TOS = 25^\circ$, $\angle SOR = 65^\circ$, and $\angle ROQ = 30^\circ$.

(a) $\angle POT$
(b) $\angle TOS$
(c) $\angle SOR$
(d) Both (b) and (c)
View Mathematical Solution & Explanation

Correct Answer: Option (b) $\angle TOS$

Calculation:

From the diagram, $\angle SOR = 65^\circ$.

The complement of $65^\circ$ is $90^\circ - 65^\circ = \mathbf{25^\circ}$.

From the given figure, $\angle TOS = \mathbf{25^\circ}$.

$$\angle SOR + \angle TOS = 65^\circ + 25^\circ = 90^\circ$$

Therefore, $\angle TOS$ is complementary to $\angle SOR$.

True or False Conceptual Verification

Page 36 • Questions 16 to 20
16. Two obtuse angles form a pair of complementary angles. FALSE

An obtuse angle is $> 90^\circ$. Two obtuse angles sum to $> 180^\circ$, which can never equal $90^\circ$.

17. Vertically opposite angles are always of equal measure. TRUE

Theorem: When two lines intersect, the vertically opposite angles formed are always equal.

18. Supplementary angles are always obtuse angles. FALSE

Two right angles ($90^\circ + 90^\circ = 180^\circ$) are supplementary and neither is obtuse.

19. A pair of supplementary angles will always have two acute angles. FALSE

The sum of two acute angles is strictly $< 180^\circ$, so they can never form a supplementary pair.

20. Vertically opposite angles form a linear pair. FALSE

Linear pairs must be adjacent sharing a common ray; vertically opposite angles share only a vertex, not a common arm.

Parallel and perpendicular line relations

A line segment $\overline{XY}$ is parallel to $\overline{PQ}$. $\overline{PQ}$ is perpendicular to $\overline{RS}$. Draw each of the lines. Determine the relation between $\overline{XY}$ and $\overline{RS}$.

View Solution & Geometric Deduction

HOTS 2

Sum of $\angle a$ and $\angle b$ when $\angle a = \angle b$ and $\angle c = \angle d$

Look at the figure given alongside. What can you conclude about the sum of $\angle a$ and $\angle b$ if $\angle a = \angle b$ and $\angle c = \angle d$? Justify your answer.

View Solution & Justification

Angles on a straight line sum to $180^\circ$:

$$\angle a + \angle b + \angle c + \angle d = 180^\circ$$

Since $\angle a = \angle b$ and $\angle c = \angle d$:

$$2\angle a + 2\angle c = 180^\circ \implies 2(\angle a + \angle c) = 180^\circ \implies \mathbf{\angle a + \angle c = 90^\circ}$$

Also, since $\angle a = \angle b$, $\mathbf{\angle a + \angle b = 2\angle a}$. If $\angle a = \angle c = 45^\circ$, then $\angle a + \angle b = 90^\circ$ (perpendicular normal bisector, matching optical reflection).

HOTS 3

Two angles supplementary to the same angle

If two angles are supplementary to the same angle, how are the measures of the two angles related?

View Solution & Congruence Proof

Let the two angles be $\angle 1$ and $\angle 2$, and let the third common angle be $\angle 3$.

$$\angle 1 + \angle 3 = 180^\circ \implies \angle 1 = 180^\circ - \angle 3$$ $$\angle 2 + \angle 3 = 180^\circ \implies \angle 2 = 180^\circ - \angle 3$$

Since both right-hand sides are identical:

$$\mathbf{\angle 1 = \angle 2}$$
✅ Conclusion: The two angles are equal in measure (Congruent Supplements Theorem).
