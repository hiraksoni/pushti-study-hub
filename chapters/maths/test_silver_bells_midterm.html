LA • 5 MarksQ7.3

Explain the concepts of one-tenth and one-hundredth using suitable real-life situations.

View Solution

One-tenth ($\frac{1}{10}$): Split ₹1 into 10 equal parts — each part is 10 paise, i.e. $\frac{1}{10}$ of a rupee $= 0.1$. Likewise 1 dm is one-tenth of a metre.

One-hundredth ($\frac{1}{100}$): Split ₹1 into 100 equal parts — each part is 1 paisa, i.e. $\frac{1}{100}$ of a rupee $= 0.01$. Likewise 1 cm is one-hundredth of a metre, and 1 minute is one-hundredth of... an hour is 60×60 seconds — a simpler example: 1 paise coin in ₹1.

Ones place $= 1$, tenths place $= 0.1$, hundredths place $= 0.01$ — each step to the right divides by 10.

✅ Answer: 10 paise = one-tenth of ₹1; 1 paisa = one-hundredth of ₹1 (similarly 1 cm = one-hundredth of 1 m)
LA • 5 MarksQ7.4

Define an equilateral triangle. Explain its properties and construct an equilateral triangle with each side measuring 6 cm.

Syllabus Gap: Compass Arc Protocol Missing in Ch 7 • Pending Notes 7.1
View Solution

Definition: A triangle with all three sides equal is called an equilateral triangle.

Properties:

  • All three sides are equal in length.
  • All three interior angles are equal, each measuring $60^\circ$ (equiangular).
  • It is always acute-angled; the altitude, median and angle-bisector from any vertex coincide.

Construction (side 6 cm):

  1. Draw a base line $BC = 6$ cm with a ruler.
  2. Set the compass radius to 6 cm (measure on the ruler).
  3. From $B$, draw an arc; from $C$, draw another arc cutting the first at $A$.
  4. Join $AB$ and $AC$. $\triangle ABC$ is equilateral with each side 6 cm.
✅ Answer: All sides equal (all angles $60^\circ$); construction by two 6 cm arcs from the ends of a 6 cm base

Case Study 1 • Irfan's Shopping (Q8.1) Irfan went shopping with ₹100. He bought a packet of biscuits for ₹15 and a packet of toor dal for ₹56. To find out how much money he would get back, Irfan wrote the expression $100 - 15 + 56$. When he calculated it, he got ₹141, which did not make sense because it was more than the amount he had. He then realised his mistake: both ₹15 and ₹56 were expenses, so they needed to be added first and subtracted from ₹100. He corrected the expression to $100 - (15 + 56)$. Using this, he calculated the total cost to be ₹71 and found that he would get back ₹29. This situation helped him understand the importance of brackets and order of operations in evaluating complex expressions correctly. Brackets ensure that related quantities (like expenses) are grouped together and calculated before other operations.

What was the incorrect expression Irfan wrote first?

✅ Answer: $100 - 15 + 56$
Case Study • 1 MarkQ8.1(b)

Why was the result ₹141 incorrect in Irfan's first calculation?

View Solution

Left to right, $100 - 15 + 56 = 85 + 56 = 141$ — here ₹56 gets added back to the remaining money, as if he received it instead of spending it. But ₹141 is more than the ₹100 he started with, which is impossible when he only bought things.

✅ Answer: Because ₹56 was added instead of subtracted; both are expenses, so the answer must be less than ₹100, never ₹141

Rewrite the correct expression using brackets and explain why it's correct. Evaluate it.

View Solution

Correct expression:

$$100 - (15 + 56)$$

Why correct: the brackets force the two expenses (₹15 + ₹56) to be added first, giving total spending, which is then subtracted from ₹100 — exactly the real situation.

$$100 - (15 + 56) = 100 - 71 = 29$$
✅ Answer: $100 - (15 + 56) = 29$ (he gets back ₹29)

Case Study • 1 MarkQ8.2(a)

What is the angle formed when two lines are perpendicular?

View Solution

Case Study • 1 MarkQ8.2(b)

What is the method to form a line parallel to a crease in paper folding?

View Solution

Double perpendicular folding: fold a line perpendicular to the existing crease, then fold a second line perpendicular to that new fold. Two lines perpendicular to the same line are parallel to each other — the second fold runs parallel to the original crease.

✅ Answer: Fold a perpendicular to the crease, then a perpendicular to that perpendicular (double perpendicular folding) — both perpendiculars to one line are parallel
Case Study • 2 MarksQ8.2(c)

How can a student prove two lines on the paper are perpendicular without using a protractor?

Hands-on Geometry: Perpendicular Fold Test • Pending Notes 5.1
View Solution

Fold test: fold the sheet along one of the creases. If the other crease falls exactly on top of itself (overlaps with its own image), the two creases meet at $90^\circ$ — only a line perpendicular to the fold line maps onto itself.

Corner test: place the intersection point exactly on a corner of the square sheet with one crease along an edge; if the second crease lies exactly along the other edge of the corner, the angle is the paper's right angle, $90^\circ$.

✅ Answer: Fold along one crease — if the other crease exactly overlaps itself (or aligns with the square paper's corner edges), the angle is $90^\circ$
Case Study OR • 2 MarksQ8.2(OR)

✅ Answer: No — perpendicular means exactly $90^\circ$; a slightly smaller angle only makes the lines almost (not truly) perpendicular
