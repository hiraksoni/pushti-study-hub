Perimeter & Area

Pushti's Super Easy Learning Guide — You've Got This! 💪

🌟

Start here, Pushti!

Before formulas, let's understand what we're actually measuring. This is the most important part!

🏡 The Real World Story

Imagine you have a garden shaped like a rectangle. Now you need to do TWO different things:

🚧

Fencing around it

You walk around the edge. How much fence do you need?

That's PERIMETER!

🌱

Planting inside it

You fill the inside with grass. How much space is inside?

That's AREA!

🔑 The Golden Difference
📏

PERIMETER

= The OUTSIDE boundary
= Distance AROUND a shape
= Measured in cm, m, km

🟦

AREA

= The INSIDE space
= Space WITHIN a shape
= Measured in cm², m², km²

💡
Easy Memory Trick: PERIMETER has the word "PERI" which means "around" (like periscope = a tube to look around). AREA sounds like "air" — it fills up the space inside like air fills a room!
🌍 Where Do We Use These?
🎨Painting a wall (Area)
🏡Fencing a yard (Perimeter)
🖼️Framing a photo (Perimeter)
🏃Running around a track (Perimeter)
🏠Flooring a room (Area)
🌿Laying a carpet (Area)
🏊Swimming pool border tiles (Perimeter)
🌾Farming a field (Area)
📐 Units — Super Important!
⚠️ NEVER forget this
Perimeter → always in cm, m, km (normal length units)
Area → always in cm², m², km² (square units — because it's 2D space)
🎯 Why "squared"?

Area = length × breadth. When you multiply cm × cm, you get cm². That's why area units always have a little ² after them! 1 cm² means one tiny 1cm×1cm square.

📏 Perimeter
The distance all the way around a shape — like ants walking on the border!
🐜 Think of it this way...

An ant starts at one corner of a shape, walks along every side, and comes back to where it started. The total distance the ant walked = Perimeter!

📌 Simple Rule
Perimeter of ANY shape = Sum of ALL its sides
▬ Perimeter of Rectangle
P = 2 × (l + b)
OR P = 2l + 2b   |   l = length, b = breadth
💡
WHY 2 × (l + b)? Rectangle has 2 equal long sides (length) + 2 equal short sides (breadth). So: l + b + l + b = 2l + 2b = 2×(l+b). Simple!
✅ Example 1 — Easy
Q: Rectangle with length = 8 cm, breadth = 5 cm. Find Perimeter.
→ P = 2 × (l + b)
→ P = 2 × (8 + 5)
→ P = 2 × 13
→ P = 26 cm ✓
✅ Example 2 — Finding Missing Side!
Q: Perimeter of rectangle = 40 cm. Length = 12 cm. Find breadth.
→ P = 2 × (l + b)
→ 40 = 2 × (12 + b)
→ 40 ÷ 2 = 12 + b
→ 20 = 12 + b
→ b = 20 − 12 = 8 cm ✓
✅ Example 3 — Word Problem
Q: A swimming pool is 25 m long and 10 m wide. Tiles are placed along the border. How many meters of border is there?
→ "Border" = Perimeter!
→ P = 2 × (25 + 10) = 2 × 35 = 70 m ✓
🟪 Perimeter of Square
P = 4 × s
s = length of one side (all sides are equal in a square)
✅ Example 1
Q: Square with side = 7 cm. Find Perimeter.
→ P = 4 × 7 = 28 cm ✓
✅ Example 2 — Find side from perimeter
Q: Perimeter of square = 48 cm. Find side.
→ P = 4 × s
→ 48 = 4 × s
→ s = 48 ÷ 4 = 12 cm ✓
🔺 Perimeter of Triangle
P = a + b + c
a, b, c = the three sides of the triangle
✅ Example 1
Q: Triangle with sides 6 cm, 8 cm, 10 cm. Find Perimeter.
→ P = 6 + 8 + 10 = 24 cm ✓
✅ Example 2 — Equilateral Triangle (all sides equal!)
Q: Equilateral triangle with side = 9 cm. Find Perimeter.
→ All 3 sides = 9 cm each
→ P = 3 × 9 = 27 cm ✓
✅ Example 3 — Find Missing Side
Q: Perimeter of triangle = 35 cm. Two sides are 12 cm and 10 cm. Find third side.
→ 35 = 12 + 10 + third side
→ 35 = 22 + third side
→ Third side = 35 − 22 = 13 cm ✓
⬡ Perimeter of Regular Polygons

A regular polygon has ALL sides equal and ALL angles equal. Easy formula:

P = n × s
n = number of sides   |   s = length of one side
ShapeSides (n)Perimeter
Equilateral Triangle33 × side
Square44 × side
Regular Pentagon55 × side
Regular Hexagon66 × side
Regular Heptagon77 × side
Regular Octagon88 × side
✅ Example
Q: Regular hexagon with each side = 5 cm. Find Perimeter.
→ Hexagon has 6 sides
→ P = 6 × 5 = 30 cm ✓

Q: A square field has side 80 m. Priya walks 4 rounds. Total distance?
→ Perimeter of field = 4 × 80 = 320 m
→ 4 rounds = 4 × 320 = 1280 m ✓
🟦 Area
How much space is INSIDE a shape — measured in square units!
🔢 What is a Square Unit?

Imagine tiny squares, each 1 cm × 1 cm. When we count how many of these fit inside a shape — that's the area!

The purple part has 12 squares = Area of 12 cm² (if each square = 1 cm²)

▬ Area of Rectangle
Area = Length × Breadth
A = l × b   |   Answer will always be in cm², m², etc.
💡
Why? If a rectangle is 4 cm long and 3 cm wide, you can fit 4 squares across and 3 rows down. Total = 4 × 3 = 12 squares = 12 cm²
✅ Example 1 — Basic
Q: Rectangle, length = 9 cm, breadth = 6 cm. Find area.
→ Area = l × b = 9 × 6 = 54 cm² ✓
✅ Example 2 — Find missing side
Q: Area of rectangle = 48 cm². Length = 8 cm. Find breadth.
→ Area = l × b
→ 48 = 8 × b
→ b = 48 ÷ 8 = 6 cm ✓
✅ Example 3 — Cost problem
Q: A hall is 10 m × 8 m. Flooring costs ₹50 per m². Total cost?
→ Area = 10 × 8 = 80 m²
→ Cost = 80 × 50 = ₹4000 ✓
✅ Example 4 — Number of tiles
Q: Room is 6 m × 4 m. Tiles are 20 cm × 20 cm. How many tiles needed?
→ Convert room to cm: 600 cm × 400 cm
→ Area of room = 600 × 400 = 240000 cm²
→ Area of 1 tile = 20 × 20 = 400 cm²
→ Number of tiles = 240000 ÷ 400 = 600 tiles ✓
🟪 Area of Square
Area = s × s = s²
s = side of the square
✅ Example 1
Q: Square with side = 11 cm. Find area.
→ Area = 11 × 11 = 121 cm² ✓
✅ Example 2 — Find side from area
Q: Area of square = 64 cm². Find side.
→ s² = 64
→ s = √64 = 8 cm ✓
💡 Think: what number × itself = 64? Answer: 8 (because 8×8=64)
✂️ Area of Irregular Rectangles — Split & Add!

Some shapes look weird but they can be cut into smaller rectangles. Find area of each piece, then ADD them up!

✅ Example — L-Shaped Figure
Q: An L-shaped room. Top part: 6 m × 2 m. Bottom part: 4 m × 3 m. Find total area.
→ Area of top rectangle = 6 × 2 = 12 m²
→ Area of bottom rectangle = 4 × 3 = 12 m²
→ Total Area = 12 + 12 = 24 m² ✓
✅ Example — Find REMAINING area
Q: A 10 m × 8 m garden has a 3 m × 2 m flower bed inside. Find the remaining area.
→ Total garden area = 10 × 8 = 80 m²
→ Flower bed area = 3 × 2 = 6 m²
→ Remaining = 80 − 6 = 74 m² ✓
💡 Area Word Problem Tricks
Problem says...It means...
"Painting a wall / carpet / flooring / tiling"Find AREA
"Cost of painting at ₹X per m²"Cost = Area × X
"How many tiles needed?"Area of room ÷ Area of 1 tile
"Yield per hectare"Total yield = Area × yield per unit
⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid!
❌ Mistake 1

Writing area without ² (e.g., "24 cm" instead of "24 cm²") — ALWAYS write cm² for area!

❌ Mistake 2

Mixing up units. If length is in m and breadth in cm — convert BOTH to the same unit first!

❌ Mistake 3

Using the area formula for perimeter and vice versa. Remember: perimeter = around the edge. Area = inside space.

🌀 Irregular Shapes — Counting Method
When shapes are weird, we count squares on a grid!
📊 The Counting Rules

Place the shape on a square grid. Count squares using these rules:

  • 1
    FULL square covered by shape → Count as 1 sq. unit
  • 2
    MORE THAN HALF of a square covered → Count as 1 sq. unit
  • 3
    EXACTLY HALF of a square covered → Count as ½ sq. unit
  • 4
    LESS THAN HALF covered → Count as 0 (ignore it!)
💡
Easy trick to remember: "More than half = 1, Exactly half = ½, Less than half = 0". Think of it like rounding off — if it's mostly covered, count it!
✅ Fully Worked Example

Suppose a shape on grid has these squares:

Counting Step by Step
Fully covered squares = 10 → count as 10 × 1 = 10 sq. units
More than half covered = 4 → count as 4 × 1 = 4 sq. units
Half squares = 2 → count as 2 × ½ = 1 sq. unit
Less than half = 3 → count as 3 × 0 = 0 sq. units
→ Total Area = 10 + 4 + 1 + 0 = 15 sq. units ✓
🎯 Make a table!

Always organize your counting in a table with 4 rows: Full, More than half, Half, Less than half. Then add up the areas in the last column. This avoids missing any square!

A cloud shape on grid. Count:

🔺 Area of a Triangle
A triangle is always HALF of a rectangle — that's the secret!
🔍 Why Half a Rectangle?

If you draw a triangle inside a rectangle, the triangle covers exactly half the rectangle. That's why:

Area of Triangle = ½ × Base × Height
Also written as: A = (b × h) ÷ 2
💡
Important: The HEIGHT must be PERPENDICULAR (at 90°) to the base. It's NOT always a side of the triangle — it's the vertical distance!
✅ Worked Examples
Example 1 — Basic
Q: Triangle with base = 8 cm, height = 6 cm. Find area.
→ Area = ½ × 8 × 6
→ = ½ × 48 = 24 cm² ✓
Example 2 — Find height
Q: Area of triangle = 30 cm². Base = 10 cm. Find height.
→ 30 = ½ × 10 × h
→ 30 = 5 × h
→ h = 30 ÷ 5 = 6 cm ✓
Example 3 — Dividing complex shapes
Q: Figure has a rectangle (5 cm × 4 cm) and a triangle on top (base 5 cm, height 3 cm). Find total area.
→ Area of rectangle = 5 × 4 = 20 cm²
→ Area of triangle = ½ × 5 × 3 = 7.5 cm²
→ Total = 20 + 7.5 = 27.5 cm² ✓

▬
Rectangle
Area = l × b
🟦
Square
Area = s × s
🔺
Triangle
Area = ½ × b × h

Question 1 — Perimeter of Rectangle 🟦
A rectangular garden is 15 m long and 9 m wide. Find its perimeter. How much wire is needed to fence it 3 times?
✅ SOLUTION
Perimeter = 2 × (l + b) = 2 × (15 + 9) = 2 × 24 = 48 m
Wire for 3 rounds = 3 × 48 = 144 m ✓
Question 2 — Area of Square 🟪
A square playground has side 25 m. Find its area. If it costs ₹12 per m² to lay grass, what is the total cost?
✅ SOLUTION
Area = s × s = 25 × 25 = 625 m²
Cost = 625 × 12 = ₹7500 ✓
Question 3 — Missing Side 🔍
Perimeter of a rectangle is 56 cm. Its breadth is 10 cm. Find its length and area.
✅ SOLUTION
56 = 2 × (l + 10) → 28 = l + 10 → l = 18 cm
Area = 18 × 10 = 180 cm² ✓
Question 4 — Regular Hexagon ⬡
A regular hexagon has perimeter 54 cm. Find the length of each side. If this hexagon were changed to an equilateral triangle of the same perimeter, what would each side of the triangle be?
✅ SOLUTION
Hexagon has 6 sides → each side = 54 ÷ 6 = 9 cm
Same perimeter (54 cm) for equilateral triangle (3 sides):
Each side = 54 ÷ 3 = 18 cm ✓
Question 5 — Area with Units Conversion 📐
A room is 5 m long and 4 m wide. Square tiles of side 50 cm are used to tile the floor. How many tiles are needed?
✅ SOLUTION
Room in cm: 500 cm × 400 cm
Area of room = 500 × 400 = 200,000 cm²
Area of 1 tile = 50 × 50 = 2500 cm²
Number of tiles = 200,000 ÷ 2,500 = 80 tiles ✓
Question 6 — Triangle Area 🔺
A triangular garden has base 14 m and height 10 m. Find its area. If plants are planted at every 2 m², how many plants can be planted?
✅ SOLUTION
Area = ½ × 14 × 10 = ½ × 140 = 70 m²
Plants = 70 ÷ 2 = 35 plants ✓
Question 7 — Counting Method 🌀
On a grid, a shape covers: 15 full squares, 6 more-than-half squares, 4 half squares, and 8 less-than-half squares. Find the area.
✅ SOLUTION
Full squares: 15 × 1 = 15
More than half: 6 × 1 = 6
Half squares: 4 × ½ = 2
Less than half: 8 × 0 = 0
Total Area = 15 + 6 + 2 + 0 = 23 sq. units ✓
Question 8 — Comparison Problem 🏆
Two shapes have the same perimeter of 60 cm. One is a square and one is a rectangle with length 20 cm. Which has greater area and by how much?
✅ SOLUTION
Square: side = 60 ÷ 4 = 15 cm → Area = 15 × 15 = 225 cm²
Rectangle: 60 = 2×(20+b) → b = 10 cm → Area = 20×10 = 200 cm²
Square has greater area by 225 − 200 = 25 cm² ✓
Key insight: Among all rectangles with same perimeter, the square has the LARGEST area!
Question 9 — Word Problem 🏡
A farmer has a rectangular field of 80 m × 60 m. He wants to fence it with 3 rounds of wire. Wire costs ₹5 per metre. What is the total cost?
✅ SOLUTION
Perimeter = 2 × (80 + 60) = 2 × 140 = 280 m
Total wire = 3 × 280 = 840 m
Cost = 840 × 5 = ₹4200 ✓

🧠 Golden Memory Tricks
⭐
PERIMETER = "peri" means around. Walk around the edge. Add all sides.
⭐
AREA = fills the inside, like air fills a room. Always cm² — because length × length = length²
