The Foundation

FACTOR What is a Factor?
Definition

A factor of a number divides it exactly (no remainder). If A × B = N, then both A and B are factors of N.

Example: Factors of 12 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12 because 12÷1=12 ✓, 12÷2=6 ✓, 12÷3=4 ✓, 12÷4=3 ✓, 12÷6=2 ✓, 12÷12=1 ✓

⚡ Key Facts about Factors
  • 1 is a factor of every number.
  • Every number is a factor of itself.
  • A factor is always ≤ the number (never bigger).
  • Every number has a finite (limited) number of factors.
  • The factors of a number are always less than or equal to the number.
MULTIPLE What is a Multiple?
Definition

A multiple of a number is what you get when you multiply it by 1, 2, 3, 4, … It is always divisible by the number.

Example: Multiples of 6 → 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, 36… (never ending!)

⚡ Key Facts about Multiples
  • Every number is a multiple of itself.
  • A multiple is always ≥ the number (never smaller).
  • Every number has infinite (unlimited) multiples.
  • Every multiple of a number is divisible by that number.
⚖️ Factor vs Multiple — The Big Difference

🧠 Memory Trick

Factor = Smaller (goes into) · Multiple = Bigger (comes out of)
Think: "Factories make parts (factors) that are smaller than the whole product."

🔢 Prime & Composite Numbers
PRIME

Exactly 2 factors: 1 and itself.
Examples: 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19…

COMPOSITE

More than 2 factors.
Examples: 4, 6, 8, 9, 10, 12, 15…

⭐ Must-Know Special Facts
  • 1 is NEITHER prime nor composite.
  • 2 is the ONLY even prime number.
  • All prime numbers except 2 are odd.
  • Twin primes: pairs differing by 2 → (3,5), (5,7), (11,13), (17,19)…
  • Co-primes: two numbers whose HCF is 1. E.g. (8,9), (4,15).
  • Perfect number: sum of all factors = 2× the number. E.g. 6 (1+2+3+6=12=2×6), 28.

🌳 Prime Factorisation
Definition

Writing a number as a product of only prime numbers. Every composite number can be factorised in exactly one unique way (Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic).

Two methods:

1
Divide by smallest prime that goes in

Start with 2, then 3, then 5, etc.

2
Keep dividing the quotient

Until quotient = 1

3
Multiply all divisors

That gives the prime factorisation!

📌 Example: 360
2 | 360 2 | 180 2 | 90 3 | 45 3 | 15 5 | 5 1 360 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 × 5 = 2³ × 3² × 5

Split the number into any two factors, then keep splitting until all branches are prime.

📌 Example: 60
60 / \ 6 10 / \ / \ 2 3 2 5 60 = 2 × 3 × 2 × 5 = 2² × 3 × 5

No matter how you split, the final prime factors are always the same! ✨

Divisibility Rules & Tricks

These rules let you check divisibility without actually dividing! Learn them and save lots of time. 🚀

÷2
Last digit is even: 0, 2, 4, 6, or 8
💡 Just check the units digit!
✅ 348 (8 is even)
❌ 573 (3 is odd)
÷3
Sum of all digits is divisible by 3
💡 Add digits, apply rule again!
✅ 681: 6+8+1=15, 15÷3=5 ✓
❌ 257: 2+5+7=14 ✗
÷4
Last two digits form a number divisible by 4
💡 Cover all but last 2 digits!
✅ 1812 (12÷4=3 ✓)
❌ 4013 (13÷4 rem 1 ✗)
÷5
Last digit is 0 or 5
💡 Easiest rule ever! 😄
✅ 485 (ends 5 ✓)
❌ 492 (ends 2 ✗)
÷6
Divisible by both 2 AND 3
💡 Check ÷2 first, then ÷3!
✅ 114: even + 1+1+4=6 ✓
❌ 308: even but 3+0+8=11 ✗
÷7
Double the last digit, subtract from rest. Result divisible by 7
💡 Repeat if needed!
602: 60-(2×2)=56=7×8 ✓
505: 50-10=40 ✗
÷8
Last three digits form a number divisible by 8
💡 Only look at last 3 digits!
✅ 5816 (816÷8=102 ✓)
❌ 12302 (302÷8 ✗)
÷9
Sum of all digits is divisible by 9
💡 Like ÷3 but stricter!
✅ 1269: 1+2+6+9=18 ✓
❌ 2013: 2+0+1+3=6 ✗
÷10
Last digit is 0
💡 Ends in zero = done!
✅ 420 ✓
❌ 231 ✗
÷11
Difference of (sum of odd-place digits) – (sum of even-place digits) = 0 or 11
💡 Alternating sum trick!
✅ 1364: (1+6)-(3+4)=0 ✓
❌ 3729: (3+2)-(7+9)= -11 ✓
📐 Important Properties of Divisibility
Rule 1

If a number divides two numbers, it divides their sum and difference too.
E.g. 4 divides 16 and 20 → 4 divides 16+20=36 and 20-16=4 ✓

Rule 2

If a number is divisible by two co-prime numbers, it's divisible by their product too.
E.g. 80 is divisible by 4 and 5 (co-primes), so 80 is divisible by 20 ✓

Rule 3

If A divides B, then A also divides every multiple of B.
E.g. 6 divides 12, so 6 divides 24, 36, 48… ✓

⚡ Mental Maths Tricks

✅ Sum of digits divisible by 3?
Both yes → divisible by 12!

HCF — Highest Common Factor

What is HCF?

The Highest Common Factor (HCF) of two or more numbers is the largest number that divides ALL of them exactly. Also called GCD (Greatest Common Divisor).

🎯
HCF → Think Highest = Biggest factor common to all numbers
"How small can I cut things equally?" → HCF!
METHOD 1 Listing All Factors
1
Write all factors of each number
2
Find common factors (appear in all lists)
3
Pick the largest → That's your HCF!
📌 Find HCF of 24 and 36
Factors of 24: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24
Factors of 36: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 18, 36
Common factors: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12
HCF = 12 ✓

⚠️ Use this method only for small numbers. For big numbers, use prime factorisation.

METHOD 2 Prime Factorisation Method
1
Write prime factorisation of each number
2
Find common prime factors
3
Multiply them (take the MINIMUM power of each) → HCF
📌 Find HCF of 42, 84, and 24
2 | 42 2 | 84 2 | 24 3 | 21 2 | 42 2 | 12 7 | 7 3 | 21 2 | 6 1 7 | 7 3 | 3 1 1 42 = 2 × 3 × 7 84 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 7 24 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 Common primes: 2 (min power: 2¹) and 3 (min power: 3¹)
HCF = 2 × 3 = 6 ✓
🔑 Key Rule

For HCF → take common primes with their LOWEST power. Think: "What do they ALL share?"

METHOD 3 Division Method (Euclid's Algorithm)

Best for large numbers where prime factorisation is hard.

1
Divide the bigger number by the smaller
2
Take remainder → divide the previous divisor by this remainder
3
Repeat until remainder = 0. Last divisor is HCF!
📌 HCF of 18 and 30
30 ÷ 18 = 1, remainder 12 18 ÷ 12 = 1, remainder 6 12 ÷ 6 = 2, remainder 0 ← STOP!
HCF = 6 ✓ (last divisor when remainder = 0)
📌 HCF of 58 and 70
70 ÷ 58 = 1, remainder 12 58 ÷ 12 = 4, remainder 10 12 ÷ 10 = 1, remainder 2 10 ÷ 2 = 5, remainder 0 ← STOP!
HCF = 2 ✓
⭐ Key Facts About HCF
HCF ≤ smallest number
HCF of co-primes = 1
HCF of prime numbers = 1
HCF is a factor of LCM
HCF of consecutive numbers = 1
🔗 Golden Relationship

HCF × LCM = First Number × Second Number
So if you know HCF, you can find LCM, and vice versa!

LCM — Least Common Multiple

What is LCM?

The Least Common Multiple (LCM) of two or more numbers is the smallest number that is exactly divisible by ALL of them.

🎯
LCM → Think Least = Smallest multiple common to all numbers
"When do they all meet again?" → LCM!
METHOD 1 Listing Multiples

Good for small numbers only.

📌 LCM of 4 and 6
Multiples of 4: 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24…
Multiples of 6: 6, 12, 18, 24…
Common: 12, 24…
LCM = 12 (smallest common) ✓
METHOD 2 Prime Factorisation Method
1
Write prime factorisation of each number
2
List ALL prime factors that appear in any number
3
Take HIGHEST power of each prime → Multiply = LCM
📌 LCM of 12, 18, and 24
12 = 2 × 2 × 3 = 2² × 3¹ 18 = 2 × 3 × 3 = 2¹ × 3² 24 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 = 2³ × 3¹ Highest power of 2: 2³ = 8 Highest power of 3: 3² = 9
LCM = 8 × 9 = 72 ✓
🔑 Key Rule

For LCM → take ALL primes with their HIGHEST power. Think: "What's needed to cover everyone?"

METHOD 3 Common Division Method
1
Write numbers in a row
2
Divide by smallest prime that divides AT LEAST ONE number. Carry forward numbers not divisible.
3
Repeat until all numbers become 1
4
Multiply all divisors → LCM
📌 LCM of 36, 45, and 60
2 | 36, 45, 60 2 | 18, 45, 30 3 | 9, 45, 15 3 | 3, 15, 5 5 | 1, 5, 5 1, 1, 1
LCM = 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 × 5 = 180 ✓

⭐ Key Facts About LCM
LCM ≥ largest number
LCM of co-primes = their product
LCM of prime numbers = their product
HCF divides LCM always

Real Life — When to Use What?

🤔 HCF or LCM? The Decision Guide
USE HCF WHEN...

You want to SPLIT, DIVIDE or CUT things equally into the largest possible equal groups/pieces.

USE LCM WHEN...

You want to find when things happen TOGETHER AGAIN, or find the smallest number that fits all conditions.

🔴 HCF Real-Life Situations

Three ropes of 120 cm, 136 cm, 160 cm. Cut all into equal pieces — no waste. Longest piece? → HCF(120,136,160)

A room 825cm × 675cm. Largest square tile that fits without cutting? → HCF(825,675)

136 apples, 170 mangoes, 255 oranges → arrange in equal heaps. Max items per heap → HCF(136,170,255)

Tanks with 403L, 434L, 465L of diesel. Largest container that measures all exactly? → HCF(403,434,465)

42, 63, 105 students in 3 subjects seated equally. Max per room? → HCF(42,63,105). Number of rooms = Total ÷ HCF

🔑 HCF Keywords to watch for

"maximum", "largest", "greatest", "longest", "equal pieces", "exactly measure", "no waste", "divided equally", "split into groups"

🟢 LCM Real-Life Situations

Three bells ring every 30, 36, 45 min. Ring together at 8am. Next time together? → LCM(30,36,45) minutes after 8am

Three joggers complete a lap in 36, 24, 12 minutes. When do all meet at start together? → LCM(36,24,12) minutes

Apples come in boxes of 12, 15, 18. Minimum apples to fill all boxes exactly? → LCM(12,15,18)

Steps of 80cm, 70cm, 75cm. Minimum distance they all cover in complete steps? → LCM(80,70,75) cm

Smallest 4-digit number divisible by 8, 10, 12 → First find LCM(8,10,12), then find smallest 4-digit multiple of that LCM.

🔑 LCM Keywords to watch for

"minimum", "smallest", "least", "together again", "at the same time", "simultaneously", "next time", "after how many days/minutes", "common meeting"

📌 Fully Solved Word Problems
🔴 HCF Problem — Type: Cutting into equal pieces
Three strings have lengths 120 cm, 136 cm and 160 cm. They are to be cut into equal pieces of maximum length. Find that length. How many pieces in total?
🔍 DECODE THIS PROBLEM

Numbers: 120, 136, 160
Keywords: "cut into equal pieces" + "maximum length" → HCF!
Goal: Find HCF(120, 136, 160), then find total pieces.

2 | 120, 136, 160 2 | 60, 68, 80 2 | 30, 34, 40 15, 17, 20 HCF = 2 × 2 × 2 = 8
✅ Maximum length of each piece = 8 cm
Pieces from 120 cm = 120 ÷ 8 = 15
Pieces from 136 cm = 136 ÷ 8 = 17
Pieces from 160 cm = 160 ÷ 8 = 20
Max length = 8 cm | Total pieces = 15+17+20 = 52
🔴 HCF Problem — Type: Maximum container
Three tankers contain 403 litres, 434 litres and 465 litres of diesel. Find the maximum capacity of a container that can measure the diesel of all three tankers an exact number of times.
🔍 DECODE THIS PROBLEM

Numbers: 403, 434, 465
Keywords: "maximum capacity" + "measure exactly" → HCF!
Why HCF? We need one container size that divides all three amounts perfectly.

HCF of 403 and 434: 403) 434 (1 -403 31) 403 (13 -403 0 HCF(403,434) = 31 HCF of 31 and 465: 31) 465 (15 -465 0 HCF = 31
Maximum container capacity = 31 litres
🟢 LCM Problem — Type: Bells ringing together
Three bells ring at intervals of 30, 36 and 45 minutes respectively. At what time will they ring together again, if they first ring simultaneously at 8 a.m.?
🔍 DECODE THIS PROBLEM

Numbers: 30, 36, 45
Keywords: "ring together again" + "simultaneously" → LCM!
Why LCM? We want the smallest time when ALL three bells coincide.

2 | 30, 36, 45 2 | 15, 18, 45 3 | 15, 9, 45 3 | 5, 3, 15 5 | 5, 1, 5 1, 1, 1 LCM = 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 × 5 = 180 minutes
180 minutes = 3 hours
Bells ring together again at 8 + 3 = 11 a.m.
🟢 LCM Problem — Type: Smallest/largest number
Find the smallest 4-digit number which is exactly divisible by 8, 10 and 12.
🔍 DECODE THIS PROBLEM

Numbers: 8, 10, 12
Keywords: "smallest" + "exactly divisible by all" → Find LCM first!
Then: Find the smallest 4-digit multiple of that LCM.

2 | 8, 10, 12 2 | 4, 5, 6 2 | 2, 5, 3 1, 5, 3 LCM = 2×2×2×3×5 = 120 Smallest 4-digit number = 1000 1000 ÷ 120 = 8 remainder 40 So 8 × 120 = 960 (3-digit) Next: 9 × 120 = 1080
Smallest 4-digit number = 1080
⚡ TRICKY Problem — Remainder type
Find the least number which when divided by 6, 15 and 18 leaves remainder 5 in each case.
🔍 DECODE THIS PROBLEM

Numbers: 6, 15, 18 with remainder 5
Trick: The number we want = LCM + remainder
Why? If remainder = 5, the number is exactly 5 MORE than a multiple of LCM.

LCM of 6, 15, 18: 2 | 6, 15, 18 3 | 3, 15, 9 3 | 1, 5, 3 5 | 1, 5, 1 1, 1, 1 LCM = 2 × 3 × 3 × 5 = 90
Required number = LCM + remainder = 90 + 5 = 95
Check: 95÷6=15 rem 5 ✓, 95÷15=6 rem 5 ✓, 95÷18=5 rem 5 ✓
Answer = 90 + 5 = 95
⚡ TRICKY Problem — "Greatest number that divides leaving remainder"
What is the greatest number that divides 615 and 963, leaving remainder 6 in each case?
🔍 DECODE THIS PROBLEM

Trick: Subtract the remainder first → then find HCF!
Why? If remainder is 6, then (number − 6) is exactly divisible. So find HCF of (615−6) and (963−6).

Subtract remainder: 615 − 6 = 609 and 963 − 6 = 957
609) 957 (1 -609 348) 609 (1 -348 261) 348 (1 -261 87) 261 (3 -261 0
Greatest number = HCF(609, 957) = 87
🚀 Quick Formula Summary for Word Problems
REMAINDER TYPE — LCM

"Least number leaving remainder r when divided by a, b, c"
→ Answer = LCM(a,b,c) + r

REMAINDER TYPE — HCF

"Greatest number dividing a, b, c leaving remainder r"
→ Subtract r from each → Answer = HCF(a−r, b−r, c−r)

DIFFERENT REMAINDERS — HCF

"Greatest number dividing a leaving r₁, b leaving r₂"
→ Answer = HCF(a−r₁, b−r₂)

FIND OTHER NUMBER

HCF × LCM = First Number × Second Number
→ Other number = (HCF × LCM) ÷ given number

Q14. (Relationship) The LCM of two co-prime numbers is 221. If one number is 17, find the other.

Q15. The HCF of two consecutive numbers is always:
(a) 2    (b) 1    (c) The larger number    (d) 0
Answer: (b) 1
Consecutive numbers are always co-prime!
Q16. LCM of two prime numbers is always their:
(a) HCF    (b) Sum    (c) Product    (d) Difference
Answer: (c) Product
Two different primes share no common factors, so LCM = product.
Q17. If HCF of two numbers is 1, then their LCM is:
(a) 1    (b) Sum    (c) Product    (d) Difference
Answer: (c) Product
HCF×LCM = a×b → 1×LCM = a×b → LCM = a×b
Q18. Can two numbers have 16 as HCF and 294 as LCM?

Answer: (b) No!
