📘 What is an Arithmetic Expression?

An arithmetic expression is a mathematical phrase made of numbers and operators (+, −, ×, ÷) — and sometimes brackets — arranged to show a calculation.

💡 Simple Expression: Only ONE operation, no brackets. E.g.: 5 + 4, 6 × 3, 12 − 5
Complex Expression: Multiple operations and/or brackets. E.g.: 8 × 5 + 2, (8 + 4) − 6 × 3 + 100
🌸 Key Insight: Different expressions can give the same value!
$8 + 2 = 12 - 2 = 5 \times 2 = 20 \div 2 = 10$ — four different ways to get 10!
🏗️ BODMAS — The Order of Operations Core Rule

When an expression has multiple operations, BODMAS tells us which to do first:

BBrackets
OOf (Powers)
DDivision
MMultiplication
AAddition
SSubtraction
⚡ D and M go left-to-right (whichever comes first). Same for A and S.
Solve: $8 \times 5 + 2$
1No brackets → do Multiplication first: $8 \times 5 = 40$
2Then Addition: $40 + 2 = \mathbf{42}$
Solve: $3 \times 50 + 100 - 20$
1Multiplication first: $3 \times 50 = 150$
2Left to right — Addition: $150 + 100 = 250$
3Then Subtraction: $250 - 20 = \mathbf{230}$
⚠️ Common Mistake: Always do Multiplication and Division before Addition and Subtraction.
$8 \times 5 + 2 \neq 8 \times 7$! Multiply first, then add.
🔤 Terms in an Expression

Terms are the individual parts of an expression separated by the + (plus) sign. When we see subtraction, we rewrite it as adding a negative number.

How many terms in: $25 + 8$?

Two terms: 25 and 8

How many terms in: $13 - 2 + 6$?

Rewrite: $13 + (-2) + 6$ → Three terms: 13, −2, and 6

⚠️ Multiplication/Division doesn't split terms!
$6 \times 5$ is a single term. $4 + 7 \times 8$ has TWO terms: 4 and (7×8).
🔁 Additive Inverse

The additive inverse of a number is what you add to it to get zero — just flip the sign!

$a + (-a) = 0$  |  Additive inverse of $a$ is $-a$
💡 Examples:
Additive inverse of $12$ is $-12$  (since $12 + (-12) = 0$)
Additive inverse of $-7$ is $7$  (since $-7 + 7 = 0$)
Additive inverse of $-(10 \times 5) = -50$ is $50$
🌸 Key Link: Every subtraction is really adding the additive inverse!
$90 - 30 = 90 + (-30)$
🔓 Removing Brackets
➕
Plus before bracket: Signs inside stay the SAME
$8 + (3 + 2) = 8 + 3 + 2 = 13$
$8 + (3 - 2) = 8 + 3 - 2 = 9$
➖
Minus before bracket: Signs inside FLIP (+ becomes −, − becomes +)
$8 - (3 + 2) = 8 - 3 - 2 = 3$
$8 - (3 - 2) = 8 - 3 + 2 = 7$
✖️
Multiplication before bracket: Multiply each term inside (Distributive)
$3 \times (4 + 2) = 3 \times 4 + 3 \times 2 = 12 + 6 = 18$
$3 \times (4 - 2) = 3 \times 4 - 3 \times 2 = 12 - 6 = 6$
Remove brackets: $55 - (4 + 3) \times 6$
1Brackets first: $(4 + 3) = 7$
2Multiply: $7 \times 6 = 42$
3Subtract: $55 - 42 = \mathbf{13}$
Remove brackets: $100 - (40 + 30 - 10)$
1Minus before bracket → flip all signs inside
2$= 100 - 40 - 30 + 10$
3$= 60 - 30 + 10 = \mathbf{40}$
🔄 Properties of Addition Must Know
🔄 Commutative (Swapping)
a + b = b + a

Order doesn't matter!
$12 + (-3) = (-3) + 12 = 9$

⚠️ Does NOT work for subtraction: $9 - 4 \neq 4 - 9$

🗂️ Associative (Grouping)
(a + b) + c = a + (b + c)

Grouping doesn't matter!
$(3 + 6) + 4 = 3 + (6 + 4) = 13$

⚠️ Does NOT work for subtraction: $(5-3)-1 \neq 5-(3-1)$

📤 Distributive
a × (b + c) = a×b + a×c

$3 \times (4 + 2) = 3\times4 + 3\times2 = 18$

Also works with subtraction: $a \times (b - c) = a\times b - a\times c$

🔢 Additive Identity
a + 0 = a

Adding zero doesn't change a number.
$25 + 0 = 25$

⚠️ Memory Trick:
Commutative = Commute (travel) = Numbers can travel/swap positions
Associative = Associate (group) = Numbers can group differently
✂️ Smart Multiplication — "Tinker the Terms" Shortcut

When we know $a \times b = c$, we can find nearby products without recalculating from scratch — using the distributive property!

If $45 \times 12 = 540$, find $55 \times 12$
1$55 = 45 + 10$
2$55 \times 12 = (45 + 10) \times 12 = 45\times12 + 10\times12$
3$= 540 + 120 = \mathbf{660}$
If $525 \times 16 = 8400$, find $515 \times 16$
1$515 = 525 - 10$
2$515 \times 16 = (525 - 10) \times 16 = 525\times16 - 10\times16$
3$= 8400 - 160 = \mathbf{8240}$
💡 Mental Math Superpower:
$99 \times 7 = (100 - 1) \times 7 = 700 - 7 = \mathbf{693}$
$25 \times 12 = 25 \times (10 + 2) = 250 + 50 = \mathbf{300}$
$18 \times 6 = (20 - 2) \times 6 = 120 - 12 = \mathbf{108}$
⚖️ Comparing Expressions — Without Calculating! NCERT

We can compare two expressions and decide which is greater without computing the exact values — by reasoning about the difference between them.

Is $245 + 289$ greater or less than $246 + 285$?
1LHS has 245 (one less than 246) → −1 effect
2LHS has 289 (four more than 285) → +4 effect
3Net: $-1 + 4 = +3$ → LHS is 3 more
✓$245 + 289 > 246 + 285$ ✅
Fill blank: $312 + 8 = 308 + \;\_\_\_$
1312 is 4 MORE than 308
2So the RHS second number must be 4 MORE than 8 to keep balance
3$8 + 4 = \mathbf{12}$ → Blank = 12 (since $312 + 8 = 308 + 12 = 320$)
TechniqueIdea
Balance the see-sawBoth sides must have equal value; if one number goes up, adjust the other accordingly
Check the net changeAdd up all +/− changes from one side to the other; net change = 0 means equal
Smart comparisonDon't calculate! Just find which side has more/less total
🌸 Why this matters: In exams, these "fill the blank" or "compare expressions" questions can be solved in seconds using reasoning — no long calculation needed!

📡 Claude's Corner Bonus · Not for Exam

Arithmetic expressions are everywhere — in your mobile bill, cricket score, GST shopping, and even space missions! Let's explore the cool side of expressions. 🚀

🛒 Expressions Run Your Life
📱 Mobile Bill: Monthly bill = (calls × rate per min) + (data × rate per GB) + fixed charge. That's a real arithmetic expression!
🏏 Cricket Score: If a batsman hits 4 boundaries and 2 sixes: $4\times4 + 2\times6 = 16 + 12 = 28$ runs. Pure arithmetic expression!
🛍️ GST Calculation: Final price = original price + (original price × GST rate ÷ 100). Item at ₹500 with 18% GST: $500 + 500 \times 18 \div 100 = 500 + 90 = ₹590$
💻 How Computers Use BODMAS

Every programming language — Python, Java, C++ — follows the exact same BODMAS rules you're learning! When you type 3 + 4 * 2 in Python, it gives 11, not 14, because multiplication comes first.

🐛 Famous NASA Bug: The Mars Climate Orbiter (1999) crashed because one team used metric units and another used imperial units in their expressions. A ₹4,500 crore spacecraft was lost due to a unit error in an arithmetic expression! This is why getting expressions right matters — even in real engineering.
🤔 Why Does Multiplication Come Before Addition?

Because multiplication is repeated addition! When you write $2 + 3 \times 4$, the "3 × 4" means "add 3, four times" = 12. So you're really saying "2 plus (3 added 4 times)" = 2 + 12 = 14.

🧩 Real-life reason: "I have 2 apples and bought 3 bags of 4 apples each." Total = $2 + 3\times4 = 14$. You'd never add 2+3=5 first — that changes the meaning completely!
🎮 The Four Fours Puzzle

Use exactly four 4s to make any number from 1–10:

1$44 \div 44 = 1$
2$4 \div 4 + 4 \div 4 = 2$
3$(4 + 4 + 4) \div 4 = 3$
4$4 \times (4 - 4) + 4 = 4$
5$(4 \times 4 + 4) \div 4 = 5$
💡 Can you make 6, 7, 8, 9, and 10 using exactly four 4s? This is called the "Four Fours Problem" — a famous math puzzle enjoyed by professional mathematicians!
🎯 Distributive Property in Mental Maths

The distributive property makes mental maths super fast!

💡$99 \times 7 = (100 - 1) \times 7 = 700 - 7 = \mathbf{693}$
💡$25 \times 12 = 25 \times (10 + 2) = 250 + 50 = \mathbf{300}$
💡$18 \times 6 = (20 - 2) \times 6 = 120 - 12 = \mathbf{108}$
🌸 Pro Tip: Whenever you need to multiply a large number, break it into a round number ± a small number and use the distributive property. This is how shopkeepers and traders have calculated for centuries!
