📘 What is an Arithmetic Expression?

An arithmetic expression is a mathematical phrase made of numbers and operators (+, −, ×, ÷) and sometimes brackets, arranged to show a calculation.

💡 Simple Expression: Only ONE operation, no brackets. E.g.: 5 + 4, 6 × 3, 12 − 5
Complex Expression: Multiple operations or has brackets. E.g.: 8 × 5 + 2, (8 + 4) − 6 × 3 + 100
🌸 Different expressions can have the same value! 8 + 2 = 12 − 2 = 5 × 2 = 20 ÷ 2 = 10 — all equal 10 in four different ways!

🔗 Additive Inverse of a Number

The additive inverse of a number is what you add to it to get zero. It's just the number with its sign flipped!

a + (−a) = 0  |  Additive inverse of a is −a
💡 Examples:
Additive inverse of 12 is −12 (since 12 + (−12) = 0)
Additive inverse of −7 is 7 (since −7 + 7 = 0)
Additive inverse of −25 is 25
🌸 Key Rule: All subtractions can be rewritten as adding the inverse! 90 − 30 = 90 + (−30)
🔤 Terms in an Expression

Terms are the individual parts of an expression separated by the + (plus) sign.

How many terms in: 25 + 8?

Two terms: 25 and 8

How many terms in: 13 − 2 + 6?

= 13 + (−2) + 6 → Three terms: 13, −2, and 6

⚠️ Important: Expressions like 6 × 5 or 12 ÷ 6 are single terms because there's no '+' or '−' sign separating parts. Multiplication/division alone does NOT break an expression into multiple terms.
🔄 Swapping and Grouping Properties
🔄 Commutative Property (Swapping)
Changing the order of terms does NOT affect the sum.
a + b = b + a
Example: 12 + (−3) = (−3) + 12 = 9
⚠️ Does NOT work for subtraction: 9 − 4 ≠ 4 − 9
🗂️ Associative Property (Grouping)
Grouping terms differently does NOT affect the sum.
(a + b) + c = a + (b + c)
Example: (3 + 6) + 4 = 3 + (6 + 4) = 13
⚠️ Does NOT work for subtraction: (5−3)−1 ≠ 5−(3−1)
📤 Distributive Property
Multiplying a number by a sum = sum of the products.
a × (b + c) = a×b + a×c
Example: 3 × (4 + 2) = 3×4 + 3×2 = 12 + 6 = 18
🔓 Removing Brackets
➕ Plus sign before bracket: Signs inside stay the SAME
Example: 8 + (3 + 2) = 8 + 3 + 2
➖ Minus sign before bracket: Signs inside FLIP (+ becomes −, − becomes +)
Example: 8 − (3 + 2) = 8 − 3 − 2
Example: 8 − (3 − 2) = 8 − 3 + 2
✖️ Multiplication before bracket: Multiply each term inside
Example: 3 × (4 + 2) = 3×4 + 3×2 = 12 + 6 = 18
Example: 3 × (4 − 2) = 3×4 − 3×2 = 12 − 6 = 6
Remove brackets: 55 − (4 + 3) × 6
1Brackets first: (4 + 3) = 7
2Multiply: 7 × 6 = 42
3Subtract: 55 − 42 = 13
✂️ Tinker the Terms

When we know a×b = c, we can find nearby products without recalculating from scratch!

If 45 × 12 = 540, find 55 × 12
155 = 45 + 10
255 × 12 = (45 + 10) × 12 = 45×12 + 10×12
3= 540 + 120 = 660
If 525 × 16 = 8400, find 515 × 16
1515 = 525 − 10
2515 × 16 = (525 − 10) × 16 = 525×16 − 10×16
3= 8400 − 160 = 8240
💡 Comparing Expressions without calculating:
Is 245+289 > or < 246+285?
245 is 1 less than 246, but 289 is 4 more than 285. Net gain = +3. So 245+289 > 246+285!

📡 Claude's Corner Bonus · Not for Exam

Arithmetic expressions are everywhere — in your phone bill, grocery shopping, game scores, and even space missions! Let's explore the cool side of expressions. 🚀

🛒 How Expressions Run Your Life
📱 Mobile Bill: Your monthly bill = (calls × rate per min) + (data × rate per GB) + fixed charge. That's a real arithmetic expression!
🏏 Cricket Score: Team total = Σ(runs per over). When a batsman hits 4 boundaries and 2 sixes: 4×4 + 2×6 = 16 + 12 = 28 runs. Pure arithmetic expression!
🛍️ GST Calculation: Final price = original price × (1 + GST rate/100). If an item costs ₹500 with 18% GST: 500 × 118/100 = 500 + 90 = ₹590
💻 How Computers Use BODMAS

Every programming language — Python, Java, C++ — follows the exact same BODMAS rules you're learning! When you type 3 + 4 * 2 in Python, it gives 11, not 14, because multiplication comes first.

🐛 Famous Bug: NASA's Mars Climate Orbiter (1999) crashed because one team used metric units and another used imperial units in their expressions. A ₹4,500 crore spacecraft was lost due to a unit error in an arithmetic expression!

This is why getting expressions RIGHT matters — even in real engineering!

🤔 The BODMAS Mystery — Why This Order?

Why does multiplication come before addition? Because multiplication is repeated addition!

When you write 2 + 3 × 4, the "3 × 4" means "add 3, four times" = 12. So you're really saying "2 plus (3 added 4 times)" = 2 + 12 = 14. It would be weird to do the 2+3 first because the 4 only belongs to the 3!

🧩 Real-life reason: "I have 2 apples and bought 3 bags of 4 apples each." Total = 2 + 3×4 = 14. You'd never add 2+3 = 5 first — that changes the meaning completely!
🎮 Expression Games — Can You Beat the Calculator?

Use 4 fours to make any number from 1–10:

144 ÷ 44 = 1
24 ÷ 4 + 4 ÷ 4 = 2
3(4 + 4 + 4) ÷ 4 = 3
44 × (4 − 4) + 4 = 4
5(4 × 4 + 4) ÷ 4 = 5
💡 Try to make 6, 7, 8, 9, and 10 using exactly four 4s! This is called the "Four Fours Problem" — a famous math puzzle enjoyed by professional mathematicians.
🎯 The Power of Distributive Property in Mental Maths

The distributive property isn't just for textbooks — it makes mental maths super fast!

💡99 × 7 = (100 − 1) × 7 = 700 − 7 = 693
💡25 × 12 = 25 × (10 + 2) = 250 + 50 = 300
💡18 × 6 = (20 − 2) × 6 = 120 − 12 = 108
🌸 Pro Tip: Whenever you need to multiply a number, break it into easy parts (round number ± small number) and use distributive property. This is what shopkeepers and traders have done for centuries!
