1.1 What Is an Arithmetic Expression?

An arithmetic expression is a combination of numbers and arithmetic operations $(+, -, \times, \div)$ that makes mathematical sense and can be evaluated to a single number.

Examples

$3 + 5$  ·  $12 - 4 \times 2$  ·  $(7 + 3) \div 2$  ·  $100 - (25 + 36)$

An expression is not an equation — it has no "=" sign. It's just a calculation waiting to be done.
Terms

When expressions are added or subtracted, each part is called a term:

Identifying Terms

In $\;7 + 4 - 3\;$:   Terms are $7$, $+4$, $-3$ (separated by $+$ or $-$ outside brackets)

In $\;2 \times 5 + 3 \times 4\;$:   Terms are $2 \times 5$ and $3 \times 4$

Additive Inverse

The additive inverse of a number is what you add to it to get zero.

Rule

Additive inverse of $a$ is $-a$,   because $a + (-a) = 0$

Additive inverse of $7$ is $-7$  ·  Additive inverse of $-12$ is $+12$

Subtracting a number is the same as adding its additive inverse: $\;a - b = a + (-b)$
1.2 Order of Operations — BODMAS

When an expression has multiple operations, we use BODMAS to decide which to do first:

B
Brackets
O
Of (Powers)
D
Division
M
Multiplication
A
Addition
S
Subtraction
D and M have equal priority — do them left to right. Same for A and S — always left to right.
Quick Example

$12 - 3 \times 2 + (4 + 1)$

Step 1 (Brackets): $= 12 - 3 \times 2 + 5$
Step 2 (Multiply): $= 12 - 6 + 5$
Step 3 (Left→Right): $= 6 + 5 = 11$

1.3 Using Brackets

Brackets change the order of operations. Always solve the innermost brackets first.

1
Round Brackets ( )
Innermost — solve these first
2
Curly Brackets { }
After round brackets are solved
3
Square Brackets [ ]
Last — after curly brackets are solved
Nested Brackets Example

$[3 + \{10 - (4 \times 2)\}]$

Inner $()$: $4 \times 2 = 8$  →  $[3 + \{10 - 8\}]$
Curly $\{\}$: $10 - 8 = 2$  →  $[3 + 2]$
Square $[]$: $3 + 2 = \mathbf{5}$

When a negative sign precedes a bracket, remove the bracket by flipping signs: $\;-(a + b) = -a - b$,   $-(a - b) = -a + b$
1.4 Properties of Arithmetic Operations
Commutative
$a + b = b + a$
$a \times b = b \times a$
Order doesn't matter for $+$ and $\times$
Associative
$(a+b)+c = a+(b+c)$
$(a \times b) \times c = a \times (b \times c)$
Grouping doesn't matter for $+$ and $\times$
Distributive
$a \times (b + c) = a \times b + a \times c$
$a \times (b - c) = a \times b - a \times c$
Multiplication distributes over $+$ and $-$
Identity
$a + 0 = a$  (additive)
$a \times 1 = a$  (multiplicative)
$0$ is additive identity, $1$ is multiplicative identity
Zero Property
$a \times 0 = 0$
Any number times zero is zero
Subtraction and Division are NOT commutative or associative. $\;8 - 3 \ne 3 - 8$   and   $8 \div 4 \ne 4 \div 8$
1.5 Smart Multiplication (Tinker the Terms)

Use the distributive property to do tricky multiplications mentally:

Trick 1 — Split to Simplify

$\;99 \times 8 = (100 - 1) \times 8 = 800 - 8 = \mathbf{792}$

Trick 2 — Add the Easy Part

$\;52 \times 6 = (50 + 2) \times 6 = 300 + 12 = \mathbf{312}$

Trick 3 — Factor to Use Known Facts

$\;36 \times 25 = 36 \times \frac{100}{4} = \frac{3600}{4} = \mathbf{900}$

Real-Life: Price of 99 pencils at ₹8 each → $99 \times 8 = (100-1)\times 8 = ₹792$
1.6 Expressions in Real Life
GST Calculation
Item ₹500, GST 18%:
Total $500 + 500 \times \frac{18}{100} = 500 + 90 = ₹590$
Discount
₹800 jacket, 25% off:
Final Price $800 - 800 \times \frac{25}{100} = 800 - 200 = ₹600$
Cricket: Team scored $45 + 32 - 5$ (wide penalties) = $72$ runs in first 10 overs. Run rate = $72 \div 10 = 7.2$ per over.
1.7 Comparing Expressions Without Calculating

You can compare expressions without computing — use the structure of the expression:

Expression AExpression BWhich is Greater?Why?
$5 + 8$$5 + 9$B is greaterSame first term, $9 > 8$
$7 \times 4$$7 \times 3$A is greaterSame factor, $4 > 3$
$12 - 4$$12 - 6$A is greaterSame base, subtracting less
$100 \div 4$$100 \div 5$A is greaterSame dividend, smaller divisor gives bigger result
$(8+3) \times 5$$8 + 3 \times 5$A is greaterA = $55$; B = $23$ by BODMAS
To compare without calculating: find what's common in both expressions, then focus on what's different.

1
Innermost $()$: $5 \times 2 = 10$, then $9 - 10 = -1$  →  $[18 - \{7 + (-1)\}]$

Curly $\{\}$: $7 + (-1) = 6$  →  $[18 - 6]$

Square $[]$: $18 - 6 = \mathbf{12}$
Answer$= \mathbf{12}$
Example 3 Use distributive property to find $\;99 \times 37$
1
Write $99 = 100 - 1$
2
$(100 - 1) \times 37 = 100 \times 37 - 1 \times 37$
3
$= 3700 - 37 = \mathbf{3663}$

Example 4 Verify: $(3+4)+5 = 3+(4+5)$. Which property does this show?
1
Left side: $(3+4)+5 = 7+5 = 12$
2
Right side: $3+(4+5) = 3+9 = 12$
3
Both sides equal $12$. The grouping (brackets) changed, but the values are the same.
This is the Associative Property of Addition — changing how numbers are grouped does NOT change the sum. (NOT Commutative — order didn't change!)
Example 5 Without calculating, which is greater: $\;46 + 73\;$ or $\;46 + 79$?
1
Both expressions add something to $46$.
2
Compare the second terms: $79 > 73$.
3
The expression that adds the larger number is greater.
Answer$46 + 79 > 46 + 73$
Example 6 A phone costs ₹12,000. GST is 18%. Write an expression and find total price.
1

2
Total $= 12000 + 12000 \times \dfrac{18}{100}$
3
$= 12000 + 2160 = ₹\mathbf{14,160}$

1
Simplify: $\;5 + 8 \times 3 - 12 \div 4$
Solution

Multiply: $8 \times 3 = 24$  →  $5 + 24 - 12 \div 4$

Divide: $12 \div 4 = 3$  →  $5 + 24 - 3$

Left to right: $29 - 3 = \mathbf{26}$

2
Simplify: $\;(15 - 6) \times 2 + 18 \div 3$
Solution

Brackets: $15 - 6 = 9$  →  $9 \times 2 + 18 \div 3$

Multiply and Divide (left to right): $18, 6$  →  $18 + 6$

$= \mathbf{24}$

3
Simplify: $\;100 - [50 - \{30 - (20 - 5)\}]$
Solution

Innermost $()\;$: $20 - 5 = 15$  →  $100 - [50 - \{30 - 15\}]$

Curly $\{\}\;$: $30 - 15 = 15$  →  $100 - [50 - 15]$

Square $[]\;$: $50 - 15 = 35$  →  $100 - 35$

$= \mathbf{65}$

4
Place $+$ or $\times$ in the blank to make the statement true: $\;5 \;\square\; 4 \;\square\; 3 = 17$
Solution

Try $5 + 4 \times 3 = 5 + 12 = 17$ ✓

Figure It Out — Set 2 (Comparing Expressions)
5
Without calculating, which is greater: $\;73 - 15\;$ or $\;73 - 9$?
Solution

Same base $73$. We subtract $15$ in A and $9$ in B.

Subtracting less gives a larger result.

Since $9 < 15$, subtracting $9$ gives more: $\;\mathbf{73 - 9 > 73 - 15}$

6
Without calculating, state $>, <,$ or $=$: $\;8 \times (5 + 3)\;$ vs $\;8 \times 5 + 8 \times 3$
Solution

$8 \times (5+3) = 8 \times 8 = 64$

$8 \times 5 + 8 \times 3 = 40 + 24 = 64$

This is just the Distributive Property! They are always equal.

$\;\mathbf{8 \times (5+3) = 8 \times 5 + 8 \times 3}$

7
Fill in: $\;12 \times 97 = 12 \times (\_\_\_ - \_\_\_) = 12 \times \_\_\_ - 12 \times \_\_\_$
Solution

$97 = 100 - 3$

$12 \times 97 = 12 \times (100 - 3) = 12 \times 100 - 12 \times 3 = 1200 - 36 = \mathbf{1164}$

8
A shopkeeper bought 25 pens at ₹12 each and 25 pencils at ₹3 each. Write one expression (without calculating separately) to find total cost.
Solution

Total $= 25 \times 12 + 25 \times 3 = 25 \times (12 + 3) = 25 \times 15 = ₹\mathbf{375}$

This uses the distributive property in reverse (factoring out 25).

9
Simplify: $\;50 - (13 - 7) + 4 \times 3$
Solution

Brackets: $13 - 7 = 6$  →  $50 - 6 + 4 \times 3$

Multiply: $4 \times 3 = 12$  →  $50 - 6 + 12$

Left to right: $44 + 12 = \mathbf{56}$

10
Find the value of $\;[5 + \{4 \times (12 - 3 \times 4)\} \div 4]$
Solution

Innermost $()$: $3 \times 4 = 12$, then $12 - 12 = 0$  →  $[5 + \{4 \times 0\} \div 4]$

Curly $\{\}$: $4 \times 0 = 0$  →  $[5 + 0 \div 4]$

Division: $0 \div 4 = 0$  →  $[5 + 0]$

$= \mathbf{5}$

BODMAS vs PEMDAS — Same Rule, Different Names
🇮🇳 BODMAS (India/UK)

Brackets · Of · Division · Multiplication · Addition · Subtraction

🇺🇸 PEMDAS (USA)

Parentheses · Exponents · Multiplication · Division · Addition · Subtraction

Both give the same result! The rules are identical — just different mnemonic words used in different countries.

Bracket Insertion — Change the Answer!

The same numbers with different brackets give completely different answers:

Without brackets $2 + 3 \times 4 - 1 = 2 + 12 - 1 = 13$
Bracket around 2+3 $(2 + 3) \times 4 - 1 = 5 \times 4 - 1 = 19$
Bracket around 4-1 $2 + 3 \times (4 - 1) = 2 + 9 = 11$
Both bracketed $(2 + 3) \times (4 - 1) = 5 \times 3 = 15$

Same numbers: 2, 3, 4, 1 — four different answers: 13, 19, 11, 15!

Inverse Operations
Extra

Every arithmetic operation has an inverse (opposite):

OperationInverseExample
Addition $(+)$Subtraction $(-)$$5 + 3 = 8 \;\Rightarrow\; 8 - 3 = 5$
Subtraction $(-)$Addition $(+)$$10 - 4 = 6 \;\Rightarrow\; 6 + 4 = 10$
Multiplication $(\times)$Division $(\div)$$6 \times 7 = 42 \;\Rightarrow\; 42 \div 7 = 6$
Division $(\div)$Multiplication $(\times)$$20 \div 4 = 5 \;\Rightarrow\; 5 \times 4 = 20$
Use inverse operations to check your answers: if $48 \div 6 = 8$, verify by $8 \times 6 = 48$.
Expression Trees (Visualising BODMAS)
Extra

An expression tree shows exactly the order of operations visually. For $\;3 + 4 \times 2\;$:

Tree structure 
          +
         / \
        3   ×
           / \
          4   2

Leaf nodes (3, 4, 2) are numbers. Inner nodes are operations. Bottom operations execute first — so $\times$ executes before $+$.

Why Does BODMAS Exist?
Extra

BODMAS is a convention (an agreed rule), not a law of nature. Mathematicians agreed on this order so that expressions mean the same thing everywhere in the world.

Without BODMAS, $\;2 + 3 \times 4\;$ could mean either $\;(2+3)\times4 = 20\;$ or $\;2+(3\times4) = 14\;$. BODMAS tells us it's always $14$.

Common Mistakes — Watch Out!
Errors students make most often — memorise these to never lose marks
✗ WRONG: $12 - 3 + 5 = 12 - 8 = 4$  (adding 3 and 5 first)
✓ RIGHT: $12 - 3 + 5 = 9 + 5 = 14$  (left to right!)
A and S have equal priority — always go left to right, don't jump ahead.
✗ WRONG: $6 \div 2 \times 3 = 6 \div 6 = 1$  (multiplying first)
✓ RIGHT: $6 \div 2 \times 3 = 3 \times 3 = 9$  (left to right: ÷ then ×)
D and M have equal priority — always left to right.
✗ WRONG: $-(3 + 5) = -3 + 5 = 2$  (only flipping first sign)
✓ RIGHT: $-(3 + 5) = -3 - 5 = -8$  (flip ALL signs inside)
A negative sign before a bracket flips every sign inside.
✗ WRONG: $[5 + \{3 + (2)\}]$ — solving square brackets first
✓ RIGHT: Always solve innermost brackets $()$ first, then $\{\}$, then $[]$
Work from inside out, not outside in.
✗ WRONG: Thinking $(3+4)+5 = 3+(4+5)$ is Commutative (order changed)
✓ RIGHT: This is Associative — the ORDER of numbers is the same, only GROUPING changed
Commutative = order changes. Associative = grouping changes.
✗ WRONG: $8 \times (3 - 1) = 8 \times 3 - 1 = 23$  (not distributing fully)
✓ RIGHT: $8 \times (3 - 1) = 8 \times 3 - 8 \times 1 = 24 - 8 = 16$
When distributing, multiply the factor with EVERY term inside the bracket.
