6.1 Periodisation of History Time Divisions

Historians break time into periods to track changes in beliefs, social structures, and ruling powers[cite: 4]:
• Ancient Period: Prehistory to ~700 CE[cite: 4].
• Medieval Period: ~700 CE to ~1700 CE (marked by rise of rulers like Harsha, Cholas, Mughals)[cite: 4].
• Modern Period: ~1700 CE to present day[cite: 4].

6.2 Rise of the Mauryan Empire 321 BCE Founding
Chanakya & Chandragupta

👑 Chandragupta Maurya (321–297 BCE)

  • Guided by scholar Chanakya (Kautilya)[cite: 4].
  • Overthrew unpopular Nanda king Dhana Nanda to establish empire in 321 BCE[cite: 4].
  • Capital: Pataliputra[cite: 4].

📌 Lineage Succession

  • Bindusara (297–273 BCE): Son of Chandragupta; expanded empire south[cite: 4].
  • Ashoka (268–232 BCE): Son of Bindusara; greatest Mauryan ruler[cite: 4].

💡 Memory Anchor

  • Empire vs Kingdom: An Empire is vast, multi-regional, and ruled by an Emperor who commands subordinate kings[cite: 4]!
6.3 Ashoka the Great & Dhamma Kalinga War Transformation
Ashokan Chakra & Pillar

⚔️ Kalinga War Turning Point

  • Fought brutal Kalinga war (modern Odisha)[cite: 4].
  • Seeing immense death and suffering, Ashoka felt deep regret and gave up war forever[cite: 4]!

📜 Ashoka's Dhamma Principles

  • Taught non-violence (*ahimsa*), peace, and truth[cite: 4].
  • Forbidden animal sacrifices[cite: 4].
  • Respect parents, elders, teachers, and treat servants kindly[cite: 4].
  • Inscribed messages on rock edicts and pillars[cite: 4].

💡 Messenger Officials

  • Appointed Dhamma Mahamattas to travel and spread ethical teachings[cite: 4].
  • Sent emissaries to Sri Lanka, Syria, Egypt, Greece, and Macedonia[cite: 4].
6.4 Features, Administration & Life Governance & Society

🏛️ Imperial Governance

  • Central Core: Pataliputra ruled directly by Emperor + Council of Ministers (*Mantriparishad*)[cite: 4].
  • Provinces: Taxila & Ujjain ruled by royal princes/governors[cite: 4].
  • Spies monitored officials across the state[cite: 4].

🪙 Sources & Trade Guilds

  • Sources: *Indica* (Megasthenes), *Arthashastra* (Chanakya), Edicts, Sanchi Stupa[cite: 4].
  • Shrenis: Craft/trade guilds that protected artisans[cite: 4].
  • Punch-marked silver/copper coins used[cite: 4].

👥 Social Structure

  • Strict Varna system[cite: 4].
  • Farming was primary job[cite: 4].
  • Women respected but restricted from property ownership[cite: 4].
Master Multiple Choice Questions
1. Which period of history comes immediately after the ancient period?[cite: 4]
Explanation: Historical periodisation goes Ancient (~prehistory-700 CE) → Medieval (~700-1700 CE) → Modern (~1700 CE-present)[cite: 4].
2. In which year did Chandragupta Maurya establish the empire?[cite: 4]
Explanation: Chandragupta Maurya defeated Dhana Nanda and took power in 321 BCE[cite: 4].
3. Which type of historical source is the Sanchi Stupa?[cite: 4]
Explanation: Buddhist stupas like the Sanchi Stupa are classified as archaeological monuments[cite: 4].
4. Where did the Emperor and central government officials reside?[cite: 4]
Explanation: Pataliputra was the imperial capital city directly ruled by the emperor[cite: 4].
5. Which of the following was NOT a cause of Mauryan decline?[cite: 4]
Explanation: Dhamma promoted peace and moral order; decline was caused by weak successors, vast territory, and invasions[cite: 4].
Confusing Two-Choice Drills
1. Ashoka fought a long, violent war with ________.[cite: 4]
Why confusion happens: Magadha was his home kingdom! Kalinga was the conquered state that changed his heart[cite: 4].
2. Craftspeople belonged to trade guilds called ________.[cite: 4]
Why confusion happens: Gramikas were village heads. Shrenis were urban craft/trade guilds[cite: 4].
3. Ashoka was the son of ________.[cite: 4]
Why confusion happens: Chandragupta was his *grandfather*. Bindusara was his father[cite: 4]!

Exercise D: Answer the following questions in brief (All 6 Questions)[cite: 4]
1. Why do historians divide history into different periods?[cite: 4]
Historians divide history into periods based on shared characteristics such as political structures, social life, beliefs, and economic developments to track changes across time[cite: 4].

2. List any two differences between a kingdom and an empire.[cite: 4]
• An empire is vast and significantly larger in territory and population than a kingdom[cite: 4].
• An empire is ruled by an emperor who controls multiple regions or provinces, whereas a kingdom is ruled by a king over a single localized territory[cite: 4].

3. Describe the events that led to the rise of the Mauryan Empire.[cite: 4]
Chanakya trained Chandragupta Maurya to defeat the unpopular Nanda king Dhana Nanda[cite: 4]. Chandragupta built a strong army, conquered Magadha in 321 BCE, captured Pataliputra, and established the Mauryan Empire[cite: 4].

4. Explain the term 'dynasty' using the example of the Mauryan Empire.[cite: 4]
A dynasty is a family of rulers who govern a realm one after another[cite: 4]. The Mauryas were a dynasty because rule passed down through family generations: Chandragupta Maurya $\rightarrow$ Bindusara $\rightarrow$ Ashoka[cite: 4].

5. Pataliputra, Taxila, and Ujjain were important cities. Explain why.[cite: 4]
Pataliputra was the imperial capital where the emperor resided and directly ruled[cite: 4]. Taxila and Ujjain were provincial capitals located on crucial trade routes connecting distant parts of the empire[cite: 4].

6. Who paid taxes in the Mauryan Empire? What were these taxes used for?[cite: 4]
• Who paid: Farmers, craftspeople, traders, and herders paid taxes[cite: 4].
• Uses: Revenue paid for army maintenance, administrative salaries, road/irrigation construction, and public welfare[cite: 4].
Exercise E: Answer the following questions in detail (All 5 Questions)[cite: 4]
1. What were some teachings of Ashoka's dhamma? Why did he introduce them?[cite: 4]
Teachings included non-violence (*ahimsa*), compassion, truthfulness, respect for parents and teachers, fair treatment of servants, and religious tolerance[cite: 4]. Ashoka introduced dhamma after seeing the massive death and suffering in the Kalinga War, deciding to rule through peace rather than conquest[cite: 4].

2. Explain how Ashoka spread his message of dhamma.[cite: 4]
Ashoka appointed officials called Dhamma Mahamattas to teach morals[cite: 4]. He had edicts inscribed on rock faces and stone pillars across his empire, and sent peace messengers to foreign lands like Sri Lanka, Egypt, and Greece[cite: 4].

3. "The Mauryan Empire was so vast that different parts had to be ruled differently." Explain governance.[cite: 4]
The central capital Pataliputra was ruled directly by the Emperor with a council of ministers[cite: 4]. Provinces like Taxila and Ujjain were managed by royal princes/governors[cite: 4]. Local officials and spies handled district and village taxes and administration[cite: 4].

4. Explain each aspect of life in the Mauryan Empire:[cite: 4]
• (a) Role of women: Treated with respect in families but restricted socially and not allowed to own property[cite: 4].
• (b) Farming: Main occupation; farmers paid land tax and were aided by state-built irrigation canals and dams[cite: 4].
• (c) Varna system: Caste rules were followed strictly, fixing occupations by birth[cite: 4].

5. Describe the reasons for the decline of the Mauryan Empire.[cite: 4]
Declined due to weak successors after Ashoka, financial burden of maintaining a vast empire, external foreign invasions, and the assassination of the last emperor Brihadratha by his general in 185 BCE[cite: 4].
