Chapter 17: Markets around Us

Key Concepts and Definitions

  • Market: A place where buyers and sellers go to exchange goods, services, and information for a price.
  • Intermediaries: Links between two people or groups (e.g., traders).
  • Producers: People who produce goods and provide services for consumers to use.
  • Traders: Intermediaries between producers and consumers. They buy goods from producers and sell them to consumers.
  • Consumers: People who buy goods and services for their own use.

Types of Markets

1. Wholesale Markets

  • Markets where goods are bought and sold in bulk.
  • Buyers are traders who buy in large quantities and sell further to consumers. Sellers are producers or large-scale traders.
  • Prices are generally low.

2. Retail Markets

  • Markets where consumers buy goods they need. Retailers buy goods from wholesale markets.
  • Prices are higher than wholesale markets.
  • Neighbourhood markets: Small markets in our neighbourhoods selling daily necessities.
  • Weekly markets: Markets that happen once a week, often with temporary stalls.
  • Supermarkets: Large, multi-storeyed, air-conditioned stores with a wide variety of products.
  • Shopping malls: Large complexes with shops, restaurants, theatres. Higher overhead costs mean higher prices.
  • Online retailers: Websites or apps to buy things online. Often have lower overhead costs.

Chain of Markets

  • The system where goods move from the producer to the consumer through a series of markets.
  • Flow: Producer → Wholesale Trader → Retail Trader → Consumer.
  • Inequalities: The chain of markets creates inequalities. Producers often earn the lowest profits, while middlemen and retailers earn more due to their ability to invest in storage and transportation.

Cooperatives: Reducing Market Inequalities

A cooperative is an organisation formed by people who share common interests and work towards common goals (e.g., farmers combining resources).

  • Easy to form: Can be started with a small number of members.
  • Reduces dependence on middlemen: Producers can sell directly and retain a larger share of profits.
  • Democratic process: Managed democratically with each member having an equal vote.
  • Government support: Often receive government loans and support in selling products.

Role of Markets in Our Lives

  • Availability of goods and services: Access to a wide range of local and distant products.
  • Bringing producers and consumers together: A common platform for exchange.
  • Creating a strong economy: Encourages production, creates jobs, and stimulates economic growth.
  • Influencing lifestyle and culture: Introduces us to new ideas, products, and ways of living.

Demand versus Supply Theory

  • Prices are set depending on two factors: demand and supply.
  • Demand: The need for products and services by consumers.
  • Supply: The available amount of goods and services in a market.
  • When demand is high but supply is low, the price goes up.
  • When supply is high but demand is low, the price goes down.

Mindful Buying

  • Making conscious choices about the things we buy, based on our needs and values.
  • Involves asking questions like 'Do I really need this?' and 'Is this product made sustainably?'.
  • Helps save money, reduces waste, and supports a fairer economy.
