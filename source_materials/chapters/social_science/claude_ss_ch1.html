Interior of the Earth

Introduction

  • The earth is made up of different layers, with rocks and minerals being important components.
  • Over 4.6 billion years ago, a giant cloud of gas and dust called a 'nebula' formed in outer space.
  • It was held together by gravity, eventually collapsing to form the sun, planets, and celestial bodies.
  • As the earth cooled over millions of years, heavier materials sank to form the core, while lighter materials floated to form the crust.

Layers of the Earth

  • Crust: The outermost and thinnest layer, made of solid rocks. It is divided into continental crust (sial - silica and aluminium) and oceanic crust (sima - silica and magnesium). Its thickness is 5-8 km under oceans and 35-70 km under continents. Temperature: 0°C to 700°C.
  • Mantle: The thickest layer (about 2,900 km) lying below the crust. It consists of the upper and lower mantle, made of semi-liquid or molten rocks (largely silicon, iron, and magnesium). Temperature: 870°C to 4400°C.
  • Outer Core: The liquid part of the core, about 2,300 km thick. Made mainly of nickel and iron (nife). Temperature: 4400°C to 6100°C.
  • Inner Core: The innermost solid layer, about 1,200 km thick. Solid due to immense pressure, made of iron. Temperature can reach 6000°C (as hot as the Sun's surface).

Rocks and Minerals

Rocks are naturally occurring substances forming the earth's outermost layer. They are classified into three types based on formation:

1. Igneous Rocks

  • Also called primary rocks. Formed when magma or lava cools and solidifies.
  • Intrusive Igneous Rocks: Formed when magma cools slowly inside the earth, creating large crystals (e.g., Granite, Diorite).
  • Extrusive Igneous Rocks: Formed when lava pours out on the surface and cools rapidly, creating a grainy texture with small crystals (e.g., Basalt, Andesite).

2. Sedimentary Rocks

  • Formed when rocks break down into sediments by wind, water, and ice.
  • Sediments are compressed under pressure into layered rocks (e.g., Shale, Sandstone, Limestone, Gypsum).
  • Often contain fossils.

3. Metamorphic Rocks

  • Formed when igneous or sedimentary rocks change their physical and chemical properties due to extreme heat or pressure.
  • Examples include Granite changing to gneiss, limestone to marble, and clay to slate.

Rock Cycle

  • The continuous, cyclic process by which rocks transform from one type to another under specific conditions.

Minerals

  • Naturally occurring substances with specific chemical and physical properties.
  • Metallic minerals: Contain metals (e.g., Iron, copper, tin).
  • Non-metallic minerals: Do not contain metals (e.g., Potash, gypsum, limestone, mica).

Key Terms

Gravity: A strong, invisible force pulling objects together.
Molten: Heated to a very high temperature so it becomes liquid.
Texture: The way a surface feels when touched.
Solidifies: Becomes solid.
Fossils: Remains of dead plants and animals.
Cyclic: Repeated many times.
