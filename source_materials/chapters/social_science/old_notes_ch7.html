7.1 New Kingdoms & Empires Post-Mauryan Period

After the fall of the Mauryan Empire around 200 BCE, no single ruler controlled all of India[cite: 4]. Instead, several regional kingdoms emerged across North, Central, and South India during the Iron Age[cite: 4].

1. The Kushanas & Satavahanas North & Deccan Rulers
Kushana Gold Coin

👑 The Kushanas (50–300 CE)

  • Ruled Northwest India, Pakistan & Afghanistan[cite: 4].
  • Famous King: Kanishka (follower of Mahayana Buddhism)[cite: 4].
  • Controlled the lucrative Silk Route[cite: 4].

📌 The Satavahanas (100 BCE–200 CE)

  • Ruled the Deccan region (Maharashtra & Andhra Pradesh)[cite: 4].
  • Famous Ruler: Gautamiputra Satakarni[cite: 4].
  • Known as "Lords of Dakshinapatha" (route to the south)[cite: 4].

💡 Memory Anchor

  • Gold coins issued by Kanishka featured images of Buddha and Greek gods, showing cultural blend[cite: 4]!
2. The Muvendar (Cholas, Cheras, Pandyas) 400 BCE – 300 CE

🌴 The Three Crowned Kings

  • Ruled Tamilakam (Tamil Nadu & Kerala)[cite: 4].
  • Known together as the Muvendar (Three Great Chiefs)[cite: 4].

📜 Sangam Literature

  • Great Tamil poets gathered at assemblies in Madurai[cite: 4].
  • Their poems are compiled as Sangam Literature[cite: 4].

💡 Roman Trade

  • Traded heavily with the Roman Empire (spices like pepper were called "black gold")[cite: 4]!
7.2 Technology, Trade & The Silk Route Economic Growth
Silk Route Network

🌾 Agricultural Tech

  • Iron ploughshares turned heavy clay soil easily[cite: 4].
  • Paddy transplantation increased crop yields massively[cite: 4].
  • Canals and wells constructed for irrigation[cite: 4].

🛣️ The Silk Route

  • Network of trade routes connecting China to Europe via India[cite: 4].
  • Indian rulers collected taxes from traders in exchange for protection[cite: 4].

🪙 Punch-Marked Coins

  • Earliest coins made of silver/copper stamped with symbols (trees, animals, sun)[cite: 4]!
7.3 Cities & Social Hierarchy Urbanisation & Guilds

🏙️ Types of Cities

  • Administrative: Capital cities (Pataliputra)[cite: 4].
  • Trade / Market: Ujjain, Taxila[cite: 4].
  • Port Cities: Arikamedu, Bharuch, Kaveripattinam[cite: 4].
  • Multi-Function: Mathura (religious, trade, capital)[cite: 4].

👥 Village Hierarchy (North)

  • Gramabhojaka: Powerful village headman & landholder[cite: 4].
  • Grihapatis: Independent small farmers[cite: 4].
  • Dasa Karmakaras: Landless agricultural workers[cite: 4].

🌴 Village Hierarchy (South)

  • Vellalar: Rich, influential landowners[cite: 4].
  • Uzhavar: Ordinary farmers[cite: 4].
  • Kadaasiyar / Adimai: Landless laborers & slaves[cite: 4].
Master Multiple Choice Questions
1. Which kingdom's rulers were known as the 'Lords of Dakshinapatha'?[cite: 4]
Explanation: The Satavahanas ruled the Deccan and controlled Dakshinapatha (the southern route)[cite: 4].
2. Which technology radically changed farming during the Iron Age?[cite: 4]
Explanation: Iron ploughshares allowed deep tilling of heavy clay soil, expanding agriculture[cite: 4].
3. Which kind of pottery developed during the Iron Age in India?[cite: 4]
Explanation: Northern Black Polished Ware (NBPW) was fine glossy pottery characteristic of this era[cite: 4].
4. Which dynasty ruled northwestern India through which the Silk Route passed?[cite: 4]
Explanation: The Kushanas controlled northwestern trade routes connecting China and Rome[cite: 4].
5. Which city is an example of a location that served multiple functions (religious, capital, trade)?[cite: 4]
Explanation: Mathura served as a trade center, religious hub, and secondary Kushana capital[cite: 4].
Confusing Two-Choice Drills
1. The Satavahana kings were known as the Lords of ________.[cite: 4]
Why confusion happens: Dakshinapatha was the southern route. Uttarapatha was the northern route[cite: 4]!
2. Craftspeople in cities organized themselves into associations called ________.[cite: 4]
Why confusion happens: Sangam was the poet assembly. Shrenis were trade/craft guilds[cite: 4].
3. In South Indian villages, wealthy influential landowners were called ________.[cite: 4]
Why confusion happens: Vellalar were rich landholders; Uzhavar were ordinary ploughmen[cite: 4].
Interactive True / False Arena
1. The Kushana king Kanishka was a follower of Mahayana Buddhism.[cite: 4]
Reason: TRUE. Kanishka patronized Mahayana Buddhism and held the 4th Buddhist Council[cite: 4].
2. Roman traders bought pepper from South India and called it 'black gold'.[cite: 4]
Reason: TRUE. Pepper was so highly valued in Rome that it was dubbed black gold[cite: 4].
3. Punch-marked coins were invented during the British period.[cite: 4]
Reason: FALSE. Punch-marked coins were the earliest coins used in ancient India during the Iron Age[cite: 4].

Exercise E: Answer the following questions in detail (All 5 Questions)
1. Post-Mauryan Kingdom (The Kushanas): Central Asian tribe ruling NW India[cite: 4]. King Kanishka controlled the Silk Route, issued gold coins, and actively promoted Mahayana Buddhism[cite: 4].

2. Other Tech Advancements: Metallurgy (iron weapons/tools), Northern Black Polished Ware (NBPW) pottery, and silver/copper punch-marked coins[cite: 4].

3. Jobs & Emergence: Farmers, weavers, potters, metalworkers, carpenters, and merchants in *shrenis* (guilds)[cite: 4]. Jobs emerged because agricultural surplus allowed specialization[cite: 4].

4. Village Power Equality: Disagree[cite: 4]. Power was unequal: *Gramabhojaka* held maximum power, *Grihapatis* were small farmers, and *Dasa Karmakaras* owned no land[cite: 4].

5. Silk Route & Religion: Traders, monks, and pilgrims traveled together, spreading Buddhism to Central Asia and China[cite: 4].
