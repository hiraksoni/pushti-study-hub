The First Indian Empire – The Mauryas

1. Periodisation of History

  • Historians organise history into periods to track changes over time.
  • Ancient Period: Prehistoric to around 700 CE.
  • Medieval Period: around 700 CE to around 1700 CE.
  • Modern Period: 1700 CE to present.

2. Rise of the Mauryan Empire

  • A kingdom is a small area ruled by a king or queen, while an empire is a much larger area ruled by an emperor or empress.
  • Magadha grew into India's first empire. Originally a powerful kingdom under Dhana Nanda, it was very prosperous.
  • A great scholar named Chanakya (Kautilya) wanted to overthrow Dhana Nanda. He trained a young man named Chandragupta Maurya in warfare and statecraft.
  • In 321 BCE, Chandragupta defeated Dhana Nanda and became the ruler of Magadha, laying the foundation of the Mauryan Empire.
  • Chandragupta expanded the empire and was succeeded by his son Bindusara, and later his grandson Ashoka.

3. Ashoka the Great

  • Ashoka came to power in 268 BCE. He is regarded as one of the greatest rulers in history.
  • He fought a violent war to conquer Kalinga. He won the battle but the vast bloodshed and suffering caused him deep regret.
  • Ashoka decided to give up war forever, becoming the only emperor in history to renounce conquest after winning a battle.
  • Inspired by Buddhism, he introduced Ashoka's Dhamma, teaching compassion, peace, and non-violence towards all living beings.
  • He appointed officials called dhamma mahamattas to travel and spread these teachings.
  • His messages were inscribed as edicts on pillars and rocks for people to read.

4. Sources of Mauryan History

  • Books: Arthashastra written by Chanakya provides details on how to govern and maintain peace.
  • Writings of Travellers: Indica, written by the Greek ambassador Megasthenes, describes Indian society, culture, and politics.
  • Monuments: Buddhist stupas, like the Sanchi Stupa.
  • Inscriptions: Ashokan edicts engraved on rocks and stone pillars.
  • Coins: Issued by Mauryan emperors (punch-marked coins).

5. Features of the Mauryan Empire

  • The empire stretched from present-day Afghanistan to Karnataka and Bengal.
  • Important Cities: Pataliputra (the capital), Taxila, and Ujjain (centres of trade).
  • Administration: The emperor directly controlled Pataliputra, advised by the mantrin parishad (council of ministers). Provinces were ruled by royal princes appointed as governors.
  • Social and Economic Life: Most people were farmers, herders, traders, or craftspeople. Craftspeople formed guilds called shrenis.
  • Taxes were collected in money or in kind by officials, used to pay for administration and public needs.
  • Women were respected and allowed to own property, but did not have the same position as men. The varna system was strictly followed.

6. Decline of the Mauryan Empire

  • After Ashoka's death, the empire was ruled by weak rulers who could not maintain its unity.
  • The massive size of the empire became difficult to manage and collect resources for.
  • External invasions by foreign powers, such as the Sakas, weakened the military strength.
  • The empire eventually collapsed, ending when the last Mauryan ruler was killed by his own general in 185 BCE.
