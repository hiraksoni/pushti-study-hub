Understanding Gender

1. Meaning of Gender

  • Gender refers to the expectations that society has about how boys and girls, and men and women must behave and the roles they must play.
  • It is not something that we are born with. It is socially constructed.
  • The understanding of gender can vary in different societies and over time.

2. Gender Inequality & Stereotypes

  • Gender Discrimination: Unequal treatment of people based on their gender.
  • Stereotype: A fixed or widely accepted belief about a particular group of people. Preconceived ideas and opinions.
  • Stereotypes often lead to biases. For example, women are frequently expected to take care of household responsibilities while men are expected to earn for the family.
  • Impacts of gender inequality:
    • Devaluing the work women do: Household work and caregiving are often not considered "real" work or are paid very low wages.
    • Unequal burden of household work: The primary responsibility for domestic chores and caregiving falls entirely on women in many societies, even if they work outside as well.
    • Limited opportunities for education: In traditional societies, girls were often denied education because their primary role was seen as household management.

3. Importance of Gender Equality

  • Gender equality is essential to live a life of dignity.
  • It means that every human being should have equal rights to think, express, and work, irrespective of the gender they belong to.
  • When we follow gender stereotypes, we take away people's freedom to make their own choices and realise their full potential.

4. Struggles for Equality

  • Many social reformers worked tirelessly to abolish discriminatory practices and promote girls' education.
  • Key Social Reformers:
    • Raja Rammohan Roy: Led the movement to abolish sati in 1829.
    • Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar: Supported girls' education; widow remarriage became legal in 1856 due to his efforts.
    • Savitribai Phule: Along with her husband Jyotirao Phule, founded the first modern Indian girls' school in 1848 in Pune.
    • Pandita Ramabai: Opened Sarada Sadan, where she encouraged widows to get educated and learn skills.

5. Promoting Gender Equality

Role of the Government

  • Right to Education: Every child in India between the ages of 6 and 14 years must get free and compulsory education.
  • Right to Equality: The fundamental right to equality prohibits any form of discrimination, including gender discrimination. It also guarantees equal opportunities and equal pay for equal work.
  • Reservation for Women in Panchayats: 33% of seats in panchayats are reserved for women to support women leaders.
  • Laws against Dowry and Domestic Violence: The government has passed laws that make dowry and domestic violence punishable offenses.

Role of Individuals

  • Treat everyone equally, irrespective of their gender.
  • Encourage people around us to think about and break the gender stereotypes they come across.
  • Be aware and spread awareness about the laws and policies that promote gender equality.
