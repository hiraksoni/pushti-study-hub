🧪 Introduction to Science

🌍 What is Science?

Science is best understood through experience, hands-on activities and experiments that allow us to observe, test and explore the world around us.

Science is more than just a process of discovery — it's a journey that comes with responsibility.

💡 Tip: In science, one idea leads to another — asking simple questions can help us understand the world better!
🍋 Why are Some Fruits Sour?

🍊 Citric Acid in Fruits

Due to the presence of citric acid, some fruits like oranges, lime and lemon taste sour.

Haldi (Turmeric) Stain Story 🟡
Haldi contains a pigment called curcumin. It strongly binds with fabric, leaving a hard stain.

🧼 Washing with soap → colour changes to reddish-brown (soap is alkaline/base)
This is an acid-base chemical reaction between curcumin (acid) and soap (base).
☀️ The stain can be reversed by exposing it to the Sun!
⚠️ Common Mistake: Curcumin is the pigment in turmeric — don't confuse it with the spice name haldi itself!

🌊 The Water Cycle

💧 Water Cycle Processes

  • Evaporation: Sun heats water → water vapour ☀️
  • Condensation: Water vapour cools → clouds ☁️
  • Precipitation: Water falls as rain/snow 🌧️
  • Transpiration: Plants release water vapour 🌿
  • Runoff: Water flows across land
  • Infiltration: Water seeps into ground → groundwater
  • Sublimation: Solid ice directly turns into gas ❄️→💨
💡 Energy source: The Sun drives the entire water cycle!

🍋 The Chemistry of Sour Fruits

Bonus
Why do lemons make you pucker? Citric acid in lemons has a pH of about 2 — very acidic! Your taste buds send a "danger" signal to your brain causing you to pucker. It's a protective reflex!
Vitamin C connection: British sailors in the 1700s ate lemons to prevent scurvy (Vitamin C deficiency) on long sea voyages! That's how British sailors got the nickname "limeys." 🍋
Kitchen science: Curcumin is used as a pH indicator in chemistry labs! It turns red in alkaline (basic) conditions and yellow in acidic/neutral ones. You can try this at home with soap solution!

⚡ From Kite to Smartphone

Bonus
Franklin's kite experiment (1752): Lightning struck the kite, travelled down the wet string, and the metal key gave off sparks — proving lightning IS electricity. This led to the invention of the lightning rod! ⚡
Why Silicon Valley? Silicon is the most important semi-conductor — every smartphone, computer, and circuit board uses silicon chips. California's tech hub is literally named after this Class 7 topic! 💻
Tungsten melts at 3,422°C — the highest of all metals! Iron melts at 1,538°C. Gold melts at just 1,064°C. Modern LED bulbs skip tungsten entirely, using semi-conductors that use 90% less electricity.

🌊 The Amazing Water Cycle

Bonus
Water is ancient! The water you drink today may have once been dinosaur urine! 🦕 Water molecules have been recycling through the water cycle for billions of years since Earth was formed.
India's water crisis: India has only 4% of the world's freshwater but 18% of the world's population. Only 0.5% of Earth's total water is available as fresh drinking water — which is why saving water matters especially in Gujarat!
Trees make their own rain: Amazon trees release so much water through transpiration that they create "flying rivers" of water vapour — causing rainfall thousands of kilometres away. Cutting trees = less rain! 🌳

🌙 Eclipses — Cosmic Coincidence!

Bonus
The perfect coincidence: The Sun is 400× bigger than the Moon, but also 400× farther away — so they look almost the same size in our sky! This is why a total solar eclipse covers the Sun so perfectly. Scientists believe no other planet has moons that create such perfect eclipses! 🌑☀️
Blood Moon: During a lunar eclipse, the Moon glows reddish-orange because Earth's atmosphere bends red light onto the Moon's surface. It's the same physics as why sunsets are red! 🔴
Eclipse proved Einstein right! In 1919, scientists used a total solar eclipse to confirm Einstein's Theory of General Relativity by observing that the Sun's gravity bent starlight — visible only during the eclipse. 🔭

🌿 Photosynthesis — Earth's Solar Panel

Bonus
Plants invented breathable air: About 3.5 billion years ago, the first photosynthetic organisms filled Earth's atmosphere with oxygen — making animal life possible. Every breath you take was "made" by photosynthesis. 🌬️
Faster than any machine: Photosynthesis converts light to chemical energy in under a trillionth of a second — faster than any human-made solar panel! Scientists are studying it to build better clean-energy technology. ☀️
Why leaves look green: Chlorophyll absorbs red and blue light but reflects green — so leaves appear green! In autumn, chlorophyll breaks down revealing hidden yellow, orange, and red pigments that were always there. 🍂
