[1M]

D Vacuole
Correct Option: (b) Chloroplast1 Mark

Q4
During aerobic cellular respiration in living cells, glucose is completely broken down in the presence of oxygen to produce:
[1M]
A Lactic acid and water
B Carbon dioxide, water, and energy
C Alcohol and carbon dioxide
D Starch and oxygen
Correct Option: (b) Carbon dioxide, water, and energy1 Mark

Lactic acid is produced during anaerobic respiration in fatigued human muscles, while alcohol + CO₂ is produced during yeast fermentation.

Q12
Why does a person's breathing rate increase significantly while performing vigorous physical exercise such as brisk cycling or skipping?
[2M]
Model Solution & Marking Rubric2 Marks
1. Higher Energy Demand: During vigorous exercise, working skeletal muscles contract rapidly and require significantly more energy (ATP) to sustain activity.
2. Increased Oxygen Supply: To produce this extra energy, muscle cells accelerate the rate of aerobic cellular respiration, which consumes substantially more oxygen.
3. CO₂ Clearance: Faster cellular respiration accumulates excess carbon dioxide waste in the blood. The respiratory brain center detects this, triggering rapid and deep breathing to supply more oxygen and quickly expel excess carbon dioxide.
Q13
Describe the mechanical process of breathing (inhalation and exhalation) in humans, focusing on: (a) Movement of ribs, (b) Movement of muscular diaphragm, and (c) Resulting change in thoracic cavity volume and air pressure.
[3M]
Model Solution & Marking Rubric1.5M Inhalation + 1.5M Exhalation
1. Inhalation (Breathing In):
• (a) Ribs: The external intercostal muscles contract, pulling the rib cage upward and outward.
• (b) Diaphragm: The dome-shaped muscular diaphragm contracts and flattens downward.
• (c) Volume & Pressure: The thoracic cavity volume expands (increases). As internal pressure inside the lungs drops below atmospheric pressure, air rushes into the lungs from outside.

2. Exhalation (Breathing Out):
• (a) Ribs: The intercostal muscles relax, causing the rib cage to move downward and inward.
• (b) Diaphragm: The diaphragm relaxes and curves upward into its original dome shape.
• (c) Volume & Pressure: The thoracic cavity volume contracts (decreases), increasing air pressure inside the lungs above atmospheric levels, forcing air out of the lungs.
Q14
(a) What are villi? State their specific structural adaptations for maximizing nutrient absorption in the small intestine. (1.5 Marks)
(b) What vital function does the large intestine carry out on undigested food material before egestion? (1.5 Marks)
[3M]
Model Solution & Marking Rubric1.5M Part A + 1.5M Part B
(a) Villi and their Structural Adaptations:
• Definition: Villi are millions of minute, finger-like vascular projections lining the inner mucous membrane of the small intestine (ileum).
• Adaptations:
1. They enormously amplify the internal surface area for nutrient absorption.
2. Their walls are extremely thin (one cell layer thick), allowing rapid diffusion of nutrients.
3. They contain an extensive network of blood capillaries and a central lymph vessel (lacteal) to immediately transport absorbed amino acids, glucose, and fatty acids into the bloodstream.

(b) Vital Functions of the Large Intestine:
• The large intestine reabsorbs excess water and essential dissolved minerals from the watery undigested food mass.
• It solidifies and compacts the remaining non-digestible residue into feces, temporarily storing it in the rectum until it is voided through the anus (egestion).

Buccal Cavity (Mouth) → Pharynx → Oesophagus → Stomach → Small Intestine (Duodenum, Jejunum, Ileum) → Large Intestine (Colon) → Rectum → Anus.

(b) Chemical Digestion in Small Intestine:
The small intestine receives bile juice from the liver, pancreatic juice from the pancreas, and secretes intestinal juice:

OR Alternative Question:
(a) Enumerate five key physiological functions of the human stomach in digestion and defense. (3 Marks)
(b) What is rumination? Explain how ruminant animals (such as cows) digest cellulose with the help of specialized stomach compartments. (2 Marks)
Model Solution (OR Alternative Option)3M Stomach + 2M Rumination
(a) Five Key Functions of the Human Stomach:
1. Mechanical Churning: Peristaltic muscular walls grind solid food into semi-fluid chyme.
2. Antimicrobial Defense: Hydrochloric acid (HCl) kills bacteria and microorganisms swallowed with food.
3. Pepsin Activation: HCl creates the acidic medium necessary to convert inactive pepsinogen into active pepsin.

• Cellulose Digestion: Plant grass is rich in tough cellulose. In the rumen, millions of symbiotic anaerobic bacteria and protozoa produce the enzyme cellulase, fermenting complex cellulose into digestible volatile fatty acids.

Q27
Give valid scientific explanations for each of the following observations: (a) Gold and platinum are widely used for jewelry, (b) White phosphorus is stored under water, and (c) Metal cookware handles are coated with plastic or wood.
[3M]
Model Solution & Marking Rubric1 Mark Each (3 Marks)
(a) Gold and Platinum in Jewelry:
Gold and platinum are noble metals situated at the lowest end of the reactivity series. They do not react with atmospheric oxygen, moisture, or household acids, meaning they never corrode, rust, or tarnish. Furthermore, their superior malleability and ductility allow shaping into intricate jewelry designs.

(b) White Phosphorus Stored Under Water:
White phosphorus is an exceptionally reactive non-metal with an extremely low ignition temperature (around 35°C). In contact with ambient air, it spontaneously catches fire and burns vigorously. Because phosphorus does not dissolve in or react with water and is denser than water, keeping it submerged completely cuts off atmospheric oxygen.

(c) Insulated Cookware Handles:
Aluminum and stainless steel are metals with high thermal conductivity, allowing heat to spread rapidly to cook food. In contrast, wood and plastic (Bakelite) are thermal insulators (poor conductors of heat). Coating handles protects hands from burning.
Q28. Case Study: Soil pH Management and Crop Productivity
An agricultural extension officer visited a farming cooperative where wheat crop yields had plummeted over two successive seasons. Soil testing revealed that heavy, repeated application of chemical fertilizers had made the soil excessively acidic (pH well below 5.5). (a) Why does excessively acidic soil hinder healthy plant root growth and nutrient uptake? [1M]
Solution (a)1 Mark
Excessive soil acidity (pH < 5.5) inhibits beneficial nitrogen-fixing soil bacteria, stunts fragile root hair elongation, and locks essential plant nutrients (such as phosphorus, nitrogen, and potassium) into insoluble compounds that plant roots cannot absorb.
(b) Name the chemical compound used to neutralize acidic soil, and identify the reaction type involved. [1.5M]
Solution (b)1M Compound + 0.5M Reaction
• Chemical Compound: Slaked lime [Calcium Hydroxide, Ca(OH)₂] or Quicklime [Calcium Oxide, CaO] or Agricultural Limestone [CaCO₃].

(c) How does the addition of organic compost help remediate excessively basic (alkaline) soil? [1.5M]
Solution (c)1.5 Marks
Decomposing green manure and organic cow dung compost release natural weak organic acids (such as humic acids) as micro-organisms break down the matter. These released organic acids chemically neutralize the basic alkalis present in the soil, restoring the pH to a fertile neutral level (pH 6.5–7.2).

• Water Pipes & AC Ducts (Thermal Insulation): Fiberglass and foam contain microscopic pockets of trapped air, which is an extremely poor conductor of heat. Wrapping pipes stops unwanted thermal exchange (prevents hot water pipes from losing heat to the air and chilled AC ducts from absorbing ambient heat).

• Vehicle Radiators (Thermal Dissipation): Copper and aluminum are superior conductors of heat. Constructing radiators with hundreds of wafer-thin metallic fins maximizes the surface area exposed to ambient airflow, rapidly carrying engine heat away via conduction and forced convection.

Q37
Coastal regions experience refreshing breezes daily. Explain the convection mechanism that causes: (a) A Sea Breeze blowing from the sea toward the land during daytime, and (b) A Land Breeze blowing from the land toward the sea during nighttime.
[3M]
Model Solution & Marking Rubric1.5M Sea Breeze + 1.5M Land Breeze
(a) Sea Breeze (Daytime):
1. During daylight, solar radiation heats the land much faster than the sea (since land has a lower specific heat capacity).
2. The air above the land warms up, expands, becomes lighter (less dense), and rises upward, creating a localized low-pressure zone.
3. The cooler, denser air over the water rushes inward toward the land to occupy this space. This convective current of cool air from sea to land is called a Sea Breeze.

(b) Land Breeze (Nighttime):
1. After sunset, the land loses heat much faster through radiation than water does.
2. Consequently, the sea water remains comparatively warmer than the cool land at night.
3. The warm air above the sea rises, and cooler, denser air from the land sweeps toward the sea to replace it. This current of air from land to sea is called a Land Breeze.
Q38. Case Study: Intercity Road Travel & Speed Measurement
Aman and his mother boarded an intercity express bus from Town X to Town Y. At the beginning of the trip at 7:00 AM, Aman noted that the bus dashboard odometer read 24,150 km. The bus reached Town Y at 9:30 AM, where the odometer indicated 24,330 km. During the trip along the four-lane highway, the speedometer fluctuated between 60 km/h and 90 km/h, briefly dropping to zero during two traffic signals.
(a) Distinguish clearly between what an odometer measures and what a speedometer measures in a vehicle. [1M]
Solution (a)1 Mark
An odometer records and measures the total cumulative distance traversed by a vehicle in kilometers (km). In contrast, a speedometer measures the instantaneous speed of the vehicle at any given moment in kilometers per hour (km/h).
(b) Calculate the total distance covered by the bus and the total time taken for the trip in hours. [1M]
Solution (b)0.5M Distance + 0.5M Time
• Total Distance (d): 24,330 km - 24,150 km = 180 km.
• Total Time (t): From 7:00 AM to 9:30 AM = 2 hours and 30 minutes = 2 + (30/60) = 2.5 hours.
(c) Compute the average speed of the bus in km/h for the entire journey, and convert this calculated speed into meters per second (m/s). [2M]
Solution (c)1M Speed in km/h + 1M Conversion to m/s
1. Average Speed Formula:
Average Speed = Total Distance / Total Time = 180 km / 2.5 h = 72 km/h.

2. Conversion into meters per second (m/s):
Multiply by conversion factor 5/18:
Speed in m/s = 72 × (5 / 18) = 4 × 5 = 20 m/s.
(Verification: 72 × 1000 m / 3600 s = 72000 / 3600 = 20 m/s).

(a) What is an electric fuse? Explain the scientific principle (heating effect of electric current) on which its protective action is based. (2 Marks)
(b) Why are modern households increasingly replacing traditional cartridge/wire fuses with Miniature Circuit Breakers (MCBs)? Give two operational advantages. (1.5 Marks)
(c) Why is connecting a thick copper wire or steel wire across a blown fuse carrier considered an extremely hazardous practice? (1.5 Marks)
[5M]
Question Choice:
Model Solution (Primary Option)2M Fuse + 1.5M MCB + 1.5M Hazard
(a) Electric Fuse & Working Principle:
• Definition: An electric fuse is an essential safety component wired in series in a circuit to prevent fire hazards and appliance damage caused by overloading or short circuits.
• Principle: Operates on the heating effect of electric current (Joule's Law: H = I²Rt). The fuse wire is fabricated from a specialized alloy (tin and lead) having a low melting point and calibrated resistance. When current exceeds the rated threshold, resistive heat quickly rises past the melting point, melting the wire and breaking the open circuit.

(b) Two Operational Advantages of MCBs over Fuses:
1. Automatic Tripping & Reusability: When overloaded, an MCB automatically trips OFF like a switch. Once the fault is cleared, it is reset back to ON with a finger without requiring replacement wire.
2. Faster Response & Safety: MCBs disconnect dangerous overloads much faster (within milliseconds) via magnetic induction, eliminating the risk of electric shock during replacement.

(c) Extreme Hazard of Thick Wire as Fuse:
A thick copper or iron wire possesses very low electrical resistance and an extraordinarily high melting point (1,085°C for Cu). If a short circuit occurs, the thick wire will not melt. Instead, the excessive surge current will overheat building wiring behind walls, causing insulation to ignite and destroying connected appliances.
OR Alternative Question:
(a) Describe a controlled laboratory experiment using two identical metal containers (one painted matte black, the other polished glossy silver) and two thermometers to prove that dark surfaces absorb radiant heat much faster than bright shiny surfaces. (3 Marks)
(b) Give scientific reasons for each of the following: (i) Why we prefer light-colored cotton clothes in hot summer months, and (ii) Why double-pane vacuum thermal flasks keep hot liquids warm for hours. (2 Marks)
Model Solution (OR Alternative Option)3M Experiment + 2M Scientific Reasons
(a) Laboratory Experiment: Thermal Radiation Absorption:
1. Take two identical cylindrical metal cans of the same size and material. Paint the outer surface of Can A with matte black paint, and polish Can B to a reflective silver finish.
2. Fill each container with an equal volume of cold water (e.g. 150 mL at 20°C).
3. Insert a laboratory thermometer into each can through a perforated rubber lid, making sure the bulbs are immersed in water without touching the walls.
4. Position both cans side-by-side in direct midday sunlight (or equal distance from a 100W lamp) for 30 minutes.
• Observation: The thermometer in the black can registers a markedly higher temperature rise (e.g. 38°C) compared to the shiny can (e.g. 26°C).
• Conclusion: Dull black surfaces are superior absorbers of thermal radiation, while bright shiny surfaces reflect radiant heat.

(b) Scientific Reasons:
1. Light-colored cotton clothes in summer: White and light shades reflect the majority of incident solar radiation, minimizing heat absorption. Cotton absorbs bodily sweat and promotes evaporative cooling.
2. Double-pane vacuum flask keeps liquids hot: The vacuum between the double glass walls prevents heat transfer by conduction and convection (as both require a material medium). The silver-mirrored glass coating reflects radiant heat back into the flask.
