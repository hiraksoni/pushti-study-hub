Artificial Intelligence

Exploring the concepts, types, and domains of AI

📖 Chapter at a Glance

This chapter introduces the fascinating world of Artificial Intelligence (AI). We will explore what AI means, the different types of AI based on their capabilities and functions, and the major domains that power modern AI technologies.

🎯 Learning Outcomes

  • Understand what Artificial Intelligence is and its core purpose
  • Differentiate between types of AI based on Capabilities (Narrow, General, Super)
  • Differentiate between types of AI based on Functionalities (Reactive, Limited Memory, etc.)
  • Understand the three main domains of AI (Data Science, Computer Vision, Natural Language Processing)

📘 Key Terms Glossary

Artificial Intelligence
The science and engineering of making intelligent machines capable of performing tasks that typically require human intelligence.
Narrow AI (ANI)
Artificial Narrow Intelligence. AI that is trained and focused on performing a single, specific task.
General AI (AGI)
Artificial General Intelligence. AI that can perform any intellectual task that a human being can do.
Data Science (DS)
A domain of AI dealing with data systems, processes, and algorithms to extract knowledge from data.
Computer Vision (CV)
A domain of AI that enables machines to visually interpret the world through images and videos.
NLP
Natural Language Processing. A domain of AI that helps machines understand, interpret, and manipulate human language.

🤖 Introduction to Artificial Intelligence

What is AI?

According to John McCarthy, the father of AI, it is "The science and engineering of making intelligent machines, especially intelligent computer programs."

AI broadly refers to any human-like behavior displayed by a machine or system, such as decision-making, object detection, and solving complex problems with increased accuracy.

The Goal of AI

Artificial intelligence deals with making computer systems capable of achieving tasks that humans are not capable of, or feel that they are risky to do so. Robotics is also a major field related to AI.

⚙️ Types of AI based on Capabilities

When categorizing AI by its overall capability compared to human intelligence, there are three main types:

1. Narrow AI (ANI)

Also known as Weak AI or Artificial Narrow Intelligence.

  • It is trained to perform a single, specific task (e.g., playing chess, recommending songs).
  • It operates under a limited set of constraints. If asked to do something outside its limitation, it will fail.
  • Examples: Apple Siri, Cortana, Google Assistant, self-driving cars, facial recognition, and IBM's Watson supercomputer.

2. General AI (AGI)

Also known as Strong AI or Artificial General Intelligence.

  • AI that has human-level intelligence. It can learn, understand, and perform any intellectual task that a human being can do.
  • It can think and make decisions independently.
  • Current Status: AGI is still largely theoretical. We do not yet have a true General AI.

3. Super AI (ASI)

Artificial Super Intelligence.

  • An outcome of General AI that surpasses human intelligence and ability.
  • It would be better at everything: math, science, arts, and emotional understanding.
  • Current Status: Completely hypothetical (sci-fi concept) at this point in time.

🧠 Types of AI based on Functionalities

When categorizing AI by how it functions internally, there are four types:

1. Reactive Machines

  • The most basic type of AI.
  • They do not store memories or past experiences to inform future actions.
  • They only focus on current scenarios and react to them optimally.
  • Example: IBM's Deep Blue (the chess-playing supercomputer that beat Garry Kasparov). It sees the board and makes the best move, without learning from past games.

2. Limited Memory

  • Can store past experiences or some data for a short period of time.
  • They use this limited past information to make better decisions.
  • Example: Self-driving cars. They store the recent speed of nearby cars, distance, and speed limit to navigate safely.

3. Theory of Mind

  • An advanced concept where AI can understand human emotions, beliefs, and thoughts.
  • It could interact socially just like humans.
  • Status: Currently under research and development.

4. Self-Awareness

  • The ultimate stage of AI where machines have their own consciousness, sentiments, and self-awareness.
  • They would understand their own existence and states.
  • Status: Does not exist; purely theoretical.

🌐 Domains of AI

AI is divided into three major domains that help machines understand different types of inputs (data, visuals, and text).

1. Data Science (DS)

Data Science is the domain of AI related to data systems and processes. Its goal is to extract hidden knowledge, patterns, and insights from large amounts of raw data.

  • Sources of Bulk Data: Data isn't just from websites! Smartphones, AI applications, social media (chatting, buying, watching videos), and searching are huge sources of bulk data.
  • Use Cases: Predicting weather, stock market analysis, targeted advertising.

2. Computer Vision (CV)

Computer Vision enables machines to "see" and interpret visual information from the real world, such as photographs and videos.

  • Use Cases: Facial recognition on smartphones, medical image analysis (like X-rays), self-driving cars identifying stop signs.

3. Natural Language Processing (NLP)

NLP is the branch of AI that deals with the interaction between computers and human language. It allows machines to read, understand, and derive meaning from human languages.

  • Use Cases: Voice assistants (Alexa, Siri), email spam filtering, language translation (Google Translate), chatbots.

🧐 Deep Blue vs. Modern AI

When IBM's Deep Blue defeated world chess champion Garry Kasparov in 1997, it was a massive milestone. But Deep Blue was a Reactive Machine. It simply calculated millions of possible moves per second and chose the best one based on programmed rules. It couldn't learn or improve.

Modern game-playing AI, like Google DeepMind's AlphaZero, uses Machine Learning (a subset of AI). It was given just the rules of chess and played against itself millions of times to learn strategy. This is a massive leap from Reactive Machines to systems with memory and learning capabilities!

🧠 How Do The 3 Domains Connect?

Think of a self-driving car. It uses all three domains simultaneously:

  • Computer Vision (CV): Cameras look at the road, identifying pedestrians, lanes, and traffic lights.
  • Data Science (DS): The car analyzes the data (speed, GPS, traffic patterns) to calculate the fastest and safest route.
  • Natural Language Processing (NLP): The driver says "Take me home," and the car understands the voice command.
