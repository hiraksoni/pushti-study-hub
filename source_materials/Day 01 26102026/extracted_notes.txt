Part 1: How the Brain Learns & Stores Memories

1. Input vs. Recall ("All input is never the answer, recalling is very important")

What it means: Reading a textbook 10 times is passive "input." It creates an illusion of knowing, but fades quickly.

The rule: True memory strength happens during active recall (forcing the brain to retrieve the image or story without looking at the sheet). If you spend 20% of your time encoding and 80% recalling, retention jumps dramatically.

2. The Three Sensory Channels (VAK Model)

The brain receives all experiences through three doors:

V – Visual: What you see (pictures, colors, shapes).

A – Auditory: What you hear (sounds, voices, rhythms).

K – Kinesthetic: What you feel physically and emotionally (textures, movement, emotion, temperature).

When all three fire together, the brain perceives it as a live personal event, which embeds deeply into long-term memory instead of fleeting semantic memory.

3. Brain Filter Plates (The NLP Deletion/Distortion/Generalization Model)

Pushti mentioned: "distrust, detect, destroy, generalize, your brain has filter plates."

In cognitive psychology, the brain receives millions of bits of sensory information every second. To prevent overwhelm, the brain's internal filters process data in three ways:

Delete (or "Destroy"): Drops 99% of unimportant background details (like the color of a random person's shoes).

Distort (or "Detect/Shift"): Alters raw reality to fit pre-existing expectations or emotional states.

Generalize: Bundles repeated events together ("all stoves are hot").

The Takeaway for Her: Plain text words get deleted by the brain's filters because the brain deems them boring. To bypass the filter and turn information into "reference data", information must be converted into vivid, emotionally engaging, absurd pictures.

Part 2: The Core Technique — "Photogenic" (Photographic) Visualization

The class outlined a 3-step ladder to photographic memory:

Step 1: Words to Thinking / Concept (translating abstract symbols like letters into a tangible concept).

Step 2: Words to Picture (translating the concept into an instantaneous, 3D mental snapshot).

Step 3: Improvement of Visualization (adding movement, emotion, kinesthetic weight, and sensory detail).

When she builds her 15-word stories or table links, ensure her mental images follow the S.E.E. Principle (Senses, Exaggeration, Energetic Action):

E – Energetic Action / Interaction: Two items must actively smash, melt, collide, or interact with each other, not just sit side-by-side.
