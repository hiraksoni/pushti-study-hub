The 5 Anatomy Subsystems of a Robot

1. Sensors (Input)

Eyes & ears that gather environmental data (IR, Ultrasonic, LDR, Temperature).

2. Processor (Brain)

Microcontroller (QBrik) that executes rules, calculates logic, and makes decisions.

3. Actuators (Muscles)

Convert electrical signals into motion, sound, or light (DC Motors, Servos, Buzzers).

4. Power Supply (Heart)

Supplies steady current to all circuits (Rechargeable Li-ion packs, 9V batteries).

5. Structure (Skeleton)

Qbits beams, chassis, and brackets providing physical rigidity and support.

Core Triad

Sense → Think → Act: If a machine lacks any of these three, it is not a true robot!

5 Core Engineering Components of XR

1. Displays & Optics

Micro-OLED screens with Fresnel or Pancake lenses focusing light at close range.

2. Low-Latency GPUs

Motion-to-photon latency under 20ms at 90Hz+ to prevent vestibular motion sickness.

3. Tracking: 3DoF vs 6DoF

3DoF (orientation only) vs 6DoF (orientation + X, Y, Z translation via SLAM cameras).

4. 3D Spatial Audio

Binaural sound algorithms dynamically calculating ear arrival delay and volume.

Indicates the Start or End boundary of a program.

Input/Output (Parallelogram)

Receiving data from user (e.g. Input Age) or printing results to screen.

Decision (Diamond)

Evaluates a Boolean condition yielding True (Yes) or False (No) divergence.

AI vs ML vs Deep Learning

1. Artificial Intelligence (Broadest Ring)

Any machine technique that mimics human intellect, perception, or decision making.

2. Machine Learning (Subset of AI)

Algorithms that discover rules automatically from sample data without hardcoded rules.

3. Deep Learning (Subset of ML)

Multi-layered artificial neural networks inspired by biological brain neurons processing massive data.

Digit Recognition & Computer Vision

How Computers Read Handwritten Numbers (MNIST):

A 28×28 pixel grid converts each square into a grayscale brightness number (0 to 255). A convolutional neural network extracts loops, curves, and vertical strokes to deduce whether the digit is a 6, 8, or 3.

The 5-Stage AI Lifecycle & 4Ws Canvas Methodology

From Problem Scoping to Data Acquisition, Modelling, Evaluation, and Deployment.

The 4Ws Problem Scoping Canvas (Stage 1)

Before training an AI model, developers must clearly define the problem using the 4Ws:

1. Who? (Stakeholders)

Who is facing the problem? Who will use the solution? (e.g., School librarians, students who cannot find books).

2. What? (The Problem)

What is the nature of the issue? (e.g., Students waste 20 minutes finding books matching their reading level).

3. Where? (Context)

Where does this occur? (e.g., School physical library catalogue terminals and online portal).

4. Why? (Value Proposition)

Why solve this with AI? (e.g., Machine learning recommends personalized books based on past loans, increasing reading time by 40%).

Machine Learning Taxonomy

Supervised Learning (Teacher Guided)
  • Classification: Predicts discrete categorical labels (Dog vs Cat, Spam vs Not Spam).
  • Regression: Predicts continuous numerical quantities (House prices, temperature forecasts).
Unsupervised Learning (Self Discovery)
  • Clustering: Groups unlabelled items by mathematical proximity (Spotify user music taste clusters).
  • Association Rules: Market basket rules ("Customers buying Bread also buy Butter 85% of the time").

The 5 Full Stages of an AI Project

1. Problem Scoping

Define goals using 4Ws Canvas.

2. Data Acquisition

Gather raw data via sensors, surveys, or APIs.

3. Data Exploration

Clean errors, remove outliers, inspect plots.

4. Modelling

Train algorithm (Supervised or Unsupervised).

5. Evaluation

Test accuracy with unseen 20% test data.

6. Deployment

Deploy to real world and monitor drift.

Responsible AI & Sustainable Development Goals Ethics

Learn how to build AI systems that are fair, transparent, privacy-respecting, and socially beneficial.

The 4 Ethical Pillars of Responsible AI

1. Fairness & Non-Bias

Training data must represent all demographic groups fairly to prevent AI from amplifying human prejudices.

2. Transparency (No Black Box)

Critical AI in healthcare or justice must provide human-understandable explanations for decisions.

3. Privacy & Data Security

Biometric scans, voices, and personal records must be strictly encrypted with informed user consent.

4. Accountability & Safety

When an autonomous machine fails, human creators and organizations must hold legal and moral responsibility.
